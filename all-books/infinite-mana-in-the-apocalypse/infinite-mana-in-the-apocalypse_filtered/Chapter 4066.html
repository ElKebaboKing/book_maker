The seed pulsed with light that suggested consideration.

I can grow without cessation, it replied, its voice like wind through grain that would never exist. I can ensure that what is planted always yields. I can make the soil rich regardless of its nature.

The First Farmer shook his head gently. That is mechanics. I speak of principle. When someone works toward something...anything, their effort should harvest results. When they plant kindness, they should harvest connection. When they sow knowledge, they should harvest understanding.

You wish me to accelerate not just farming but existence itself, the seed said with growing comprehension. To make reality more generous with its rewards.

Trying!

I could speed the growth of not just plants but possibilities. Make patience yield results faster. Transform waiting from emptiness into active cultivation.

Not guaranteeing success...that would break important lessons, but ensuring that something always grows from what is planted. Even from salted earth, even from poisoned ground, something could emerge if the farmers intent is true.

They continued this exchange, philosopher and possibility discussing what principle meant.

But as their conversation reached its crescendo, the seeds light dimmed with sorrow.

I am missing a critical component, it admitted, its voice heavy with the weight of incomplete potential. I cannot become a Seed of Perpetual Harvest without a perpetual force of harvest driving me. The principle is sound, the mechanics are prepared, but the engine... the engine is absent.

The First Farmers expression crumbled from hope to sorrow with speed that suggested practice.

He had failed before. He would fail again. But this failure felt heavier because the success had been so close.

He tried.

He tried again!

And still he failed.

They watched such a thing happen again and again.

Until the First Farmer cried.

He cradled the failed seed against his chest, and when he spoke, his words carried the particular pain of dreams deferred.

Even though I failed here today, there will come a time when principles bloom like weeds after rain.
