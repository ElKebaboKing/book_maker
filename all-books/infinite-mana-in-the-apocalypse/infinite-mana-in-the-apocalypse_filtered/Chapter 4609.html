Chapter 4607: The First Lesson VII

How does one conduct themselves when they stand in a position of genuine superiority?

This was a question that revealed the true character of entities across all of Existence.

Many, when they achieved victory or accumulated power or wealth beyond their opponents, felt an overwhelming urge to express that superiority loudly and visibly. They needed others to witness and acknowledge their elevation.

The rich would throw gold coins at the poor in contempt, not to help but to humiliate.

The powerful would grind the weak into dust while laughing, not because it served any purpose but simply to demonstrate that they could.

These beings who expressed their superiority through such displays held immense arrogance and pride.

And more often than not, such beings were inevitably the first to collapse spectacularly and lose absolutely everything they had accumulated.

Because they mistook temporary advantage for permanent supremacy. They confused a single victory for eternal dominance.

No, the truly superior entities did not revel excessively in their gains or gloat over their elevated position.

They simply kept moving forward steadily because this was what they needed to do.

Not out of humility or false modesty, but out of understanding that superiority was not a destination to celebrate but merely a waypoint on an endless journey.

To keep moving forward without pause. This was the way.

At this moment, Noah was moving forward.

He looked down at the figure of Raijin Katsuro kneeling on the smooth arena floor, his tremendous power unable to be expressed despite his repeated desperate attempts.

The warrior monks body trembled with contained fury and shock as he tried again and again to break free from the linguistic prison surrounding him.

Noah felt the seven Logos of Imprisonment pulse and tremble under the constant pressure.

THE Civilizational Authority was so overwhelmingly powerful that even contained by The First Tongue itself, it continuously strained against the barriers.

Noah had to constantly smooth out and reinforce the connections between individual Phonemes that comprised each Logos, ensuring the grammatical links remained intact under impossible stress.

If it was not him specifically, if any other entity attempted to maintain this prison, Raijin would be able to exploit the smallest weakness and shatter the containment to continue the battle.

This was the terrifying nature of THE classification.

Even while completely surrounded by multiple Logos of The First Tongue working in perfect harmony, Raijins sheer existential weight was almost breaking apart the very links and linguistic locks that bound him.

Noah had to continuously monitor each connection, each grammatical flow, ensuring no critical links fractured while simultaneously preparing for the next phase.

Anyone else who did not possess his Way, anyone whose Existence was not fundamentally aligned with The First Tongue itself, would not be capable of maintaining such precise control.

So no, Noah did not stand on ceremony at this critical moment.

He did not gloat over his impossible achievement or waste time celebrating.

He had promised to bring this entity to his knees, and so he had done exactly that.

Now the noise of recognition and reward could begin.

In Existence, there were many paths to power and countless methodologies for advancement.

Some entities accumulated strength through patient cultivation across eons, building their foundations slowly and carefully.

Some stole power from others through violence and consumption, growing rapidly but unstably.

Some inherited power from ancient lineages or were born with advantages others could never possess.

But the rarest path, the one that defied all conventional understanding, was the path of those who built bridges where none should exist.

Who looked at the impossible gap between themselves and entities of higher Scales and decided that gap was merely an engineering problem to be solved through sufficient understanding and effort.

Noah had just demonstrated that The First Tongue could serve as such a bridge.

Not through overwhelming personal power, but through linguistic authority that operated on principles deeper than Civilizational classification.

The battle against Raijin had proven something fundamental: mastery of The First Tongue could allow those of lower Scales to stand against, suppress, and even imprison those of higher Scales.

The bridge had been built and tested.

And Noah stood upon it calmly, already looking toward the next impossible challenge.

Because truly superior entities did not stop to celebrate their achievements.

They simply kept moving forward.

Always forward.

Toward whatever came next.
