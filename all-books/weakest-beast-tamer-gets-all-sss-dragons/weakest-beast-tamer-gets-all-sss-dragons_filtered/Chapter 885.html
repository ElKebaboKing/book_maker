Mins improvement was a huge surprise...

It was another technique Ren hadnt expected to see until much later in his development.

Skin secretions from the amphibian. Substances that Ren had noticed before losing access to his funguss complete knowledge and hadnt analyzed fully because he hadnt expected to encounter them so soon.

The mechanism was elegant in its brutality. The Amphibs skin constantly produced various compounds, oils, mucus, enzymes, as part of its natural biology. Most amphibians did this to maintain skin moisture and provide basic defense against parasites and minor injuries.

But Mins control over water had apparently reached a level where he could manipulate exactly how those secretions were constituted and behaved upon contact. It was something like specific chemicals being released in concentrations that caused a massive exothermic reaction when exposed to oxygen or when mixed with foreign substances or pressure.

The energy from the Mantiss strike had apparently acted as a catalyst. The moment it hit, it triggered a cascade that reacted... Violently.

The result was an explosion that turned defensive hide into an offensive weapon. Every attack became a risk now, every strike carried the possibility of detonating in that specific area of the amphibians body.

Min had even waited until Ren calculated the speed with his preemptive strike and committed to a bigger, slower one.

It was brilliant... Frustrating, but brilliant.

It seemed, making jokes was not the job of an "unintelligent clown" as Jin had once called him.

The Amphibian lunged forward suddenly, faster than its previous movements.

More surprises...

Min had apparently been feinting slowness, keeping the beasts full speed in reserve for a trap.

It even attempted to follow up with a bite while the mantis was still recovering from being thrown backward, its massive jaws opening and snapping forward with surprising speed for something so large.

But the Mantis had already activated her own incredible agility, using her superior speed to twist in midair and avoid the incoming attack by maybe 0.2-0.3 seconds. Close enough that it could feel the displaced air from the jaws snapping shut, but far enough that teeth didnt connect with the exoskeleton.

Too close still... Much too close.

Rens jaw tightened.

Min snapped his fingers in disappointment at the bites failure, but he smiled with satisfaction at seeing the explosive techniques effectiveness. "Do you like it?" he called across the arena with pride he didnt try to hide. "I spent weeks perfecting the exact mixtures of secretions and the concentration necessary to make the explosive defense work consistently. Turns out water isnt just for healing or cutting or making ice. It was a defensive tank with spikes strategy carried to perfect execution against the Mantiss charged scythes: receive attacks deliberately, convert each impact into a counterattack through explosions, and prevent any damage from its elemental weakness.

The approach favored a creature with massive bulk and resistance that could absorb punishment during an extended period. And with the capability to convert passive defenses into active offensives through explosive magic chemistry, it transformed every enemy attack into a risk that had to be carefully evaluated.

Attack too hard and youd trigger bigger explosions. Attack too soft and you didnt penetrate enough to matter.

Ren observed the dynamic developing and recognized that the standard approach of simply hitting harder wouldnt work here.

He needed to adapt his method of attack, to find a weakness he could exploit in a way that negated the advantages Min had built so carefully.

He allowed the Mantis to maintain distance using its superior speed while using its own water and light elements to heal the damage shed sustained. The healing wasnt as efficient as the amphibians... the Mantiss water affinity was secondary rather than primary, but it was sufficient to close the cracks in its exoskeleton and reduce the burns from first-degree to superficial redness.

This was going to be very difficult indeed.

Not yet, anyway.

The Mantis changed her approach completely, abandoning direct physical attacks in favor of elemental manipulation that didnt require contact.

It was much less efficient than charging the scythes with wood energy for close-range strikes. Direct contact allowed for maximum energy transfer. Ranged manipulation meant significant power loss through distance and required much more mana to achieve similar effects.

But efficiency didnt matter when contact meant taking those explosions point blank. Better to spend 5x to 10 the mana on a safe attack than spend 1 the mana on an attack that would injure you much more than it injured the opponent.

The Mantis could also control wood over a much larger area so it began exerting control over the element to which amphibians like the Royal Invisible Amphibians species had fundamental vulnerability.

It wasnt simply making plants grow from the ground, although that was part of the technique. It was more subtle control over what Matilda called "seeds of life"... the capability to manipulate living growth across a large area in ways that would bombard the enormous beast from multiple directions simultaneously.

Could the Amphibs explosions counter attacks if its entire body was invaded rather than just struck at specific points? That was the question Rens tactic was designed to answer.

Roots began growing from the sand around and beneath the Amphibian, not attacking directly but preparing a massive bombardment.
