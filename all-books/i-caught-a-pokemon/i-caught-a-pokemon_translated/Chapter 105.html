Accompanied by Sabrina, Ethan Vale arrived at the Silph Co. headquarters.

Traffic flowed endlessly through the streets, and the building's exterior had long since been fully repaired. If they hadn't experienced it firsthand, no one would have imagined that fierce battles had once taken place here—or that the building had even been occupied by Team Rocket.

After passing through security, the two were escorted by a male secretary to the president's office on the top floor.

On this second visit, Ethan noticed that the building's security had been significantly strengthened. Armed guards accompanied by Pokémon patrolled nearly every floor.

It seemed that after the previous incident, Mr. Silph had realized that only by possessing his own defensive force could he properly protect the company.

The secretary knocked on the office door, and the two once again met President Silph.

The old man looked radiant and full of energy, showing none of his age.

Seeing Ethan, Silph immediately stood up and stepped forward with a warm smile.

"Mr. Ethan Vale, Miss Sabrina, welcome."

"Mr. Silph, you're looking wonderful," Ethan remarked. "Did something good happen recently?"

Silph shook hands with them both. His experienced eyes briefly swept over Ethan and Sabrina, seemingly noticing that the atmosphere between them was somewhat different than before.

He chuckled.

"Having the two of you here is already good news for me."

As expected of a company president—his conversational skills were truly masterful.

Ethan glanced back at the security guards standing outside the office.

"In such a short time, you've managed to build such a well-trained security force. That's impressive."

"The impressive thing isn't me," Silph laughed. "It's the money in my hands. In this world, any problem that can be solved with money isn't really a problem."

Then his tone shifted.

"A few days ago, I watched the video of your battle against the giant Tentacruel online. To be honest, while your mask is certainly distinctive, your equipment is far too crude."

"Well..." Ethan scratched his head awkwardly. "To be honest, it isn't really equipment at all. I bought that mask in the Game Corner after bargaining for only one hundred Poké Dollars. As for the cloak, it's just a raincoat I picked up somewhere."

"Oh?"

Silph blinked in surprise.

"Let me see that mask."

Ethan searched through his backpack, retrieved the Gengar mask, and handed it over.

After examining it carefully, Silph frowned with obvious disdain.

"This won't do. Such low-quality materials aren't worthy of my Silph Scope."

"Our company is famous for cutting-edge technology. As your friend, how could I allow you to continue using something this crude?"

Turning to his secretary, he instructed:

"Go to the Equipment Department immediately. Have them create a new mask and cloak. Also, bring over our newest line of outdoor products."

"Understood."

The secretary bowed and left.

"Isn't this a bit too much?" Ethan hesitated. "It must be expensive."

"It doesn't matter."

Silph waved his hand dismissively.

"Compared to the favor you did for me, this is insignificant."

The three chatted for a while longer.

When Silph learned that Ethan still hadn't used the Master Ball, he smiled approvingly.

"The Master Ball is extraordinary. It truly shouldn't be used casually."

"Remember—save it for the most important moment, to capture the most important Pokémon."

"Don't worry."

Ethan nodded.

"I'll use it carefully."

Since Silph still had business matters to attend to, Ethan and Sabrina didn't want to disturb him further. They politely said their goodbyes and headed to a lounge downstairs.

Coincidentally, it was the very same room where Ethan and Sabrina had hidden inside a locker to evade Team Rocket during the previous incident.

Dropping onto a sofa, Ethan sighed nostalgically.

"Even now, I can still vividly remember Team Rocket's attack."

Sabrina glanced toward the locker.

Recalling something, she softly responded,

"Mhm..."

Her cheeks carried a faint blush.

After stealing a glance at Ethan, she asked,

"Where will you go after leaving here?"

"Viridian Gym, of course."

Ethan replied,

"I've heard that after Giovanni disappeared, Agatha temporarily became the Gym Leader there. The Gym's specialty also changed from Normal-type to Ghost-type Pokémon."

"Agatha won't be easy to defeat, but since it's my final badge, I have to beat her no matter what."

Sabrina asked again,

"And after you obtain all eight badges, you'll challenge the Pokémon League?"

"Of course. Why?"

Ethan looked at her curiously.

After all, this was the standard progression. As a Gym Leader, Sabrina certainly knew that.

As expected, she continued:

"Then what are your plans after the League?"

Ethan thought for a moment.

"After that, I'll probably leave Kanto and continue traveling through other regions."

"Leave Kanto..."

Sabrina lowered her eyes thoughtfully before nodding softly.

"I understand."

Before Ethan could figure out what she meant, the secretary returned carrying a large case.

Opening it, he began taking out item after item.

A windbreaker.

Athletic pants.

Running shoes.

A utility belt.

Knee pads.

Wrist guards.

A backpack.

And much more.

The collection practically replaced Ethan's entire wardrobe.

The secretary explained:

"These are our company's newest products. For example, this windbreaker is made from specially developed materials. It's waterproof, fire-resistant, and highly durable. Even knives can't easily cut through it."

Picking up the jacket, Ethan found it light, comfortable, and stylish.

At this point, there was no reason to refuse.

He entered a nearby changing room and changed into the new outfit.

The results were remarkable.

Ethan was already naturally handsome, but after changing clothes, he looked far more energetic and athletic, radiating youthful vitality.

While he couldn't claim to charm every girl he met, when he stepped back out, Sabrina's gaze clearly contained a bit more admiration than before.

Just then, a researcher entered carrying a newly crafted mask.

Although it retained the shape of a Gengar face, it had undergone significant upgrades.

First, the material had changed.

The cheap plastic was gone, replaced by a lightweight alloy that reflected a subtle metallic sheen.

It looked more sophisticated, more durable, and far more professional.

The biggest change, however, was the lenses.

The old glass eye pieces had been replaced with the Silph Scope itself.

To ordinary objects, it appeared completely normal.

But whenever something mysterious or hidden was involved, it would reveal their true forms.
