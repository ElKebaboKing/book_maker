That afternoon, inside the Ice Field competitors' waiting room...

Chloe Cerise sat leisurely in a chair, occasionally swinging her fair legs back and forth. Her auburn ponytail swayed gently, making her normally calm and composed demeanor seem a little more lively and adorable.

On the table before her lay her phone, positioned horizontally.

On its screen, footage from Gary Oak's previous tournament matches was playing.

In the video, two Pokémon were battling fiercely. As the commentator's passionate narration echoed from the speakers, Chloe jotted notes onto a sheet of paper, only occasionally glancing up at the screen.

By the time the phone had become warm from continuous playback, she picked up the paper and walked over to Ethan Vale.

"I have to admit," she sighed, "compared to you, this Gary guy really has a huge number of Pokémon."

She handed him the paper.

"Look. In each of his previous three matches, he used an entirely different lineup. There's no pattern at all. I honestly have no idea which Pokémon he'll send out today."

At the moment, Ethan was crouching on the floor, carefully brushing Jolteon's fur.

After taking a quick glance at her notes, he smiled and shook his head.

Back at school, Chloe had always been an excellent student. Growing up with Professor Cerise as her father had also influenced her to organize and analyze information systematically.

However, she still lacked extensive knowledge about Pokémon.

Beyond the basics, she couldn't yet identify deeper details such as Abilities, strategic weaknesses, or advanced battle patterns.

"If you can't figure it out," Ethan said casually, "then don't overthink it."

"No matter what Pokémon he sends out, I'll just defeat them all."

"Besides, having more Pokémon doesn't necessarily make someone stronger. If you own too many, it's impossible to properly communicate with all of them or devote enough time to training each one."

Chloe thought about it before nodding.

"You're right."

Then she asked,

"Do you think you can beat him?"

"Maybe."

Ethan shrugged.

"But who can honestly guarantee victory?"

"That said, I do know Gary fairly well."

"He usually acts arrogant, but once he's on the battlefield, he's surprisingly calm and has good tactical instincts."

"He's especially skilled at using Ground-types and physically powerful Pokémon."

"So he definitely isn't someone to underestimate."

At that moment, the stadium loudspeakers announced:

"Contestant Ethan Vale, your match will begin shortly. Please report to the player entrance immediately. Contestant Ethan Vale..."

Checking the time, Ethan stood up and recalled Jolteon into its Poké Ball.

Leaving Chloe only his confident back, he smiled.

"Well, it's time for me to go."

"Make sure you cheer for me."

Chloe smiled warmly, clasping her hands in front of her chest as she watched him walk away.

"Do you even have to ask?"

"I'll definitely be cheering for you."

Ice Field Stadium

"Ladies and gentlemen, welcome to today's final match in the Ice Field!"

The host's energetic voice echoed throughout the arena.

"This is also the final battle of the entire fourth round! Once this match concludes, the winner will join the other victors as one of the Top 16, advancing into the official tournament bracket!"

The spectators, who had waited eagerly for this matchup, erupted into thunderous applause.

"First, let's welcome the contestant in the red corner—Ethan Vale!"

"He is currently the tournament's most popular competitor!"

"In the previous round, he eliminated one of the championship favorites and has advanced here with a perfect record, without losing even a single Pokémon in any battle!"

Ethan walked calmly out of the entrance tunnel, smiling as he waved to the crowd.

His handsome face was magnified across the giant stadium screens, clearly visible to every spectator.

The audience exploded into deafening cheers.

With both his impressive strength and outstanding appearance, Ethan had become the most popular remaining contestant in the tournament.

The biker gang screamed themselves hoarse in support.

However, by this stage of the competition, Ethan's fanbase had already grown dozens of times larger than before.

Their voices were now completely drowned out by the overwhelming roar of the crowd.

"And now, please welcome the contestant in the green corner—Gary Oak!"

"His enormous collection of Pokémon has left a lasting impression throughout the tournament!"

"As one of this year's championship favorites, he has defeated numerous powerful opponents and has demonstrated exceptional skill!"

Gary stepped out of the tunnel with one hand tucked into his pocket, wearing his usual cool, confident expression.

What irritated him, however, was obvious even without looking into the stands.

The applause he received was nowhere near as enthusiastic as Ethan's.

Hmph.

Once this battle is over, everyone will know who's truly superior.

Meanwhile, Ethan stepped onto the trainer's platform and glanced briefly at Gary before surveying the battlefield.

The Ice Field was rectangular, apparently converted from a swimming pool.

A thick sheet of solid ice covered the entire surface, while dozens of ice blocks of various sizes were scattered around the arena.

Even from the trainer's platform, he could feel the chilly air rising from below.

Seeing that both trainers were ready, the referee announced,

"Both trainers, send out your Pokémon!"

Hearing the command, Gary took out the Poké Ball he had already chosen.

Looking confidently at Ethan, he couldn't help but smile.

He had spent considerable time preparing for this battle.

After studying Ethan's previous matches, he had noticed one consistent pattern:

Ethan loved exploiting type advantages.

Since Ethan didn't own any Ice-type Pokémon, Gary boldly predicted that he would likely send out either a Water-type or a Fire-type Pokémon to counter Gary's expected Ice-type choice and prevent him from gaining the terrain advantage.

After careful consideration, Gary had selected a Pokémon that wasn't weak to either Water or Fire.

"Come out, my Pokémon!"

As his Poké Ball opened, the commentator exclaimed in surprise,

"Oh?! Gary has chosen Fearow!"

"Who would've expected a Normal- and Flying-type Pokémon to appear on the Ice Field? That's certainly an unexpected choice!"

At the same moment, Gary fixed his gaze on the Poké Ball in Ethan's hand.

But when Ethan released his Pokémon and the blue light faded...

The spiky fur and sleek body made Gary's expression instantly change.

Jolteon!?

Why Jolteon?

Electric-type attacks don't have any special advantage against Ice-types!

This is bad...

Fearow wasn't weak to Fire or Water...

But as a Flying-type, it was weak to Electric!

Damn it... I guessed wrong!

Gary clenched his fists.

I thought Ethan was operating on the second level...

I thought I was on the third...

But he actually predicted my prediction!

Seriously...

On an Ice Field, how did he ever decide that an Electric-type should be his opening Pokemon ?
