Saizo's second Pokémon was Parasect.

Facing it, Ethan recalled Gyarados and sent out Charizard instead.

Unfortunately for Parasect, battling Charizard turned into an absolute nightmare. It was nearly roasted into a grilled bug!

By the time Saizo returned the unconscious Parasect to its Poké Ball, wisps of black smoke were still rising from its body, carrying the unmistakable smell of burnt flesh.

Left with no other choice, Saizo reluctantly sent out his most trusted partner—Marowak, the very Pokémon that had secured his victory in the previous round.

After a moment's thought, Ethan also recalled Charizard and chose to send out Haunter, allowing it to experience the atmosphere of the League tournament.

As soon as the battle began, Saizo prepared to have Marowak fight with everything it had in one last desperate attempt.

However, what followed completely overturned everything he expected.

Ethan barely needed to issue any commands.

Haunter's overwhelming strength allowed it to dominate the battle entirely on its own.

Rather than ending the fight quickly, Haunter seemed more interested in playing with its opponent. It constantly teased and toyed with Marowak, leading it around in circles until Marowak had exhausted every last bit of its stamina.

Only then, wearing its usual mischievous grin, did Haunter finally finish the battle.

What frustrated Saizo the most—and left him utterly helpless—was that throughout the entire match, Marowak never managed to land a single hit on Haunter.

At last, the referee raised the red flag high.

"The Green side's Pokémon are all unable to battle! The winner is the Red side!"

As the result was announced, the commentator exclaimed loudly,

"What an unforgettable battle! Across these three matches, Ethan Vale has used six different Pokémon, and every single one claimed a dominant victory! His strength and momentum rival every championship favorite. He has truly become the tournament's biggest dark horse!"

The audience rewarded Ethan with thunderous applause.

With another decisive victory under his belt, the number of his supporters continued to grow rapidly.

After the match, everyone gathered once again at the same dessert café.

Ethan, Chloe, Masha, and Misa sat together, watching the live tournament broadcast on television while waiting for Ash's match to finish.

Unlike Ethan's clean 3–0 victory, Ash's battle turned into a grueling struggle.

Both Trainers were reduced to their final Pokémon.

At the critical moment, it was Squirtle that defeated his opponent's Nidorino, narrowly securing the victory.

"That was way too close," Chloe said worriedly after watching the match. "It's only the second round, and Ash already had such a difficult battle. Won't the later rounds be even tougher?"

"It shouldn't be too much of a problem," Ethan explained. "Even though he almost lost, did you notice? Neither Charizard nor Pikachu appeared. He's probably still hiding some of his strongest cards."

"You're still holding back at a time like this?" Masha rested his hands behind his head and sighed. "If you keep hiding your strength, you might end up hiding it until next year! If it were me, I'd go all out every match and secure my advancement first."

At that moment, Ethan noticed one of the bikers quietly flipping through his wallet in the corner before letting out a silent sigh.

Only then did Ethan suddenly realize something.

Unlike him and Ash, these people couldn't stay in the Athletes' Village, nor did they receive free meals.

Every expense—food, lodging, transportation—came entirely out of their own pockets.

Just covering their daily living costs was already expensive.

"Misa," Ethan said, "I really appreciate all of you coming to cheer me on, but what about your jobs? And aren't some of you still in school? Since you've already shown your support, maybe you should head back. Watching on television would still mean a lot."

Following Ethan's gaze, Misa immediately understood what he meant.

"Don't worry about us, Boss." She waved dismissively. "Honestly, even if we didn't know you, we'd still come every year. We're the leftovers of society—we can't sit still anyway. Whether it's skipping work or skipping class, we'd always come to watch the League and enjoy the excitement."

Masha nodded enthusiastically.

"Exactly! Besides, I brought a bunch of tents. Camping outdoors together with over a dozen friends is actually a lot of fun!"

Hearing that, Chloe lowered her head and glanced at her shoes.

That's... incredibly reckless...

She had always been the model student.

Someone like her would never have skipped school just to watch a tournament.

If her mother hadn't personally arranged leave from school—and if she hadn't wanted so badly to accompany Ethan—she would never have even considered doing something so unconventional.

Then, as the conversation shifted to money, Masha suddenly remembered something.

"Oh, right, Boss! Did you know? I heard this year's prize pool is even bigger than last year's. The champion gets over three million Pokédollars!"

Chloe blinked in surprise.

"There's prize money too?"

"Of course!" Masha shrugged. "Even third place gets more than 800,000 Pokédollars. The Pokémon League isn't exactly short on money. Trainers work incredibly hard to compete here. Besides fame, what do you think they're after? Prize money is definitely part of it!"

Prize money...

Ethan nodded thoughtfully.

If he managed to win the championship, that reward alone would free him from worrying about money for a very long time.

Although the tournament schedule had become shorter with fewer remaining competitors, the second round still took a full day and a half to complete.

Compared to the opening round, however, things were much more relaxed.

Every Trainer now had much longer breaks between matches, and no one had to battle late into the night anymore.

Beyond the daytime matches, several streets surrounding the Indigo Stadium came alive every evening with stalls, performances, and festival events.

That night, enormous illuminated parade floats decorated like Pokémon rolled slowly through the streets while upbeat music filled the air, adding even more excitement to the League festival.

After dinner, Ethan and Chloe strolled through the streets together, sampling local snacks while enjoying the lively scenery.

When they passed a plush toy stand, Ethan bought a cute stuffed Pokémon doll and gave it to Chloe.

However, now that Ethan had become somewhat famous, spectators frequently recognized him.

Many enthusiastically approached to greet him.

Some asked for his autograph.

Others wanted to take commemorative photos with him.

After politely dealing with more than a dozen groups, Ethan sighed helplessly.

Seeing his expression, Chloe couldn't help but laugh.

"Ethan, you're becoming quite the celebrity now."

"A celebrity?" Ethan shook his head with a wry smile. "More like a public name."

Although his growing popularity was undeniable, it hadn't changed his mindset in the slightest.

He was still the same person he had always been.

Fame wasn't going to make him arrogant or complacent.
