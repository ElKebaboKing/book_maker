# *I Caught a Pokémon*: English translation continuity notes

## Scope and alignment

These notes compare all 150 available Webnovel English chapters with the corresponding Chinese chapters saved here. The English release skips the Chinese catalog's entry 92, an author note (`上架感言`). **English 1–91 = raw 1–91; English 92–150 = raw 93–151.** The numbers in the rest of this document are *English* chapter numbers unless marked “raw.” The remaining raw chapters have no matching published English chapter in this collection.

The comparison covered every chapter's title and text structure, tracked recurring terminology across the full run, and checked parallel narrative passages across the opening, the Gym journey, the Team Rocket/Mewtwo material, and the League tournament. The rules below describe observable translator practice, not a license to add new plot facts. Later English files contain Webnovel creator notes, Patreon links, and advertisements; these are outside the novel's prose.

## Prose and localization pattern

1. **Keep the source scene and causal sequence.** The English version is generally close to the Chinese events, including battle tactics, the protagonist's prior-life Pokémon knowledge, the light romance/comedy, and abrupt chapter endings. Ch. 14, for example, keeps both reasons Ethan wants Charmander: Charizard's strength *and* its cool design. Ch. 70 keeps his fear that Mewtwo glimpsed private memories. Do not “improve” a scene by changing what happened or what a character knows.
2. **Write fluent, direct English rather than copying Chinese syntax.** Recast long topic–comment sentences into clear subject–verb sentences. Ch. 3 changes a phrase meaning roughly “the three royal families” into **the three starters / starter Pokémon**, because that is what the characters mean in context. Ch. 99 turns `乘兴而来，败兴而归` into “They had come with great expectations and left in complete defeat.” Translate the effect of an idiom, not its individual words.
3. **Give dialogue room to breathe.** The translation often moves a spoken line into its own paragraph and separates a physical beat from the next line. Ch. 1 splits Rattata's impact into the throw, “Bang!”, and the railing strike; ch. 92 separates Ethan clearing his throat from his next speech. Use standard double quotation marks and contractions where the voice calls for them.
4. **Keep battles legible and energetic.** Name each move clearly, state who acts and what the hit does, and retain the trainer/referee rhythm. Ch. 1 changes the referee's announcement to “Rattata is unable to battle! Ralts wins!” Ch. 135–143 keeps announcer commentary and type-matchup explanations for readers following the tournament. Short standalone beats and exclamation marks are part of this translation's action style; use them where the scene earns them.
5. **Preserve humor and voice.** The English retains the protagonist's self-aware franchise knowledge, the banter around Looker's codename (ch. 27), the gender/romance jokes, the drama of Ash's eating with his mouth full (ch. 123), and Gary's competitive inner monologue (ch. 147–150). Localize a joke so its target is clear in English, but avoid adding a new joke or personality trait.
6. **Use concise, informative chapter titles.** Most titles translate the subject and turn an idiom into an English hook: `一球超人` → “The One-Ball Wonder” (ch. 5), `一穿三` → “Sweeping the Match” (ch. 57), `年度狗血情感大戏` → “The Year's Most Dramatic Soap Opera Under the Sea” (ch. 110). For new work, use a consistent `Chapter N: Title` heading. The published files vary between colons and dashes and sometimes have stray middle dots; these are formatting artifacts, not a style rule.
7. **Separate story from platform material.** The Chinese ch. 1 closes with an author pitch and `(本章完)`; many later English files end with Patreon promotions, unrelated novel advertisements, or `CREATORS' THOUGHTS` blocks. Do not carry any of these into a clean continuation unless the user specifically wants a separately labeled author note.

## Inconsistencies and errors to resolve, not imitate

- **Beast Tamer's name:** raw `小明` is **Damian** in ch. 11–12 and 133–136, but the same Sandshrew trainer is **A.J.** in ch. 131. Choose one name for continuation and any cleanup. **Damian** is the dominant choice in this translation; **A.J.** appears to be a one-chapter lapse.
- **Cerulean sister:** raw `樱花` is **Daisy** in ch. 6–9 and 108–111, then **Sakura** in ch. 112–114. Continue with **Daisy** for the same person.
- **Pokémon identity:** raw `刺甲贝` in ch. 96 is **Cloyster** (as elsewhere in ch. 95–96), but its opening English paragraph says **Shellder**. Continue with **Cloyster** at that point in the battle.
- **Pokémon Tech girl:** raw `优藤圣代` appears as **Giselle** in ch. 87 and **Serena Yuto** throughout ch. 141–145. The later tournament arc establishes **Serena Yuto** more extensively; use one form consistently once decided.
- **Looker:** the English oscillates between **Handsome Guy**, **Handsome**, and **Looker** beyond the introductory joke. Use **Looker** in regular narration; use “Handsome Guy” only where the codename itself is being discussed.
- **Team Rocket trio:** ch. 112 unexpectedly uses **Musashi** in one line where surrounding chapters call her **Jessie**. Continue with **Jessie**.
- **Proofreading noise:** some headings are cut off or end with `·`; ad copy and unrelated titles appear inside the English article element. The translation also sometimes over-explains or misidentifies a Pokémon. Verify names, move effects, counts, and who speaks against the Chinese source before using a passage as a model.

## Working checklist for the next translation

1. Align by catalog position, including the skipped raw entry 92; do not assume file number equals story chapter number after English 91. The immediate next untranslated entry after English ch. 150 is raw file **152**.
2. Use the 150 published English chapters alongside their matching Chinese originals as the fixed reference for translation style. Read the immediately preceding story chapters for continuity, but do not treat newly translated chapters as the style standard. Translate each new raw chapter's story text in full.
3. Apply the established character, Pokémon, move, location, badge, and organization terms above. Add a new term to a running glossary only after checking its first appearance and later context.
4. Preserve plot information and point of view; edit syntax, paragraph breaks, idioms, and dialogue for natural English.
5. Check the seven inconsistency cases above and remove website/author-promotion material from the story output.
