Juan had not expected this result. He had recognized Dragonite as his greatest threat, dealt with Milotic first, and focused both his remaining Pokémon on it. Even then, he had underestimated its strength.

Dragonite's performance had been astonishing. Walrein had struggled under a two-on-one attack in the first round. Dragonite, by contrast, fought more fiercely as the pressure mounted. It took the initiative and drove Politoed and Whiscash back. Their attacks sometimes reached it, but anyone watching could see that Dragonite held the advantage.

How could it be so strong? Juan thought it could enter the Ever Grande Conference today and make at least the quarterfinals. Was it Ethan's ace?

He sighed at his two motionless Pokémon floating on the pool and recalled them. Their defeat was not entirely bad news. Dragonite had battled through two rounds, ending the second against two opponents alone. It looked too exhausted to fight the third.

Juan's final Pokémon would be his own ace. Without Dragonite, how would Ethan respond? Juan felt a little better—until he remembered that Ethan had three Pokémon he had yet to send out. His Milotic would have to face all three by itself. His spirits sank again.

Juan no longer dreamed of an overwhelming victory or of teaching Ethan a lesson. He hoped he could win somehow, or at least force a draw. Two rounds had changed his ambitions that much.

Ethan recalled his Milotic. When Dragonite returned to his platform, he rubbed its round belly. “Well done. Rest now.” He recalled it too, then looked up as Juan released his last Pokémon.

The third round would be a series of one-on-one battles. Juan had no reason to hold back. His final choice was his ace, Milotic.

Since arriving in Hoenn, Ethan had now seen three Milotic, including his own. The other belonged to Robert, the Coordinator at the Slateport Contest. Ethan's newly evolved Milotic could not yet compare with either Robert's or Juan's. He wondered which of those two was stronger. This battle might give him an answer.

Juan's Milotic stretched gracefully across a platform. Its scales glittered beneath the Gym lights. Like its Trainer, it knew how to make an entrance. Ethan could easily imagine how many admirers Juan had won while performing with it.

What should Ethan use against it? His fingers passed over the Poké Balls at his waist. He still had three Pokémon to try, so he decided to use this rare opportunity to test one of them against a powerful opponent.

He threw a Poké Ball and sent out Metang.

Juan stared at the Pokémon, which had not yet reached its final evolution. He had thought Ethan's Milotic looked newly evolved. Did sending out Metang mean Ethan had no stronger partners left? Or did he believe a Metang alone could beat Juan's ace?

Whatever Ethan meant, Juan saw a chance to recover his standing in front of Lisia and perhaps turn the match around.

Sebastian signaled the start. Both Pokémon attacked at once. Floating above a platform, Metang opened with Confusion. In a field surrounded by water, though, that approach was hard to make work. Milotic simply dove beneath the surface, evaded the move, and slipped closer for an attack from below.

Ethan had expected that tactic and kept Metang from being caught completely off guard. Even so, Milotic had closed the distance and could make full use of both the pool and its superior strength.

The two fought in earnest. Ethan directed Metang well, and Metang performed admirably, but after several exchanges Juan's ace found an opening and knocked it down.

Metang fell toward the pool. Ethan recalled it before it struck the water.

Only when Sebastian confirmed his win did Juan breathe easily again. His Milotic was still in good condition. If it beat the next Pokémon too, victory would draw much nearer.

Ethan's expression did not change. He had known Metang was unlikely to win. That was no lack of faith in it. The first two rounds had shown him that Juan's Pokémon were well trained. They had lost because Dragonite was exceptionally strong, not because Juan's team was weak. His ace would be stronger still, and Metang was not ready to defeat it.

Ethan selected another Poké Ball. Blue light flashed, and Eevee appeared on a platform. It began hopping eagerly from one platform to another.

Juan raised an eyebrow. Metang at least had evolved once. Eevee had not chosen an evolution at all. Was Ethan taking this challenge seriously?

He suspected Ethan had sent out a weak-looking Pokémon to insult him. Fine. Keep doing it. When I beat every one of your partners, we'll see how you feel.

Once the next one-on-one battle began, Juan realized he had judged Eevee too quickly. It performed even better than Metang. Its attacks, defense, and leaps were exceptional for its species. The little Pokémon was far more dangerous than its innocent appearance suggested.

Even when Milotic knocked it into the pool, Eevee paddled back to a platform. It made Juan's ace work for every opening. Milotic could move freely through the water and was the stronger Pokémon, however. After a harder fight than Juan had expected, it defeated Eevee.

Juan could not smile at the result. Milotic had taken more damage. It was not seriously injured, but its condition was now roughly as poor as Dragonite's had been after the second round. If Juan had to face a rested Dragonite now, he doubted Milotic could win.

This might be the most emotionally exhausting Gym challenge of Juan's career. His hopes had risen and fallen too many times already.

Ethan recalled the unconscious Eevee and reached for another Poké Ball. Juan's heart rose into his throat as Ethan threw it.

The fifth Pokémon was not Dragonite. It was Combusken. Juan exhaled in relief, then immediately frowned. What was Ethan thinking? Why send a Fire-type Pokémon into a Water-type Gym, especially on a field covered by water?

Could this Combusken, like Eevee, be strong enough to overcome the type disadvantage? Juan doubted it. Even a Blaziken should struggle against his Milotic here.

He glanced at Milotic's worn condition and faltered. Perhaps, after all, a Blaziken could do it now.

Ethan's eyes sharpened. Metang and Eevee had worn Milotic down. He had chosen Combusken to settle the match.

From the start, Juan felt a hunger to attack that had been less obvious in Ethan's previous two battles. Metang and Eevee had searched for openings, but this was different. Combusken leaped from platform to platform, looking for its chance at every step.

If Juan had kept a cool head, he could have stayed in the water and worn Combusken down with ranged Water-type attacks. Time and the field would have favored Milotic.

But he was impatient to beat Ethan in front of Lisia and prove himself. He knew Ethan might have sent out a Fire type to provoke him, yet he could not bear to retreat from the man he imagined was leading his dear student astray.

Instead of hiding beneath the surface, Juan had Milotic fight half-submerged beside the platforms, trading attacks as Combusken jumped from one to the next.

At first glance, the decision was reasonable. Water beat Fire, and Juan's Milotic had the better field. His mistake was allowing Combusken close. He had also underestimated how quickly Ethan could spot an opening.

One fleeting chance appeared. Combusken struck with Double Kick while leaping and knocked Milotic out of the water onto a platform.

“What?” Juan had been preparing his next command. He stared, then paled as he understood what had happened.

Milotic was like a fish stranded beside a Pokémon skilled in close combat. Misty and Lisia both stood up in the stands. They knew this was the decisive moment.

Combusken landed, turned, and reached Milotic before it could slip back into the pool. Quick Attack closed the last gap and dealt the first hit. Peck and Scratch followed in a tight combination.

The blows left marks across Milotic's once-beautiful scales. It tried to break free, return to the water, and find time to recover its strength. Ethan would not let such a hard-won chance go. Combusken cut off its route and drove another Double Kick into its face.

Milotic fell into the pool at last—but only because Combusken had kicked it there. The battle was over.

Juan's mouth moved without words. Defeat had left him dejected. Then a white light enveloped Combusken, making the sight even harder for him to bear.

He knew that light. Combusken had met the conditions to evolve, and the change had begun naturally. Losing his best Pokémon was painful enough. Now the Pokémon that defeated it was evolving before his eyes.

For Juan, it was a second blow on top of the first.
