No matter how Ash called to Squirtle from the sideline, it showed no sign of waking. The referee raised a flag and announced the result of the first bout.

Ash recalled Squirtle and hurriedly tried to decide whom to send out next. Before he could choose, Pikachu had already taken off on its own, using Quick Attack to charge Butterfree.

Butterfree had no time to dodge. The hit landed squarely. When it tried to fly away, Pikachu grabbed one of its legs and used Thunderbolt, leaving Butterfree looking like a roasted moth.

Pikachu had acted without an order, but thanks to it, the score was even again.

Its condition was worrying, though. It must have spent a great deal of energy before reaching the stadium. Now it was sweating heavily, its movements slow and labored. It looked close to its limit.

Even Chloe, who knew little about battling, could tell something was wrong from the stands.

"Is Ash's Pikachu all right? It looks exhausted."

Ethan nodded. "Remember what I said? Watch. Low stamina and a Pokémon that won't obey him—those are going to be Ash's biggest problems in this match."

With the score at one each, Richie sent out Charmander without hesitation.

Charmander kept breathing fire, forcing Pikachu to dodge over and over until its last reserves of strength were gone. Then Charmander struck with Tackle and knocked it out of bounds.

The crowd had waited a long time, only to get a battle it found dull. More spectators began to boo.

"What is this? Is this really a Top 16 match?"

"Exactly! I've seen better fights in a street performance."

"They should watch the last round's Ice Field match! Gyarados was magnificent. And when those two Thunderbolts collided and threw up that huge column of water—I can still see it!"

Misty and Brock, Ash's traveling companions, sat up awkwardly as they heard the criticism around them. They wanted to defend him, but what could they say?

Pikachu really had been in terrible shape. Ash was not the kind of trainer who put on a grand spectacle, either. The ordinary dirt field offered little chance for the sudden surprises that had made earlier matches so exciting.

The stands were too far from the field for Ash to hear any of it.

With Pikachu defeated and no other choice, he sent out Charizard.

But when Charizard saw that its opponent was its own first form, it could barely muster any interest.

Richie's Charmander attacked first. Its Ember glanced past Charizard, and the provocation seemed to make the larger Pokémon angry. Charizard opened its mouth and loosed a great burst of flame, sending Charmander running all over the field.

Richie had to recall Charmander. He sent out his last Pokémon: a Pikachu with a tuft of hair over its forehead.

Now Charizard seemed to consider its new opponent too small to bother with. It lay down on the field, closed its eyes, and pretended to sleep.

Ash pleaded with it and begged it to get up. Charizard did not move.

The crowd erupted in disbelief. Nothing like this had happened often in the history of the Indigo League.

The referee waited. Once it was clear that Ash could not get Charizard to fight, he raised his flag.

"Charizard has lost the will to battle! Richie wins!"

Ash took a dispirited step backward. He was frustrated, but he pulled himself together, managed a smile, and walked out to shake Richie's hand.

The spectators were nowhere near as accepting. Even the commentator remained stunned for several seconds.

What had just happened? Neither trainer had given them the show they wanted, and then the match had simply ended?

Online opinion was sure to turn against Ash. Fortunately for him, he almost never used electronic devices. The criticism would have little effect on him.

Ethan felt sorry about the result. He had told Ash to spend more time communicating with Charizard, but apparently nothing had changed.

Nearby, spectators were leaving while complaining loudly. Ethan and the others hurried to the competitors' exit to wait for Ash.

A few minutes later, Ash emerged from the tunnel with Pikachu in his arms, looking miserable. He glanced around at all the people who had come because they cared about him and gave a bitter smile.

"Sorry, everyone. I let you down."

Delia, Professor Oak, Misty, and Brock all stepped forward with a few words of comfort and encouragement.

After hearing them out, Ash looked better. He rubbed his nose and finally turned to Ethan.

"I lost, Ethan. It's up to you now!"

Ethan patted him on the shoulder.

"It's all right, kid. Don't get too discouraged. You still have a long road ahead of you. This won't be your only defeat, so you'll get used to it sooner or later!"

Ash stared at him.

Was that supposed to make him feel better? It sounded more like a curse!

Because Ash's match had been delayed, Ethan's match was pushed back as well.

By the time it began, evening was drawing near. The crowd had lost none of its enthusiasm.

Ethan was popular for his looks as well as his skill, and cheers rolled across the stadium. Compared with the reception Ash had just received, it felt like a different world.

Ethan and Jimmy stood at opposite ends of the field.

At the referee's signal, Ethan sent out Venusaur. Jimmy chose Persian.

Neither had a type advantage over the other, but that did nothing to hinder Venusaur.

Once the battle began, Ethan showed Jimmy exactly why he had knocked out so many championship favorites and become the tournament's most popular trainer.

Persian relied on close combat. Venusaur gave it no chance to get close.

Razor Leaf kept it at a distance. Vine Whip restrained it. Sleep Powder and Growth followed. Finally, a charged Solar Beam briefly lit the darkening stadium and drained the last of Persian's strength.
