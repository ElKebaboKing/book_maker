Ethan looked down at the Plain Badge in his hand.

Like the type favored by the Gym that awarded it, the badge was remarkably plain.

It was a yellow diamond with a white border. Simple and ordinary, much like Olivine City's Mineral Badge, it looked as though no one had seen a need to spend much time designing it.

Ethan took out his badge case and placed the Plain Badge in its last empty space. Looking at the two full rows of eight badges, he let out a breath of relief.

He had collected all eight. Now he only had to wait for the Silver Conference to begin.

After exchanging contact numbers with Whitney, he and Erika said goodbye and left the Goldenrod Gym.

The first thing they did was head for a nearby Pokémon Center. None of Ethan's Pokémon was hurt; he wanted to register for the tournament in advance.

He had learned of the option by chance. Rather than queue at the venue in the days before the tournament, a Trainer with eight badges could register at any Pokémon Center.

At the Goldenrod City Pokémon Center, Ethan told Nurse Joy what he needed.

"You're registering for the Conference? Please give me your badges and Pokédex."

She took his badge case and scanned it. Once she had verified the badges, she connected his Pokédex to her computer.

Ethan's records appeared on the screen. Nurse Joy could see his achievements.

Indigo League Champion.

Orange League Honorable Trainer.

Sea Hero of the Whirl Islands.

He had entered only three different competitions, but he had won every one. What an impressive Trainer!

After a few keystrokes, Nurse Joy returned his badge case and Pokédex.

"You're registered. I hope you do well at the Conference."

"Thank you," Ethan said. "When does the Silver Conference begin?"

"On the first of next month."

"And where will it be held?"

"As in previous years, at Silver Stadium in Silver Town, at Mt. Silver."

So, Mt. Silver on the first of next month. Ethan counted the days in his head. He had more than half a month. That was enough time to reach Mt. Silver and make any final adjustments.

Outside the Pokémon Center, he and Erika stood on the street and discussed where to go next.

"Where should we go now?" Erika asked. "Straight to Silver Town?"

"We have time as long as we arrive before the Conference," Ethan said with a smile. "Why don't I take you somewhere else first? You came to catch Grass-type Pokémon, didn't you? And I'm sure there are other places you'd like to see."

"Finding a Pokémon is partly a matter of chance. But there is somewhere I'd like to go..." Erika took her travel guide from her bag, turned to the second page, and pointed to a picture of a bustling city on the water. "I'd like to see this place."

Ethan read the caption: "Altomare, the City of Water—the most beautiful city in the world!"

The most beautiful city in the world? That was quite a claim.

Still, if Erika wanted to go, Ethan had no objection.

Altomare lay offshore to the south of the peninsula where Azalea Town stood. It was far enough from the Johto mainland that they would have to travel by ship.

A cruise ship sailed there directly from Goldenrod City. They made their plans and started toward the port, but before they had gone far, several people blocked their path.

It was an odd group: a woman around twenty-four or twenty-five with two very young boys.

"I finally found you! I've been looking for ages!" she said.

Ethan was puzzled. He had never met these three. Why had they been looking for him?

"Who are you?"

Once they introduced themselves, he understood.

"We're the Week Siblings. I'm Monica, and these are my younger brothers, Santos and Sunny."

That one sentence was enough to make Ethan sigh.

He had beaten one sibling and another had appeared. Then he had beaten two, and now all the remaining siblings had come at once! He had finally met every one of the Week Siblings.

"Hello," he said.

Monica smiled. "I expect you know why we're here?"

"You want to battle me?"

"Of course."

"I knew this day would come. Let's go somewhere suitable."

They had only just left the Pokémon Center, so the group retraced its steps and went to the outdoor battlefields behind it.

Several children were already battling there. Ethan's group passed them and took another field.

He knew the rules by now: defeat each sibling to earn their respective badge. Everyone understood, and no one bothered to explain them again.

Even without looking at their faces or ages, the siblings' day-themed names made their relationship apparent.

For example, as Ethan took his place in the Trainer area, the youngest boy hurried to the opposite side. Monica had introduced him as Sunny. He was only seven or eight, and even his name marked him as the last of the seven siblings.

It was fortunate their parents had stopped at seven children. An eighth might have been hard to name.

The children battling nearby noticed another match about to begin and turned to look. They recognized the Week Siblings at once.

"Hey, it's the Week Siblings!"

"That's Monica!"

"I can't believe they're here!"

They gathered excitedly around the field. Here in Johto, the local Week Siblings were evidently better known than a Champion whose victories had come in other regions.

Sunny rubbed his nose, plainly enjoying the attention. He glanced at Ethan and was the first to bring out a Pokémon.

Blue light flashed, and a Heracross appeared before Ethan. It raised its forelimbs as if to show off its strength.

Ethan thought for a moment. He did not look down on Sunny for being young, but sending out one of his usual Pokémon would hardly be fair.

He chose Eevee.

If his opponent was a child, he could battle with a Pokémon that had yet to evolve.
