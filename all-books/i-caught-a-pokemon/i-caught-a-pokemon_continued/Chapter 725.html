Ethan followed Zinnia across the yellow sand toward the center of the island. He could see almost all of it at a glance, yet there was no Sky Pillar in sight. A tower that reached into the clouds should have been visible from far away. Where was it?

Zinnia soon gave him the answer. Between two weathered rocky hills, she took a small key-shaped ornament from inside her clothes and placed it in a carefully concealed recess in a stone statue. Light flared, and the view beyond the hills changed.

There was still yellow sand, but the cramped island opened into an immense desert, dozens of times larger than it had appeared from outside. At the far edge of his sight rose a huge stone tower that thrust toward the sky.

Sky Pillar.

So this was the barrier Zinnia had said the people of Sootopolis had put in place. It hid the island's true nature completely, making it look from outside like an unremarkable deserted island. Inside was another world. How had they created it? And how had they built a stone tower hundreds, perhaps a thousand, meters tall? It was astonishing.

Zinnia glanced at Ethan again. His expression hadn't changed, so she looked away. With protection like this, she truly couldn't understand how he could have found the place before.

“One final trial remains,” she said, looking ahead. “The sand here can swallow your feet. The sun is fierce, and the winds are strong. Anyone acknowledged as worthy must cross the dunes on foot to reach the tower. It tests their resolve, and it is how we Draconid people honor our ancestors and Rayquaza—”

A shadow shot past her before she could finish. Ethan was already on Dragonite's back. In the blink of an eye, he had flown far ahead.

Zinnia stood speechless.

Misty covered her mouth, smiling at her. “Looks like I'll have to walk there with you.”

Zinnia had no answer to that either. She clenched her fists, watched Ethan recede into the distance, and finally let out a helpless sigh.

...

The dunes swept past beneath Dragonite. The entrance was at least five or six kilometers from Sky Pillar. That would be an easy distance on level ground, but trudging through this sand would be exhausting.

As for Zinnia's trial? He wasn't here because the Draconid people had chosen him. He had come to take Rayquaza. There was no reason to observe their customs or waste his strength on the walk.

Within minutes, he reached Sky Pillar. From below, it was even more magnificent. He tipped his head back, but its upper reaches disappeared into the white clouds.

Heavy stone formed its walls. After untold years, the exterior remained largely intact, marked by age but scarcely damaged. One great door was the tower's only entrance. Dozens of narrow windows on each level admitted light.

The Dragonhark altar lay at the very top. If he flew straight up, he might be able to draw Rayquaza there. First, though, he needed something else.

He patted Dragonite's neck and had it land beside the door. After jumping down onto solid ground, he looked back. Zinnia and Misty were nowhere in sight. Even after he had gone ahead, Zinnia was sticking to her people's tradition and crossing on foot. He hardly knew what to make of her stubbornness, but it gave him time.

He pushed open the heavy door and entered Sky Pillar.

Creak...

The great door, unopened for who knew how many years, swung aside. The ground floor was a broad hall, perhaps two or three hundred square meters. It was much more ruined inside than the tower's exterior suggested. Broken pillars lay everywhere, and murals covered the walls, though most were hard to make out. Wind had blown sand through the windows and buried parts of the floor. The weight of years was palpable.

Sunlight came through the small windows, keeping the hall brighter than he had expected. Yet the moment Ethan stepped inside, he sensed something strange he couldn't put into words. The air and walls felt as though an unusual power protected them. That made sense. Without something sustaining it, a building this old would surely have collapsed long ago.

He looked at the murals. Though they had no captions, their subject was clear: the ancient battle between Primal Kyogre and Primal Groudon, and Mega Rayquaza's intervention thousands of years ago. The Draconid people had preserved the event in stone as well as in oral tradition. Even if their history was interrupted, the murals could carry the legend forward.

Ethan took out his Poké Balls and released several of his Pokémon.

“Spread out,” he told them. “Search every floor from here upward for a peculiar stone.”

Gengar emerged as well. “A stone? What does it look like?”

“I'm not entirely sure. I only know it's unusual and resembles a Key Stone.”

“A Key Stone?” Gengar sounded startled. “Here? Is it meant for Rayquaza? You told me Rayquaza doesn't need a Key Stone or a Mega Stone to Mega Evolve!”

“It doesn't.” Ethan hesitated. “That's why I don't know what this thing really is. But I know Rayquaza responds to it.”

He found it puzzling himself. In theory, Rayquaza only needed to learn Dragon Ascent to Mega Evolve. Neither a Key Stone nor a Mega Stone was required. Yet he clearly remembered the official anime showing the black Rayquaza notice this stone, turn away from Zinnia, and react to it instead. Whatever it was, getting hold of it couldn't hurt.

Ethan clapped to bring his Pokémon's attention back to him. “Let's move. We have to find it before Zinnia catches up.”

They scattered through the tower, searching floor by floor. As he remembered it, the stone was round and about the size of a fist. Most of it looked like an ordinary pebble, but one face bore a mark like the distinctive part of a Mega Stone.

Each floor wasn't especially large, yet the search was slow. The first two levels held plenty of sand. Higher up, there was almost none, making it less likely that the stone had been buried. The real problem was the damage: broken columns, stairs, and reliefs had left countless fragments of similarly colored stone scattered around. They hid and confused anything that might be the one he wanted.

Some parts of the floor were cracked as well. A patch that looked solid could give way under a foot and send someone tumbling down with the stones. That slowed Ethan further. At least he remembered the stone was round, allowing him to dismiss many fragments at a glance.

Then an idea struck him. He took out a flashlight and swept its beam across the tower. The stone's polished face should reflect the light, and his Pokémon would notice even a brief glint.

It worked. As they reached the stairs between the seventh and eighth floors, the beam crossed a corner and a flash of reflected light appeared for an instant. Eevee's sharp eyes caught it.

Eevee called excitedly, scampered over, and brought back a round stone in its mouth. Ethan took it. Just as he remembered from the anime, a Key Stone-like pattern was faintly visible on one face, gleaming brightly in the flashlight beam.

He had actually found it.

“Well done!” Ethan laughed and stroked Eevee's head. With this, he could attract Rayquaza's attention. Perhaps it might even let Rayquaza Mega Evolve without Dragon Ascent someday.

He had barely put the stone in his bag and was about to call his Pokémon back when Misty's voice came from below.

“Huh? Ethan, what are you doing?”

He turned. Misty was climbing the stairs, looking baffled. Beside her, Zinnia watched him with open suspicion.

Ethan glanced at Zinnia and smiled. “Nothing. Just looking at the murals.”

Did looking at murals require him to release his Pokémon? His answer only made Zinnia more suspicious. She looked at the Pokémon around him. He had left her behind and reached the tower early; surely he had done something while she wasn't there. But she had arrived too late to catch him at it.

How infuriating. She had no idea what he was plotting. Since he plainly wasn't going to explain, she suppressed her unease, kept her face blank, and continued up the stairs.

Once she was halfway to the next floor, Ethan recalled all his Pokémon except Dragonite and followed. He fell into step beside Misty.

“You walked the whole way?” he asked.

“Yes. The dunes really were hard to cross, and the wind was strong. I got sand in my eyes more than once.”

Ethan glanced ahead and lowered his voice. “Did Zinnia say anything to you?”

“No. She looked preoccupied. We didn't exchange a word the whole way.” Misty couldn't hold back her own question. “Why did you bring her along? Was it only so she could open that barrier?”

She knew why Ethan had come, but to her, it made little sense. He had already had chances to catch Rayquaza before. Why come all the way here?

Ethan only smiled. Finding the island and opening the barrier were part of it. The main reason he had brought Zinnia, though, was luck.

Yes, luck. He was the only one who knew Zinnia had been the key to summoning the black Rayquaza in the anime. It was like drawing a rare prize in a game. Any Rayquaza might respond to a disturbance atop Sky Pillar, and he had already encountered Rayquaza twice. But would some other approach bring the exceptionally rare black one? Perhaps. Probably not.

He didn't dare gamble on his own luck. He had never even found a single Shiny Pokémon. Maybe they simply weren't meant for him.

Zinnia, on the other hand, had summoned that Rayquaza once in the anime. Even if it had been coincidence, he trusted her luck more than his own. That was why he had arranged for her to come. He sincerely hoped her extraordinary luck would hold and call the black Rayquaza again.
