Ethan gently steadied the two Ultra Balls at his waist and continued deeper into Cerulean Cave with Gardevoir and Gengar.

The cave was dim. Its floor was wet and slippery, and underground water blocked the way in places. With Gardevoir and Gengar beside him, none of it posed much of a problem. His long travels had also made him more agile. He could climb over smooth boulders with care and edge along narrow, steep paths by gripping the stone beside him. Only at the broad underground lake did he ask Gardevoir to teleport him across so his clothes would stay dry.

After another half hour, he reached the cave's deepest chamber. The narrow passage opened into a wide, circular space. Crystals scattered throughout the chamber caught and reflected a faint light, bathing it in a soft glow.

Mewtwo sat cross-legged on a flat stone at the far end.

It was still here. Its appearance had not changed, but Ethan had grown stronger since their last meeting. This time he could feel the oppressive power Mewtwo kept hidden within itself.

As though it sensed him enter, Mewtwo slowly opened its eyes.

“I knew it was you the moment you stepped into the cave.”

Its voice was as calm as Ethan remembered, reaching his mind directly through telepathy. He was not surprised it had noticed him. Deoxys and Rayquaza had sensed Mewtwo from inside their Ultra Balls; Mewtwo could surely sense them in return.

“It's been a long time, Mewtwo.” Ethan smiled as if greeting an old friend. “I wasn't sure I'd find you here. After all this time, you're still using the cave as your hideout.”

Mewtwo floated off its stone and came slowly toward him. Its gaze passed over Gardevoir and Gengar, then settled on Ethan's belt.

“Your Pokémon have grown considerably stronger since we last met. Why have you come this time?”

Its presence sharpened. A wave of air swept out beneath it, stirring Ethan's hair and blowing grit into the air. He had to keep dust from his eyes.

Only two sentences in, and Mewtwo was already putting on pressure. Ethan complained to himself, but he could hardly blame it.

Imagine sitting peacefully at home when someone walked in unannounced, came straight to your bedroom, brought two bodyguards, and kept two more powerful companions hidden at his waist. Anyone would be on guard. The fact that Mewtwo had spoken to Ethan first showed it remembered their connection. A stranger might already have been attacked.

It also showed how wary Mewtwo had become of Ethan's growing strength. It remembered that on his last visit, Ethan had tested it with the Psychic-type Pokémon beside him. Now he had returned with even stronger partners. His purpose was obvious.

Ethan did not hide it. He took the two Ultra Balls from his belt.

“I met two new partners on this journey. They're both strong, and they heard you were strong too. They want to test themselves against you, so I've taken the liberty of visiting.”

Stopping by to test Mewtwo was becoming a habit whenever Ethan returned to Kanto. His words were polite, but Mewtwo understood. He wanted a battle. If Ethan won, he would walk away unharmed. If Mewtwo lost, the man would likely throw a Poké Ball without hesitation and try to catch it.

Mewtwo was used to such challenges. It understood what it meant to exist as a Pokémon, and, more than anything, it did not fear a fight.

It was Mewtwo, a being made to surpass Mew. Did Ethan think a couple of stray Pokémon could defeat it? How naive.

Mewtwo did not say yes outright. Its rising power was answer enough. Strange force filled the chamber until even the air seemed solid.

It was Mewtwo, all right. It had felt Deoxys and the black Rayquaza and was not intimidated in the least.

When Ethan saw it was ready, he pointed upward. “There's too little room here. Let's take this outside.”

Without waiting for its agreement, he had Gardevoir teleport him out of Cerulean Cave.

…

Ethan, Gardevoir, and Gengar appeared in a clearing and landed steadily. Before entering the cave, Ethan had asked Gardevoir to mark this spot so it could bring them back in an instant. The preparation made it plain that he had planned to challenge Mewtwo all along.

Mewtwo appeared moments later. Ethan did not begin the battle there. He released Dragonite, climbed aboard, and flew north until they reached a remote forest lake.

Mewtwo followed. When Ethan stopped, it hovered over the water.

“Is this the battlefield you've chosen?”

“Yes.” Ethan looked around. “We won't have to worry about people seeing us. Even if the battle causes a commotion and someone comes looking later, they won't discover your cave from this far away. We won't have to destroy the vegetation around it either. Thoughtful of me, wasn't it?”

Mewtwo ignored his self-satisfaction. Disorder had followed it since the day it was created. If people noticed it, so what? It had never cared much for human opinion. Coldness was part of its nature.

Under Mewtwo's gaze, Ethan took out both Ultra Balls. He tossed them lightly in his hand but released only the black Rayquaza first. He wanted to see what a single Rayquaza could do against Mewtwo.

The sky darkened at once. Clouds spread overhead until the forest and lake lay beneath a lid of black. Wind began to rise. Rayquaza had not even appeared, and already the weather was changing around it.

The Ultra Ball opened. Blue light flashed. With a deafening clap of thunder, a huge, winding dragon emerged. It roared and climbed toward the clouds.

Mewtwo watched calmly from the air as the black Rayquaza vanished into the cloud cover. Then Rayquaza came down again and fixed its immense head toward Mewtwo.

The two faced each other, one enormous and the other small by comparison. Rayquaza's presence was overwhelming even as it hovered still. Mewtwo answered with a surge of power of its own. Against the dark sky, its pale body stood out sharply.

Mewtwo was small only beside Rayquaza. At nearly two meters tall, it was larger than most adult humans.

They studied each other. Rayquaza looked wary; Mewtwo's expression scarcely changed.

“I've seen this species in the upper atmosphere. Rayquaza, isn't it? An ancient Pokémon and a Legendary Pokémon. It has quite a collection of titles. I didn't expect you to catch one in so little time.”

It seemed to be acknowledging Ethan's progress, but its tone suggested it was unimpressed. Power gave Mewtwo confidence. It had not fought Rayquaza yet, but it was certain it would not lose.

Ethan let its ancient-sounding pride pass. Mewtwo had been created only a few years ago, but it possessed the strength to justify its words. He knew that well enough. He even suspected his Rayquaza would lose if they fought one-on-one.

Rayquaza was formidable too. Both were Legendary Pokémon. Only a battle could show how this meeting would end.

Ethan waved a hand. High overhead, the black Rayquaza opened its mouth and fired a deadly beam at Mewtwo.

Mewtwo's gaze sharpened. It formed a psychic shield before itself and caught the entire blast.

With a heavy roar, the beam struck the shield. It slowly dispersed without breaking through. Mewtwo might dismiss its opponent in words, but it was taking the battle seriously. It would not leave itself exposed to a hit like that.

It must have felt the force packed into Rayquaza's attack. Otherwise it would not have put up so strong a barrier.

Watching from Dragonite's back beyond the field, Ethan could only smile and shake his head. Rayquaza belonged among the world's mightiest Pokémon, especially as an attacker. Few could defeat it at all, and none could safely ignore a direct hit.

If Mewtwo had treated it as lightly as its words suggested, it would have paid for the mistake. But Mewtwo was experienced and clever. Its actions were more cautious than its voice, and it gave Rayquaza no easy opening.

Its own attacks were dangerous, even in its present form, and not far behind Rayquaza's. More importantly, Mewtwo was far faster. Rayquaza would have to guard against every opening it gave away.

The probing exchange was over. They fought in earnest, and the battle grew fierce at once.

Soon Ethan could hardly follow Mewtwo with his eyes. A pale streak moved constantly before the black Rayquaza, which twisted its great body to meet it. Attacks flew without pause. Ethan recognized some; others he could not even name.

Missed attacks struck the lake below, sending up one explosion after another. The surrounding forest shuddered, whole stands of trees rolling in waves beneath the force of the fight.

Ethan patted Dragonite, and it carried him farther from the field. Even there, the shock waves made it beat its wings hard to hold steady. They retreated again.

From this distance, Ethan was only a spectator. That was fine. He had not planned to direct Rayquaza closely today. At this level, his commands would mostly concern the timing of attacks and evasions. The actual clash depended on the Pokémon themselves.

This might be the first time even Mewtwo had faced a Pokémon this powerful. Rayquaza had its own challenges, but Mewtwo too would have to learn whether it could adapt and bring out all its strength.

Wind drove the clouds into thicker masses. Thunder cracked. Then torrential rain fell over the battlefield. Ethan was glad he had moved away before it soaked him. From a dry vantage, he could watch and even think through what each Pokémon was doing.

They were among the world's strongest. Their battles could change the weather, alter the terrain, and send wild Pokémon fleeing. Yet neither was without weaknesses.

From a Trainer's point of view, both Mewtwo and the black Rayquaza still fought much like wild Pokémon. They drew on overwhelming power and instinct, but had not yet developed the disciplined habits or refined control that came from training within a team. For all their ferocity, they wasted effort at times.

That was why a trained Pokémon could sometimes defeat a wild Pokémon of similar type and strength. If Mewtwo and Rayquaza ever truly learned to fight as partners with Trainers, both could become stronger still.

That was a distant prospect. Ethan had not caught Mewtwo. Rayquaza was gradually accepting that it belonged to a human Trainer, but it would take time before he could direct it as freely as he directed the three Legendary birds.
