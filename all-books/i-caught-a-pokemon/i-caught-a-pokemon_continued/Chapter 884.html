Ethan did eventually fall asleep. When he woke again, evening had come and Zuki was gone.

He stared at the clear impression she had left beside him, then shook his head with a helpless smile. He had spent time with all three sisters again. This time they had come to him one by one, but the outcome was much the same.

There was one difference: they had all chosen it freely and with clear minds. That changed how it felt to him.

Still, Zuki could have woken him before leaving. Perhaps she had thought he was sleeping too soundly.

May arrived outside the door just then to call him to dinner. The disordered bedding and lingering scent in the room seemed to tell her what had happened. She stood outside, blushing, and called softly without coming in.

“I heard you,” Ethan answered.

He stretched, dressed, and followed her toward the dining room. On the way he asked how her practice was going.

“I've improved a lot,” May said confidently. “I'm sure I'll do better at the Grand Festival!”

Her confidence made Ethan look forward to seeing her perform. She might not win the whole competition, but after this training, she was bound to leave an impression.

In the dining room, Miki, Kuni, and Zuki greeted him. Miki and Kuni gave him gentle smiles. All three still looked a little flushed, and Ethan felt a private sense of satisfaction.

Then he noticed their clothes. They wore formal kimonos, hair ornaments, and light makeup. Naoko and Sayo were dressed the same way.

“Why are you all dressed up? Do you have a performance tonight?”

“Idiot.” Zuki put a hand to her forehead. “We're summoning Ho-Oh tonight. Remember?”

“Ho-Oh? What does that have to do with your clothes?”

“Zuki, just tell him,” Miki said, stepping in before her sister could tease him further. She turned to Ethan. “Our family traditionally wore ceremonial dress when making offerings or calling to the legendary Pokémon. We no longer perform those rites, but we're going to summon Ho-Oh tonight. It feels right to honor the custom.”

“I see.” Ethan nodded. “I have to say, you all look beautiful in kimonos, however many times I see you wear them.”

All five sisters blushed. Miki, Kuni, and Zuki were still new to their relationship with him; praise from Ethan carried weight. Naoko and Sayo kept stealing shy glances at him too.

Ethan noticed May sitting quietly off to one side in her everyday clothes. He remembered how much she had admired the sisters' costumes when they first arrived.

“Do you have a kimono that would fit May?” he asked. “Why don't you help her dress for tonight too?”

“Me?” May looked up, startled. Then warmth spread through her. Ethan had remembered what she had said about the kimonos.

“Of course,” Kuni said, looking her over. “You're about Naoko's size. We still have a kimono she wore a couple of years ago. Come with me.”

May blushed at being measured in front of Ethan, but she followed Kuni happily. Naoko and Sayo went along too, curious to see her dressed and made up.

The dining room grew quieter. Ethan ate and talked with Miki and Zuki. Before long, the others returned.

May looked transformed. The hair at the back of her head was gathered with a pretty clip. She wore light makeup and an orange kimono that reached her ankles. It had few ornaments or patterns, giving it a simple elegance that suited her.

“Ta-da!” Sayo clapped. “Look who we have here!”

May turned once in front of Ethan, a little self-conscious, then looked at him expectantly.

He applauded. “You look lovely, May. It suits you.”

He meant it. The outfit lent her a more composed air. Miki and Zuki nodded in approval too and told her how pretty she looked. May beamed at the compliments and sat down to eat.

Everyone was comfortable enough together now to enjoy dinner. By the time they finished, it was past eight. Night covered the city, the moon hung at an angle in the sky, and a murmur of activity had risen outside.

Midnight was still hours away. Ethan appeared calm, but everyone else was restless. To most people, a legendary Pokémon was something from a tale; Ho-Oh, so rare and so important to Johto, felt more distant still. The thought that they might summon it tonight made sitting still difficult.

They talked to pass the time. By a little after ten, Ethan could see they were too distracted to stay inside. He led them out into the city early.

Ecruteak was an appealing place to live. Its old buildings gave it a sense of history, and life moved at a slower pace. At night there were no bright clubs or dazzling signs. Instead, a busy pedestrian street filled with small vendors and their stalls.

Usually the Kimono Sisters hesitated to go out even this late. Almost everyone in Ecruteak knew them, and many had watched their performances. They had a great many admirers.

Tonight they wore no heavy stage makeup, and no spotlight shone on them. Most pedestrians were looking at the stalls. Many women on the street wore kimonos too. In the dim, lanternlike light, few people paid the sisters close attention.

A passerby might notice a group of beautiful women and realize ten steps later that they looked familiar. Even so, the sisters kept to the less crowded parts of the street.

Walking behind Ethan, Miki watched the crowd and listened to the vendors calling out. “It's been so long since we went out like this. I've missed it.”

“Me too,” Kuni said. “Since our shows became popular, we've barely had an evening just to wander.”

“Is it this lively every night?” Ethan asked.

“Not every night,” Miki said. “You came at a good time. Ecruteak is celebrating a traditional festival. There'll be a big fireworks display later.”

Fireworks. Ethan filed that away, thinking of their plan for midnight.

Zuki gave him a sidelong look. “We have someone to thank for these days off, don't we?”

She meant him, of course. Ethan pinched her lightly as he passed, drawing an indignant look, then moved ahead laughing. He could hardly dispute that he was the reason their shows had been canceled.

At a mask stall, he bought one for each of the five sisters. Once they put them on, they no longer had to worry about being recognized.

The group continued along the street toward Bell Tower. Naoko and Sayo were especially delighted. They rarely had a chance to browse at leisure. Before long, their hands were full of trinkets and snacks, and they had tried games where visitors shot at balloons or tossed rings for prizes.

Everyone seemed to be enjoying themselves.

May and the two younger sisters stopped before a vendor selling Magikarp. They crouched to watch the Pokémon leap in a basin. Ethan watched for a moment too, then glanced around.

For an instant he thought he saw Ho-Oh. It was only a kite shaped like the Pokémon, wound with strings of little lights.

A neat-looking boy of seven or eight with a bowl haircut carried it through the crowd, heading alone toward Bell Tower. The kite was long and awkward for him. As he passed Ethan, one wing came loose and fell.

Ethan looked down. The wing had been fixed to the body with tape. Even if the kite managed to rise, it would probably come apart in the air.

He bent, picked up the wing, and handed it to the boy.

“Oh, thank you.” The child took it in a fluster and tried several times to put it back. It would not stay. Worry spread over his face.

Miki and the others had noticed and came over. With tonight's ceremony on her mind, Miki crouched beside him.

“Why do you have a Ho-Oh kite, little one? Where are you taking it?”

It was late for flying a kite, even during a festival. Lanterns would have been less surprising.

The boy looked nervous surrounded by the dressed-up women in Pokémon masks. Then he saw May's bright smile and blushed. He glanced at her once more before answering haltingly.

“I'm taking it to Bell Tower. I want Grandpa to see Ho-Oh again.”

“Again?” Kuni asked. “Has your grandfather seen Ho-Oh before?”

“He has!” The boy nodded. “At home there's a photo of him when he was young, with a Rainbow Wing. He's looked for Ho-Oh for years, but he hasn't found it again. He's started to give up.”

His voice grew quieter. It hurt him to see his grandfather lose hope in a lifelong dream.

“I see,” Kuni said. “You hoped seeing a lit-up Ho-Oh kite would encourage him to keep looking?”

“Yes! If he sees Ho-Oh flying in the sky, he'll be like he used to be.”

He looked at the broken kite and sighed. “But it won't fly tonight.”

“I'm not sure a kite with lights could convince him,” Ethan said. “You'd at least need a Pokémon such as Fearow dressed up to look the part.”

He turned toward Bell Tower, waving without looking back. “Go home for now. Tonight, you and your grandfather will get your wish. Ho-Oh really will appear.”
