They walked through the dark cave for more than ten minutes. Ethan guessed they must be well inside the mountain by now. Several hundred steps later, light began to appear ahead.

They emerged into a circular chamber. At its center, a silver meteorite pitted with round hollows lay embedded in the ground. Sunlight fell through a great opening in the ceiling and struck its surface, giving the stone a cold, mysterious glow.

“Is that the meteorite with Deoxys inside?” May asked. She went closer and realized how enormous it was. Even the part above ground rose three or four meters high.

Then another question occurred to her. “If a meteorite this big hit Earth, shouldn't it have caused far more damage than this?”

She had a point. A stone that size falling from space should have devastated the surrounding area, not merely punched a small hole through the mountain.

“Perhaps Rayquaza had something to do with it,” Ethan said. He did not know, but he could make a guess. “Rayquaza lives in the ozone layer and treats this planet as its territory. It wouldn't stand by while a meteorite destroyed it. One or more Rayquaza might have intercepted the stone and slowed it. That might also explain why the Deoxys inside took ten years to awaken.”

“That could be right.” Solana consulted the documents in her hand. “The records say there was a violent explosion when it fell, but nothing more. There was no other disaster, so people assumed it was a small meteorite and paid little attention. Even this cave was reported only after local villagers found it by chance, and the report attracted hardly any interest.”

The area was remote. Few people had reason to come here, let alone search for a meteorite.

May looked around. “Then where is Deoxys hiding?”

Solana swept the chamber with her geomagnetic detector. The readings pointed to this place as the source, but she could not locate Deoxys itself.

“This is where a specialist comes in,” Ethan said.

He had no reason to keep his own Deoxys hidden now. He took its Ultra Ball from his pouch. “Come out, Deoxys.”

At his quiet call, the ball opened from within. Blue light flashed, and the strange, almost alien-looking Mythical Pokémon appeared before May and Solana.

So this is Deoxys? They stared at it from close range. Neither saw it quite as Ethan did. Its mysterious appearance inspired awe, but also a touch of fear. Like the meteorite beside it, the creature had come from space, and its alien shape made their skin prickle.

Deoxys paid them no attention. It floated toward the meteorite. At once the chamber shook. Solana's geomagnetic detector blared, and even her Capture Styler briefly stopped working.

“The disturbance is getting stronger… Something is about to come out.”

Solana nearly lost her footing. May caught her, and they held each other upright.

The space above the meteorite twisted, much as the sky beneath the aurora had. Another Deoxys appeared before them.

To an outsider it looked identical to Ethan's in size, markings, and form. Ethan, as its Trainer, could sense the differences between them.

Perhaps the presence of another of its kind reassured the newcomer. It made no threatening move and began communicating with Ethan's Deoxys. Soon a message reached him through his partner.

It…feels…pain…

Deoxys was speaking into Ethan's mind. Because it was an alien life-form with an alien way of expressing itself, however, its telepathy was never as precise or clear as Gardevoir's. Sometimes he had to guess what it meant.

He knew the wild Deoxys was in pain, but could not make out the cause from his partner's halting message. Fortunately, there was a more direct way to address the problem.

“My Deoxys says the wild one is suffering,” Ethan told May and Solana. “We need to get it away from here.”

“Shouldn't that be easy?” May asked cautiously. “Your Deoxys can talk to it. Couldn't it ask the wild one to leave?”

“That's what worries me. If it could leave so easily, it would have gone already. I suspect the magnetic disturbance is keeping it here.”

“But we saw it in the sky earlier. How did it get out then?”

“I think that was a double,” Ethan said, following his hunch. “It may have sent a copy outside to make the aurora, hoping another Deoxys would see it and come to help.”

It was only his analysis, but May thought he was probably right. She sighed. “It's a shame our Poké Balls still won't work. If we could put it in one, this would be much simpler.”

Solana had followed their discussion. “All we need is to bring Deoxys away from here? Leave that to me!”

She changed her Capture Styler into its working configuration, as she had when they first met, and launched a Capture Disc toward the wild Deoxys. She hoped to form a temporary bond with it, communicate directly, and enlist its help.

The idea was sound. She had overlooked both its power and its reluctance.

White capture lines curled around Deoxys. Solana thought she had succeeded, but a sphere of energy suddenly expanded from its body and tore through every line.

“What? How did it do that?” Her eyes widened. In all her time as a Ranger, she had never seen a Pokémon break free of the capture lines that way.

May teased her gently. “I suppose it doesn't want to be your friend.”

The failure robbed Solana of her earlier confidence. Embarrassed, she stepped back. It was obvious she could not handle this on her own.

She scratched her cheek and gave an awkward smile. “I…have another thought. I'll ask headquarters if they know anything that could help.”

Ethan turned from her to the wild Deoxys. He was certain now that he had never met this one. Its glance from the sky had not meant it recognized him. It must have sensed the presence of his Deoxys.

He would need to use his own method to free it. “Can you move the meteorite out of here?” he asked his partner.

It was a blunt solution. If the geomagnetic anomaly kept the wild Deoxys near its meteorite, they could move the meteorite itself. Whatever the precise cause, perhaps taking the stone elsewhere would remove the constraint.

His Deoxys nodded without speaking. It rose above the meteorite and lifted both hands. An invisible force wrapped around the huge stone, which began to shake violently.

Ethan could not judge its weight without knowing its density, but it had to weigh at least a dozen tons. An ordinary Pokémon might have no way to move something so massive. This was Deoxys, one of the world's most powerful Psychic-type Pokémon.

The meteorite slowly rose, exposing fresh earth beneath it. Deoxys guided it through the gap overhead and flew out beside it.

Once it was gone, Ethan turned to May and Solana. “Come closer. We should get out of here too.”

Solana wondered how he intended to bring them out. Before she could ask, another ball at his waist opened on its own, and the Gardevoir she had met earlier appeared.

So that's how…

The view before Solana changed abruptly, and she felt a moment of softness beneath her feet. When she found her bearings, the three of them stood outside the mountain under blue sky and white clouds, on yielding grass. They had arrived in the blink of an eye.

“Teleport really is convenient,” she murmured. Then she looked up and saw Deoxys flying toward them with the enormous meteorite.

Ethan gestured behind them. “Let's go. We're still within the disturbed area. We need to get farther away.”

They retraced their route out of the hills, stopping only when they had gone a long way. Deoxys set the meteorite down with a heavy boom that shook the ground.

Strangely, the strong geomagnetic anomaly persisted despite the distance. Ordinary devices and Poké Balls still would not work.

“Ethan…” May looked puzzled. “We took the meteorite out of the cave and brought it all this way. Why is the magnetic disturbance still here?”

“Then there's only one likely explanation.” Ethan shifted his gaze from her to the stone behind them. “The anomaly may not be affecting the meteorite. The meteorite itself may be causing the anomaly.”

At that moment, Solana received a response from headquarters. “I think I know what's behind this,” she said with a rueful smile. “Have you heard of the solar wind?”

“Solar wind?” May shook her head, then noticed Ethan nodding. He seemed to know everything. “What is it? A kind of wind on the Sun?”

“Not really wind,” Solana explained. “It's a supersonic stream of charged plasma particles released from the Sun's upper atmosphere. Because it blows outward and reaches planets like ours, we call it the solar wind.”

May waited for her to continue.

“Headquarters says sunspots are growing larger right now, strengthening the solar wind. Its wave pattern exactly matches the geomagnetic anomaly here. So the solar wind appears to be involved. After what we just saw, though, I think it's affecting this meteorite in particular, and the meteorite is producing the local disturbance.”

All three looked at the stone.

Ethan rubbed his chin. “Every clue leads back to it. The meteorite must be the source.”

May finally understood what had happened. “No wonder moving away from the cave didn't help. The meteorite brought Deoxys to Earth, but now it's trapping it too?”

“That seems to be it,” Solana said. She turned to Ethan. “Mr. Vale, what should we do next?”

“What else can we do? If this stone is a prison, we'll have to break it and free Deoxys.”

“Huh?” May hesitated. “Do we really have to? Isn't there another way? This meteorite is…its home too.”
