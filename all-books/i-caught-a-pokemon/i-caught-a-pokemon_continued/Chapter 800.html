The large screen finally gave Ethan a clear look at Tucker. He was ordinary-looking, hardly anyone Ethan would call handsome. His bright purple and pale white outfit made him resemble a huge dragonfly, and shiny purple boots completed the strange effect.

What Ethan found hardest to accept was the man's long reddish-purple hair. Tucker had tied it into two tails, with the hair at his temples swept out to the sides. It looked like a great windmill. On a man around thirty, the whole ensemble was well beyond Ethan's taste.

Ugly, he thought. Bizarre. Still, Tucker had a right to dress as he liked. People pursued beauty in different ways, and Tucker clearly enjoyed drawing eyes with elaborate clothes and presentation.

He must also be strong. That went without saying. He was said to be skilled at performing for his fans and engaging with them, which helped explain his popularity.

Once Tucker had finished showing himself off, the match finally moved toward the battle. A referee who also served as host stepped onto a mobile, hovering commentary platform. It rose above the field, making Ethan and the girls take another look at the Dome's technology.

Neither the Indigo nor the Silver Conference had possessed a floating commentary stand like this. Scott and Tucker had plainly poured money and effort into the Battle Dome. The investment was paying off. The packed, enthusiastic crowd grew wilder under the lights and special effects.

The host explained the rules. Ethan listened closely enough to learn that Battle Dome matches were Double Battles. A side lost when both its Pokémon were unable to battle. It was another change from the Battle Arena.

As the Frontier Brain, Tucker released his Pokémon first. Two flashes of blue light revealed Swampert and Arcanine.

Sending out two Pokémon should have been ordinary. Tucker's exaggerated movements, the host's commentary, and the lights turned it into a performance.

“Ah! Arcanine, the swift runner of fire! Swampert, ruler of water and earth! These are the two opponents our challenger faces today! Tucker has brought us a fusion of blazing heat and icy cool!”

The Pokémon cried out on cue, and the cheers surged again.

“He really knows how to put on a show,” Misty murmured as the audience chanted Tucker's name.

May's eyes glinted. She seemed to be taking inspiration from the entrance. Misty noticed at once and hurried to stop her.

“Wait! Don't copy him. Stick with your own style.”

The young woman challenging Tucker released her own two Pokémon. Having seen Swampert and Arcanine first, she chose a team with favorable type matchups. That gave her confidence, but a Frontier Brain was still a Frontier Brain. After barely a dozen exchanges, Tucker defeated her easily.

The crowd's cheering reached its loudest point yet. Short as the battle had been, Ethan had seen enough to judge Tucker's ability. He was good. Among official Gym Leaders, Ethan would place him around the lower middle.

Tucker was especially fond of using his Pokémon together, with tactics that gave him the initiative. Their cooperation let him recover even when he faced an unfavorable type matchup. That was the main reason he had won so comfortably. But Ethan had seen the approach now. It would be less effective against him.

Tucker gave another victory performance. Only when he had finally finished did the crowd, still hungry for more, begin to leave.

Ethan took Misty and May to a member of staff to register for a challenge. The process presented a few complications. Whether Tucker needed time to gather spectators, advertise, and arrange the show, or was simply busy, the earliest available match was the next morning. The staff also required a list of the challenger's past achievements.

The reason was plain. Tucker's every appearance was meant to draw attention. What could produce more excitement than a superstar beating a celebrated opponent?

Ethan considered giving a made-up identity to avoid a fuss. Then he remembered that many spectators might have watched him battle. A false name could cause confusion. He registered as himself.

As soon as the staff member entered Ethan's name, his tournament record filled the computer screen. The man stared, mouth open.

Indigo Conference champion. Silver Conference champion. Ever Grande Conference champion. Honorable Trainer of the Orange League. Sea Hero of the Whirl Islands.

There were so many titles they almost blurred together. Was this really someone he should be handling at an ordinary registration desk?

Several seconds passed before he remembered to stand. “M-Mr. Ethan. Your registration is complete. Under our rules, your match is scheduled for nine tomorrow morning. Please make sure you're here on time.”

That was what reputation could do. Ethan hardly had to say anything. A champion with those accomplishments was coming to challenge the Dome; how could any staff member treat it casually?

The man watched Ethan leave, then let out a breath. Without wasting another moment, he hurried upstairs to Tucker's lounge.

Inside, Tucker was posing before a large mirror while his manager organized something nearby. The manager frowned at the staff member's hasty entrance. “What's happened?”

Still out of breath, the man told them who had registered.

“Ethan?”

Both Tucker and his manager followed international news as part of their work. They knew about Ethan's victories in three regions. Even so, hearing that he had come to the Battle Dome shocked them.

“Are you certain?” Tucker asked. “It's really him?”

“Absolutely. I checked the records. His face matches too. There's no mistake.”

The manager thought for a moment and sighed. “Ethan… I've watched his battles. He has ambitious plans. He used different Pokémon in all three conference finals. It looks as if he's trying to raise every one of his partners to championship strength. Why come here now? Does he have some other purpose?”

He glanced at Tucker for a reaction and found his eyes shining.

“What an opportunity!” Tucker laughed. “Once the news spreads, tomorrow's tickets will sell out. The crowd will go mad for me!”

The manager frowned. “But he's extremely strong. Are you sure you can handle him? If you lose…”

“Win or lose, we benefit.” Tucker turned to him, eyes bright. “Publicize the match. If he beats me, he's a three-time champion, so no one will blame me. But if I win…”

He let the thought hang. The manager understood.

“You think you can beat him?”

“How will we know unless I try?” Tucker said confidently. “I'm the Battle Dome's superstar. If I hadn't promised Scott I'd run this facility, winning the Indigo Conference would have been easy for me. Ethan wouldn't have had his chance!”

The manager seemed encouraged. He turned to the staff member. “Publish this immediately. Make sure every spectator knows. Call the reporters too. I want this match advertised everywhere—ideally, all of Kanto should hear about it before tomorrow.”

“But…” The staff member hesitated. “Isn't that difficult on such short notice?”

“I'll double your pay this month.”

“Understood. I'll get it done!”

…

Unaware of what the Dome was planning, Ethan and the girls checked into a nearby Pokémon Center. Their match would not happen until the next day, so they had to stay another night.

The pause suited May. She needed time to adjust her plans and practice for the Contest. Ethan did not have to help. Misty was already working through ideas with her. The two girls talked animatedly, heads together, leaving Ethan little chance to join in. He went to find something to do on his own.

The Center stood on a low hill, overlooking a wide stretch of countryside. Clouds drifted slowly overhead. They were far from Kanto's busy central cities, in a sparsely settled region of forests and plains. From here, the main road continued toward Cerulean City if one turned west farther on.

Cerulean City. No wonder Misty had grown chattier as they came closer, while her expression grew more downcast. Once they reached her hometown, she would be home. And going home meant leaving Ethan.

She was young enough that every thought showed on her face. No one had to guess what was troubling her. But there was little to be done. She could not travel the world with him forever. Everyone eventually had to return to their own life.

Ethan stood on the grass and looked into the distance for a while. Then another thought came to him. If Cerulean City was close, so was that other place.

He had time free. Why not go see it?

Ethan let Misty and May know where he was going, released Dragonite, and climbed onto its back. Dragonite rose through the clouds and raced toward Cerulean Cave.

There was only one reason for Ethan to make such a detour: Mewtwo.

Dragonite, his second flying mount, pushed its speed to the limit. The landscape flashed past beneath the clouds. A little more than an hour later, after skirting Cerulean City, Ethan spotted Nugget Bridge in the distance.

That brought back memories. On his first visit to the bridge, five Trainers had blocked his way. Were they still there after all this time? It made no difference. He had not come to see them.

Dragonite landed by the riverbank near the bridge. Following his memory of the route, Ethan soon found the entrance to Cerulean Cave.

He did not know whether Mewtwo still lived here. He did not know where else to look for it, either. He hoped it had stayed.

He recalled Dragonite, released Gardevoir, and called Gengar out. With both partners beside him, he entered the cave.

The path beneath his feet felt familiar and strange at once. Everything looked much as it had when he first came here.

Wild Pokémon began peering out before he had gone far. They sensed the power coming from Gardevoir and Gengar and wisely kept their distance. Even territorial Pokémon that lived in groups stayed in their nests, pretending they had seen nothing.

Ethan was only halfway through the cave when two Ultra Balls at his waist began shaking violently.
