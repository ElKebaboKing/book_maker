Early the next morning, Ethan met Ash and the others and went down to the ground-floor lobby of their lodgings. The match schedule for the preliminary groups was already displayed on the large screen.

Sixteen groups had been drawn, with three Trainers in each one. A photograph appeared beside every name.

"There I am!" Ash spotted his picture at once. Pointing at the screen, he said, "I'm in Group H. Let's see who I'm up against... Huh?"

At his surprised voice, Ethan and the others turned to look.

"What is it?"

A closer look showed that Ash's group included a young girl and a slightly chubby older boy. The boy was Vincent, Jimmy and Marina's childhood friend from New Bark Town.

"So it's him." Misty gave Ash a teasing pat on the shoulder. "Marina told me Vincent is really strong. What bad luck to end up in his group."

Ash looked eager for the challenge. "It doesn't matter who I face. I won't lose!"

Once they had checked Ash's group, everyone searched for Ethan's name.

Misty narrowed her eyes and found it first. "There he is! Ethan's in Group C!"

Following her finger, Ethan found his photograph. His two group opponents were Trainers he had never seen before.

"That's not bad luck. I didn't draw Jimmy or the others."

He was relieved not to be grouped with anyone he knew. Only one Trainer could advance from each group, and he would feel bad if he had to eliminate a friend himself.

They were bound to meet if they kept winning, but the later it happened, the better his friends could place. He would feel less guilty about knocking them out then.

He did not even want Gary eliminated this early.

After checking the draw, they read the rules more carefully.

Each group would play a three-person round robin. A win earned three points, a loss none, and a draw one. The Trainer with the most points would advance to the final tournament.

"There's a small chance of a complication," Brock said, rubbing his chin. "If every match ends in a draw and the points are tied, they'll hold tiebreaker matches until there's a clear winner."

"That..." Ash tried counting it out on his fingers, but the scoring was more than he wanted to work through. He gave up before he strained his limited supply of brain cells. "Anyway, if I beat both of them, I definitely go through, right?"

"Exactly." Ethan smiled. "You're here to compete. Don't get bogged down in the details. That's the spirit—go straight at them!"

Once they knew where to go, Ethan parted ways with Ash and took Erika toward Group C.

Misty had traveled with Ash all this way, and with such an important competition beginning, she could hardly leave him on his own. She and Brock accompanied him to the Group H venue.

...

Beside the main Silver Stadium were four smaller arenas, A through D. The sixteen preliminary groups would compete in those four arenas.

Group C, Ethan's group, had been assigned to Arena A according to the schedule.

When Ethan emerged from the competitors' tunnel, Arena A was already full of spectators. Judging by the crowd, it had drawn the most attention of the four venues.

Misa, Masha, and their friends had claimed conspicuous seats early. As soon as Ethan entered, they waved their flags wildly.

They were impossible to miss. Erika sat quietly beside them, her gentle gaze fixed on Ethan across the arena.

One of Ethan's opponents was a popular local Trainer from Johto. He had taken his place in the red Trainer's box before Ethan arrived, but while the others seemed relaxed, his expression was grim.

No one else could quite understand the pressure of standing before the Indigo League champion and the favorite to win this year's Silver Conference.

When he learned he had been drawn into Ethan's group, he had made a point of watching Ethan's earlier matches. They had left him feeling as though he could scarcely breathe.

Gardevoir, Charizard, Blastoise, Venusaur—every Pokémon he had seen Ethan use was formidable, and his other Pokémon seemed no weaker. Their strength was worthy of a champion. Gyarados in particular had frightened him.

He had no idea how to fight an opponent like that.

Now Ethan walked toward the blue Trainer's box, waving to the crowd with an easy expression. The other Trainer could only pray that Ethan would leave his strongest Pokémon out of the preliminaries.

Once Ethan was in position, the referee stepped to the center of the field.

"We'll now decide who sends out a Pokémon first!"

Unlike the usual matches, these preliminaries used a spinning wheel to decide the first Trainer to send out a Pokémon. The method was not entirely fair, but it reduced some uncertainty and offered a measure of protection to the weaker side.

On the other hand, if the weaker Trainer drew the first move, the battle would likely end faster and more decisively against him.

A small wheel appeared on the nearby screen. Red and blue sections representing the two Trainers were spaced evenly around it. As the pointer began to turn, both Trainers watched intently.

When it slowed and came to rest on Ethan's blue section, his opponent could not help breathing a sigh of relief.

The referee glanced at the result and gestured to Ethan. "Ethan, please send out your Pokémon first."

Ethan nodded, showing no disappointment. He trusted his Pokémon. Even at a type disadvantage, they would not go down easily.

He took a Poké Ball from the pouch at his waist. He had made his choice before the match. He threw the ball, and in a flash of blue light, a Pokémon appeared on the field. Green leaves and a red flower rose from its back, above its blue-green limbs.

It was Venusaur! Once a star of Ethan's battles in Kanto, it had spent a long time at Cerise Park without appearing in a match.
