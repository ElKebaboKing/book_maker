Danny gave a rueful smile. Faced with Articuno's overwhelming power, he had no trouble accepting his loss.

"You're right. I lost. But the second round isn't a test of strength. This time you'll use the ice you just made to build something."

He pointed to a board. Ethan looked over and saw a simple diagram of a boat made of ice.

"You may use three Pokémon. The first to carve their ice into a boat wins the second round!"

So this is the next event. Danny's challenges really were just as they had been in the anime. He had not changed a thing.

"All right," Ethan said. "I accept."

Sabrina listened to Danny explain the event with a straight face and shook her head. Making boats out of ice? Was this the standard at Navel Gym? First they froze water, then they made a boat. What would come next, sliding down the mountain?

The whole thing struck her as absurdly childish. Even the water-gun target shooting at Mikan Gym had been more worthwhile. Compared with the Indigo League, these events fell far short. They dispensed with Pokémon battles altogether and seemed to bear no relation to the usual rules of a Pokémon League.

Sabrina had enjoyed her time in the Orange Islands. The local scenery and customs had fascinated her, and her days with Ethan had given her many happy memories. Deep down, though, she could not take this almost playful Orange League seriously.

Its Gym Leaders and their events seemed amateurish to her. What was the point of choosing a winner without a single battle? Were they trying to find the best all-around athlete?

Ethan was the one competing, and he had no objection. Sabrina knew he must have his reasons, so she kept her thoughts to herself.

Danny sent out Machoke and Scyther to join his Nidoqueen. Ethan thought it over, recalled Articuno, and sent out Gardevoir and Jolteon. Haunter emerged from his shadow, completing his own team of three.

Danny's gaze passed over Jolteon and Haunter, then lingered on Gardevoir. What kind of Pokémon was that? It was beautiful. Had Ethan caught it in another region? The strange Pokémon, along with Articuno, made him even more curious about Ethan's identity.

Once Ethan was ready, Danny gave a signal. Both teams set to work on their blocks of ice. Danny stood to one side with his arms folded and gave no instructions.

His Pokémon had ridden ice boats countless times since the Gym opened. They knew how to make them and worked quickly without his guidance. Scyther in particular sliced through the ice with its scythe-like forearms, easily cutting it into the shapes Danny wanted.

Ethan's team was much slower. Articuno had frozen their water so solidly that carving it was difficult. His Pokémon also had no experience with this kind of work, and had to take care with every cut for fear of cracking the ice.

"Gardevoir, use Confusion to turn the ice pillar a little!"

"Haunter, cut off that extra section!"

"Jolteon, clear away those ice chips!"

Shards flew with every cut. Ethan's three Pokémon worked hard under his direction, but Danny's finished a complete ice boat first.

Ethan could only sigh. This was Danny's specialty. No matter how strong Ethan's Pokémon were, power could not decide a contest that depended on practice and technique.

He did not mind losing this round too much. He had treated it as a game with his Pokémon, and they had all enjoyed themselves.

"That puts us at one round apiece," Danny said. He was privately relieved not to have lost two straight rounds. Then his smile returned. "The third round will decide it. Before we start, let's push our ice boats to the starting point."

The snow had stopped entirely, and the dark clouds had begun to break up. The starting point was not far away. Rather than have Gardevoir move the boat with her psychic power, Ethan and his Pokémon pushed it together to a flag marked START.

Only then did Ethan realize that the face Danny had led him up was the steepest side of Navel Island's volcano. Ahead of them, the mountain sloped down like a giant slide. The view from here was wider and even more beautiful.

They stood side by side for a moment, enjoying it. Then Danny pointed toward an open area at the foot of the mountain.

"The last round tests how well you work with your Pokémon. We each ride our ice boat with our team. Whoever reaches the shore first wins!"

Ethan surveyed the slope. "What route do we take? Can we choose our own?"

Danny nodded. "As long as you and your Pokémon stay aboard your ice boat, you can take any route and use any method you like. Get to the finish line, and it counts."

So it was a race with almost no rules. Ethan made an OK sign to show he understood.

For the deciding round, Danny changed his team. He recalled Nidoqueen and Machoke and sent out Geodude and Electrode. They joined Scyther, whose work had been so good in the second round, aboard his boat.

Ethan kept his team as it was. He sat at the front of his ice boat, with Gardevoir, Jolteon, and Haunter lined up behind him.

A signal light stood nearby. The instant it changed from red to green, Ethan and Danny both leaned forward and pushed off. Their ice boats shot down the thick snow.

Sabrina boarded the cable car again. It followed a different route toward the finish, giving her a clear view of the race from above.

At first the ice boats slid slowly. Then they gathered speed and went faster and faster. Snow-covered scenery streamed past on either side of Ethan. Rocks and odd-looking obstacles appeared ahead of him without warning.

At this height and speed, the race was dangerous. One careless collision would certainly smash an ice boat to pieces, even if the riders survived.
