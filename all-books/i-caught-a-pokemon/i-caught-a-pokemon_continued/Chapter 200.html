"Gardevoir, there's a boulder ahead! Turn left!"

"Right this time! Use Confusion to knock down that tree in our way!"

As their ice boat rushed downhill, Ethan gave quick commands whenever an obstacle appeared. His Pokémon destroyed what they could or steered the boat around it, getting them through each danger.

Whoosh!

The speeding boats threw up waves of snow. Sometimes they raced side by side; sometimes one pulled ahead before the other caught up.

Ethan and his Pokémon weighed less than Danny's team, and Haunter floated above the boat rather than adding weight to it at all. On the straight sections, Ethan could not press the boat down to lower its center of gravity and keep it stable at speed as Danny could.

The lighter boat had an advantage in turns, though. It was easier to steer around obstacles without pushing so hard that it overturned. Again and again, Ethan used a turn to pull ahead.

Halfway down, the scenery changed. They had passed below the snow line. The snow thinned out, and a winding mountain path of rock and dirt lay ahead.

The rough ground scraped the ice boats with every meter they traveled. Two long white trails of ice shavings marked their passage. Fortunately, Articuno had frozen Ethan's ice so hard that his boat showed no immediate sign of breaking apart.

High above them, Sabrina watched through the cable car window. The lead kept changing, and she could not yet tell who would win. She was firmly on Ethan's side, of course, and barely spared Danny a glance.

The stalemate finally broke in the second half of the course, when they approached a broad forest.

Danny knew from years of racing here that the trees were too dense to pass through. The usual route curved widely around the forest before reaching the finish. He knew the terrain by heart and began his turn without hesitation.

Then he glanced toward Ethan and saw his ice boat heading straight for the trees.

Oh no! Had he misjudged the route? Couldn't he see what was ahead?

Danny did not want anyone hurt at his Gym. "Turn now! You can't get through that forest. You're going to crash!"

Ethan gave no sign that he had heard. His boat kept bearing down on the trees.

Danny frowned, wondering if he should try to stop him. Before he could decide, Ethan steered toward a raised slab of rock. The ice boat struck it, jolted upward, and flew high above the forest.

Danny understood at once. Ethan meant to use the height of Navel Island's mountain and the speed of his descent to jump over the forest, taking the shortest route to the finish.

It was a daring choice. Most people would never have the nerve to try it. But the forest was hundreds of meters wide. No ice boat could travel that far on momentum alone.

Danny could no longer do anything to stop him. Ethan's boat began to drop, and Danny's heart leaped into his throat.

A breeze ruffled Ethan's bangs. He could feel the boat descending, but he remained calm. He even looked up at the cable car and met Sabrina's eyes through its window.

He flashed her a broad grin, then said quietly, "Haunter, let's fly."

A sudden powerful wind rose around the boat. It arrested their fall and carried them toward the finish.

What?!

On the ground, Danny could only stare. Ethan sailed over the forest, passed him completely, and left him far behind.

Bang!

The strange wind died away. Ethan's boat struck the ground, scattering chips of ice, and its remaining momentum sent it straight across the finish line.

It slid another dozen or so meters before coming to a stop. Ethan climbed out and waited more than ten seconds for Danny's boat to cross the line.

"How... how did you do that?" Danny asked. Such a long jump seemed impossible. And where had that wind come from?

"With my Pokémon's help, of course," Ethan said with a smile. "The third round is mine, right?"

Danny applauded, his admiration sincere. "Of course. In all my years as a Gym Leader, I've never seen anyone get over that forest from the air. That was quite a sight!"

Ethan smiled. He had planned his route back at the summit while looking over the course. He did not know the terrain as Danny did, and he lacked Danny's racing experience. He had needed another way to win.

Sabrina stepped off the cable car and walked toward them. When she reached Ethan, Danny took a badge from his pocket and held it out.

"Congratulations on clearing Navel Gym. This Sea Ruby Badge is proof of your victory."

Ethan looked down. Just as Cissy had told him, the Sea Ruby Badge was made from a shell. A red Pokémon paw print marked its white surface, and a shining glass bead was set into the print. It was not as beautiful as the Coral-Eye Badge, but it was distinctive.

Ethan thanked Danny, put the badge in his case, and left with Sabrina.

Danny stood watching them for a long time. The challenge had given him plenty to think about. It was not just that Ethan owned an Articuno. Danny still could not make sense of the man himself.

Most of all, Ethan's beautiful companion had not spoken to Danny once or even looked him properly in the eye. For the first time, he found himself questioning both his appeal and the status he drew from being a Gym Leader.

But Danny was an easygoing man, and the encounter was only a brief episode in his life. He shook it off. Without bothering to investigate Ethan's identity, he recalled his Pokémon and headed back toward the sea.

Blue sky, white clouds, and a beautiful ocean—what could be more inviting?

Time to enjoy some more surfing!
