Clack!

The gears stopped. The Battle Factory's roof had opened completely, revealing a clear blue sky and the sun high above. Warm light and fresh air poured in, changing the atmosphere of the field.

Without waiting for orders, both Articuno spread their wings and flew out of the building into the open sky.

The Trainer areas were on raised platforms. Ethan could stay where he was, see the whole battlefield, and call commands to his Articuno.

With both sides ready, Sergio raised his hand again. “Battle, begin!”

Ethan and Noland called their first moves at the same instant.

“Articuno, use Blizzard!”

“Articuno, Steel Wing!”

Ethan wanted to control the field and test his opponent. Noland sent his bird straight in with a Steel-type move that was strong against Ice types.

Their choices reflected their temperaments. Ethan usually fought methodically. He tested an opponent before pressing his attack, leaving fewer openings and waiting for the chance to strike decisively. Strategy, together with the strength of his Pokémon, had brought him so many victories.

Noland's rugged, forthright manner led him to attack directly. As Blizzard formed, his Articuno hardened its wings like steel and plunged into the storm. Snow swallowed both birds, hiding them from view.

With the Pokémon out of sight, some of the tension in the viewing area eased. Max had been clenching his fists ever since the battle started. He relaxed his hands and turned to the others.

“Who do you think will win?”

This was a battle between Legendary Pokémon. Encounters with them were rare enough; watching two fight was thrilling.

Scott, nearest to Max, shook his head. “Hard to say. They're both Articuno. Either could win.”

Ash hesitated too. “Maybe it depends on which one fights better?”

Brock smiled with more certainty. “I think Mr. Vale's Articuno will win.”

“Why?” the others asked.

They were the same species, with much the same moves and type. Leaving their Trainers' commands aside, their strengths ought to be close. Why was Brock so sure?

“Remember,” Brock told Ash, who knew Ethan's bird best. “Noland's Articuno has only been here a short time and rarely battles other Trainers. Ethan caught his long ago, and it's faced many powerful opponents. That experience matters.”

Brock kept one more thought to himself. Ethan's Articuno wore a collar that seemed to increase its power. That could only improve its chances.

Ash remembered its battles at Shamouti Island and the crystal tower. It had fought other Legendary Pokémon, the birds and the beasts, under tremendous pressure. Those were only the matches Ash had witnessed. Ethan must have used it in others during his travels.

In experience and strength, Ethan's Articuno probably had the advantage. Ethan's commands would add to it.

A cry rang from inside the Blizzard. One of the birds had been struck, but the storm concealed which one. Explosions and flashes of light showed that a fierce fight was underway, yet no one could see its course. Snow drifted down over the spectators.

Everyone looked up, waiting for the birds to emerge. Ethan remained calm. Noland looked more anxious. With neither Pokémon visible, their Trainers could do little; the birds had to rely on themselves.

The Blizzard was fierce but short-lived. After two or three minutes, it dissipated. Snowflakes falling into the warmer air quickly turned to water.

At last they could see both Pokémon. To the crowd's surprise, Noland's Articuno, the one that had charged into the storm, bore several small wounds. They were little more than scratches and did not hamper its movement, but they showed that it had come off worse inside the Blizzard.

Brock's prediction appeared sound. In a direct contest of strength, Ethan's Articuno had the edge.

Noland saw it too. He had no easy answer, but he could at least keep his bird out of another storm that blocked his view. Now that the sky was clear, he could direct the battle again.

He ordered Articuno forward. Its setback had not broken his aggressive rhythm. The two birds fought high above the field, one giving chase and then the other. Ice-type moves met in the air, scattering crystalline snow.

Ash and his friends barely noticed the beautiful flakes. They watched each exchange, their attention fixed on the two Articuno.

They were Legendary Pokémon indeed. They had only just begun, and neither had used its full strength, yet the weather was already changing. Ice energy leaked into the air. Their attacks altered the surrounding atmosphere. Dark clouds gathered across what had been a sunny sky, blocking the light.

The temperature dropped abruptly. Even from the viewing area, the others felt the chill.

“So this is Articuno's power,” Max murmured. “They've darkened the whole sky.”

May and Max had never watched a battle of this scale so closely. The moves were familiar, yet whether because of the birds' power or the awe surrounding Legendary Pokémon, the fight felt very different from an ordinary match.

Ash pulled down the brim of his cap. “You haven't seen anything yet. The real battle's only beginning.”

He was right. Ethan had spent these exchanges measuring Noland's Articuno. Now he knew its strength, and it was time to finish the match.

The rhythm changed in an instant. Ethan's bearing sharpened. At his command, his Articuno turned and drove forward like an unsheathed blade, cold power gathering around it.

Noland did not know Ethan's fighting style. The sudden shift caught him off guard, and his Articuno took a heavy blow before he could adjust.

He stared in alarm. He had only just grown used to the earlier pace and was looking for his own opening. Ethan had acted first.

Noland reacted quickly, ordering his bird to pull away before it took more damage. He frowned at its labored breathing. He needed a way to recover, but Ethan's Articuno was already closing in again.

Left with little choice, Noland copied Ethan's earlier move. He called for Blizzard to buy time, blunt the attack, and let his Articuno regroup.

At that moment, it was a good decision. Thick clouds now covered the sky. As Noland's bird drew on its power, it created a Blizzard larger and stronger than the first.

Snow-laden winds swept over the entire Factory. They blew through the open roof and down into the building, striking Ash and the others in the face.

They wore only light clothes. At such close range, the bitter cold was unbearable. Huddling and shivering, they fled farther back, found towels to wrap around themselves, and crowded together for warmth.

May glanced at Ethan. He was still standing on the Trainer's platform, exposed to the wind. She grabbed another towel and ran back toward him.

She need not have worried. When she struggled through the storm to his side, she found a barrier surrounding him. The wind could not reach it.

“Huh?”

Gengar rose from Ethan's shadow with a grin. “You made that trip for nothing, little girl.”

Of course Gengar was protecting him. With Gengar there, May thought, even a falling meteor would have trouble reaching Ethan. She felt foolish for forgetting it.

Embarrassed, she looked at Ethan. His attention remained on the battle. She was about to leave when he spoke without turning.

“Stay. Come stand beside me and watch the battle from here.”

May stopped. Had she heard correctly? Did he want her beside him to spare her the Blizzard, or was there another reason?

She stepped closer. Ethan turned to her with a smile.

“You're a Coordinator now, and battling is an important part of that. I don't know much about teaching Contests. But as we travel together, I hope you'll watch my battles from my point of view and find something you can learn for yourself.”

From his point of view? May's heart beat faster. She was not quite sure how she felt, but she nodded and took her place beside him on the Trainer's platform.

This was not a League tournament. It was a formal challenge, but its rules were less rigid, and the two sides already knew each other. Even if Noland had seen May joining Ethan, he was unlikely to object. In any case, he was occupied by the Blizzard and had no time to look over.

Visibility inside the storm was almost nonexistent. Once May was beside him, Ethan looked up again. He could make out only two blue shapes, like ice crystals, circling and darting in and out of the snow. He could not tell which was his Articuno and which was Noland's.

They would have to wait for the Blizzard to end. Judging by the heavy clouds, this storm would last much longer than Ethan's first one.

He could neither direct his Pokémon nor follow the fight. Now it came down to which Articuno could defeat the other unaided in those harsh conditions.
