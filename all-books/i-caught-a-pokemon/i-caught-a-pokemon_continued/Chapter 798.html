A battle was unfolding inside the Saffron Gym, though it was taking place in Sabrina's room rather than on the battlefield.

Ethan performed admirably. Over the course of more than an hour, he defeated Sabrina so thoroughly that she could only beg him to stop. They had gone three rounds, with only a few minutes of rest between them. By the end, Sabrina was overheated and lay breathing hard, unable to get up.

Evidently Ethan's Pokémon were not the only ones who could put on an impressive performance in battle.

He lay on the bed, gazed at the unfamiliar ceiling, and stretched. Youth really was his greatest advantage. He had energy to spare.

He drew an exhausted Sabrina into his arms and breathed in the scent of her hair. For the moment, he felt completely content. The desperate longing and passion of their reunion had settled into a quiet warmth.

One concern came to him. In the heat of the moment, he had forgotten to ask whether she was in a safe part of her cycle, and he had not prepared a condom. He glanced at Sabrina, lying peacefully beside him like a tame cat, and could not bring himself to spoil the moment by asking now.

He would ask later. And even if there were consequences? They had already chosen each other. They were still young, though, and it would be early for them to become parents. For now, he wanted to enjoy the tenderness of being together again.

…

That afternoon, Ethan showered and came out of Sabrina's room feeling refreshed. The world seemed brighter. His legs and lower back were tired, however, and he had no wish to fight another “battle” any time soon.

Fortunately, he had had the foresight to send Misty and May to explore nearby before entering Saffron City. With those two around, he and Sabrina could hardly have enjoyed even a quiet embrace, let alone time alone together.

He checked the time. Misty and May had agreed to meet him at the Gym, but they had not returned yet. He decided to wander around while he waited. Sabrina needed to wash carefully after all the exertion before she came out to see anyone.

Ethan strolled through the Gym. He realized he had never properly explored it. Besides serving as an official Pokémon League Gym for challengers, it was also a center for studying psychic abilities. The lessons were Sabrina's principal income apart from League funding.

It reminded him of Erika's flower and perfume shops, and of Roxanne's work as a teacher. Different as their businesses were, each Gym Leader had a way to earn money beyond the Gym. Of course, some pursued pleasure more than business. Brawly of Dewford, for instance, spent much of every day surfing.

Several rooms opened off the first-floor hall. In one, men and women dressed in white sat at desks with spoons in their hands. They tried to bend the spoons with psychic power or guess what was on the other side of cards.

Not everyone had a talent for psychic abilities. Some stared until their eyes ached without making a spoon move at all. Their occasional correct guesses about the cards seemed more like luck.

With nothing else to do, Ethan opened the door and went in. No one noticed him at first. Only when he reached a table and picked up a spoon did the man across from him look up.

“Who are you?” The man studied him. “I haven't seen you here before. You're not one of us, are you?”

“No. I'm visiting the Gym Leader. I had some time free, so I thought I'd look around.”

“Visiting Miss Sabrina?” The man looked doubtful. He did not believe some stranger could casually come to see the famously distant psychic Gym Leader. Still, he let it pass. “You're welcome to look, but don't handle our equipment.”

Ethan glanced at the table. “You mean the spoons?”

“Of course. Psychic ability is something you're born with. Without the talent, you can train all you like and never reach it.”

Ethan looked around. Everyone here seemed to be a beginner; no one had shown much power. Curious, he asked, “You've been training here a while, haven't you? What can you do now?”

The question was sincere, but the man smiled proudly. “Since you want to know, I'll show you.”

He took a spoon, fixed his eyes on it, and shouted as his entire body strained. Veins stood out on him. Under the pressure of an unseen force, the spoon slowly bent until it formed a right angle.

The man exhaled and looked at Ethan with unmistakable pride. “See? That's my psychic power. Someone without the gift could never do it.”

Leaving Pokémon aside, the gap between this class and Sabrina was enormous. Even Tate and Liza at the Mossdeep Gym were far beyond them. Perhaps with psychic abilities, talent really did set a hard limit. Years of practice could accomplish something, but it could hardly turn a beginner into Sabrina.

Ethan gave a polite little clap. He was about to speak when the man grinned. “That took me three years to learn. What do you think? Want to try?”

“I'll pass. I know myself. I have no talent for this, and I've never practiced.”

He started to leave. His shadow shifted beneath his feet. A lock of hair beside his temple moved, as though something invisible had brushed against it. Two seconds later, Ethan shook his head helplessly and looked back at the man.

“All right. I'll give it a try.”

“You really will?” The man had only wanted to impress him. He had not expected this young stranger to try it himself. Well, it would teach him the difference between a psychic student and an ordinary person.

He placed a fresh spoon on the table, eager to see Ethan's expression when he failed.

Ethan held out his hand. He made no show of concentrating. To the man's shock, the spoon flew straight from the table into Ethan's palm.

“What?”

The man's eyes bulged. Worse was to come. With Ethan still apparently doing nothing, the spoon in his hand twisted itself into a tight spiral.

“I… I… I…” The man could barely form a word.

His reaction brought the others over. When they heard that Ethan had supposedly never used psychic power before, yet had twisted a spoon like that, they gaped.

“How is that possible?”

“He must be joking. We've practiced for so long…”

“A prodigy!”

They crowded around Ethan, all talking at once. Before they could question him further, Sabrina's voice came from the doorway.

“I wondered where you'd gone. So this is where you were.”

Ethan waved to the students, smiled, and went to meet her. They walked out side by side. The sight shocked the students even more than the spoon trick.

“Did I see that right?” one whispered. “Miss Sabrina spoke to him. And they left together?”

The man who had challenged Ethan remembered his claim to be visiting Sabrina. He had been telling the truth. But who was he? How could Sabrina treat a man with such warmth? Both surprises had overturned everything the student thought he knew.

Outside the room, Sabrina laughed softly. “I didn't know you felt like playing tricks on my students.”

“It wasn't me.” Ethan spread his hands. “It was Gengar.”

Gengar appeared in midair beside him with a wicked grin. The flying spoon and its twisted shape had been its work.

“Heh. I only wanted to tease them a little,” Gengar said. “He questioned my Trainer first. I had to show him what we could do.”

“That wasn't showing him. That was making a fool of him.” Ethan sighed, then remembered something. “Sabrina, I sent that Jigglypuff I caught—the one that puts people to sleep—to stay here. How is it doing?”

“I'm glad you still remember it.” Sabrina smiled. “You never asked about it when we talked. I thought you'd forgotten.”

It was true. Ethan had not checked on Jigglypuff once since catching it and sending it here. He had not caught it because he wanted another partner, though. He had wanted to spare the world a menace. A Jigglypuff carrying a microphone could create a disaster wherever it went.

“I left it with you because I knew you could keep it under control. Where is it now?”

“Probably wandering around the city. I hid its microphone, then let it roam. It used to sneak back at night to steal the microphone. After I turned it into a doll a few times, it settled down.”

Ethan nodded. Sabrina had not confined Jigglypuff, only taken away its weapon. Until it recovered the microphone, it should remain quiet. Taking the microphone back from Sabrina would be difficult for it.

“Still, be careful,” he said. “It might find another one somewhere.”

The Gym doors opened as he spoke. Misty and May came in.

“Good, it isn't closed after all.” Misty exhaled in relief when she saw Ethan. “There was a sign outside saying the Gym was closed today. I thought no one was here. I'm glad we came in to check.”

Closed? Sabrina tilted her head. Perhaps her father had put up the sign. She said nothing about it.

“Hello, Sabrina. It's good to see you again.” Misty brought May forward. “This is a friend we met in Hoenn. She came with us to visit Kanto.”

Misty had evidently told May that Sabrina mattered to Ethan. May greeted her a little stiffly. “Hello, Sabrina.”

Sabrina nodded a greeting to both girls. Ethan turned to them. “Where did you go all this time?”

Misty brightened and held up a flyer. “Brother Ethan, look what we found! Saffron City is holding a Pokémon Contest in a few days. We can't miss a chance like this!”

Ethan looked at May. “Do you want to enter?”

“I do.” May nodded firmly, hope in her eyes. “I want to see how a Kanto Contest differs from the ones in Hoenn!”
