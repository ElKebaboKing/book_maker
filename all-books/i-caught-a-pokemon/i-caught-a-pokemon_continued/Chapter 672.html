This was Ethan's first battle against a Slakoth.

In all his travels, he had rarely seen a Trainer use one, whether in a street battle or a formal match. The reason was simple: Slakoth was too lazy.

Its evolved forms, Vigoroth and Slaking, were another matter. But whatever modest advantages Slakoth had were overshadowed by its laziness, leaving it little chance to shine in battle.

Most people lacked the patience to raise one. Slakoth hardly wanted to move and preferred to stay in one place, while Trainers needed to travel constantly. It was difficult to accommodate both.

Worse, when Slakoth fell asleep, nothing seemed to wake it. A Trainer who sent one out at such a time would make a laughingstock of themselves.

Only a Gym Leader like Norman would take the trouble to train one so carefully.

Ethan chose his own Pokémon. His Poké Ball opened, and Metang appeared on the battlefield.

In terms of type, Metang could not exploit a weakness in Slakoth. But Metang's Steel typing meant that Slakoth's Normal-type moves would deal only half their usual damage.

Slakoth was also in its first evolutionary stage, while Metang had already reached its second. Metang's body was far stronger. On those two points alone, the advantage seemed to belong to Metang.

Yet although Ethan had trained his Metang privately, this was its first Gym battle. Its lack of experience might bring the two Pokémon closer to even footing.

Once both Trainers were ready, the referee signaled the start of the match.

“Metang, use Confusion!”

“Slakoth, Shadow Ball!”

Norman was every bit the near-Elite Four Trainer Ethan had expected. Perhaps he had guessed Ethan's opening move: he issued his order at the same moment.

Invisible psychic force struck the Shadow Ball in midair, setting off a violent explosion. Norman had anticipated the attack and countered it immediately.

Then a shape burst through the smoke.

Metang had slipped forward under its cover and floated across the middle of the field toward Slakoth. As it drew near, it raised both iron claws and slashed down with Metal Claw.

The claws gleamed as they came in, swift and precise. Just before they connected, Slakoth sidestepped with startling agility and avoided the blow with ease.

Oh? Ethan raised an eyebrow, but gave it little thought. He ordered a second attack.

Metang swept its claws at Slakoth again. This time Slakoth took a single shifting step and dodged once more.

Now Ethan had to pay attention. He narrowed his eyes and studied the Pokémon's movements. Norman's Slakoth was unusual.

People tended to think of Slakoth as slow and clumsy because the species rarely moved. What Ethan saw before him was entirely different.

Slakoth swayed and drifted from side to side, evading every one of Metang's attacks with an almost weightless ease.

So that's it... Norman must have trained it specifically to compensate for its weakness. No wonder it could move like that.

Close combat would not work. Ethan needed another approach. He told Metang to move back toward his half of the field.

Norman, however, had no intention of letting it leave. Seeing Metang retreat, he ordered Slakoth to stick close.

“That's it! Slakoth, use Feint Attack!”

Slakoth suddenly thrust out an arm at Metang. Ethan had been watching for a counterattack and had Metang float upward just in time. The strike missed by a narrow margin.

Just as Norman had read Ethan's intentions, Ethan understood Norman's. He was defending and waiting to counterattack. There was nothing mysterious about it.

The exchange finally gave Metang enough room to break away and return to its side of the field. Slakoth did not follow. Its nimble steps let it dodge close-range attacks, but they had not made it any faster over a distance. On a field this wide, catching a floating Metang would be another matter.

“Metang, Confusion!”

Ethan repeated his opening attack. He did not believe Slakoth could intercept Confusion with Shadow Ball every time. One mistake would give him an opportunity to press.

Norman understood the plan, but he had another answer.

“Slakoth, Blizzard!”

Slakoth opened its mouth and blew out glittering snowflakes. They grew larger and thicker in the air until a fierce blizzard swept across the field toward Metang.

Ice-type Blizzard would deal only half damage to Steel-type Metang. Norman was aiming for something else.

Among the countless flakes, the previously invisible path of Confusion suddenly took shape. Slakoth saw where it was going, twisted aside, and dodged it easily.

That was not all. The freezing snow coated Metang's metal body with frost. As the ice built up, its joints began to lose their freedom of movement.

So that's what he's after! Ethan understood with a glance.

Metang was not a warm-bodied creature. It might not even have blood to circulate; it was more like a cold stone, with a very low surface temperature. Once snow and ice covered it, Metang could neither melt the coating with its own warmth nor easily clear it away.

A moment later, Metang wobbled in the air. Even the simple act of floating seemed to have become difficult to control.

Norman really was something. He had given a Pokémon usually seen as weak a distinctive way to evade attacks, taught it an Ice-type Blizzard, and perhaps kept still more surprises in reserve. He had raised his Pokémon exceptionally well.

That tactic might have defeated another Trainer. Against Ethan, it was not quite enough.

The thought flashed through his mind as he gave his next order without hesitation. “Use Zen Headbutt!”

Zen Headbutt concentrated psychic power in the user's forehead for a charging attack. The force of the blow could even make an opponent flinch.

Protected by psychic energy, Metang rushed toward Slakoth again. Retreating only to charge back in had wasted the distance it had gained, but Ethan had little choice. Of the moves Metang currently knew, this was his best option.

The space between them gave Metang time to accelerate. By the time it reached Slakoth, its momentum had built to a peak.

But a Gym Leader's Pokémon would not be so easily intimidated. At Norman's command, Slakoth shed its lazy manner. Its presence changed sharply, and instead of dodging, it drove forward to meet Metang head-on.

Ethan, Norman, Caroline, and Misty watched the two Pokémon close the gap. At last, they crashed into each other...
