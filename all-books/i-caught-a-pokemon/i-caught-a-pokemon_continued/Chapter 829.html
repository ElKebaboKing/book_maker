May won the Silver Town Contest and earned her second Ribbon in Kanto.

When Ethan had traveled with Sabrina, Erika, or Misty, his own adventure had always come first. His companions had arranged their journeys around him. With May beside him, he would have to make time for Contests like this one. On the face of it, that took time away from his travels.

In truth, he was glad to take part as a spectator. Watching from a different perspective gave him fresh insights, helped him grow, and let him learn things from the Coordinators that he might never have encountered otherwise.

He had no role to play in the Contest, but that made it both a rest and a chance to relax. There was value in balancing effort with leisure.

As for Jeremy, the man May had defeated, he changed back into the honest-looking office worker after the Contest and left the hall with a faintly lonely air.

He had not won or quite fulfilled his dream, but at least he had gained his family's acceptance. That was a great deal in itself. Perhaps they would meet him again at another Contest someday.

That could wait. With May's victory behind them, it was time to continue their journey.

They had bought their tickets the day before, so they did not return to the Pokémon Center after leaving the Contest hall. They waited at the docks, boarded a small passenger ship, and set off from the Kanto mainland.

As planned, they were bound for Metallica Island, not far from Cinnabar Island and home to the Battle Palace. The voyage would take much longer than a few hours through the complicated sea routes.

The three-deck ship pulled slowly away from the pier. A little over an hour after leaving Silver Town, they could make out Vermilion City in the distance. The ship did not stop there. As the sun went down, it left the coastal waters for the open sea.

Out there, the view was much the same. The water was bluer and the air saltier and more damp. Instead of a long mainland coastline, there were islands scattered around them.

The novelty of being at sea soon wore off. Passengers stopped staring at the unchanging water and found other things to do.

Even on a ship this small, a Pokémon battle was inevitable. Two boys who could not yet have been ten were directing their Pokémon across the deck, enjoying themselves immensely. Their unpolished match drew a few adults over to watch.

Ethan had no interest in looking. He lay back in a deck chair and enjoyed the last sea breeze before sunset. May sat beside him, studying a map and chatting with him now and then.

A school of Arrokuda swam up from somewhere in the surrounding sea and leaped alongside the ship. Then someone suddenly shouted, “It's Lugia!”

The cry drew most of the passengers to the rail. They looked out over the water and did indeed catch a glimpse of a silver shape passing beneath the surface. It was partly submerged; though it looked like Lugia, no one could be sure what it was.

That was enough to excite them. Marveling at their luck, the tourists took out their phones to film it.

May went over for a look too. When she returned, Ethan was still lying in his chair, his eyes closed and his expression unmoved. She could not help smiling.

“Ethan,” she asked quietly as she sat down, “did you ever see Lugia while you were traveling through Kanto and Johto?”

“Of course. I've seen more than one.” His tone did not change, as though a Legendary Pokémon like Lugia could no longer interest him. “It's just a big water bird. Once you've seen enough of them, there's nothing particularly special about it.”

He was showing off now, even if he made it sound casual. Then again, he had the experience to back it up.

A strong wind rose after dark, churning the waves and rocking the little ship. The weather made it easy to wonder whether the vessel could withstand it. For a frequent traveler like Ethan, though, the motion only helped him sleep.

They spent the night aboard and reached their destination early the next morning.

From a distance, Metallica Island looked like an ordinary large island covered in greenery. Ethan did not know where its name came from. Did it have rich metal deposits? Or did it have many Pokémon with bodies as hard as metal?

He had found no explanation on the map. It hardly mattered. He had come to challenge the Battle Frontier, not investigate the island's name.

They disembarked at a tiny town. Their ship sailed away almost as soon as they left the dock. With its noisy horn gone, the surroundings fell so quiet that it felt as if they stood inside an isolated primeval forest.

Perhaps few people came here apart from the occasional challenger. Even the town seemed deserted. Ethan and May stopped briefly at the Pokémon Center outside it, then followed their map toward the mysterious Battle Palace.

The walk was less peaceful than they expected. Dense forest covered nearly the entire island and sheltered all kinds of wild Pokémon. With so little human disturbance, the Pokémon were fiercely territorial. Ethan and May were attacked several times despite merely passing through.

The Beedrill were especially numerous and aggressive. They harried the pair for a long stretch of the path. In the end, Ethan brought out Scizor and Dragonite to escort them, and only then did they reach the Battle Palace without further trouble.

“Palace” was hardly the best word for it. It looked more like a ruin.

An imposing tower of neatly stacked stone rose among the trees, built in a style that seemed foreign to the region. Moss covered its steps, blocks, walls, and stonework. Parts had crumbled. There was no telling how many years it had stood here or why anyone had built it in this forest.

Scott clearly had not paid to build this place. Perhaps its history had something to do with the Frontier Brain who lived here.

Ethan and May had only just climbed the steps when an old man came out of the ruins. He looked to be in his seventies or eighties, with a round bald head, white eyebrows and beard, a bare chest, unusual tattoos on his arms, and a staff in his hand.

“More challengers have come to my door. Welcome.”

Despite his age, the man was full of vigor. He moved easily and spoke in a strong, ringing voice. Sunlight even flashed off his bald head.

His distinctive appearance let Ethan recognize him at once: Spenser, the Frontier Brain of the Battle Palace. Ethan knew his name, but little else. He did not know which Pokémon Spenser used or how he liked to battle.

“You must be Mr. Spenser,” Ethan said. Since the Frontier Brain had already guessed their purpose, he went straight to it. “My name is Ethan. As you said, I've come to challenge the Battle Palace.”

“There is only one reason people cross the sea and brave the waves to get here.” Spenser smiled. “I am Spenser. Whenever I see a young trainer like you, with sharp eyes and so much spirit, I'm glad to know the next generation of Pokémon Trainers is in good hands.”

Even from those few words, Ethan could feel an elder's concern for someone younger. It fit the impression Spenser gave him: a wise, generous old man.

Spenser glanced up at the bright sky. “I can see confidence in your eyes, and that's good. I'll meet your challenge with everything I have. But on such a fine day, why battle indoors? Would you like to have our match outside?”

There it was. Although Spenser phrased it as a question, his tone made his preference plain. Ethan remembered the outdoor battle from the anime. It was the true test of the Battle Palace.

That suited him. He had been worried the challenge would be too easy. An interesting new kind of match appealed to him far more than a dull indoor battle.

“Of course,” Ethan said without hesitation. “The air here is wonderful, and you can really feel the natural world around you. I'd like to breathe more of it.”

Spenser smiled at his agreement. Staff in hand, he led them away from the Palace.

At first May assumed “outside” meant a clearing near the building. But Spenser kept walking, and she began to wonder where he was taking them.

After more than ten minutes she quickened her steps to walk beside Ethan. “Where do you think he's taking us?” she whispered.

“Who knows?” Ethan shrugged, then joked, “At least he won't take me off the island.”

They crossed a stretch of forest. The ground began to climb sharply, and before May quite realized it, they were ascending a modest mountain.

At the summit, the landscape opened around them. They could finally see the whole Battle Palace, though the trees and vegetation hid most of it and left only its outline visible.

May stopped to catch her breath and took in the hilltop. It looked as if someone, or some Pokémon, had sliced away half the mountain with tremendous force. The flat top had been made into a simple battle arena. A cool sea breeze reached them there, lending the air a chill despite the sunshine.

So this was what Spenser meant by an outdoor battle. They had certainly come a long way for it.

May was impressed by the old man's stamina. She and Ethan were young and used to traveling, yet the climb had left her breathless. Spenser was far older and had reached the top without even looking winded.

Then he gave her a still greater surprise. Spenser pointed first at the ground beneath them, then toward a distant coastline, and finally to a winding river on the other side.

“This arena is where our battle begins,” he told Ethan. “From that coast to the river, the entire forest is our outdoor battlefield.”

“What?” May blurted out. “All of it?”

It was hard to blame her. The area Spenser had marked with two casual gestures was enormous. By her estimate, it covered at least a third of Metallica Island.

“Yes, all of it.” Spenser nodded to May, then turned an approving gaze on Ethan.

The young man had remained calm from the moment they met. Even after seeing the full extent of the battlefield, his expression had not changed. Compared with earlier challengers, his composure was remarkable.

Ethan did not know the impression he had made. He simply studied the boundaries Spenser had indicated, fixed them in his memory, and nodded.

A larger arena and more complicated terrain were nothing to be alarmed about. When he had battled Legendary Pokémon, the sky and the earth themselves had been the battlefield. Why should this scale shock him?

With the battlefield explained, Spenser set out the rules. “We will have a three-on-three elimination battle. Defeat all three of my Pokémon and you win. You may switch your Pokémon freely during the match and choose whichever lineup best serves you.”

May still had a question. Since Ethan said nothing, she asked, “Um, Grandpa Spenser, with a battlefield this large, won't we lose sight of the Pokémon as they fight? How will anyone decide the result?”

Spenser laughed and beckoned. A small flying robot appeared before May, hovering in the air.

“Don't worry. This is the Battle Palace's referee robot. Its camera will let us follow the Pokémon and see how they're doing wherever they go!”
