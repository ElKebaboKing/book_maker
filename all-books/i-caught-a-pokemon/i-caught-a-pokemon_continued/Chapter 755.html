Harley opened his mouth, then closed it without a word.

Perhaps Ethan had done what Harley himself had done: sent out one of his strongest Pokémon first to steal an unexpected early win. If so, the man was devious.

Harley told himself to stay calm. He drew a deep breath, recalled Wigglytuff, and took out his second Poké Ball.

“Well played,” he said, unable to resist a sneer. “That little trick won you the first round. You won't be so lucky again!”

Ethan stared at him. What trick?

Since Feebas, Beldum, and Torchic had evolved, Eevee really was the weakest Pokémon he currently carried. If Harley could not even beat Eevee, perhaps he ought to examine his own team rather than blame Ethan. What a strange way to think.

Ethan shook his head. Under Harley's rules, even a winning Pokémon could not fight again, so he recalled Eevee and took out a second ball.

Harley once again waited without showing his Pokémon. Ethan was used to it by now. If Harley volunteered to go first, that would be more suspicious.

Blue light flashed, and Milotic appeared on Ethan's side of the field.

Harley hesitated. Milotic was rare in his experience. Robert had used one to great effect in the preliminaries, and Harley had seen perhaps one or two others during years of travel. He could count every Milotic he had encountered on one hand.

Anyone who knew the species knew it evolved from Feebas and became remarkably beautiful. Even so, few Trainers seemed to raise one. Harley had seen too little of them to judge their strength precisely, but he was sure Milotic was formidable.

He considered the ball in his hand, looked at Ethan, put it back in his pouch, and selected a different one.

Changing his choice after Ethan had revealed Milotic was shameless. Max protested loudly, and Misty booed him. Harley ignored them. Only winning mattered.

He opened the new ball. Manectric appeared before Milotic.

Misty booed even louder. Ash, Brock, and May frowned. Harley had deliberately chosen an Electric-type to face Ethan's Water-type and seemed perfectly pleased with himself. Still, Ethan did not object, so the others let it go.

Ethan truly did not care. An Electric-type might make the fight harder, but it was still a fight. Before the Ever Grande Conference, he welcomed chances for his Pokémon to practice against unfavorable type matchups. Rehearsing them now would keep them from losing their heads when such a battle mattered.

Besides, he understood Harley well enough by now. Complaining about the switch would achieve nothing; Harley would only mock him. Defeating his Pokémon would make the point better.

Both sides ready, the second round began.

The type advantage did demand some caution. An Electric-type attack could hurt Milotic badly, though a twofold weakness alone would not decide the match. It was better to prevent Manectric from landing a hit at all.

Milotic did not immediately flood the field with Water-type moves, which could make the surroundings unpredictable. First it used Safeguard, protecting itself from paralysis even if an Electric attack connected. Then it sent a Twister at Manectric, both damaging it and obscuring Milotic's position.

Manectric darted about to escape the swirling wind. Once released, the Twister followed a predictable path. Thanks to Manectric's speed, it took only one hit before escaping the affected area.

It had barely cleared the wind when a powerful Hydro Pump met it head-on.

Milotic had held back its Water-type attacks only until the right opening. Taken by surprise, Manectric caught the blast full in the face and flew backward, almost beyond the boundary line.

Knowing Harley, Ethan doubted he would stop fighting over a line anyway. But the blow visibly unsettled him. They had only just started, and already the battle looked like the first round.

“Manectric, what are you doing? Shock it!” Harley yelled.

Landing an attack was not as simple as saying so. On an open field, a Pokémon's condition, distance, position, timing, and state of mind all mattered. This was no turn-based game with an opponent waiting to be struck. Ethan had used Twister's interference to set up Hydro Pump; Manectric could not simply fire a bolt and expect it to connect.

Still, it answered its Trainer's urging and attacked with everything it had. Spark, Discharge, Thunder Fang—Milotic dodged several moves, but one Electric attack did land.

That was where Ethan's preparation paid off. Safeguard kept Milotic from being paralyzed, allowing it to press on.

With Manectric close, Milotic drove it back with Aqua Tail. Before it could use Discharge again, Milotic launched Surf.

A huge wave rose before Milotic and crashed down, burying Manectric in spray and carrying it across the field.

Like many Fire-types, some land-dwelling Electric-types handled a soaking badly. Wet fur could slow them and interfere with their attacks. Manectric, a doglike Pokémon with a golden triangle of fur atop its head, was particularly troubled by the water.

Some dogs were strong swimmers, but even a paddling Manectric could not break free of a wave this large. It tumbled helplessly through the water until the surge reached the opposite Trainer's position and began to lose force.

Manectric washed up at Harley's feet. Above it was his dark, shifting expression.

“Get up and fight!”

Manectric had plainly crossed the boundary. Harley did not care. This was a personal dispute, not a formal match, and he had no intention of stopping over a rule like that.

Ethan had expected as much. Milotic rode the tail of its own wave and arrived behind Manectric before it could rise. In front of Harley, it whipped Manectric across the face with another Aqua Tail. The sharp crack rang out. Manectric fell unconscious.

Harley flinched as though the blow had landed on him. He stared at Milotic standing so close, his chest tight and his stomach turning.

Gengar had not even entered the match, yet he had lost twice. Both defeats had been decisive. He finally understood that none of his Pokémon could beat Ethan's. He had challenged someone far beyond him.

What was the point of continuing? Milotic's last strike had knocked all the fight out of him. He wanted only to leave this humiliating place.

“I... I just remembered something I have to do. We'll call today's battle a draw for now. I'm leaving!”

Before anyone could respond, Harley recalled Manectric, threw out that dry excuse, and hurried away. He walked as though a monster were about to devour him from behind, picking up speed until he vanished from sight.

“He... just left?” Ash and the others stared after him, then looked at one another in disbelief.

They had expected Harley to lose. They knew the gulf between the two men. They had not expected him to abandon the match halfway through. He had proposed five rounds, and only two were over. There were still three chances—though, realistically, none would have helped him.

Had he lost all five, he might never have been able to hold his head up around them again. By leaving before the end, he could insist, as he already had, that the match was a draw. No one could force him to admit otherwise. He was unlikely to challenge Ethan again, though.

“Let him go,” Ethan said. He recalled Milotic, stroked its head in praise, and turned to the others. “The match didn't finish, but I doubt he'll bother May anymore. That's what I wanted. And those battles weren't much practice for my Pokémon anyway.”

The others agreed that the result was enough. Only Misty remained indignant.

“He got off too easily! You should've beaten every Pokémon he has and shown him exactly how strong you are!”

She was firmly on Ethan's side and could not stand hearing anyone speak badly of him. Harley's harassment of May only made her dislike him more. Two quick defeats did not satisfy her.

“Don't worry,” Ethan said with a smile. “There are Contests in plenty of places. We'll probably see him again, and when we do, we can be the ones to give him trouble.”

He said it lightly because he knew how far ahead of Harley he stood. Without that confidence, he could not have approached the battle so calmly. Harley's words were not worth getting angry over; they were on different levels altogether.

Ethan had grown rather self-assured. Given his achievements, perhaps that was understandable. Only Elite Four members and regional Champions could truly be compared with him now.

Both rounds together had taken less than five minutes. Ethan had not even worked off breakfast. The group chatted and laughed as they returned to the dormitory, soon putting Harley and the entire incident out of their minds.

At the entrance, they split up again. May needed to practice for tomorrow's main competition. Ethan and Ash did not want to waste the day either. Since they were together, they decided to find a quiet spot and prepare for the approaching Ever Grande Conference.

Max and Brock went with May, their traveling companion. Misty looked back and forth between May and Ethan, then stayed beside Ethan.

He and Ash were only going to train, not battle each other. The practice would likely be dull. Misty would not take any chances, though. Lisia had already stolen a march on her once. This time, Misty intended to keep Ethan in sight.

It would not happen again!
