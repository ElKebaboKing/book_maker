With Pikachu's defeat, the score stood at three to two. Morrison had recovered some ground, but the two rounds he had practically given away at the start still left him in danger.

Ash had four Pokémon that had yet to appear. Morrison would have to approach every remaining round carefully. He could not afford another easy loss.

Ash carried Pikachu back, returned to his Trainer's area, and sent out Torkoal.

As a Fire type, Torkoal had an advantage over Steelix. Morrison did not switch. He let Steelix face it, brimming with energy despite the poor matchup. He looked like a different person from the boy who had fought the first two rounds.

As soon as the round began, he ordered Steelix to trade attacks with Torkoal. Dragon Breath collided with Flamethrower. The flames proved stronger, pushing through the Dragon Breath and striking Steelix.

Fire did double damage to Steelix, and it cried out in pain. Ash seized the chance and told Torkoal to press its advantage. But when Torkoal breathed fire again, Steelix reacted and answered with another Dragon Breath.

This time Flamethrower did not break through. The moves exploded against each other, filling the air with flame and smoke.

Steelix used the cover to Dig underground. Unsure where it would emerge, Torkoal hurriedly used Withdraw. Steelix burst up directly beneath it and launched it high into the air.

As Torkoal fell, it accidentally flipped onto its back. Steelix took the opening and hit it with Dragon Breath. Torkoal was unable to battle.

The referee raised a flag. “Torkoal is unable to battle! Steelix wins!”

That made two victories in a row. Steelix had beaten Torkoal despite the type disadvantage, knocking out a second opponent. From another point of view, Torkoal's Conference performance was as consistent as ever.

“Morrison is fighting better and better,” Brock said as he watched Torkoal fall. The others nodded. Morrison had plainly set aside his conflict. He no longer hesitated when he attacked, and his old cheerful energy had returned.

“He is,” Ethan agreed. “He's fired up now. Ash may be the one in trouble.”

Once Morrison recovered, he had won two straight rounds and erased the lead Ash had gained. Without the two victories he had handed Ash earlier, Ash might already have been far behind. Morrison was every bit as capable a Trainer as Ash. If Ash could not bring out his Pokémon's full potential, one misstep might end his tournament.

Both now had three Pokémon left. After a moment's thought, Ash sent out Grovyle.

Grass-type attacks would not do full damage to Steelix, so the matchup did not seem ideal. But this was Ash's Grovyle: powerful, coolheaded, and entirely unruffled.

Like Ash before him, Morrison chose not to switch. Perhaps he had forgotten he could. Steelix had already fought two rounds and was looking tired, yet Morrison sent it against Grovyle all the same.

As expected, Grovyle won. It dodged Steelix's Dig with agile steps. Then, against an incoming Dragon Breath, it created a moment the crowd would remember.

Grovyle leaped high, crossed its arms before its head, and used Leaf Blade to split the Dragon Breath. It drove forward until it reached Steelix's massive head and struck with all its force.

Boom! Steelix's enormous body crashed down, shaking the entire Grass Field. The big screen showed that it had fainted.

Ash's number one Pokémon had earned its reputation. The whole sequence looked impressive. “Steelix is unable to battle! Grovyle wins!”

The score was tied again. Once Ash and Morrison had begun fighting in earnest, every round had been exciting. The audience was finally seeing the strength expected of Trainers in the final eight. It was a shame one of these two would have to fall in the round of sixteen.

Watching the field, Ethan reached for the snacks on the seat beside him. His hand found nothing. He tried another bag and found that empty too.

Huh? He looked down. All that remained of the snacks he had bought were a few empty wrappers. Had he eaten them that quickly?

He turned to May. Her cheeks were puffed out, and she was still chewing like a little squirrel. There was no question who had eaten them.

But when had she managed it? She was fast. He had bought so much food, and she had not left a bite.

By the time Ethan looked back to the field, Ash and Morrison were fighting again. Morrison had sent out Gligar, a purple, scorpion-like Pokémon with wings on its back.

The insides of Gligar's wings were blue. Two pointed fangs protruded from its mouth. Large pincers tipped its forelimbs, and a fearsome stinger sat at the end of its tail.

Gligar were said to nest on steep cliffs. They could glide silently through the air as though sliding over the ground, then hop back to their nests after landing. Clinging to rock walls, they caught the wind with the membranes on their limbs. When they spotted prey, they spread their wings and swooped down, gliding toward it. Their large claws and talons seized a victim's face. They would cling to it, startle it into panic, and then strike with their poisonous stingers.

Ethan knew Gligar from the anime and games, but this was the first time he had seen a Trainer use one since coming to this world. He studied it with interest.

Grovyle remained Ash's Pokémon. The two moved quickly and fought evenly at first. Grovyle used the thick grass to its advantage. When Gligar skimmed low over the ground, Grovyle leaped onto its back, hoping to attack from above.

Gligar reacted at once. It carried Grovyle high into the air and threw it off. Grovyle could neither maneuver nor dodge up there. Gligar seized its neck and drove it violently into the ground.

The impact knocked Grovyle unconscious. It was unable to battle. The whole exchange had taken only a few minutes.

The fast attack brought both Trainers fully into the excitement of the match. The referee had scarcely raised a flag before Ash recalled Grovyle and, without hesitation, sent out Swellow.

The Pokémon took their contest into the sky. Trainers and Pokémon alike refused to yield. They tangled and chased each other, accumulating wounds but never stopping their attacks.

After a final clash at very close range, both Pokémon dropped from the air and hit the ground. Neither could battle. They had taken each other out.

The crowd loved the sheer force of it. Seeing both fall together brought a roar of delight. They seemed to have forgotten Morrison's poor start entirely. Now they eagerly awaited the next round.

The crowd's energy swept up Ash and Morrison. Both looked even more excited. Yet each had only one Pokémon left and one chance remaining. Reason kept telling them to calm down.

Max and the others clenched their fists in the stands. At this point, none of them could say who would win.

May hesitated, then leaned toward Ethan. “Brother Ethan, who do you think will win?”

Somewhere along the way, the others had begun to treat Ethan as though he knew everything. If he named a winner, it almost felt as if that person would win.

Honestly, even with his memories of the anime, Ethan could not call this match with confidence. Katie had been different. Morrison and Ash were almost evenly matched. Indeed, Morrison had given away two rounds and still forced Ash into a final showdown. Judging by today's battle alone, Morrison might be stronger.

He had almost fought a six-on-four match.

But a battle did not produce the same result every time simply because the Pokémon, Trainers, and field were the same. A single thought or a moment's distraction could change the outcome. Ethan did not know whether his presence in this world had altered how this match would end.

After thinking it over, he chose to trust Ash.

“If Morrison had fought this way from the beginning, Ash would probably have lost by now,” Ethan said. “But there are no second chances for the start of a match. A Trainer's state of mind is part of their strength. They're tied now, and at a moment like this, I think Ash will still win.”

May nodded. She thought so too. Brock, Max, and Misty probably all hoped Ash would win as well. They had met Morrison only a few times. Ash was their friend and traveling companion. They could hardly help favoring him.

Both Trainers sent out their final Pokémon. Ash chose Glalie, which had fought so well in the previous round. Morrison chose Metang.

This was their last exchange. The winner would advance. After the last two rounds, the crowd was eager to see what these Trainers would show them next. The stadium grew quiet as everyone watched.

Ice-type Glalie faced a Steel and Psychic type, a difficult matchup. But with Ash cornered again, perhaps his knack for overcoming bad type matchups would emerge.

Metang and its evolution, Metagross, had appeared surprisingly often in this year's Ever Grande Conference, though they were seldom seen in ordinary matches.

A brief pause seemed to have cooled Ash and Morrison down. The instant the round began, though, both eagerly ordered their Pokémon forward. How could they truly be calm in a match this important?

Metang met Glalie head-on. Its larger, sturdier body won the collision. Ash did not lose heart. If Glalie could not overpower Metang up close, it could attack from a distance. He ordered Ice Beam.

Metang used Confusion to bend the beam back. It curved through the air and struck Glalie itself. While Glalie reeled, Metang closed in and hit it with Take Down.

Metang had the advantage after their first exchange, just as most spectators had expected. Then Ash showed another sign of his growth.

To the crowd's confusion, he ordered Glalie to use Ice Beam again. Morrison gave Metang the same command as before. Confusion reflected the beam back toward Glalie.

Morrison did not know that this was exactly what Ash had been waiting for. As Ice Beam returned, Glalie used Icy Wind. A frost-laden gale burst from its mouth and turned the beam around once more, speeding it up and strengthening the attack. A moment later, both moves struck Metang together.
