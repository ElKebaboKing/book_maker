Mt. Ember stood in the north of One Island, the highest peak in the Sevii Islands.

Several centuries ago it had been an active volcano. At some point it had quieted, until people came to regard it as extinct. Even after so many years of wind and rain, its shape remained remarkably intact.

After another night's rest at Lorelei's house, Ethan and Sabrina returned to One Island with her as their guide.

According to stories passed down by the island's elders, Moltres lived somewhere on the mountain. Its fiery glow shone like a bright lamp, visible even at night. That was how Mt. Ember had earned its name.

They walked for a long time before reaching the mountain's foot. Ethan looked up. Shrubs covered its lower slopes, but the vegetation thinned as it rose, giving way to bare rock above the midpoint.

Smoke occasionally curled from cracks in the stone. Ethan had his doubts. Nothing about this looked particularly extinct.

They followed a path called Kindle Road higher up the mountain. The scent of the forest faded, replaced by a faint smell of sulfur. Even through his shoes and socks, Ethan could feel heat rising from the rock underfoot.

He wiped the sweat from his forehead and turned to Lorelei. "Are you sure this volcano is extinct?"

"Well..." Lorelei looked around, beginning to doubt it herself. "I haven't climbed it in a long time. I didn't expect it to change this much. Compared with my last visit, it does seem... perhaps... a little warmer."

Ethan knew the lines between active, dormant, and extinct volcanoes were not fixed. An active one could fall quiet, a dormant one could erupt, and even a supposedly extinct one could awaken. Why else would a heat-loving Moltres make its home here?

He looked back and saw Sabrina more than ten meters behind. She was breathing hard, her clothes soaked through with sweat. The climb had left her hot and exhausted.

She wasn't used to this kind of exercise. Even so, she said nothing and did not use her psychic powers to help herself along. She kept trying to match Ethan's pace.

Smiling, Ethan started to go back for her. Then his gaze fell on her. Sweat had made her clothes cling to her figure, outlining it with startling clarity.

He stared a little too openly. Sabrina seemed to sense it and looked up. Ethan reacted with all the speed of an Indigo League champion, pulled his gaze away, and greeted her with a broad smile.

"Tired? Do you want to rest for a while?"

Sabrina had apparently noticed nothing. She shook her head. "No. Let's keep going."

"We should take a break. You don't usually exercise this hard. If you push yourself on a climb like this, your legs will be miserable tomorrow."

She seemed to consider it. Lorelei added, "There's a place called Ember Spa not far ahead. The hot springs are good for tired muscles. People from the island go there to relax, especially the older residents."

After a moment's thought, Sabrina agreed.

The broad road had ended. Only a winding mountain path remained. They followed it to Ember Spa—and fell silent at the sight of the spring.

Steam billowed from water that churned like a pot at a rolling boil. The three stood side by side, staring, until Ethan finally turned to Lorelei.

"That must be close to a hundred degrees. Are you sure the islanders come here to bathe, rather than make soup?"

Lorelei shot him a look, but had no answer. "It shouldn't be like this. How did it suddenly get so hot?"

"Could the volcano be about to erupt?" Sabrina asked.

"It won't erupt," Lorelei said firmly. "Maybe the earthquake out at sea a while ago changed something here."

The heat around the spring was worse than anywhere else. They could not rest there, so they continued deeper into Mt. Ember.

After a while, even the narrow path disappeared. Lorelei led them back and forth among broken rocks until they reached a cave almost blocked by a boulder.

She looked pleased with herself. "People climb this mountain every year, but I'm the only one who knows this place. If Moltres really is here, I believe it's on the peak beyond the cave."

The boulder covered nearly the whole entrance. Only a thin gap remained, barely wide enough for a small child to slip through sideways.

Ethan was about to ask Gardevoir for help, but Sabrina stepped forward first. She raised both hands, released her psychic power, and slowly pushed the rock aside.

It was almost three meters across and weighed several tons. Even she found the task difficult. Fortunately, she only needed to open a passage wide enough for them to enter.

With a rumble, the gap widened. Cool mountain air flowed out before they had even stepped inside. After the heat outdoors, it felt like standing under an air conditioner.

They entered one after another. Ethan looked around and saw no sign of tools on the walls. The cave appeared to have formed naturally.

It was cool and damp. The floor was slick in places, and green moss grew along both sides. They climbed another few dozen meters through the cave before reaching the far opening.

A strong sea breeze lifted their hair. They stood above a cliff, high enough to look over all of One Island. In the distance, they could even make out parts of Two Island and Three Island.
