After their first match, Eevee and Combusken fought three more practice rounds. By the time May finished working with Squirtle and looked over, both Pokémon were panting and marked with scrapes. They had spent a great deal of energy.

Ethan offered only the occasional hint during those three rounds. Combusken did not win a single one, and its new ambition to beat Eevee began to fade. There was a real difference in their strength. Combusken had managed to avoid being knocked out in all three only because Ethan guided it at critical moments.

The matches were still worthwhile. Fighting Eevee improved Combusken's control of its moves and its ability to respond to an opponent. It had grown.

May wiped her forehead and came over. “How did the training go?”

“Combusken improved quickly. Give it a few more days like this and it could enter a Pokémon League tournament,” Ethan said. “How did things go with Squirtle?”

“Great!” May gave him a thumbs-up. “You'll see tomorrow.”

They wanted Squirtle and Combusken in good condition for the Contest. At dusk they took all the Pokémon back to the Center, had Combusken's injuries treated, fed everyone a hearty meal, washed and brushed them, and finally sent them to rest.

By the time they finished, it was fully dark. Ethan and May sat on a bench in the corridor, looking up at the Pokémon Center ceiling without speaking.

May was exhausted. She had been responsible for every little task and now did not want to move a finger. Ethan saw how tired she was and let her rest in silence.

After a long while, she stretched and smiled. “I never noticed how much work it is when I have to prepare for a Contest on my own.”

Ethan understood. At her earlier Contests, Max and Brock had helped. They had shared jobs she scarcely needed to think about. Now that they were gone, everything fell to May. Other Coordinators dealt with that routinely. She had simply been well looked after until now.

Ethan was about to answer, but May spoke first. “It's all right. I'll keep trying. I'm a little overwhelmed now, but before long I'll know how to handle everything.”

He looked at her in surprise, then smiled. She was still the cheerful, optimistic girl he knew. These difficulties had not discouraged her. She looked forward to learning. It was one of the reasons she had been so loved in the anime.

“I believe you,” he said. “I believe you'll become a Top Coordinator one day.”

There was no trace of a joke in his voice. May stared at him, wondering why he believed in her so firmly. Everyone could voice a dream. How many people truly achieved theirs?

…

The Silver Town Contest began on schedule at ten the next morning. The small hall was full. Though the crowd was smaller than at a city Contest, its enthusiasm was just as strong.

As expected, Lilian hosted again, with Mr. Contesta, Mr. Sukizo, and Nurse Joy judging. The only likely change was that this Nurse Joy belonged to the Silver Town Pokémon Center rather than Saffron's. She looked and dressed exactly like the other Joy, and her judging standards seemed the same.

After Lilian introduced the judges and explained the rules, the competitors came in. There were only a dozen or so, May included, and Ethan recognized no one. It appeared to be a modest field. If May performed as well as she could, she had a good chance of winning.

Cheers greeted the start of the Contest. A few seconds before it began, however, a middle-aged man dressed like an office worker hurried in with a briefcase. He tripped on his way up to the stage, drawing good-natured laughter from the audience.

Ethan did not laugh. He frowned, remembering something. So it was him. The scene was familiar from the anime. This small-town Contest had appeared there after all. As he recalled, May had won it. He had even less reason to worry now.

With everyone present, the performance round began. The first few entrants finished their acts to applause. May was fourth.

She brought out Squirtle, just as she had rehearsed. The instant it emerged from its Poké Ball, before it could land, Squirtle drew its head and limbs into its shell. It spun through the air, firing Ice Beam as it turned. Faster and faster it spun, becoming a dazzling top.

When Squirtle finally landed and bowed to the crowd, countless clear ice crystals fell around it. They glittered beneath the lights. The audience cheered for the entrance.

May had more planned. Squirtle released a cloud of bubbles and formed one enormous bubble at the center. It jumped onto the giant bubble and struck it with Ice Beam. The burst became a great flower-shaped formation of ice spikes across the stage. Squirtle landed steadily atop it.

In the front row, Ethan applauded with the other spectators. May and Squirtle were better than they had been before. Squirtle had joined her recently, yet May had helped it find confidence. It no longer feared the stage and could show its skill with openness and flair. Both had grown quickly. May really was made to be a Coordinator.

An act like that would easily carry her into the second round.

After May came the office worker who had nearly arrived late. Lilian had introduced him as Jeremy. He was about forty, short and a little heavyset, with an honest face. In his ordinary gray suit, he looked like any unremarkable man one might pass on a street or train.

Silver Town was small, and Jeremy was local. Many spectators knew him and saw him regularly. Recalling his fall, they chuckled kindly and waited to see what he would do.

The stage lights dimmed. To a burst of electric guitar, a man strode out with hair stiff as a broom, dark sunglasses, a leather jacket, leather gloves, leather pants, leather shoes, and an electric guitar in hand. He looked like a rebellious rocker.

Who was he? That seemed to be everyone's question. Only Ethan, the director, and Lilian knew: this was Jeremy, transformed. His whole manner differed so much from the mild office worker's that he seemed like another man. When the audience realized it, jaws dropped.

He had more surprises in store. Jeremy released Butterfree and launched into rapid guitar playing. Butterfree used Poison Powder, then Stun Spore. Great purple and orange clouds spread across the stage. It used Whirlwind to scatter them farther, then Safeguard. Rainbow light appeared in an instant, turning the powders into something like brilliantly colored clouds.

The display stunned the spectators. Butterfree's beauty showed through the combination of moves.

Then an interruption came. A woman and a boy appeared below the stage and stopped Jeremy's performance. His wife and son tried to keep him from pursuing his dream. With May's help, Jeremy spoke honestly from the heart. The crowd, already moved by his act, rose and applauded. In the end, his family gave him their support.

It was a small incident, yet it made the otherwise ordinary Contest feel more meaningful.

Jeremy and his family left the stage to the audience's encouragement. The Contest resumed, and the remaining entrants performed. May and Jeremy were among four Coordinators who advanced to the battle round.

Large city Contests often sent eight people through. With fewer entrants at a small-town event, four was enough. There would be fewer battles, though no less excitement.

The matchups appeared on the big screen. As though the anime's story had a hand in the draw, May and Jeremy did not face each other immediately. Both beat their separate opponents with little trouble and met in the final.

To Ethan, the outcome seemed to hold little mystery. He knew what May and Combusken could do, especially after yesterday's practice. It felt like watching a film whose ending he already knew.

For everyone else, it was a gripping match. May and Jeremy each recognized the other as a serious opponent.

May sent out Combusken. Jeremy brought out a Venusaur larger than most of its kind. Both Pokémon had performed well in their previous battles. Combusken had the type advantage, giving May an apparent edge.

But a Contest Battle was about more than type matchups. How beautifully a move was used mattered too, leaving room for surprises. Jeremy's huge Venusaur had imposing strength and could not be dismissed.

Still, Ethan knew how the match would end. Venusaur showed its power at the start. Its Vine Whip broke apart Combusken's Fire Spin despite the unfavorable typing. It even used the formidable Grass-type move Frenzy Plant, pushing Combusken into a dangerous position.

After yesterday's training, though, Combusken was harder to corner. With May urging and directing it, it fought back. It set the plants covering the stage alight and finished Venusaur with Sky Uppercut.

May had won without the desperate reversal Ethan remembered from the anime. Combusken had answered Venusaur more calmly. It had never beaten Eevee during their later practice rounds, but the experience and lessons from those bouts were real. They helped it defeat Venusaur and earn May another Ribbon.

The victory brought less applause than Ethan expected. The audience was not lacking in enthusiasm. After Jeremy's moving reconciliation with his family, many had hoped to see him win and fulfill his dream.

Competitions could be cruel that way. Jeremy had a reason to want the victory. May had her own determination not to lose.
