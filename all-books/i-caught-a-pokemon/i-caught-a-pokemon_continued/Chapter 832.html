Ethan shook his head, amused and a little helpless at May's declaration.

“So you want to compete against me,” he said with a smile. “I wasn't planning to enter, but if you're challenging me, let's see how we do. Beating me won't be easy. Tomorrow I'll show you that experience counts for something!”

They laughed and talked over dinner, imagining what the next day's race might be like. Then Ethan's phone rang. He checked the screen and saw a name he had not expected: Anabel.

Why would she call now? Several possibilities crossed his mind, and then he began to suspect the reason. He gestured to May, got up, and went to an empty corner before answering.

Anabel's familiar voice came through. Without much small talk, she asked, “I hear you've returned to Kanto and started challenging its Battle Frontier?”

Those people had sharp noses for news. Ethan was not surprised she knew what he was doing. Perhaps he had even told her himself before; he could not remember after so long.

“That's right. I've already won several Symbols,” he said. “Is something wrong?”

Anabel took a breath. Her voice turned solemn. “What I'm about to tell you may sound unbelievable, but please trust me. Every word of it is true.”

The more seriously she spoke, the more certain Ethan became of his guess.

“Don't worry. I've seen plenty of unbelievable things. Tell me.”

As he spoke, he glanced across the room. May was sitting where he had left her, happily absorbed in her meal. He smiled to himself.

“There's a Battle Tower in Kanto's Battle Frontier,” Anabel began. “Its Frontier Brain is also named Anabel. In a sense, she and I are the same person.”

She paused, perhaps listening for a reaction. Ethan offered only attentive silence. His lack of surprise impressed her, and she went on.

“I don't mean that I secretly run the Battle Tower while working for the International Police. In one sense that description comes close, but the truth is that there are two of us in this world.”

To someone who knew nothing of the matter, her explanation would have sounded contradictory. Ethan had known about it for some time.

“So there are two Anabels living in the same world,” he said.

“Yes.” Now she sounded more surprised than he did. “Doesn't that shock you?”

“This Pokémon world is full of mysteries. I've seen too many to be startled by one more. You might not know this, but I'm something of a miracle myself.”

He had spoken the truth as if it were a joke, the surest way to make it unbelievable. Anabel assumed he meant the things he had accomplished on his journey or the Legendary Pokémon he had caught. She paid the remark little attention.

She had no idea that Ethan, like her, had come from another world—one he thought of as much more “real.”

“I see. That puts me at ease.” Anabel exhaled. “A few years ago, I passed through a strange space called an Ultra Wormhole and came here from another world. I became what the International Police call a Faller, and I lost my memories of the past. Later I joined the International Police and eventually reached my present position. That's why I said the Anabel here and I seem to be one person, yet strictly speaking we aren't.”

A Faller was the International Police's term for a person carrying energy from Ultra Space. Passing through an Ultra Wormhole left that energy on a traveler. Ultra Beasts sensed it and pursued such people, attacking them. Once the International Police learned of this, they recruited Fallers to serve as bait in hunts for Ultra Beasts.

Anabel was one of those people. But her skill and record had carried her far beyond that role. Her achievements grew, she rose through the ranks, and she eventually became a regional leader.

When she first joined, she had worked under Looker. Her diligence earned her a promotion until she became his superior instead. The reversal had bothered Looker for a long time. Whenever it came up, he sighed over it.

Looker's feelings aside, crossing worlds was Anabel's greatest secret, bound up with classified matters such as the Ultra Wormholes. If she could tell Ethan now, the International Police must hold him in high regard. It also meant she had a pressing reason.

“Let me guess,” Ethan said. “You're telling me because you want me to keep it secret when I reach the Battle Tower.”

“Exactly.” Anabel laughed a little. “You're easy to work with. As I said, my arrival here is highly classified even within the International Police. The Anabel of this world knows nothing about it. When you meet her, please act as though you've never met me. Don't make her suspicious.”

“Why not tell her?” Ethan asked.

“It's complicated.” Anabel gave a rueful laugh. “Suppose someone identical to you appeared one day and said they'd come from another dimension, or from a world a few years in the future. How would you feel?”

“Uncomfortable, I think.” Ethan tried to put himself in that position. “We might have the same name and face, perhaps even similar memories. Living alongside someone who looked like my reflection and knew all my secrets would be unsettling. Sooner or later, it would cause trouble.”

“That's it.” Anabel sighed. “More than anything, learning about me could irreversibly disrupt the life of the woman I used to be—or rather, the version of me in this parallel world. We have to keep it from her and deal with the Ultra Wormhole incident as quickly as we can.”

Ethan did not press her further. “I understand. When I meet the Anabel at the Battle Tower, I won't give her any reason to think I know you.”

He ended the call and returned to May. She tactfully did not ask who had called or why he had been away so long. Instead, she picked up their earlier conversation and talked eagerly about tomorrow's race.

The event's full name was Pokémon Orienteering. Each contestant would team up with a Pokémon and visit five designated places on the island. Whoever completed the route in the shortest time would win.

Similar races were held from time to time in Kanto and other regions. Their spectacle and unpredictability had made them popular, almost a third major form of competition beside Pokémon League tournaments and Contests. Ethan thought of them as a Pokémon version of the marathons held on Earth.

There was no single organizer for all these events, so the prizes varied. On Potpourri Island, the main award was a Pokémon Teamwork Medal, accompanied by a great quantity of the island's famous fruit.

The fruit sounded like a modest extra, but it was the finest harvest the island produced at this time of year. Plump and delicious, it was a special prize that money could not buy. It represented the island's abundant harvest, too. Many locals entered for the fruit rather than the medal. May was not the only food lover drawn to it.

After breakfast the next morning, Ethan and May left the Pokémon Center and stepped onto the grass outside. Nearly a hundred people had gathered: contestants, their friends and families, and even more spectators.

Ceremonial salutes fired into the air, bursting into clouds of smoke. A short distance ahead stood a red arch marked “START.” It served as both the beginning and the finish of the race.

They had registered the night before. Now they slipped into the group of contestants like any other entrants.

Nurse Joy handed a map and a compass to each of them. When she had finished, she addressed the crowd.

“Everyone, please listen! The map shows five locations, each with a stamp. To complete the race, visit all five, stamp your map at each one, and return here. The fastest contestant wins this year's Pokémon Orienteering competition. You may use only your map and compass. Anyone caught using another aid or riding a Pokémon will be disqualified!”

The crowd called back in acknowledgment. Ethan looked over his opponents. Men and women of every age had entered. The youngest appeared several years younger than May, while the oldest looked about as old as Spenser. Ethan wondered if all of them could really finish the course.

The entrants released their partner Pokémon. Flashes of blue light appeared all around. Ethan and May opened their Poké Balls too.

Unlike in the anime, May did not have an Eevee yet. The Egg she had received at the farm had not hatched. She chose Squirtle as her partner instead. Ethan brought out his own Eevee.

Among the other entrants were Oddish, Machop, Growlithe, Bellsprout, and many more. Most were young or inexperienced Pokémon, and evolved ones were rare. This really was a casual sport with a low barrier to entry, open to nearly anyone.

Once everyone was ready, the contestants gathered at the starting line. Spectators on either side of the course cheered them on. Ethan even spotted one or two television crews filming the scene.

A sharp whistle sounded. The race began.

People and Pokémon surged forward together. In the packed group, Ethan and May could not pass anyone or run at full speed. They kept an even pace with the crowd.

Fortunately, there was no fixed route. A little more than a hundred meters beyond the start, several paths branched away. The contestants could choose among them according to their own judgment.

The crowd split in several directions. In moments, more than half the people who had blocked Ethan and May's way were gone.

Ethan had studied the map as soon as Nurse Joy gave it to him and worked out a reasonably good route. He took the path on the far right without hesitation. May followed as though that were the obvious thing to do.

When he looked at her, she grinned. “What? Is it strange? I said I was going to beat you. Of course I have to take the same route to race you!”

Ethan knew she simply did not want to choose a route for herself, but he let it pass with a smile. He and Eevee gradually picked up speed.

Now the benefits of all his time on the road began to show. Young and fit, Ethan passed the runners ahead one after another and soon reached the leading group on their path.

May did not fall behind. She and Squirtle stayed half a step back, keeping close on his heels.
