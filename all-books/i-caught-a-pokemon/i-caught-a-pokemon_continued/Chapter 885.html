They passed through the lively food street, left the city center, and followed Bellchime Trail through a wood filled with red and yellow maple leaves all year round. At last Ethan and the others reached Bell Tower.

Compared with the bustling center of Ecruteak, the place was remarkably quiet tonight. The occasional hoot of a bird Pokémon sounded from the trees. Otherwise there was an almost frightening silence, without so much as a light in sight.

However many years the tower had stood here, the locals seldom came out to it at night, even during a festival. By day, tourists visiting Ecruteak for the first time made their way to the famous landmark. After dark, the thought of the fire more than a century ago made the place feel rather eerie.

That suited Ethan and his companions.

He had been here once before, but he still took a moment to look over Bell Tower. It was a Japanese-style tower of nine stories, built within a fortified compound on a hill outside the city. From its top, a visitor could see all of Ecruteak. The city's distant festivities were still faintly audible.

With no one else around, the five sisters removed their masks. They switched on their flashlights and approached the compound's gate in the moonlight.

The compound resembled a castle. In former times, regional lords had surrounded their residences and keeps with walls to protect themselves and their families. The sisters' ancestors had not been such lords, but they had built similar defenses around the two towers.

The family had left the compound over a decade ago. Monks now cared for it and opened it to visitors during the day. Tonight the gates were shut.

Miki started toward the gate to knock, but Ethan stopped her. “We're here to summon Ho-Oh. We should avoid drawing attention if we can.”

“That's true.” Kuni looked at the closed gate above its dozen or so steps, then at the high stone walls. “How do we get in?”

“With a Pokémon, of course!” Ethan took out a Poké Ball and grinned. “You've spent too long in polite society. Trainers don't always take the usual route.”

The sisters exchanged glances. Entering without a word to the monks in the middle of the night felt improper. It also gave them a little thrill. Their lives were usually lived under spotlights and according to rules; they had rarely done anything so furtive.

A flash of blue light released Ethan's Gardevoir. With Teleport, it carried them silently past the walls and into the compound.

Inside, the silence was deeper still. They could no longer hear even the bird Pokémon. Miki and her sisters looked as though they expected to be caught at any moment, but Ethan caught the excitement in their eyes too. Amused, he said nothing and went with them into Bell Tower.

Moonlight lit the grounds outside. The tower's closed windows kept almost all of it from reaching the interior. Their flashlights revealed a broad, mostly empty space with a faint smell of aged wood.

The building was well cared for, yet its floorboards creaked under their feet. Every sound echoed through the hollow tower, adding to the unease.

Sayo was the sort of person who wanted company even on a nighttime trip to the bathroom. The tower sent a shiver through her, though having everyone close by kept her fear in check.

Ethan's flashlight swept across a wall and found a large wood carving of Ho-Oh. The Pokémon spread its wings before a rainbow and auspicious clouds, soaring toward the sun. It looked noble and imposing.

The likeness was good, he had to admit. Given his quarrel with Ho-Oh, he spared it only a glance.

A few minutes of climbing brought them to the ninth floor. Beyond it lay the roof. They emerged onto the outdoor platform, where cool night air immediately brushed their faces.

A huge brass-colored finial rose from the tower's summit. Its shape was peculiar, and Ethan could not tell what purpose it served. Perhaps centuries ago it had been part of Ho-Oh's dwelling.

They spent a while looking out over Ecruteak, then gathered together again. Zuki took the Clear Bell from the box she had carried with her.

Even up here, the strange transparent bell showed no change. She shook it hard, but it made no sound.

“Just like before,” Miki said. “It still needs a catalyst.” She turned to Ethan. “Shall we begin?”

“Wait a little.” He looked toward the sky above Ecruteak. “When do the fireworks you mentioned start?”

“About now.” Miki checked the time. “They mark the end of the night market. Most people go home after the display.”

As she spoke, streaks of color rose above the city and burst into brilliant flowers. Within moments, fireworks filled the sky, bathing Ecruteak in shifting colors. Their reports reached the tower despite the distance.

Ethan nodded to the sisters. “Now.”

They understood his timing. The fireworks would help conceal Ho-Oh's arrival from ordinary people, both in the sky and beneath the noise of the explosions. The display would not last long, so they set to work at once.

From their robes, the sisters brought out strands of fur from Raikou, Suicune, and Entei. The three legendary beasts did not have to stand here in person. Something from each of them, infused with their power, would serve as a catalyst for the Clear Bell, much as Ho-Oh's own Rainbow Wing could.

Holding the fur, the five sisters stood together in the middle of the platform and began to dance. Their steps matched precisely. In their splendid ceremonial dress, their graceful movement made a beautiful sight.

This was their family's ritual for calling Ho-Oh: a particular dance accompanied by the rhythmic shaking of the bell. As Zuki moved it gently, the Clear Bell finally gave a bright, clear chime. The fur of the three beasts glowed faintly in answer.

The fireworks thundered over Ecruteak, and the chime could be heard only nearby. Yet Ethan felt as if its sound had passed through earth and sky and was traveling ever farther away.

He had once asked Miki what would happen if Ho-Oh was too distant to hear it. Ho-Oh did not stay in one place for long; it might be in another region altogether.

“Don't worry,” she had told him with complete confidence. “The Clear Bell has a special power. Ho-Oh gave it to our family as a token of its promise when it lived in Bell Tower. Its sound rings directly in Ho-Oh's heart. Wherever it is, once it hears the bell, it will come.”

Her ancestors must have made that agreement with Ho-Oh centuries before the fire at Brass Tower and passed it down ever since.

Even if the bell failed, Ethan had another option. He could bring out all three beasts together and let their power call Ho-Oh. That was why he had dared promise the little boy on the food street that Ho-Oh would appear tonight.

The sisters continued their measured dance, their expressions intent and their gestures unhurried. Now and then a movement revealed a pale wrist beneath a sleeve.

Ethan took out his camera. Five sisters dancing atop a tower against a night sky full of fireworks were too lovely a sight to let pass without a photograph.

The shutter had barely clicked when something changed overhead. A golden shape broke through the clouds.

Even at night, its brilliant body shone like the sun. There was no mistaking it. Ho-Oh had come.

Rainbow light spilled from its feathers as it beat its wings and flew toward Bell Tower, a bright presence cutting through the dark.

Ethan's gaze sharpened. With a turn of his wrist, he brought three Great Balls into his hand.

The sisters stopped dancing as Ho-Oh descended. Zuki alone continued to shake the Clear Bell softly. All five looked up at the beautiful bird in awe.

To Ethan, Ho-Oh looked rather like a Fearow with finer feathers. He knew that judgment was colored by the dispute between them, but he could hardly view it impartially.

Ho-Oh came to a hover more than ten meters from the rooftop. It looked first at the Clear Bell, then at the sisters and the fur of the three beasts in their hands. Finally, its gaze settled on Ethan.

Ethan remembered Ho-Oh well, and Ho-Oh remembered him. Few humans in all its long years had made such an impression. This was the man whose attempt to catch the three beasts it had once stopped.

Now the sisters held the beasts' fur. Ho-Oh sensed the beasts' presence on them, along with traces of Ethan's. It understood that he had caught them after all.

It also remembered the bell. Though it had not heard that summons for a very long time, it had honored the old promise and come. It had not expected the people who once worshiped it to be standing with this man.

Ho-Oh gave a high cry and looked down at Ethan, studying him. Ethan met its gaze.

“Surprised? You stopped me last time, but I caught them in the end. And now we meet again!”

He threw the three Great Balls. Blue light flashed across the sky as Articuno, Zapdos, and Moltres appeared around Ho-Oh. The three birds cried out and began circling it.

Ethan had told the sisters about these Pokémon while explaining his history with Ho-Oh. They knew he had them. Seeing all three legendary birds before their eyes still left them astonished and proud of him.

Ho-Oh felt the hostility in the birds' gazes at once. It ignored them for the moment and kept watching Ethan. A voice with a quiet authority, neither distinctly male nor female, sounded in his mind.

“Human. Why have you summoned me?”

Ethan hesitated. He could hardly say that he had called Ho-Oh here to fight it and settle his grievance from last time, even if that was what he intended.

“I want to test myself against you,” he said instead. “As a Trainer, I want to keep growing stronger. You can see that I've gathered all three beasts. Now it's time for us to have a proper battle.”

To his surprise, Ho-Oh took his challenge seriously.

“You are indeed worthy of challenging me. I accept.”

As its telepathic words faded, Ho-Oh's presence shifted. Light blazed from its body, and it readied itself to fight.

The sisters had not heard Ho-Oh's answer, but Ethan's words and the change in the air told them a battle was about to begin. They watched wide-eyed, eager to see how their man would face a Pokémon more legendary than legend itself. Zuki, especially, could not look away.

Articuno, Zapdos, and Moltres cried out together. Their own fighting spirit surged, and all three turned in the air and charged straight at Ho-Oh.
