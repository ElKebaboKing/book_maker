Carl led Ethan and Sabrina out of Orange League headquarters and up the stone steps of a nearby hill.

The hill was neither high nor heavily wooded. Its bare slopes made the imposing building on its summit visible even from the sea beyond Pummelo Island. This was Pummelo Stadium, built for the Winner's Cup.

The stadium resembled an ancient Roman coliseum. Two tiers of high outer walls were lined with columns, giving it an air of ceremony and grandeur.

At the top of the steps, Ethan looked down over the whole of Pummelo Town. He had noticed on arriving the day before that most of its shops and houses had white walls and red-tiled roofs. The consistent, European-style buildings gave the place the feel of a town devoted to sport, exactly the atmosphere the Orange League seemed to want.

"This way, please." Carl led them inside.

They passed through a long, narrow spectator passage. Beyond it, an enormous arena opened before Ethan, with a standard, plain battlefield in the middle. The stadium was empty apart from the three of them, and the silence made it feel almost deserted. Dragonite statues stood here too, just as they had in the headquarters lobby. Carl had meant what he said about the island's guardian.

"Mr. Vale," Carl said, pointing ahead, "this is where you'll battle tomorrow. The design may look ancient, but we have screens, lighting, sound, and all the other facilities a match needs. It seats twenty thousand spectators. It's our biggest and best venue."

Carl would not have bothered with so much detail for every challenger. For an Indigo League champion, he was happy to explain. Ethan nodded and offered a few polite responses. He could hear Carl's genuine pride.

Still, twenty thousand seats felt modest beside the Indigo League stadium, which held fifty thousand. It made no difference to Ethan's state of mind. The crowd might be vast or tiny, the equipment new or old; he had come to win and would give his best even if only a handful of people watched.

He was more interested in the match itself. "Mr. Carl, what are the rules for the Winner's Cup?"

Carl's expression turned serious. "It's a full six-on-six battle. After either side has lost three Pokémon, the battlefield changes."

"Oh? I thought this plain field was where we'd fight to the end."

Carl smiled. "It's an ordinary practice field at the moment. Beneath it is a lift that lets us exchange it for fields of different types during a match."

"I see." Lifts like that were common at major events, and even many Gyms had them. Ethan had asked mostly to pass the time.

They left the stadium and approached a slightly lower building beside it. Like the stadium, it was built with stone columns and marble. A great dome covered the whole structure, making it look darker and more solemn.

"This is the Palace of Victory," Carl said. "You can also think of it as our Hall of Fame. It was built to honor Trainers who won the Winner's Cup."

They went inside. Two more Dragonite statues stood at the entrance. In the main hall, Ethan saw more than a dozen statues of other Pokémon. Most were familiar species found around Kanto and the Orange Islands, such as Venonat, Poliwrath, and Pidgeot.

Behind the statues were display cases containing rows of rectangular stone slabs. From a distance, they looked peculiar. Up close, Ethan saw that each bore impressions of Pokémon hands or feet in various sizes. Framed photographs stood in front of them.

In one photograph, a victorious Trainer crouched with a glittering gold trophy in his hands. His Pokémon surrounded him, and he smiled broadly at the camera.

Carl continued his tour. "The photos and prints belong to the Honorable Trainers who won here, and to their Pokémon. If you win tomorrow, Mr. Vale, your name and your Pokémon's prints can be preserved here forever for future visitors to see."

Forever? The idea stirred Ethan. He had come to this world from another one. At times he felt like a drifting plant without roots, with no firm sense that he belonged here. He feared that one day he might vanish as suddenly as he had arrived, leaving no trace.

A name and a photograph in this hall would be a mark that he had truly been here. The thought strengthened his resolve to win.

"Mr. Carl, are the statues at the front the signature Pokémon of those Honorable Trainers?"

"That's close," Carl said. "More precisely, each represents a Pokémon that gave an outstanding performance in its Trainer's Winner's Cup match."

Ethan looked around the somewhat empty hall. "The Orange League has been running for decades. There must have been hundreds of matches. Why are there so few statues and photographs here? Have so few challengers won the Winner's Cup?"
