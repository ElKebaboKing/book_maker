After learning a little about Juan, Ethan wanted a proper battle with him. A man who had trained both Wallace and Lisia had to be an exceptional Trainer, even if his students had since surpassed him. This Gym challenge was an opportunity Ethan would regret missing.

As Juan strode ahead, Lisia stuck out her tongue and spoke quietly to Ethan. “Sorry. My teacher is usually elegant and composed. He only acts childish like that around me. Has he made things difficult for you?”

“I should be the one apologizing,” Ethan said. “A challenger shouldn't provoke a Gym Leader. I did it because I want to face him when he's serious and using his best Pokémon, not a second-string team.”

“So that's why you suddenly changed your tone.” Lisia nodded, then grinned. “Be careful, though. My teacher is strong. You might get more than you bargained for.”

They followed Juan into the Sootopolis Gym's battlefield. Compared with the luxury of the other rooms, it was surprisingly plain: a rectangular pool, like one used for swimming. More than a dozen circular platforms of various sizes floated on its cold, deep water, giving land-bound Pokémon places to stand.

Misty and Lisia went to the spectator seats. Despite their dislike of each other, appearances obliged them to sit together. Each edged away to her own side of the bench to avoid even brushing shoulders.

Ethan and Juan took their places at opposite ends of the pool. Each Trainer's platform rose about two meters above the water, like a diving board reached by stairs. From there, they could see the whole field clearly.

An elderly man with silver hair appeared at the side of the field. His well-fitted black suit made him look like a butler. He probably served the Gym in that capacity and refereed challenges as well.

“I will explain the rules,” he told Ethan. “Each side may use five Pokémon. The Gym Leader may not freely switch Pokémon during the battle. The match ends when all the Pokémon on one side are unable to battle.”

“Five?” Ethan had visited many Gyms, but none had allowed so many Pokémon in a challenge.

The butler seemed accustomed to that reaction. “Yes. In the first round, each side must send out two Pokémon together for a Double Battle. That round ends when both Pokémon on one side are unable to battle. The winning side's Pokémon continue into the second round. After that round, the remaining battles are one against one until one side has lost all five Pokémon.”

It sounded complicated, but Ethan nodded to show he understood. Most Gyms had similar rules, apart from Leaders who adjusted the conditions to suit their own needs. Ordinarily he only had to check whether a match used two Pokémon or three. Sootopolis was a rare exception.

When the butler had finished, Juan glared at Ethan. “The rules require me to introduce myself. I am Juan, Leader of this Gym. We may now begin the official challenge. Are you ready?”

If the League had not required the formalities, Juan would not have wasted another word on the man he thought was leading his precious student astray. He wanted to send out his best Pokémon at once, teach Ethan a humiliating lesson, and show Lisia how strong her teacher really was. Not just anyone can sweet-talk my student away!

“Don't worry.” Ethan patted the Poké Balls at his waist. “The rules surprised me, but I came prepared.”

Juan fell silent. This fellow had not even introduced himself, yet he claimed to be prepared for rules he had only just heard. How arrogant could he be?

Juan drew several sharp breaths. He could not remember the last time he had wanted so badly to defeat an opponent. After all these years, Ethan had stirred his competitive spirit.

In the stands, Lisia realized her teacher had not recognized Ethan and was treating him like an ordinary challenger. She worried Juan would be overconfident and suffer an ugly defeat. She shifted, ready to warn him.

Misty spoke before she could. “What? Don't you trust Mr. Juan? It's only a battle. Surely he doesn't need a warning.”

Lisia settled back into her seat. “I was just getting comfortable. I wasn't going to warn anyone.”

“Really? My mistake.” Misty smiled without quite smiling. “Ethan never uses his strongest team for Gym challenges anyway. He brings Pokémon he's still training. Even if he loses, it'll be close. You needn't worry about your teacher.”

“What?” Lisia forgot their quarrel for a moment. “He uses only Pokémon he's training? That might work against weaker opponents, but wouldn't it make a loss much more likely against someone strong?”

“That's why his commands matter so much.” Misty lifted her chin proudly. “Every decision, every quick idea can pull his Pokémon out of danger and turn the match in their favor. Ethan is that kind of Trainer.”

“I see.” Lisia considered this, then smiled. “Now I'm worried about Ethan instead.”

Misty looked at her in surprise. “What do you mean?”

“Didn't you hear Sebastian explain the rules?” Lisia nodded toward the butler. “This Gym asks more of a challenger than most. There are five Pokémon to manage, and two must battle at the same time. A Trainer needs the skill to command both together.”

She paused while Misty thought it over. “I agree Ethan is excellent, perhaps among the very best. But this match also demands teamwork and strength from his Pokémon. If he uses partners who are still developing, he really could lose.”

Misty's confidence faltered. Lisia had laid out the difficulty too clearly to dismiss. Even so, she chose to trust Ethan. Do your best.

...

On the battlefield, Juan sent out his Pokémon first. He threw two Poké Balls together. In flashes of blue light, a Walrein appeared on a platform and a Kingdra in the pool.

He folded his arms and addressed Ethan. “A Trainer aiming for the Ever Grande Conference must be as adaptable as water, taking the shape of whatever vessel it enters. Show me which Pokémon you'll choose to challenge my Gym.”

Ethan looked over the pair. Juan was plainly using Pokémon he trusted. Ethan did not know the details of Juan's team, but two fully evolved partners would be formidable.

Which of his own Pokémon should he use? Setting aside Gardevoir, Gengar, and his Legendary Pokémon, he currently had seven to choose from: Dragonite, Tyranitar, Scizor, Eevee, Metang, Combusken, and Milotic.

Five would have to battle today. The first decision was the hardest. Winning the opening Double Battle would put him ahead, an advantage he badly wanted with a team that was not his strongest.

His choices also had awkward type matchups here. After considering them, Ethan sent out Dragonite and Milotic, one for the air and one for the water.

Most of his other Pokémon would face unfavorable types. Scizor had a somewhat better matchup, but its steel-heavy body was dangerous on a field surrounded by deep water. If it fell in, it might not be able to swim back out. Eevee faced no obvious type problem either, yet it would struggle to move freely around this pool.

Ethan was not overly worried. He had spent plenty of time practicing battles against unfavorable types. This was exactly the sort of situation that training was meant to prepare him for.

Juan was surprised by Ethan's choice, then disappointed. Living on an isolated island, he followed Sootopolis and Hoenn news more closely than events in other regions. He had heard a little about them, but he did not know who Ethan was.

Without that knowledge, the choice seemed dreadful to him. Dragonite was Dragon and Flying, making Walrein's Ice-type attacks especially dangerous. A single hit could do tremendous damage. Milotic was a pure Water type facing Walrein and Kingdra, both of which also had Water typing. Their Water attacks would do reduced damage to one another. What did Ethan hope to gain from that exchange?

Most of all, Juan's own ace was a Milotic. He knew the species intimately. One look at Ethan's Milotic's scales and bearing told him it had evolved only recently. Bringing such an inexperienced Pokémon to a Gym challenge looked reckless.

Judging only by type matchups, Juan would have given Ethan's opening pair a terrible score. He began to think less of his opponent. Was the handsome young man all appearance and no substance?

Had Lisia been free to warn him, or had Wallace stayed, Juan would never have relaxed his guard. But Misty had kept Lisia occupied and Wallace was gone. Juan took Ethan for just another challenger.

Even so, he had not forgotten why he had chosen Walrein and Kingdra. Whatever Ethan's abilities, Juan intended to teach him a lesson.

Dragonite circled overhead while Milotic swam a few delighted laps. Once all four Pokémon were ready, Sebastian gave the signal to begin.

Juan meant to watch his opponent's first move. Before he could, Ethan swept an arm forward and issued an order. Dragonite and Milotic both used Twister at the same time.

Two thick waterspouts rose from the pool and surged toward the ceiling. Their combined force churned the water, sending waves across the entire battlefield.
