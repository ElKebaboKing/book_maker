Tyranitar brought its arms and legs down in a wild barrage. Dust filled the air as it hammered Ampharos into the ground.

One Thrash would not be enough to finish Ampharos. When the move ended, Tyranitar became confused. It did not stop attacking; it simply began striking at everything around it. That gave Ampharos the chance to escape.

Ampharos scrambled out of the crater and fled to a safe distance. It looked back at Tyranitar, which had finally gone still, and let out a shaken breath. Only then did it register how badly its whole body hurt, as though every bone had been broken.

“Ampharos, don't just stand there! Counterattack!” Wattson called.

His partner came back to its senses and gathered energy for a move. Ethan saw it preparing to attack, but all he could do was wait for Tyranitar's confusion to pass. The aftereffect was unavoidable; worrying would not make it end sooner.

He had to admire Wattson's ace. Ampharos had been rammed hard, then pounded into the ground, and it could still fight back. It was far tougher than an ordinary opponent.

A powerful Thunder Shock shot from Ampharos's tail and struck Tyranitar. Electricity crackled over its body. Tyranitar cried out in pain, but the jolt also cleared its head.

There was no need to close the distance again. Ampharos was wounded and had already spent much of its energy. At Ethan's command, Tyranitar attacked from afar with Dark Pulse.

Ampharos answered with the Bug-type move Signal Beam. This time Ethan deliberately changed Tyranitar's angle of fire. The two attacks skimmed past each other instead of colliding, and each struck the opposing Pokémon.

Both swayed. Ampharos's remaining strength gave out first, and it fell.

Watt raised his flag. “Ampharos is unable to battle! The challenger, Ethan, wins the Mauville Gym match!”

With Ethan's current team, this had been a difficult Gym challenge. That wasn't because Wattson's Pokémon were overwhelmingly strong. The difficulty lay chiefly in the way he had chosen and ordered them.

Other Gym Leaders often sent out their Pokémon from weaker to stronger, but Ethan had rarely seen such obvious gaps between their abilities. Wattson had made matters worse by giving no warning. Luckily, Ethan's partners had come through for him.

It was a small matter in the end. Wattson had tested challengers with the mechanical Raikou before letting them reach the battlefield. Anyone who passed that entrance test would be capable enough that he could adjust his team to match.

Wattson had lost, and losing with Ampharos clearly disappointed him. Still, his familiar hearty smile returned as he came over to Ethan.

“Impressive! You may not know it, but Ampharos is one of my strongest Pokémon. Beating it proves you're ready to compete in the League tournament!”

Wattson plainly did not know much about Ethan. If he had, he would not have felt quite so downcast about losing. Ethan had no reason to volunteer that information.

“You're too kind,” he said with a smile. “My Pokémon still have a long way to go. They need more training.”

Wattson was pleased by the lack of arrogance. “Then keep working hard, young man. I have high hopes for you!”

Watt came over with a badge. Wattson took it from him and passed it to Ethan.

“Here. The Dynamo Badge, proof that you defeated me.”

The badge was a fairly plain yellow ring shaped like an electromagnetic coil. Ethan thanked him and put it in his badge case.

With the Dynamo Badge, he had defeated three Hoenn Gyms and collected three badges. One more would bring him halfway to the eight he needed.

Wattson had sharp eyes. As Ethan opened the case, the old man spotted the Heat Badge and laughed.

“So you beat that fellow Moore too?”

Ethan thought he heard a note of satisfaction at someone else's misfortune. Wattson was bound to be disappointed.

“No. Mr. Moore had retired by the time I reached Lavaridge Gym. His granddaughter Flannery was running it.”

“He retired? He really gave up that Gym?”

Wattson looked stunned. It was evidently the first he'd heard of it. Moore was about his age. If Moore had retired, perhaps Wattson ought to give his own future some serious thought.

Watt smiled and tried to persuade him. “Sir, you're getting older, and you've hurt your back. Why not retire like Mr. Moore?”

“Hmph! What nonsense are you talking?” Wattson pursed his lips. “I'm still going strong. Retirement can wait another ten or twenty years! Though Moore should have told me something this important. The next time I see that old fellow, I'll give him a piece of my mind!”

The way he spoke made clear that he and Moore shared a long, close friendship. Their history was not Ethan's concern. He put away his badge case, recalled Tyranitar, and exchanged polite nods with Wattson. Then he and Misty left Mauville Gym.

They were traveling Trainers. With their business in Mauville finished, it was time to head for their next destination.

The city had left a strong impression on Misty. A little way beyond its limits, she turned for one last long look at the city at the heart of Hoenn. It was her first visit, and perhaps her last.

Goodbye, Mauville City.
