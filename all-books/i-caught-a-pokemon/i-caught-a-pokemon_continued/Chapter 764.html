Ethan finally put both interviews off until later. He had only cleared the first preliminary stage, and the preliminary tournaments had yet to begin. An interview now would be premature. He ought at least to reach the final tournament before talking about his prospects. Boasting too early could put viewers off and make him look conceited.

Alexa and Gabby saw that his mind was made up and reluctantly let the matter rest. Alexa had interviewed him twice and understood his manner. Gabby was less at ease. Ethan had won in two regions and was a leading contender here. Getting the first, perhaps exclusive, interview with him might decide whether she could return to her station. She glanced at Alexa with fresh determination.

...

With their opponents known, the competitors began preparing for their matches. Ash, Morrison, and Tyson studied the people they might face and considered which Pokémon to use. Ethan alone seemed to have nothing to do.

He had spent more than ten days training privately. Every adjustment and preparation he could make was done. His Pokémon were in top condition. Their strength gave him confidence against anyone drawn across from him.

The early matches were already underway. Morrison's was scheduled first among the group, so Ash and the others went to cheer him on. Ethan barely knew Morrison or Tyson and felt no urge to cultivate a friendship. He took Misty and walked unhurriedly toward his own arena.

He was confident enough not to scout these potential opponents. Their unknown teams might add a little difficulty and make the Conference more interesting.

Cheers rang out from distant arenas. Outside, spectators unable to buy tickets gathered in small groups before enormous screens to follow the action.

The preliminary tournaments used larger venues in the inner ring instead of the fifteen outer arenas from the first stage. There were five of these, labeled A through E, each seating ten thousand people. Television crews had begun broadcasting the matches too. Coverage was not yet as extensive as it would be for the final tournament, but plenty of viewers watched at home or online. The Ever Grande Conference was steadily drawing all of Hoenn into its excitement.

Ethan watched several matches from the stands. His turn came in the second-to-last match of the afternoon.

As Alexa had said, the Hoenn crowd did not recognize him. His entrance caused no special stir, though some women applauded and cheered more loudly when they saw his handsome face. That small difference made Anthony envious as he entered from the other side. Why was the applause for him so thin? And why were his own cheering supporters all men?

“Ladies and gentlemen,” the announcer called, “next is the ninth match in Arena F! In red, Ethan! In green, Anthony! Let's see what they can show us!”

At the referee's signal, both Trainers threw their Poké Balls. This was a Double Battle, testing how well each pair could work together. Ethan had no concerns on that score.

Blue light flashed, and Milotic and Metagross appeared. The stands grew louder. Both species were difficult to raise and rarely seen compared with more common Pokémon. Seeing them together was a novelty.

Anthony's team looked more ordinary, but he had brought his two strongest partners. One was Pelipper, the same Pokémon that had once carried Poké Balls in its mouth while he claimed it was the world's strongest. The other was Magneton, evolved from a Magnemite that had also hidden in Pelipper's beak during that earlier deception.

Water and Electric moves gave Anthony a broad range of attacks when the pair worked together, despite the poor matchup between their own types. He had planned carefully. Ethan had beaten him with Dragonite before; Anthony had brought Magneton partly to counter it. Ethan had not sent Dragonite out, but Magneton still held a type advantage over Milotic. Anthony felt he had guessed well enough. Today he would settle the score.

The referee started the match. Anthony's hopes faltered in the first exchanges.

A type advantage mattered only if he could land his attacks. Milotic and Metagross gave him no easy chance. Pelipper's Water Gun and Hydro Pump met stronger attacks from Milotic. Its Air Slash and Wing Attack could not break through Milotic's defense either, and Milotic's counters left Pelipper injured.

Anthony tried to send Magneton against Milotic instead, but Metagross kept it occupied and battered it badly. His attacks seemed to land on padding: every advantage he expected was blunted before he could use it. An old, unhappy memory returned. Ethan was not even using Dragonite, yet the battle felt like their first meeting, when he had been defeated so easily.

He had traveled too far, earned eight Badges, and reached this stage to suffer the same fate again. Anthony shoved the thought aside and tried everything he could imagine. Shouting from his position, he ordered his Pokémon to cross paths and exchange opponents. This time the switch worked—but the battle did not improve.

Milotic's ranged attacks kept Magneton away. Streams of water stopped its electrical attacks from doing meaningful damage. Pelipper fared no better against Metagross. Both of Anthony's Pokémon were now at a clear disadvantage. He had no answer left.

Ethan's expression never changed. He stood calmly, issuing an occasional order. His team was simply stronger. Anthony had traveled, but not through as many battles or experiences as Ethan and his Pokémon. Ethan's keen eye and sense of timing let him read Anthony's plans and counter them. The gap between the Trainers compounded the gap between their Pokémon.

He did not finish the match immediately. He wanted Milotic and Metagross to spend more time battling in the Conference atmosphere. He did something similar at nearly every tournament, helping newer team members grow accustomed to competing before a large crowd.

Unfortunately for Anthony, that meant a longer ordeal. Milotic and Metagross defended and countered only enough to keep him under pressure. They did not rush to knock his Pokémon out. Under nearly ten thousand watching eyes, Ethan's pair gradually settled in. Milotic moved more fluidly, and Metagross hovered more steadily. The crowd no longer seemed to affect them; they could fight at their normal level.

That mattered. Many Trainers and Pokémon lost some of their ability to nerves on a stage like this. Anyone who overcame that fear already held an advantage over much of the field. Ethan's older partners had passed that hurdle long ago, but the Pokémon he had caught in Hoenn still needed experience. That was why he was willing to let the match continue.

Anthony's condition moved in the opposite direction. His spirits sank, and his Pokémon's coordination fell apart. Next to Ethan's increasingly smooth pair, Pelipper and Magneton looked clumsy together. Everyone could see that Anthony would lose.

In a corner of the stands, Gabby and Ty kept their camera rolling. Their outdoor family program had no access to the official camera area, so they had bought tickets and filmed like ordinary spectators.

“He's incredible,” Ty said. “He's playing with that opponent. The man can't do anything.”

Gabby was no battling expert, but she had a reporter's eye. She nodded, excitement brightening her face. “If he performs like this in every match, the championship is only a matter of time.”

She grew more determined. After failing to find the Pokémon behind Hoenn's changing weather during their trip off Mossdeep's coast, Ethan had become her greatest—and perhaps only—chance to regain her place at the station. She could not let another reporter get his exclusive interview first, even if she had to use a few tricks.

Ethan knew nothing of Gabby's thoughts. He saw that Anthony had lost the will to fight and judged the practice sufficient. With a wave, he ordered Milotic and Metagross to finish. Both opposing Pokémon went down, and the arena erupted in cheers.

Fans loved a close, exciting contest, but an overwhelming victory could be just as thrilling. They had watched Anthony's resistance fail against Ethan's strength and followed every exchange from Ethan's side. His win brought a flood of applause.
