From the man's tone, it was clear he wasn't just a casual passerby. He knew something about Pokémon Contests, or perhaps even worked in that world.

Before Ethan and the others could ask, he introduced himself. “Oh, I forgot to tell you my name. I'm Mr. Big. I live nearby, and I used to be a Pokémon Coordinator.”

A Coordinator? Mr. Big?

Ethan thought carefully, but neither the man's face nor his name was familiar. Even if he had been a Coordinator, he couldn't have been a particularly famous one.

Beside him, May, Misty, and Ash introduced themselves in turn. When it came to Ethan, he gave Mr. Big a nod of greeting.

“I heard your battle and came over to have a look,” Mr. Big said with a smile. “I didn't expect such a good match. That Taillow in particular used its moves with real flair!”

Ethan and the others weren't sure exactly what he meant by flair, but they could tell it was praise.

Mr. Big and Drew were both Coordinators, yet their personalities could hardly have been more different. One was warm and friendly; the other was aloof and sharp-tongued. Drew had youth and looks on his side, but the group found Mr. Big much easier to like.

Learning that he had competed in Contests, May couldn't help asking, “Um... how many Ribbons did you win?”

It wasn't a very polite question for someone she'd just met. Such a personal subject could easily touch a sore spot and offend him.

Fortunately, Mr. Big didn't mind. With a hint of pride, he answered, “Three Contest Ribbons altogether!”

Three! To most people, and especially to May as a newcomer, that was an impressive record. A Contest Ribbon wasn't like a Gym Badge, which a Trainer could earn by challenging and defeating a Gym Leader. A Coordinator had to compete against other entrants and win through judging. The competition was fierce. Three Ribbons meant this man really knew what he was doing.

May's eyes shone. “Mr. Big, can you tell me the secret to competing?”

Her excitement was understandable. Ethan and Ash were Pokémon Trainers surrounded by other Trainers who were both friends and rivals. When one of them had a problem, a few words from someone else might point the way forward.

May had no other Coordinators beside her. She had been figuring everything out by herself, and there were things even an experienced Coordinator might consider common knowledge that she still didn't know. Of course she was thrilled to meet someone who had won three Ribbons.

Mr. Big looked from May to her Beautifly and rubbed his chin. “The most important thing for a Coordinator is to learn to stay calm. I watched your battle. You were flustered and kept scrambling to respond. That's a serious mistake for a Coordinator. Your state of mind affects your Pokémon's confidence and performance. Even actions and moves they can usually manage are much more likely to fail.”

It was heartfelt advice. Ethan could tell from those few words that Mr. Big had experience and genuinely wanted to help May.

Ethan had noticed the same problems, but he followed a different path from May. As an outsider to Contests, he had hesitated to speak too bluntly. He'd intended to say something after the battle, but having a senior Coordinator explain it to her was better.

May frowned and thought back over her performance. There had indeed been many moments she wished she'd handled differently.

She went on to ask Mr. Big about other things that had puzzled her. He answered each question patiently, and his explanations made things fall into place for her.

May straightened and bowed deeply. “Thank you so much! I've been wondering about these things for ages, and now I finally understand!”

As confidence gradually returned to her face, Ethan and the others exchanged smiles.

Pokémon Contests and ordinary battles both involved Pokémon, but they were different fields. Specialist knowledge was best taught by someone who knew the field well.

Perhaps May's willingness to learn moved Mr. Big. Wanting to help his young fellow Coordinator further, he reached inside his clothes and brought out a rectangular container that looked like a candy box. He handed it to her.

“If you want to become an excellent Coordinator, you need to pay attention to more than training. Your Pokémon's food and nutrition matter too. That helps them look healthy and beautiful. These are Pokéblocks, made from a mixture of different Berries. They can improve a Pokémon's condition. You can give your Pokémon one from time to time.”

“I... I couldn't take these...”

“It's all right. I've made plenty, and I hardly need them anymore. Please, don't be shy.”

May tried to refuse at first, but Mr. Big insisted. Since she truly needed something like this, she finally accepted it.

Everyone could see that May had met a kind man today. Max often bickered with his sister, but he was happy for her.

Then something Mr. Big had said struck him. “Mr. Big, you said you used to be a Coordinator. Have you stopped?”

Mr. Big smiled and nodded. “I'm getting older. I can't dance across the stage the way I did when I was young. These days, I make fireworks.”

“Fireworks?”

“That's right, exactly the sort you're thinking of. My daily work is making fireworks in the shapes of Pokémon!”

May hadn't expected that. A man with three Contest Ribbons had changed careers. But it did nothing to weaken her resolve. She bowed again and asked loudly, “Mr. Big, would you help me practice now?”

He stared at her in surprise. He hadn't expected the girl to ask him that.

Perhaps her earnest expression touched him. After thinking for a moment, he sighed. “All right. I've finished my work for the day, and there's still time before tomorrow's Contest. I'll give you a little guidance...”
