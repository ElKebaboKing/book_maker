May took Beautifly and followed Mr. Big off to one side to continue practicing.

With a professional helping her, there wasn't much Ash, Misty, or even her younger brother Max could do. They all stepped back.

Seeing May concentrate on her training, Ethan turned to the others. “She doesn't need us for the moment. Instead of standing around, why don't we explore somewhere else?”

Everyone agreed. They told May they'd meet her at the Pokémon Center that evening, then headed back toward the city.

Without anyone noticing, the day had slipped into afternoon. Warm sunlight fell across the streets, making the air pleasant.

After wandering for some time in the sea breeze, Misty stopped at an intersection and looked both ways. “Do you know anywhere good to go?” she asked Ash and the others.

She and Ethan had only arrived in Slateport that day. She knew nothing about the city and had no idea where they should spend the time.

Max didn't hesitate. “I want to go to the Oceanic Museum!”

“The Oceanic Museum?” The others turned to him.

“That's right!” Max adjusted his glasses. “It's famous. There are all kinds of displays about the ocean, and models of lots of unusual Pokémon. I've wanted to see it for ages, but we never had time. It's supposed to be open today, so this is our chance!”

That was Max for you. Most boys his age would choose an amusement park or a playground. Few were as eager to learn as he was.

Ethan and the others had no objection. But just as they were about to set out for the museum, Brock stayed where he was. “You go ahead. I need to buy ingredients and supplies at the market. Like May, I'll meet you at the Pokémon Center tonight.”

Sometimes Ethan envied Ash. With Brock taking care of everything like a full-time caretaker, Ash hardly had to worry about daily necessities. He could focus entirely on his battles.

Ethan had enjoyed that sort of help only briefly, when Erika was still traveling with him.

Ah, another day without Erika. As always, he missed her...

...

Slateport's Oceanic Museum stood on open ground beside the sea, not far from the lighthouse.

As Ethan's group approached, they saw three tall glass-domed buildings joined together into one enormous museum.

Max walked ahead, so excited he was humming. But the moment he reached the entrance and read the notice there, his face fell.

“How could this happen...?”

He backed away, devastated. Ethan raised an eyebrow and stepped up to read the notice. The museum was normally open today, but it had been closed temporarily because its curator had something to attend to.

“Looks as though we came for nothing.”

The others were disappointed, but it wasn't a serious loss. They could treat the trip as a walk. Max, however, couldn't accept it. He looked around, ran to a museum window, jumped, and caught the sill with both hands.

“Hey...” Misty raised an eyebrow in a way that looked remarkably like Ethan. “What are you doing? That's dangerous! Get down!”

“I don't care!” Max gritted his teeth and tried to pull himself through the window. “I'm going into that museum today! There's so much for me to learn in there! I've been looking forward to it forever!”

He had completely lost his head over this.

Worried he might hurt himself, Ethan started toward him to lift him down. Just then, Max's arms gave out. His fingers slipped, and he fell from the windowsill.

Thump.

He landed on his backside. Fortunately, there was grass beneath him and he hadn't climbed very high, so the fall did him no harm.

Ash helped him to his feet. “Come on, let's go. If they're closed today, we can come back tomorrow.”

Perhaps their voices had been too loud. The folding window suddenly opened from the inside, and a neatly dressed man in his forties leaned out to look at them.

“May I ask what you're doing?”

None of them had expected anyone to be in the room. Max looked embarrassed, but answered honestly. “We came to visit the museum, but the front door was locked. I thought I could get in this way!”

If an adult had done what Max had just tried, it would have looked very much like trespassing. Coming from a child, the frank confession sounded different. The man merely smiled and nodded.

“I see.” He glanced at Ethan and the others. “I'm Captain Stern, the curator of this museum. There is a reason we're closed today. Still, since you've come all this way, I wouldn't want you to leave disappointed. I'll make an exception and let you in.”

He disappeared from the window. A little later, a side door opened, and he stood there beckoning to them.

Max was overjoyed. He ran over at once. Ethan and the others exchanged surprised looks. Their luck had turned, and the man offering to let them in was the museum's curator himself.

May and Max had both been fortunate today. Each had met someone willing to help.

They followed Max into the museum. Although they entered through a side door, it was near the main entrance, and a few steps brought them into the central hall.

The sight that greeted them stopped everyone in their tracks: a life-size Wailord model suspended from the ceiling.

“It's huge...”

Looking up from below, they saw its great blue body hanging over nearly the entire hall. It made them feel how small a human being was beside such a vast Pokémon.

Ash couldn't help asking, “Captain Stern, that Wailord isn't real, is it?”
