The three of them did not enter the Gym itself. They followed the long staircase up the outside of the pyramid toward its summit.

It had looked high from the ground. Climbing it revealed just how high: the equivalent of at least twenty-six or twenty-seven ordinary floors. After the long passage they had already walked, the ascent was exhausting. Misty was panting before they were halfway up.

“It's so tall...” She wiped her forehead. “Why didn't they put in an elevator? I can understand wanting to test challengers, but they could think about those of us who just came along to watch.”

The staff member seemed accustomed to the climb. He was not even breathing hard. “We're a Flying-type Gym,” he said with a smile. “You can ride a Flying-type Pokémon straight to the top. Still, I think taking the stairs occasionally is good for you.”

Steep and long as they were, the stairs finally ended. At the top, Ethan and Misty stopped at the sight before them.

From the tallest building in Fortree, they could see the entire city. Below lay a sea of green. Trees hid almost everything except the tallest structures, which rose above the canopy. Having climbed so far, they felt a curious satisfaction in looking out over it.

“It is beautiful,” Ethan said. “Worth the climb.”

He turned toward the rest of the summit. A standard battlefield occupied the top of the Gym. Its floor was covered with a thick layer of earth, deep enough for a Ground-type Pokémon to use Dig. On either side stood a lift that could raise a Trainer into the air to direct a battle.

At the far end, a woman in her twenties watched him. She wore a flight suit and a helmet with goggles. Her long hair was grayish purple, and years of flying had given her a fit, shapely figure. She was attractive in a quiet way.

This must be Winona.

“Welcome to the Fortree Gym,” she said when their eyes met. “I'm Winona, its Gym Leader.”

Known as a bird Pokémon Trainer who soared around the world, Winona specialized in avian Pokémon and could read the wind. She believed living in harmony with nature was the best way to live, which was why she had chosen Fortree for her Gym. Like Wallace, she valued elegance and spectacle in battle. People respected both her grace and her strength.

“I'm Ethan.”

“And I'm Misty.”

They kept the introductions brief. Winona knew why they had come. Misty went to the spectator area, while the staff member guided Ethan to a Trainer's platform. Winona took the opposite side. Both lifts rose, carrying them more than ten meters above the battlefield.

So that was this Gym's specialty. Directing a battle from the air suited a Flying-type Leader. Anyone afraid of heights might have trouble, especially with the whole arena already atop a tower. Ethan often flew above the clouds on his Pokémon. This platform was low by comparison.

The staff member moved to a raised place near the center of the field to referee. “The Fortree Gym battle between Leader Winona and challenger Ethan will now begin. Each side may use three Pokémon. The battle ends when all Pokémon on one side are unable to battle. Only the challenger may switch Pokémon freely.”

The rules were ordinary enough. Despite the aerial arena, there were no unusual battle requirements.

Once Ethan was ready, Winona threw a Poké Ball. An Altaria appeared before him.

He was not surprised. Altaria was her signature Pokémon and one of her strongest. It was the first Pokémon many people associated with Winona.

Sending it out before she knew her challenger's strength told Ethan she did not hold back. She took every Gym match seriously. He welcomed that, but a novice Trainer might face a long struggle here, perhaps failing repeatedly. Some Gym Leaders preferred to guide beginners with a gentler team. Others fought in earnest from the start. Each approach had its advantages; overcoming a hard Gym could bring tremendous growth.

As for his answer to Altaria, Ethan did not deliberate. He took a Poké Ball from his belt and threw it onto the field. Blue light flashed, and Winona saw his choice.

“Metang?” She nodded appreciatively. “Steel-type attacks won't be especially effective against Altaria, but its Flying- and Dragon-type moves won't do much to Metang either. A sound choice.”

She was confident enough to assess his Pokémon aloud.

Altaria circled high above the field. Metang floated about a meter from the ground. Despite the distance between them, both fixed on each other, ready to fight. Pokémon could sense an opponent's presence and gauge its strength more directly than their Trainers could. Altaria's eyes were alert and expressive. Metang, with its cold, stony heart, showed little emotion.

The referee signaled the start.

Winona opened aggressively. Altaria dove straight toward Metang. It was the quickest way to close the distance, but a direct approach made it easier to hit. She must trust Altaria's speed.

Ethan wanted to test that speed. He ordered Metang to attack. An invisible wave of Confusion shot toward Altaria. With a slight shift of its wings, Altaria changed course, arced around the attack, and swept toward Metang from the side.

It was fast. No wonder Winona had chosen that approach.

Ethan had suspected as much. Still, Altaria had reached Metang before it could attack again. With the bird moving so quickly, Metang could not dodge either.

“Now! Peck!” Winona called.

Peck? Ethan was taken aback. With such an opening, why use a basic move?

Then he saw what made Winona's Peck different. Altaria's short beak flashed. In a few seconds, it struck Metang dozens of times, as quickly as a bird pecking at scattered grain. The beak blurred, and the repeated ringing of metal filled the arena.

Each Peck was weak, but so many blows together could do real damage. Ethan had to admire the training behind it. Altaria had earned its place as Winona's signature Pokémon.

Even so, as Winona had said, Altaria's attacks did only half their usual damage against Metang. Metang's sturdy defenses reduced their effect further. The assault looked alarming but achieved little.

“As I thought,” Winona said, shaking her head. “That move has surprised and beaten many challengers. I expected this result against a Pokémon that resists it, though.”

Ethan heard the disappointment in her voice. He had not chosen Metang simply to exploit her typing. Only a few Pokémon on his current team needed training, and he had to send them out in some order.

The instant Altaria finished, Metang countered. It swung its two heavy claws with Metal Claw. Altaria had committed to close combat knowing the risks; it spread its wings and slipped away as the claws rose. A Flying-type Pokémon's agility served it well.

Ethan had not counted on Metal Claw connecting. As soon as Altaria climbed, he had Metang attack from range with Confusion.

Altaria kept its distance now. It dodged the psychic attacks and answered with Dragon Breath and Dragon Pulse. Firing while moving brought mistakes, though. It landed one Dragon Breath, but Metang found an opening of its own.

One lapse was enough. Confusion struck Altaria and held it, drawing it lower toward Metang while dealing damage.

“Altaria, break free!” Winona ordered.

It could not muscle its way out. Winona quickly found another approach. Though Altaria could not move, it could open its beak. At her command, it fired Dragon Pulse into the ground below. The impact pushed it upward, and the blast threw up a cloud of smoke and a wave of air. The disruption broke Metang's hold.

Altaria had no time to relax. “Above you!” Winona shouted.

Altaria looked up. Metang had already rushed toward it, both claws gleaming as they swung. With no time to dodge, Altaria wrapped itself in its fluffy plumage and braced for the hit.

Ethan had recognized Winona's plan as soon as she called for Dragon Pulse. He had anticipated the escape and ordered Metang to use Agility, closing the distance before striking with Metal Claw again.

He had not expected Altaria's instant response: Cotton Guard. Only a few Pokémon with thick plumage could use the move. It sharply raised Altaria's defenses, an effective answer to a close-range strike.

Impressive. Winona had not even called for Cotton Guard. Altaria had reacted on its own instinct. Ethan had admired its training once already; now it had earned that admiration again.
