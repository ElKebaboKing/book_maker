In moments, Milotic followed the water across the ice into Shizuko's half of the field. All the effort she had put into closing the distance had been wasted. Worse, with the water still there, the same thing would happen again.

How had Ethan thought of it? Shizuko had to deal with the water first.

At her command, Piloswine drew a deep breath and blew Icy Wind over the puddles. The shallow water froze into a thin layer of ice at once.

“An effective response!” the announcer cried. “However much water Milotic spreads, Piloswine can freeze it immediately! That isn't good news for Milotic. What will Ethan do now?”

Ethan saw Shizuko relax a little and smiled. Did she think she had found the answer? It would not be that simple.

“Milotic, use Rain Dance!”

Milotic lifted its head and called to the sky. Clouds gathered across what had been a clear blue morning. A few low rumbles of thunder sounded, and rain began to fall steadily over the entire Ice Field. Rain Dance had taken effect.

Ethan looked back at Shizuko. Let's see how you answer this.

She stared up at the unending rain, her brows knotted. What could she do? She could clear the sky or wait for Rain Dance to end, but she had no way to stop the weather now. Piloswine could use Icy Wind until it wore itself thin and still fail to keep up.

Blizzard might disrupt the rain clouds for a while. Its effect would not last as long as Rain Dance, though. Once it ended, the rain would return, and nothing would have changed.

Fighting Milotic on this field was a poor choice. With a helpless shake of her head, Shizuko recalled Piloswine.

“Shizuko has chosen to withdraw,” the announcer said regretfully. “She had a chance to close in on Milotic, but the rain has put an end to that plan. Who will she send out next? Ah, it's Swampert!”

Swampert was the last of Shizuko's Pokémon that the crowd had yet to see. Bringing it out now suggested it was her first partner and perhaps her strongest.

Many Trainers saved their strongest Pokémon for the second half. They could conceal their strength, study the opponent, and choose a favorable matchup. Their ace could also steady the team and turn the match around when things went wrong.

Objectively, Shizuko had switched at the right time. Piloswine had barely been on the field and had taken no damage. If she had waited until it was as battered as Houndoom, she might have acted too late. Taking a hard lesson early had made her quicker to adapt.

Ethan did not switch. Milotic remained out to face Swampert, drawing Ash and the others' attention again.

“Ethan isn't changing Pokémon this time either?”

“Is he trying for another three-Pokémon sweep?”

Two Pokémon, each knocking out three opponents in a single match? That would take extraordinary confidence.

“You're reading too much into it,” Misty said before they could get carried away. “Ethan is giving his Pokémon battle experience. He hasn't set a target for how many opponents each one has to beat.”

Ash gave an embarrassed smile and sat up straight rather than argue with her.

Milotic and Swampert began to fight. Both were Water types, but Swampert was also a Ground type. Water-type attacks would be resisted, so it could use Ground moves to strike Milotic instead. How those moves would work on ice was another question.

The rain showed no sign of stopping. Spectators closest to the field even felt it dampen their clothes. Beneath the steady, moderate shower, the Pokémon skipped any cautious opening and went straight into a fierce exchange.

Both knew Water-type attacks would have limited effect and chose other moves. Swampert found mud somewhere and repeatedly hurled it with Mud Shot. Milotic answered with Twister, blowing the mud away.

When its ranged attacks failed, Swampert used all four limbs to run at Milotic for a close fight. Ethan had no reason to fear it. He did not counterattack at once, though. Milotic acted as if it could not match Swampert up close and slipped around the ice blocks, giving Shizuko that impression.

Once she grew less careful, Ethan gave the order. Milotic's Dragon Tail whipped into Swampert.

Smack! The heavy blow drove Swampert back several steps. Shizuko startled and stopped sending it so close. Privately, she cursed Ethan's cunning. He had this much strength and still used a little trick like that.

Ethan could not hear her thoughts. He only regretted that Swampert had become more cautious. If Shizuko had kept attacking as recklessly as she had in the first two rounds, he could have won easily. Now it would take more work.

As Swampert's attacks grew less aggressive, Milotic pressed forward. The balance of offense and defense shifted in a moment. Its smooth scales and long body made it more agile than Swampert on the rainy, slippery ice. Its attacks came from more varied angles and were harder to avoid.

Swampert still had two arms, an advantage over Milotic in a close fight. But that advantage did not last.

Milotic dodged an Iron Tail, slid in like a snake, and caught Swampert at the right moment. Its long body coiled tightly around it with Wrap. As Milotic squeezed, Swampert's face reddened.

“Oh no!” Shizuko cried. She had been so careful, yet Ethan had found an opening again. “Swampert, break free!”

That was easier said than done. Swampert stood a meter and a half tall. Ethan's fully grown Milotic measured 6.2 meters when its snake-like body was stretched out. Its length gave it an advantage. Swampert might have had greater raw strength, but Milotic had bound its arms and legs together, leaving it no room to use them.

Swampert kept struggling. At last it pulled one arm free and tried to push Milotic away. Rain slicked Milotic's already smooth scales. No matter how Swampert tried, its hand could find no grip.

Time passed. Its strength drained away under the pressure, until it felt as though the bones throughout its body might be crushed.

“Swampert!” Shizuko saw that leaving it there meant certain defeat. She raised its Poké Ball to recall it. Red light flashed toward Swampert, but Milotic shifted and blocked the beam with its back. The light could not reach Swampert, so the recall failed.

Shizuko's face changed. Ethan lowered his gaze. “It's over. Milotic, finish it.”

Milotic lifted its tail over Swampert. Realizing what was coming, Swampert struggled harder. It even spat water at the tail, hoping to knock it away. But a Water-type attack would do little to another Water type, and Milotic simply twisted aside.

Violet light spread across its tail. In one smooth movement, Milotic loosened its coils and brought the tail down with Dragon Tail.

Smack! Swampert had no time to react. The blow landed squarely on its head and knocked it unconscious.

“Swampert is unable to battle! Milotic wins!”

As the referee raised the flag, Shizuko felt cold all over. Her strongest Pokémon had fallen. What hope did she have now? Her two remaining Pokémon both had poor matchups against Milotic.

The rest went as she feared. She sent Piloswine out again, then Houndoom. Milotic defeated both with ease. Shizuko lost so completely that she never even saw Ethan's third Pokémon.

The defeat shook her. For a moment she doubted whether she had deserved a place in the main tournament at all, or whether she was truly as strong as people had said.

Ethan, by contrast, stood amid a tremendous roar from the crowd. With two Pokémon, each taking down three opponents, he had sent all of Hoenn a message about who was strongest at the Ever Grande Conference. He had not set out to make that statement, but the result spoke for him.

Seeded favorites and dark horses alike had fallen easily before him. Only now did the crowd fully grasp what kind of Trainer Ethan was.

He had drawn less attention before because his victories looked almost too simple, with none of the dramatic swings that helped an audience judge a contest. By beating a highly regarded competitor from the previous round even more easily, he finally made everyone look at him.

He had the image and the skill. Opinion shifted at once. Calls for him to take the championship rose louder than ever.

Judging by his performance and control of the tournament, people no longer wondered whether he could win. They wondered who his final opponent would be and how he would defeat them. They had seen enough of the remaining Trainers to know their abilities. None seemed his equal. None even appeared capable of threatening him.

The main tournament had run for only two rounds, and only a few quarterfinalists had been decided. Yet across those rounds, Ethan had used eight Pokémon. Every one of them had overpowered its opponents without a single loss. Other Trainers' matches looked pale beside his. He was the only competitor still unbeaten, and he seemed far ahead of the field.

Unless he placed a bet and manipulated the result himself, or performed disastrously in the final rounds, the crowd could not imagine anyone defeating him.

Ethan waved calmly to the cheering spectators and walked into the competitors' tunnel.

From the same spot behind the same window, Goodshow watched him go. The old man's face seemed to have aged another decade.

John drew on his cigarette and stepped beside him with a bitter smile. “Public opinion has turned. We can't stop his rise now. It looks as though the championship is his.”

Goodshow said nothing. After a pause, John asked, “What will you do? Keep arranging the strongest possible opponents for him?”

Only a helpless sigh answered him.
