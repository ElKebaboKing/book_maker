Ethan and Erika returned to the ground-floor lobby of their lodgings. They settled on a sofa in the lounge, ordered two cocktails, and watched Ash's match with Gary at their leisure.

In the final tournament, the battlefield was randomly chosen for each match.

There were four possibilities: grass, rock, water, and ice. Each field could change a Trainer's tactics, strategy, and even how a Pokémon performed. The random draw made the matches less predictable and more exciting to watch.

Ethan and Casey had drawn the grass field. They had battled among dense greenery, though neither had made much use of the terrain.

On the screen, the announcer was introducing Ash and Gary.

"...Incredibly, both Ash and Gary come from Pallet Town in Kanto, and they set out on their journeys from there at the same time! Now these friends and rivals finally meet in what seems a fated clash in the final tournament!"

Gary's cheerleading squad, absent for so long, had appeared again from somewhere. Behind him in the section for friends and family, they performed a cheering dance.

The tall, beautiful young women drew nearly every man's gaze and made Gary impossible to forget.

The announcer continued, "And we've learned that Gary is the grandson of Professor Oak, a highly respected figure in Pokémon research! Ash, meanwhile, reached the final sixteen at the Indigo League. Their match today is sure to be one of the most watched and exciting of the tournament! Both Trainers are ready, so let's see the sparks fly!"

The camera cut to Professor Oak. Seeing himself on the screen, he froze for a moment. He had clearly not expected to be mentioned. Sitting beside Delia, he looked terribly awkward. But he had weathered plenty of public attention before, and a second later he managed a stiff smile and waved at the camera.

When the audience's enthusiastic applause died down, the announcer returned to the match.

"Before the battle begins, let's find out which field they'll draw!"

Everyone watched the wheel on the large screen spin. When it stopped, the pointer indicated the rock field.

The ground before Ash and Gary began to change. It parted to either side, opening a dark pit below. A huge lifting platform rose from the darkness, carrying a battlefield strewn with giant boulders into place.

Next came the draw for who would send out a Pokémon first. Ash was lucky enough to go second, leaving Gary, on the red side, to choose first.

Gary looked indifferent. It seemed he did not care which of them had to lead.

When the match began, Gary sent out Nidoqueen. Ash thought for a moment and answered with Tauros. Both Pokémon had plenty of strength; a contest of raw power seemed inevitable.

Erika glanced away from the screen. "Which one do you think will win?"

"Probably Ash," Ethan said, rubbing his chin.

If he had to judge only from knowing the two boys in person, Ethan could not say which would win. But he also remembered the animated series, and that made him favor Ash.

"Ash..."

Erika frowned slightly as she weighed their strengths.

Before the Indigo League, both boys had challenged her at the Celadon Gym. Gary had seemed stronger then. He had since shed much of his arrogance and become steadier, which likely made him an even tougher opponent.

Yet Ash was a more astonishing boy. As Erika traveled with him and came to know him better, she saw how unpredictable his ability could be. He could be slow to catch on and rash in his actions, but he also had a way of producing miracles.

She still did not know who would win, but she was willing to trust Ethan's judgment.

On the screen, Nidoqueen and Tauros had already begun fighting.

Nidoqueen opened with a powerful strike, slamming its fist into the ground. A wide fissure spread toward Tauros. The force shattered the towering boulders, sending smaller stones raining down on it.

Tauros took some damage, but at Ash's command it charged straight at Nidoqueen with astonishing force, aiming its two horns directly at its opponent.

Gary's Nidoqueen was no weakling. It seized both horns with brute strength and stopped Tauros in its tracks.

At that close range, Nidoqueen fired a Hyper Beam straight into Tauros. The blast hurled it through several boulders before it collapsed on the ground, unable to rise.

Seeing Tauros unconscious, the referee raised his flag.

The crowd erupted in surprise. No one had expected Tauros to be knocked out so quickly. Hadn't the announcer just promised an exciting match?

Was that it?

The announcer himself had not expected this outcome and fell silent in embarrassment.

The Pokémon had barely exchanged blows. The audience had not even settled into the match before the first round ended.

Ash was not shaken. He calmly recalled Tauros and took out his second Poké Ball.

This time he sent out Heracross. Whether out of caution or for some other reason, Gary recalled Nidoqueen and chose a second Poké Ball too.

A flash of blue light revealed Magmar opposite Heracross.

It was a strong choice. Magmar's Fire-type attacks had a clear advantage against Heracross's Bug typing. Gary was taking the match seriously and had no intention of holding back.
