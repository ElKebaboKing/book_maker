A flash of blue light revealed a Heracross whose entire body was purple. Its color was so different from an ordinary Heracross's that Ethan and May recognized it at once as a Shiny Pokémon.

That was unexpected. Ethan had not imagined a Bug Catcher who rarely left the forest would own a Shiny Pokémon. Then again, perhaps that was precisely why he had found one. He devoted his time to searching deep among the trees for Bug-type Pokémon.

“So your Shiny Heracross is your strongest partner?” Ethan asked.

May did not underestimate it. She recalled Eevee and took out Combusken's Poké Ball. Samurai was a useful opponent for practice, but that did not mean she wanted to lose through carelessness.

Another flash of blue light brought out her first partner. Combusken's Fire-type moves gave it an advantage over Heracross. May clearly had no intention of handing Samurai an opportunity. In that respect, her style was growing more like Ethan's all the time. She was almost a smaller, female version of him.

Ethan shifted on his seat and called one more warning. “Be careful with fire. We're in a forest. One mistake could start a disaster.”

“I know,” May said.

Both Trainers and Pokémon were ready. The battle began.

Despite the unfavorable matchup, the Shiny Heracross went straight on the attack. That aggression suited its species. Lowering its great horn, it charged Combusken with Horn Attack.

Combusken did not try to meet the heavy blow head-on. It leaped high over Heracross and landed behind it, avoiding the attack and leaving its opponent's back exposed.

May's temperament kept her from getting carried away by a contest of strength. She preferred to find an opening rather than force a clash, and that caution showed in the way she fought.

Heracross plowed through two trees before it stopped. Its horn was formidable, especially with Horn Attack behind it. A direct hit could have badly injured Combusken. But now its unguarded back offered the perfect chance to strike.

Combusken ran forward without waiting for May to tell it and used Double Kick.

Samurai had improved since their last meeting, though, and Heracross was his strongest Pokémon. He stayed calm. At his command, Heracross beat the thin wings on its back and flew a short distance. It soon landed again, but the movement was enough to avoid Combusken's kick.

“Heh. Did you think I wouldn't be ready?” Samurai said smugly. “Now you'll see what my Heracross can do!”

Heracross attacked again, this time with Pin Missile. Wave after wave of sharp needles whistled through the air toward Combusken. Samurai plainly wanted to finish the fight quickly.

He still underestimated May. She might have spent fewer years battling other Trainers, but her ability to adapt was on another level. She had not won so many Contests by accident.

“Combusken, Quick Attack!”

At her command, Combusken ran in a zigzag. It was not moving at extraordinary speed, but it was fast enough to slip past every volley of needles.

“Damn it…” Sweat gathered beneath Samurai's armor. He had fought strong Trainers in the forest before, yet this girl felt strangely harder to handle. Against both Butterfree and Heracross, he could not bring his strength to bear. Moves that usually worked against passing Trainers had failed him again and again.

The more that happened, the more anxious he grew. At this rate he would never challenge Ethan. He could not even beat May. He wanted to change the course of the battle, but she gave him no chance.

May admitted to herself that Heracross's short flight had surprised her. Her experience in Trainer battles was still limited. Now that she had seen the trick once, though, she would be ready if Samurai tried it again.

It was her turn to attack. Combusken used the momentum of Quick Attack to close on Heracross. It kept running, apparently ready to settle things with a close-range Fighting-type move.

Heracross was part Fighting type too. Under Samurai's direction, it planted its feet and waited. Why should it fear another Fighting-type Pokémon?

What reached it was not a Fighting-type move. Just before impact, flames flared around Combusken's body, and it slammed into Heracross.

It was Flame Charge.

May had taken Ethan's warning to heart. She did not dare use Ember or Flamethrower freely among the trees. Flame Charge kept the fire close to Combusken's body, where she could control it.

The feint caught Samurai unprepared. He could only watch Combusken strike like a ball of fire. Fire was strong against Bug types, and even a sturdy Heracross cried out in pain.

Combusken did not let up. It followed with a string of Peck attacks, using the Flying-type move to bring the Shiny Heracross down.

“How…” Samurai's legs weakened at the sight of his fallen Pokémon. He sank to his knees, unable to accept the result.

He had lost again. The second time he met Ethan, he and the ninja Aya had tried to overwhelm him together in Viridian Forest. Their fight had ended before a final result, but Samurai knew he had been losing then too. He had trained for so long and beaten many passing Trainers, yet he had lost to Ethan's side just as easily upon meeting him again.

This time he had not even fought Ethan. Ethan's supposed assistant had beaten him first. It was infuriating.

Then another thought occurred to him. The pretty girl traveling with Ethan last time had been beautiful, but now he was accompanied by a different pretty girl. What a womanizer! And, if Samurai was honest with himself, how enviable.

“I lost today.” He got up and faced Ethan, fists clenched. “But next time we meet, I'll beat you! Then I'll draw all over your face and make you see what it's like to be left sleeping outside!”

Draw on his face?

The remark finally brought back what had happened before. Ethan spread his hands. “That wasn't me. Remember the Jigglypuff we met? It drew on everyone. I got caught by it too…”

He stopped. He had since caught that Jigglypuff. It was his Pokémon now. Did that make its mischief his responsibility after all?

Perhaps Samurai was not entirely wrong. Ethan sighed inwardly. Somehow the blame had all landed on him. No wonder the boy held such a grudge. Still, there was no chance he would admit to it now.

Samurai recalled his Pokémon and left, still seething. Once he had disappeared among the trees, May returned to Ethan with a laugh.

“It sounds as if the two of you have quite a misunderstanding.”

“We do.” Ethan shook his head. “It's more than I can explain in one sentence. I'll tell you when we have time. Besides, I have a feeling that if we come back to Kanto, we'll run into him again.”

…

After the interruption, they gathered themselves and continued deeper into the forest. In a little more than ten minutes, they saw the tip of a pyramid above the trees. Both quickened their pace.

Several hundred meters later, they rounded a stand of large trees and finally saw the Battle Pyramid in full. Its shape matched its name: a great pointed pyramid. It was dark rather than stone-colored, made of joined panels that looked like metal plating or solar cells. The whole structure had a technological air.

It rose fifty or sixty meters high and was too broad for Ethan to judge at a glance. The massive building looked magnificent and utterly out of place in the forest.

“That's the Battle Pyramid?” May gasped. “It's enormous!”

Ethan nodded. It was hard to imagine a building this size flying from place to place. He had no idea how Brandon had managed it.

What caught Ethan's attention more, though, were the ancient ruins beside it. Broken walls stood amid vines and moss. They looked centuries old. Wind and sun had worn the writing on their pillars and stones until it was almost illegible.

May noticed the ruins too and frowned. She was about to say something when the ground suddenly split beneath them. A hole opened, and both stepped into empty air before they could react.

May had no time even to scream. Something caught her falling body. When her eyes adjusted to the darkness, she saw Gengar holding her and Ethan suspended with an uncanny power.

“You two are impossible,” Gengar said with an exaggerated sigh. “Falling into a trap like that? Careless.”

It started to float them back toward the opening, but Ethan stopped it.

“It isn't a trap.” He looked down, then up. “Part of the ruins' underground structure gave way under our feet. It's been here too long. Take us down, Gengar.”

“Ruins?” Interested now, Gengar lowered them gently to the bottom. Within seven or eight seconds, Ethan and May were standing safely on solid ground.

Without help, a fall from that height might have left them scratched and bruised. The loose earth that fell with them would at least have cushioned the landing, so it probably would not have been deadly.

Once he was sure there was no other hidden danger, Ethan looked around. They were in a passage belonging to the ruins. Stone slabs covered the floor, murals marked both walls, and Pokémon statues stood at intervals. Like the broken structures aboveground, everything showed its age.

Then Ethan noticed an electric lamp farther down the passage. He and May looked at each other.

“Is someone down here?” May whispered. “Could an ancient Pokémon have survived all this time?”

“You're getting carried away.” Ethan shook his head. “The Battle Pyramid has been here for days, right beside the ruins. I'd guess its Frontier Brain is investigating them.”

“So we might meet him down here?”

“Who knows? It seems likely.”

They followed the passage toward the light. At a fork, they chose one route and kept walking. To guard against mechanisms or other hazards, Ethan released Gardevoir. With it and Gengar beside them, they could handle an emergency.

As they walked, Ethan took photographs of the writing and pictures on the walls. He could not read the inscriptions, but he could preserve them and send them to Professor Cerise. Cerise might have no use for them himself, yet he could pass them to other researchers. That way, Ethan could at least help someone study the ancient ruins.
