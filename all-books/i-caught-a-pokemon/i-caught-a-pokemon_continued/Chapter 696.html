“Lads, look who I've brought aboard!” Drake laughed to his assembled crew, drawing every eye to Ethan.

As Drake had said, the sailors had watched videos of Ethan's tournament matches. They had talked about him more than once. Several recognized his face immediately.

“No way! It's Ethan Vale!”

“What's he doing here?”

“He's in Hoenn?”

“Are you entering the Ever Grande Conference?”

They crowded around him, delighted and astonished. One sailor even touched his arm, as though checking whether he was real.

“He really is here!”

“Of course he is, idiot.”

“I know! I just can't believe I'm meeting him in person.”

Their excitement startled Misty. She knew Ethan's looks and his Pokémon made him popular with girls. Where had he acquired so many male fans? Ethan, too, had trouble with the attention. It reminded him of the enthusiastic biker gang he had met back in Kanto. If he had to be surrounded, he would have preferred a group of pretty girls to a circle of brawny sailors.

The man who had lowered the ladder stepped forward. “Welcome aboard. Will you be battling the captain?”

The other sailors perked up. A winner of two regional tournaments against a member of the Elite Four would be a match worth seeing.

Drake introduced the man. “This is Liangguang, my leading student and the ship's first mate. He's a reliable fellow. He sails with me and helps me hunt for treasure.”

“A pleasure.” Liangguang offered Ethan his hand. “I watched all your matches at the Indigo League and the Silver Conference. You're an exceptional Trainer. It's an honor to meet you.”

Ethan shook his hand. Liangguang was slightly tanned by the sun, but otherwise looked polished and carried himself calmly. It was easy to see why Drake trusted him as both student and first mate.

“You're too kind,” Ethan said. “I was only testing myself in those tournaments. It helped that I didn't meet too many opponents I couldn't handle.”

Drake watched their friendly introduction with satisfaction. He could deny it aloud, but he knew he was old and did not have endless years ahead. He had invited Ethan aboard because he liked him, and because he wanted Liangguang to know a capable Trainer who might be useful to him later. A man devoted to the sea could still plan sensibly for his student's future.

He waved the sailors back and cleared his throat. “There won't be a battle today, so stop pestering him. Ethan is my guest. Give him room to breathe.”

The crew obeyed. With Drake leading and Liangguang accompanying them, Ethan and Misty toured the deck. The five-masted ship felt steeped in history. Even Misty began to see some of its appeal.

They eventually went belowdecks. Away from the crew's curious stares, the four could speak more freely. Ethan learned that Drake and his ship would soon depart on another long voyage in search of unknown treasure.

Treasure hunting was Drake's chief pleasure between his duties with the Elite Four. Each return to land was often little more than preparation for the next departure. He truly lived for the sea.

The visit was pleasant, but Drake had a voyage ahead and Ethan had his own journey. After a little more than half an hour, Ethan rose to leave.

During their conversation, Drake had treated them to his special hot Moomoo Milk. It was sweet—astonishingly sweet. Ethan guessed he had added at least five heaping spoonfuls of sugar, far beyond what anyone needed. Beneath that sweetness, the milk itself had a rich flavor. Misty seemed to like it.

Only a kid would enjoy this much sugar. Ethan shook his head and forced himself to drain the cup. When he set it down, Drake looked satisfied. Liangguang wore a practiced, obliging smile. Being Drake's first mate might not always be easy.

...

Back on the dock, Ethan and Misty waved as Drake's ship spread its sails and left Lilycove Harbor. Their meeting with an Elite Four member had been a pleasant interruption to the trip.

Knowing Drake might make little practical difference. The world was vast, and the sea vaster. Unless Ethan arranged a formal challenge, meeting him by chance again would be like searching an ocean for a needle. Not everyone had Ash's knack for crossing paths with the same people.

They left the harbor and walked into Lilycove City. It was attractive, but it had no official Gym. They could stop briefly, then move on.

Signs, mascots, and advertisements lined the streets. Misty noticed a coming event. “There's a Pokémon Contest here soon! Maybe we'll see May.”

Ethan was studying the map on his phone as he walked. “She travels from Contest to Contest, so she probably won't miss this one. But it doesn't start for another day or two, and we aren't staying that long. I doubt we'll meet her.”

“True.” Misty looked disappointed. “I wonder how much she's improved.”

Ethan glanced at her. “You haven't talked privately? Didn't you exchange contact details?”

“We made a promise. She won't tell me how she's doing in advance. The next time we meet, she wants it to be a big surprise.”

That's an unusual kind of friendship, Ethan thought, and returned to the map.

He still needed two Gym Badges. The remaining Gyms lay in different directions: one far out at sea, the other on the mainland. Now that they had returned to land, the immediate choice was clear. They would go to Fortree City and challenge the Fortree Gym.

It would be a long walk from Lilycove, through a large Safari Zone. The journey would not be effortless. They visited a nearby shop to replenish their supplies. As they were about to set out, Ethan's phone rang.

It was Anabel. That can't be good. An International Police officer rarely called him without needing help. He answered.

“Ethan? I heard you're in Lilycove City,” Anabel said urgently.

“Who told you? Don't say you've had a satellite following me.”

“Drake told me.” She ignored the joke. “I called him just now to ask for help with an urgent matter, but he'd already sailed. He recommended you, and that was how I learned you were in Lilycove. If I'd known, I would have called you first.”

Drake told her? They had only just met and said goodbye. Already the captain had sent trouble Ethan's way. This was a fast return on a new friendship. Still, a man who loved the sea would hardly turn his ship around just after setting sail.

“We've helped each other before,” Ethan said. “Tell me what happened.”

“That's why you're our friend,” Anabel said, then grew serious. “Phoebe of the Elite Four has asked for help. Team Magma is after a remarkable stone on Mt. Pyre, like the one you stopped Team Aqua from using a few days ago. It's said to awaken Groudon and cause its Primal Reversion. We can't let Team Magma take it.”

Ethan considered the information. “If Phoebe asked the International Police, why can't you handle it yourselves? You have the resources.”

“We're still at sea,” Anabel admitted. “After helping you stop Team Aqua, we found several of their secret bases nearby. We're dealing with those and can't spare a force right now.”

So they had turned to Drake, only for the request to come around to Ethan.

“Another question,” he said. “Why is Phoebe at Mt. Pyre? Being in the Elite Four doesn't oblige her to guard two stones most people have never heard of.”

“You don't know? Her grandparents guard the mountain. They've always protected the stones. Team Aqua injured them when it stole the Blue Orb, so Phoebe came back to protect the remaining one in their place.”

That explained it. Ethan remembered the guardians from the games but had not known they were Phoebe's family.

“Then I need to meet Phoebe at Mt. Pyre and stop Team Magma.”

“Yes. I'll gather officers to support you as soon as I can. Until then, I'm counting on you.”

Ethan ended the call and shrugged helplessly at Misty. She covered her mouth and laughed. She had heard enough to know Fortree would have to wait.

“You stay busy,” she teased. “Your travels, that international company, criminal organizations, the International Police—apparently everyone needs you.”

“What can I say?” Ethan grinned. “The more capable you are, the more people ask of you.”

He considered stopping at the Pokémon Center to change his team before a possible fight. Then he remembered that he had Deoxys now. Who in Team Magma could defeat it? With Raikou and Suicune alongside him, his strength was at least comparable to what he had brought to the battle in which he caught Deoxys, perhaps greater.

He felt a surge of confidence. There was no need to visit the Center. He led Misty toward Mt. Pyre instead.

...

Mt. Pyre lay southwest of Lilycove, near the coast but tucked among remote, continuous hills. Most Trainers had heard of it, yet few had gone there. Those who had loved a Pokémon deeply might make the long journey after its death, crossing mountains and water to bury it there, hoping it could rest in peace—or simply to keep a place where they could remember it.
