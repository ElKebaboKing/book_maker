Deep in the mountains near Lavaridge Town, in southwestern Hoenn, a helicopter flew over the foothills of Mt. Chimney. Meteor Falls was not far away.

Ethan looked out the window. After challenging Lavaridge Gym, he had planned to leave these mountains behind. Yet here he was again so soon. Things rarely went as expected.

He remembered catching Beldum here, encountering Professor Cozmo after Team Magma and Team Aqua kidnapped him, and then meeting Zinnia of the Draconid people during their escape. It all felt as though it had happened yesterday.

After a while, the helicopter descended onto a stretch of level ground. The person in the copilot's seat turned and raised his voice over the rotor noise.

“The magnetic interference ahead is too strong. We can't fly any farther. You'll have to go on foot. Our people have set up an observation post farther along. Follow this path and meet them there first!”

Ethan nodded. He and Misty gathered their equipment, climbed down, and set off in the indicated direction. Behind them, the helicopter took off again and soon vanished into the distance. The roar of its rotors faded at last, giving their ears a welcome rest.

Unlike Mt. Chimney's bare main slopes, these foothills were green. Trees covered the mountains, grass grew underfoot, and wild Pokémon appeared from time to time.

They had not walked far when Ethan saw a few simple tents ahead. Several men in Devon security uniforms stood guard outside. This had to be the observation post.

As they approached, a man who appeared to be the team leader came to meet them. “Mr. Vale and Miss Misty?”

He already knew who they were. Headquarters had sent word of their arrival, along with their photographs. He knew, too, that Ethan's strength far exceeded that of anyone on his team.

When Ethan nodded, the leader said, “The meteorite is at the bottom of a small lake not far ahead. Would you like us to take you there?”

“No. There may be a battle. Leave it to us from here. Wait for me to contact you, then come and transport the meteorite.”

“Understood. Good luck, sir. Deoxys is extremely strong. It has injured a great many of our people, so please be careful.”

Ethan and Misty left the post and followed the path toward the lake. Almost at once, they sensed that something was wrong. It was too quiet.

Before the observation post, they had seen wild Pokémon. Here there were none, and not even a cry broke the silence.

For once, Gengar emerged from Ethan's shadow on its own. Lately it had been absorbed in manga, showing itself only now and then at mealtimes. Ethan had barely spoken to it in ages.

It still held a manga volume, but it was no longer reading. It frowned at the surroundings. “Something's wrong here. It makes my whole body feel uncomfortable.”

“Probably the magnetic field,” Ethan said. “Deoxys and its meteorite can alter it. Mr. Stone wouldn't have gone to all this trouble or asked me to help otherwise.”

Ahead, a clear lake lay quietly among patches of forest. Nothing about it looked unusual.

Gengar stared toward it for a long moment. “Be careful. I can feel a powerful presence up ahead. It should be Deoxys. I've felt something like this from only two other Pokémon.”

“Which ones?” Ethan asked.

“Mewtwo and Ho-Oh!”

“Mewtwo and Ho-Oh?” Misty's eyes widened. “Deoxys is that powerful?”

Gengar shook its head. “I mean the feeling it gives me, not necessarily its strength. It might have something to do with the magnetic field.”

Ethan said nothing as he considered the warning. The movies had shown him how formidable Deoxys could be. It had even gained the upper hand for a time against Rayquaza—what Ethan jokingly thought of as Caterpie's ultimate evolution.

Deoxys could switch freely among Normal, Attack, Defense, and Speed Formes. It was highly intelligent, could alter the magnetic field around it, fly, dive into the sea, and regenerate at astonishing speed. Its powers were outrageous. An ordinary Pokémon would have little chance against it. That was why Ethan had changed his team before leaving Devon.

As they neared the lake, he sent out Gardevoir as a precaution. With her and Gengar beside them, he could at least be confident that he and Misty would be safe.

To his surprise, they reached the shore without being attacked.

Ethan checked his phone. There was no signal, and the battery was draining rapidly. Only seventeen percent remained. Kennedy's special communicator had been affected too, though it could still carry a broken conversation. Ethan had no idea what material it was made from.

Misty glanced from side to side. “What do we do now?” she asked softly. She looked as if she expected Deoxys to leap out at them at any moment.

“Kennedy said the meteorite fell into this lake. I think Deoxys is hiding here too.”

Misty studied the water. Pieces of a boat or aircraft drifted on its surface. They must be the remains of Devon's equipment, destroyed in the earlier attempts to retrieve the meteorite. Those fights had been disastrous, almost a complete rout.

“How will you lure it out?” she asked.

“Lure it?” Ethan shook his head and drew a Poké Ball from his pouch. “I'm going to force it out.”

He aimed the ball over the lake. “Come out, Gyarados!”

A flash of blue light revealed a gigantic red Gyarados above the water. For this mission, Ethan had brought back some of his strongest Pokémon. Once Kennedy told him the meteorite had landed in a lake, he knew there might be a battle in the water. He had contacted Chloe and asked her to transfer his red Gyarados to him.

He had brought other Pokémon too, but there was no need to reveal them yet.

Misty knew Gyarados belonged to Ethan and would obey him, but the sight of the terrifying giant still made her uneasy. It was simply so big.

Ethan had no attention to spare for her fear. He met Gyarados's red eyes and swept his right hand downward.

“Do what you do best. Stir up the lake and drive that thing out!”

Gyarados roared and plunged headfirst beneath the surface. Great waves rolled outward. At first they spread in regular circles, but as Gyarados churned the water below, the lake began to heave wildly. Nothing living down there could avoid the disturbance.

Ethan and Misty saw a red light flash beneath the surface. Then came a second, and a third. Water exploded upward. An orange-red, humanoid Pokémon flew out first, while Gyarados's vast jaws followed close behind, snapping at it.

The newcomer moved too quickly. Gyarados's bite closed on empty air.

“Is that...?” Misty hurriedly aimed her Pokédex at it.

“Deoxys, the DNA Pokémon. Psychic type. No further data available.”

So it was Deoxys.

Up close, it hardly looked like a Pokémon from Earth. Its orange-red body had an alien shape. Its arms twisted like strands of DNA, and its blue-green face resembled a mask. White circular eyes sat within rectangular black sockets. A crystalline structure ran through its chest, where a round, jewel-like core was embedded.

Misty found it frightening. It reminded her of the hunters in science fiction films: beings from an advanced civilization that regarded humans as prey and cared nothing for their lives.

She turned to Ethan, about to speak, and found his eyes shining with excitement.

As soon as Deoxys appeared, Ethan reached into his pouch again. Three Great Balls filled his hand. He tossed them high and called, “Come out, my Pokémon!”

Three large birds appeared in the sky: one red, one blue, and one yellow. They were Moltres, Articuno, and Zapdos.

Ethan had brought out the first of his hidden trump cards to face Deoxys. Before setting out, he had contacted Sabrina and had the three Legendary birds transferred back to him, just as Chloe had sent Gyarados.

The birds flew in wide circles overhead. Even while still inside their balls after the transfer, they had sensed their Trainer's purpose. Now they could feel the power of the unfamiliar Pokémon in the air with them. They recognized their target at once, and the prospect of battling it fired their fighting spirit.

On the ground, Ethan watched those familiar shapes and let out a long breath. His blood was stirring in a way it had not for some time.

He fixed his gaze on Deoxys. “Fly! Dance! Let's defeat it!”

At his signal, all three birds cried out and turned toward Deoxys. Zapdos loosed lightning. Moltres breathed fire. Articuno fired an Ice Beam. Powerful attacks of three different types converged on Deoxys from three directions.

Deoxys hovered in place, its cold eyes on the approaching birds. From their magnetic fields, it could tell they were unlike ordinary Pokémon. That was all the notice it gave them.

It raised its arms across its chest. A spherical barrier sprang up around it and easily stopped all three attacks.

Then its DNA-like arms drew together. A great ball of energy formed between them and shot toward Moltres, flying in the middle. Moltres could not dodge in time. The ball struck it head-on and exploded in a blinding flash.

Moltres was wounded, but strong enough to endure the blow. Furious now, the Fire-type broke away from the others. With Agility, it accelerated in an instant and lunged at Deoxys, claws extended. Articuno and Zapdos attacked from the sides to cover it.

Deoxys remained calm. Its barrier stopped the two supporting attacks. Then it changed from Normal Forme to Attack Forme, its arms dividing into four. As Moltres closed in, Deoxys slipped aside and lashed it with an arm like a whip.

Moltres was injured again.

The brief exchange made one thing clear: Deoxys was stronger than any one of the Legendary birds. But Ethan had not sent out all three so they could take turns fighting it alone.

They were too far away to hear him directly, so he had Gardevoir carry his command by telepathy. “Don't get caught up fighting it one at a time. Use Tri-Bird Assault!”

The combined technique of three Legendary Pokémon could deliver devastating force. The birds did not hesitate. They climbed higher, circling in wide, upward spirals.

A chill touched Misty. She looked up, and the sky had suddenly darkened. Clouds had gathered from nowhere, covering most of it in a thick black mass.

Her bangs stirred lightly.

The wind was rising...
