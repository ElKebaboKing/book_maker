The semifinals were over. Ethan and Jon Dickson had reached the final, exactly as most people had predicted.

Each had dominated the other Trainers in his half of the bracket.

Pokémon commentators boasted about their accurate forecasts on television. Ordinary spectators found that amusing. Even people who rarely watched a match could have guessed the finalists from what they had shown before the semifinals.

What made those commentators experts? They had said what everyone else was thinking.

The final was scheduled for the day after tomorrow. The date had been set long ago, and it would be the last battle of this Silver Conference.

Whoever won would be the champion.

The day between matches gave spectators more time to anticipate the final. It also let both Trainers study their last opponent more closely.

Ethan, however, intended to take the match as it came. He had already studied what he needed to know and saw no reason to wear himself out thinking about it again. He had a limited team to choose from; Jon could worry about which Pokémon he would send.

The Pokémon League increased its promotion for the final. Advertisements were everywhere, pushing public interest to its peak.

As people compared the finalists, Jon's two early losses in his semifinal became a subject of argument.

Some said he had no chance against Ethan. His side of the bracket had been weaker; had Ash or Harrison been placed there, Jon might not even have reached the final.

Others insisted Jon would win. They believed the League could not allow the same Trainer to take two regional titles in succession and would surely arrange something behind the scenes.

Online opinions went in every direction, but about eighty percent still expected Ethan to become champion.

The rest day arrived amid all that talk.

More spectators poured into Silver Town. Tickets for tomorrow's final were nearly impossible to get, and scalpers charged outrageous prices.

Several more of Ethan's friends arrived to support him, joining Ash and his group.

Professor Elm came first. After arranging his work, he had found two days free and brought his wife and child to watch the final in person.

The Kimono Sisters' arrival was more unexpected. The five were public figures in Johto, and people followed what they did. By coming to the tournament, they risked being recognized.

Ethan was pleased to see them all. He booked a private room at a Japanese restaurant and invited both groups to dinner.

Although they all lived in Johto, a scholar like Professor Elm had no reason to know performers like the Kimono Sisters. The sisters were easy to get along with, though, and Ash and his friends helped keep the conversation moving. Soon everyone was comfortable together.

Professor Elm's wife might have been the one exception. Seeing so many beautiful women in one room made her a little jealous, even though none showed any interest in her husband.

"Mr. Ethan," Miki, the eldest sister, said as she raised her cup. "I hope you win tomorrow and take the championship!"

Perhaps to avoid recognition, the sisters wore ordinary clothes rather than their familiar kimonos. They had also left off their heavy stage makeup. From a distance they looked like five attractive young women out for dinner.

"I hope so too. Thank you for coming so far to cheer me on!"

Ethan drank his sake. Truthfully, he did not care for its taste. He would have preferred beer or a cocktail, but neither suited this dinner as well.

"Sister," Zuki said from across the table. She smiled at Miki but spoke with pointed seriousness. "Mr. Ethan is an outsider. Jon Dickson is the local Trainer from Johto. Are you sure you're cheering for the right person?"

Her words had a sharp edge. Ethan glanced at her.

Zuki was the sister who had battled him in Ecruteak City. Even now, he had the feeling she was singling him out.

Sensing the change in mood, Kuni covered her mouth and laughed softly before Miki could answer. "Mr. Ethan is our friend. Of course we can cheer for a friend."

Naoko agreed. "Besides, this isn't a battle between regions. The championship belongs to an individual Trainer."

Zuki rolled her eyes at them. She had not expected her sisters to take Ethan's side.

The conversation moved on. Apart from Zuki's odd attention toward Ethan, everyone got along well. Erika, whose manner suited theirs, quickly became close to all five sisters.

But then Miki and Kuni exchanged a glance.

Neither was married, yet both could see that Erika and Ethan were closer than when they had last met. Warmth filled Erika's eyes whenever she looked at him. There was a new softness about her, too.

Together, the signs suggested that she and Ethan had recently become intimate.

The sisters saw the same surprise in each other's eyes. They liked Erika and thought of her as a traditional woman. They had not expected her to sleep with Ethan before marriage.

On reflection, it was hardly unbelievable. In the sisters' view, pure friendship between a man and a woman was rare unless neither had looks nor money. Ethan and Erika had traveled alone together; it would have been more surprising if nothing had changed.

Had they not known how close he was to Erika, the sisters might have been drawn to him themselves.

...

That night, after making love, Ethan lay in bed with Erika in his arms.

The room was warm from their exertion. Moisture clouded the windows, and a distinct scent lingered in the air.

They leaned against the pillows together, enjoying the quiet afterward.

Breathing in the scent of her hair, Ethan asked casually, "What did Miki and Kuni want to talk to you about after dinner?"

Professor Elm and his family, along with the Kimono Sisters, had returned to their respective hotels after the gathering. Before leaving, the five sisters had drawn Erika aside. Ethan had seen them laughing together but had been too far away to hear.

"Nothing important. Just things women talk about. You wouldn't be interested." Erika smiled. "They invited me to visit them after the tournament, though."

She hesitated, then added, "Actually... Zuki isn't trying to pick on you. She just..."

"She just what?" Ethan asked lazily.

"She admires you."

"What?" Ethan coughed and stared at her. "Admires me? Are you joking? Is giving me trouble over and over her way of showing it?"

Erika hid a laugh behind her hand. "Some proud girls do act like that. It's how they try to get a man's attention."

"Seriously?"

Ethan frowned. Zuki certainly was proud, and most people probably could not catch her interest. But deciding that her barbs were meant to attract him seemed awfully presumptuous.

Erika did not look as though she was joking. Could it be true?

Then he looked down at her bright eyes and had a sudden thought.

Was she testing him? Did she mean something else by bringing this up?

He cleared his throat and shrugged with studied indifference. "Who cares? We can't control how other people act. If you want to visit the sisters after the tournament, we'll go. As for Zuki, I'll keep my distance."

...

Salutes boomed and fireworks burst over Silver Town. Colorful flags fluttered around Silver Stadium, where the sacred flame burned and a packed crowd filled the stands with cheers.

"Everyone, after a long Silver Conference, the final has arrived!" The announcer's voice rang with its usual energy. "Two Trainers have fought through round after round to stand here today. Now they'll give everything they have for the honor of becoming champion!"

After warming up the crowd and introducing the finalists, the announcer welcomed them to the field. Ethan and Jon Dickson walked out of their respective tunnels, both wearing serious expressions.

They shook hands in the center, then went to their Trainer areas.

Pokémon League President Charles Goodshow gave a brief opening speech. Ethan paid little attention. He studied his opponent instead.

Jon was about Ethan's age. He had brown hair and wore a T-shirt and shorts. His looks were unremarkable, but his strength showed in his confident smile.

Ethan had heard Jon came from Cherry Town in Johto, known for its large, plump cherries and the bright blossoms that covered the town each spring.

In the waiting room, Erika was no longer alone. Ash, Misty, Brock, Professor Elm's family, and the Kimono Sisters sat with her, watching the big screen.

Every stadium ticket had sold out before they could get one. Ethan had brought his friends and family here instead. It lacked the atmosphere of the stands, but they had soft sofas, cool air, and fruit to eat.

Miki looked at Ash. "Mr. Ethan will win this match, won't he?"

She performed onstage and had an Eevee of her own, but knew little about competitive battles. Professor Elm's family looked to Ash as well. They wanted a Trainer's judgment rather than another guess.

"Of course," Ash said with conviction. "Apart from people like Lance, Ethan is the strongest Trainer I've met. I don't think anyone can beat him!"

Miki and the Elms relaxed a little. They had traveled far to support Ethan. No one wanted to watch him lose.

Only Zuki stood apart with her arms folded, leaning against a wall. As she watched Ethan on the screen, the faintest smile touched her face.

On the field, the opening formalities finally ended.

"We'll now draw today's battlefield and decide who sends out first!"

The rules were the same as in the earlier tournament rounds.

Ethan and Jon watched the screen. The pointer spun past Grass, Rock, Ice, and Water before slowing to a stop on the Water Field.

The next draw gave Ethan the second choice again.

Overall, he could hardly complain. Jon's ace was Rapidash, and the Water Field would restrict a Fire-type Pokémon. By sending out second, Ethan could also choose a good response to Jon's opener. Both draws seemed to favor him.

"Jon Dickson, on the green side, send out your first Pokémon!" the referee called.

The final was underway. Jon took a deep breath, lifted the Poké Ball he had already prepared, and threw it toward the center.

"Go, Rhyhorn!"

Blue light flashed. A sturdy Rhyhorn, built like an armored vehicle, appeared on the field.

Ethan had watched footage of Jon's matches. He knew this Rhyhorn had helped eliminate many competitors with its sheer strength. It had left an impression on him.

Sending it first showed Jon wanted an early win and hoped to seize the momentum of the match.
