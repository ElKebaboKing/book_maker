Ethan and Sabrina followed the narrow passage to the deepest part of the cave.

It opened into a vast chamber crowded with crystal formations, like a maze made of glass. Sunlight slipped through a crack overhead and bounced between the crystals until the whole place was almost as bright as day.

"If I ever run out of money, I'll come back and mine this place," Ethan said. "I'd be rich in no time."

Sabrina rolled her eyes. "Former champion forced by poverty to work as a miner. That headline would shock everyone."

She rarely made jokes like that. Perhaps spending so much time with Ethan had rubbed off on her.

He laughed, put away his flashlight, and looked toward the middle of the chamber. A stream no more than a few inches wide ran steadily into a small underground lake. The water was clear and cold. He could feel the damp chill before he even reached it.

Ethan scanned the cavern but saw no Crystal Onix. The lake showed no movement beneath its surface, only faint ripples above.

"Has it gone out to feed?" he wondered. "Or did someone catch it before we got here?"

"No." Sabrina pointed to a place in the water. "I can sense something there. I don't know how it's hidden, but it's probably the Pokémon we came for."

"She's right," Haunter said. "There's a Pokémon concealing itself there."

Sabrina and Gardevoir had psychic powers, and Haunter was skilled at sensing what others could not see. With all three beside him, an ordinary hiding place was unlikely to fool them.

Ethan remembered that the Crystal Onix's unusual body allowed it to hide in water. He took his Gengar mask from his backpack and put it on.

The mask had been fitted with the Silph Scope. It could reveal things that were invisible, though Ethan had not had a chance to use it until now.

He looked where Sabrina had pointed. There, floating at the lake's surface and watching them coldly, was an enormous Pokémon seven or eight meters long.

Ethan could see how it had hidden. Like the Vaporeon he had encountered in Stone Town, which seemed to merge with water, this Onix's clear, glasslike body bent the light around it. Against the ripples of the lake, it had become almost invisible.

"It's here. Everyone, get ready!"

The Onix appeared to realize it had been found. It rose from the water, revealing its whole form to Sabrina and Ethan's Pokémon.

"So that's the Crystal Onix?" Sabrina gazed at it. "It's beautiful. I didn't think such a strange Pokémon really existed."

Apart from its crystal body, it looked like an ordinary Onix: a horn on its head and a huge body made of rounded, stone-like segments. The crystal mine must have had something to do with its unusual form.

Ethan took off his mask. He glanced at Haunter and Gardevoir, considered them, then reached for the Poké Balls at his waist. He was curious about this Onix's type. Before catching it, he wanted to test what it could do.

"Jolteon, Blastoise, Venusaur—come out!"

Three Pokémon appeared in flashes of blue light. Already wary, the Crystal Onix gave an angry roar.

No Pokémon liked strangers entering its home. Outnumbered by people and Pokémon, though, it did not rush into an attack.

"Interesting," Ethan said. "We've found its home, and it's angry, but it hasn't fled or struck first. I thought it might have Gyarados's temper. It seems steadier than that."

If the Onix would not move, he would test it himself. He signaled to Jolteon. "Use Thunder Shock!"

Jolteon's stiff fur crackled, and electricity struck the Crystal Onix in the lake. Current spread through the water with a sharp hiss and a haze of white vapor. When the air cleared, the Onix appeared unharmed.

Sabrina understood the test. "It took no damage from an Electric-type move, even while it was in water. Like other Onix, it seems to have a Ground type."

Ethan nodded. The Onix finally lost patience and fought back. It opened its mouth and roared. Rocks formed in the air and fell toward them.

Rock Throw. "So is it still a Ground and Rock type?" Ethan asked. If so, its type was no different from an ordinary Onix's.

He did not want to decide too quickly. He would keep fighting to see what else it could do.

Before Sabrina had to act, Gardevoir raised both arms and held the falling rocks in midair.

"Good work, Gardevoir! Blastoise, use Water Gun!"

A thick stream hit the Crystal Onix. A Ground and Rock Pokémon should have been especially vulnerable to water, but it barely seemed to feel the attack.

Resistance to Water-type moves? Ethan nodded again.

He had suspected as much from the Onix's ability to live in the lake. Now Water Gun had confirmed it. To Ethan, the results suggested it still had the Ground and Rock typing of an ordinary Onix, while the density of its crystal body gave it a special resistance to water.
