Courtney still had her Camerupt, but this time she had also released a Weezing. One fought on the ground and the other in the air, working together.

The other Team Magma elites used the same kinds of Pokémon as their ordinary members, but theirs were noticeably stronger. Their arrival lifted the morale of the grunts, whose earlier attacks had failed to break through. Ethan had to devote most of his attention to stopping them.

Through Gardevoir's telepathy, he sent precise orders to his Pokémon.

Charizard, drive those Golbat overhead away with Flamethrower. Don't let them attack from above.

Blastoise, switch from Water Gun to Hydro Pump. Aim at the Camerupt. They're slower and easier to hit.

Jolteon, use your speed to draw the Mightyena after you.

Suicune, use your strongest attacks wherever the enemy Pokémon are thickest.

And Gengar, stop holding back. Show them what you can really do!

His Pokémon moved into their assigned roles. With Misty's Pokémon helping, they barely held Team Magma's charge. The line shifted back and forth, but they kept the fighting near the foot of the peak where Ethan stood.

A stalemate was not what he wanted. He turned to Gardevoir. “Go down and help them.”

She hesitated. Ethan understood why and laughed. “Don't worry. Psyduck is still here. I'm sure it can protect us.”

Psyduck? Gardevoir gave the vacant duck a cool glance. It stood there looking at her with its head tilted. She could not bring herself to entrust Ethan's safety to it. Her worry reached him through telepathy.

Ethan's smile faded. He patted her shoulder. “It's all right. I still have one more card to play. If things turn bad, I'll take care of myself.”

She did not trust Psyduck any more than before, but she reluctantly turned and joined the battle. Her arrival could not reverse the situation on its own. It did ease the pressure on the Pokémon at the front.

The enemy still had an overwhelming advantage in numbers. No matter how well Ethan's Pokémon fought, they could not cover every gap.

Courtney and two elite grunts broke through. They climbed the peak and came face-to-face with Ethan.

When Misty saw them, she pulled Psyduck in front of her and stepped before Ethan. You've always looked after me and protected me, Ethan. Today it's my turn.

She faced the hooded girl with purple hair. “You won't hurt Ethan while I'm here!”

Courtney gave Misty no more than a glance. Her eyes paused for a second on Psyduck, then moved past them to Ethan.

“Why go to all this trouble?” Ethan asked Courtney, shaking his head. Despite the enemy reaching him, he remained remarkably calm. “Team Aqua is surrounding Deoxys. Aren't they your sworn rivals? Are you really going to ignore them?”

“Them?” Courtney's mouth twisted in a cold smile. “Shelly thinks she's clever. She believes one Pokémon can change everything. I know better. People like you are the greatest obstacle to Team Magma's goal.”

She had seen the problem clearly. Finding Groudon would be difficult, but if Team Magma searched carefully and followed every clue, they would find it eventually, provided it still lived. What truly hindered them were powerful Trainers who repeatedly interfered with their plans. Each intervention made success harder and failure more likely. Removing Ethan was the wiser priority.

Ethan shrugged. “Then I have one last question. Were you hiding here waiting for me to show up?”

It was a reasonable concern. Devon had asked him to help recover the meteorite only an hour or two ago, yet both teams had arrived with substantial forces and seemed ready for him. Had they learned where he was going, assembled their people, and located him that quickly? If so, something was badly wrong. There might even be an informant inside Devon.

Courtney did not answer. She was close enough to attack him now; she had no reason to explain herself. At her signal, the two elite grunts ordered their Pokémon forward too.

“Psyduck, stop them!” Misty shouted.

Psyduck tilted its head and did nothing, as though it had not understood a word.

Its inaction did not make it safe. It stood between the attackers and their target, so Team Magma's Pokémon chose it first. Several streams of fire surged toward Psyduck.

It might be foolish, but it could recognize danger. Psyduck dodged frantically. One stream still caught its tail. As a Water-type, it took only half the usual damage from the flames, but the burn hurt enough to send it running in circles with its tail clutched in its hands.

Courtney and the grunts stared. On their way up the peak, they had seen how powerful the Pokémon defending Ethan were. How could this one be so helpless? Did it really belong to the same side?

Whatever the explanation, Courtney welcomed it. How could such a weak Pokémon protect anyone?

Misty grew desperate. “Psyduck, pull yourself together! Use Confusion!”

Psyduck heard none of it. It kept racing around, holding its tail.

I'm such an idiot, Misty thought. Why did I trust Psyduck? I shouldn't have brought it on this journey. Regret was useless now. She had no other Pokémon left with her.

Ethan had never depended on Psyduck to keep him safe. He watched the Team Magma members close in and touched his wrist, ready to speak. Then he heard a sound behind him.

As he turned, a blue, winged Pokémon with four limbs rushed past. It slammed headfirst into a Mightyena that had been about to attack Ethan and sent it flying.

“What?”

Ethan was startled. Courtney frowned and looked sharply past him. A black-haired girl in a cape braced her hand on a rock and vaulted out from behind it.

Ethan and Misty recognized her at once: Zinnia of the Draconid people, whom they had met near Meteor Falls.

So she was here too. Why?

Ethan finally got a clear look at the Pokémon that had struck Mightyena. It was a Salamence, surely Zinnia's.

“Zinnia!” Misty's face brightened. “Did you come to save us?”

To Misty, they had been on the verge of being overwhelmed. Zinnia's timely arrival felt like a rescue.

Zinnia hurried to their side. She first directed Salamence to drive back several Pokémon pressing in on them, then turned to Ethan and Misty.

“I've been here for a while. I was watching you from behind that rock.” She studied Ethan with amazement. “You kept a lot hidden. I saw Raikou last time, but I never imagined you had this many Legendary Pokémon.”

Born and raised in these mountains, Zinnia was evidently good at hiding. Ethan had not noticed her at all.

“Everyone has secrets.” He lowered his right hand from his left wrist and smiled at her. “Why are you here? You didn't come just to watch, did you?”

Zinnia admired his composure. Psyduck was the only Pokémon left beside him, and the rest were fighting at the front. Yet he showed no fear with enemies so close. Where did his confidence come from?

Perhaps she had stepped in too soon. If she had waited a little longer, she might have discovered whether Ethan was bluffing or had another way to turn the fight around. But she could not have watched silently while he and Misty were attacked.

“Of course not,” she said. The thoughts passed quickly, and she looked Ethan in the eye. “You don't really think Devon was the first to find that meteorite, do you?”

Ethan understood what she was suggesting. “You found it?”

Zinnia did not answer immediately. She glanced at Salamence, still battling Team Magma. It was strong enough to hold its own against Courtney's Camerupt and the two elite grunts' Pokémon together. Satisfied, she turned back to him.

“I found it three days ago, when it fell here. I saw the powerful Pokémon that came with it too.”

Ethan knew she meant Deoxys, so he waited for her to continue.

“Remember when we talked about Rayquaza in that cave?”

“Of course.” Ethan knew more about Rayquaza than Zinnia realized.

Zinnia drew a breath. “After that Pokémon appeared, Rayquaza came. It dropped out of the sky and fought it. Their battle shook the mountains and frightened away all the wild Pokémon nearby.”

Now Ethan understood the unnatural silence beyond Devon's observation post. The wild Pokémon had fled the area three days earlier.

“What happened next?” Misty asked.

“Both were injured. The other Pokémon went back into the water and fought Devon's people the next day. Rayquaza returned to the sky, and I haven't seen it since.”

Ethan realized why Zinnia had stayed. As one of the Draconid people, she wanted to see Rayquaza again, perhaps even speak with it. She had been waiting here in hope of its return. Instead she had watched Ethan and Misty arrive.

Misty swallowed. “Do you think Rayquaza will really come back?”

“It will!” Zinnia nodded firmly. “It's territorial, and it doesn't let a defeat go. It left wounded. Once it heals, it'll return for revenge.”

Ethan could not say whether Rayquaza would return. It might, or it might not. Zinnia, though, needed to believe it would. For the Draconid people, this might be the closest they had come to Rayquaza in hundreds of years. Her chance to meet it could come only once in her lifetime.

Misty saw the sudden light in Zinnia's eyes and wisely kept her question to herself: Would you try to catch Rayquaza if it came?

Rayquaza and the Draconid people's plans were not her concern. She wanted Ethan and herself to survive this battle. Legendary Pokémon were beyond her ability to catch anyway.

Ethan glanced at Courtney nearby. “What about these people? When did they arrive?”

“Team Magma and Team Aqua?” Zinnia shook her head. “They got lucky. They've been roaming these mountains since you rescued Professor Cozmo. They noticed the fight between Rayquaza and that Pokémon a few days ago, so they came to see what they might find.”
