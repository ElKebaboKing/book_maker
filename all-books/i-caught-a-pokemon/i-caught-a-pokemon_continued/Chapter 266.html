The sky was clear. Warm sunlight fell across Pummelo Stadium. At ten o'clock, the announcer's excited voice rang through the arena.

"Ladies and gentlemen, thank you for waiting! Welcome to the Orange League Winner's Cup! I'm your announcer for today's match. I know many of you arrived early, just as eager as I am to see this contest. The moment we've all been waiting for is finally here!"

As at the Indigo League, the announcer knew how to warm up a crowd. A few more remarks sent the spectators' excitement soaring. Then he turned to the entrances.

"Now let's welcome the pride of the Orange League, our undefeated Supreme Gym Leader—Drake!"

A man in a black vest walked slowly out of the competitors' tunnel, his arms bare and his expression resolute. The stands erupted. The cheers swelled until they seemed to strike Ethan even through the stone walls.

When they finally subsided a little, the announcer raised his voice again. "And now, please welcome the Indigo League champion—Ethan Vale!"

In the tunnel, Ethan heard his name. He took a deep breath and rose. "It's our turn."

Haunter poked its head out of his shadow and floated beside him with a grin. "I've been waiting for this! They keep saying how strong that man is. Let's see which of us has the last laugh."

Trainer and Pokémon walked out through the stone-lined tunnel and into the stadium. A great cheer greeted Ethan too. It was not even half as loud as Drake's welcome, but the Orange Islanders were showing their respect to the Indigo League champion.

One spectator reacted differently. On the giant screen, a man past fifty saw Ethan's face and gaped. It was the fruit vendor who had wagered with him the day before.

"How can it be him?" The man rubbed his eyes. He had never imagined that the young customer would turn out to be Drake's challenger. His thoughts tangled together as he tried to make sense of it.

Following the match procedure, Ethan and Drake met at the center of the field and shook hands. Each studied the other. Drake was about Ethan's height, though broader and more solidly built. Years beneath the Orange Islands sun had given him a healthy bronze complexion. The determined set of his face added to his commanding presence.

Ethan noticed a necklace made from a Poké Ball hanging at Drake's throat. He guessed it held Dragonite, the man's ace.

Drake spoke first. "Welcome to the Orange Islands. Ever since I heard you were challenging the Winner's Cup, I've looked forward to facing you."

"I've looked forward to it too," Ethan said.

The announcer could not hear their words, but the sight of them together was enough. "What a meeting! The Orange League's strongest Trainer stands face-to-face with the Indigo League champion. Which of them will win? We're about to find out!"

Drake's fans waved signs and shouted his name. They expected a close battle, but were certain he would win. Some local women in the crowd, seeing Ethan for the first time, found themselves less certain about whom to cheer for. They had come to support Drake, yet the visiting champion was strikingly handsome. Perhaps they could give him a little encouragement too.

After their brief exchange, the Trainers returned to their command areas. Drake, as the defending Trainer, took the red side. Ethan stood on the green side.

The plain field split apart beneath them, revealing a rocky battlefield with a round pool at its center and smooth stones scattered around it.

The referee raised a flag to start the match. Drake immediately threw a Poké Ball.

"Go, Ditto!"

Blue light flashed, and a pale-purple Pokémon appeared before Ethan. Its small eyes peered out of a soft, elastic body that looked like a puddle of putty.

"There it is!" the announcer shouted. "Drake's Ditto has defeated many challengers over the years. How will Ethan Vale handle it?"

Ditto, just as expected. Drake had kept to his usual opening. Ethan had spent the night thinking through a response.

Ditto could copy an opponent's cellular structure in an instant, rearranging its own body to take on the same appearance and abilities. Battling one meant facing a version of your own Pokémon.

That would trouble most Trainers, but Ethan had two Pokémon whose imitation did not worry him. The first was Haunter. Even if Ditto became a Haunter with matching types and abilities, Ethan's partner had lived for more than two thousand years. Its battle experience and other powers would give it an easy advantage. Ditto could never reproduce the strange wind Haunter wielded, because it was not a Pokémon move.

As for the other...

Ethan threw a Poké Ball. "The first match is yours. Go, Gyarados!"

"Rrrroar!"

A flash of blue light revealed the enormous Gyarados. Its arrival showed the spectators in the stadium and watching on television what sheer presence looked like. Its roar drowned out the cheers of more than twenty thousand people.
