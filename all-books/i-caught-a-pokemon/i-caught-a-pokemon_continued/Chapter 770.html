Dragonite flew above the pool while Metagross hovered over a rock. Despite fighting on the Water Field, neither Trainer had chosen a Water-type Pokémon. That was unusual in a match this important. Most competitors seized every advantage the field offered.

Tyson had not used a Water type from the start, so his choice was less surprising. Ethan, though, had just watched his uninjured Blastoise perform brilliantly on this very field. Why switch it out?

Only Ash and the others who had known Ethan since Kanto recognized the habit. Unless he had a particular reason to do otherwise, Ethan changed Pokémon after each battle.

There were benefits and costs. Rotating gave every member of his team practical experience. It also exposed his lineup early, robbing him of the element of surprise. A Trainer needed immense confidence in his Pokémon to fight that way, and Ethan had always been such a Trainer.

Metagross and Dragonite were already exchanging attacks. Like Blastoise before it, Metagross opened from a distance. It used Confusion, trying to defeat Dragonite before it could cross the field.

Dragonite was no Sceptile. It had an answer. Calmly, it used Twister. Wind and water rose together, forming a waterspout in the middle of the pool. Hidden from Metagross behind the churning column, Dragonite plunged into the water.

Only then did many spectators remember that Dragonite loved water. It often lived beside it. Even its earlier forms, Dratini and Dragonair, spent much of each day in it. Dragonite could fly above the field and swim beneath it. Apart from lacking pure Water-type attacks such as Water Gun and Hydro Pump, it could use this arena almost like a Water Pokémon.

The crowd's oversight was understandable. Dragonite was rare, and its earlier forms rarer still. Most people knew the species only from videos or glimpses of other tournaments. Experienced Trainers such as Tyson and Ash had faced Dragonite during their travels and knew more about how it fought.

With Dragonite out of sight, Tyson would not send Metagross into the pool after it. He ordered Metagross higher, away from the waterspout and the surface, guarding against an attack from below.

But Dragonite did not come from below. It burst straight out of the waterspout, both fists burning, and closed on Metagross in the blink of an eye.

Tyson and Metagross had fixed their attention on the water. Neither expected Dragonite to emerge from the spinning column. Now its speed decided the exchange. The dozen or so meters between them vanished. Droplets from the waterspout spattered Metagross's body; it barely had time to move its eyes before a flaming fist struck.

Bang!

Fire was strong against Steel. With Dragonite's momentum behind it, Fire Punch dealt a heavy blow. Flames streamed beyond Metagross and burned for a moment in the air behind it.

The crowd gasped. The sight of Dragonite bursting through the water with a burning fist aimed straight at its opponent was hard to ignore. Dragonite could use moves of many types, even if it did not specialize in all of them. That versatility had put a close-range Fire-type move—Fire Punch—at its disposal.

Metagross's body glowed red from the heat. It lost its balance and slammed into a rock.

After a stunned pause, the announcer found his voice. “What a creative attack! We thought Ethan used Twister to block Metagross's Confusion. Instead, Dragonite hid inside the waterspout and struck from the last direction Tyson expected!”

In the stands, Morrison swallowed. He had not expected that either. Dragonite might have made the Twister itself, but entering it after it formed would still hurt. What sort of madman planned an attack like that?

Ash, on the other hand, hardly looked surprised. Morrison's understanding of Ethan shifted again. Ethan did not fight by fixed rules. Two exchanges into the match, Morrison had gone from favoring Tyson to doubting his own prediction.

Tyson had more than surprise to contend with. He gave a wry smile. Now he understood Team Rocket's Meowth a little better. He had raised his guard, but he had still underestimated Ethan.

From the opposite side of the field, Ethan's strength and the strength of his Pokémon were impossible to miss. Their defense offered no opening, and Ethan's unexpected attacks left Tyson little room to answer. He had never felt so thoroughly outmatched. Where had Ethan trained Pokémon that could both attack and defend this well? How did he command them so precisely?

The ease Tyson usually felt against other Trainers had vanished. Facing Ethan, he felt inexperienced, almost like a beginner. For a moment, he had the absurd impression that he had already lost, though they had barely begun.

He drew a deep breath and forced himself to settle down. An unusually bad start had made him nervous, and that was magnifying the pressure. Ethan was the strongest Trainer Tyson had met on his journey, but Tyson was strong too. He had beaten plenty of opponents. His most trusted Pokémon had not even taken the field yet. There was still a way back.

Besides, like Morrison, Tyson could not believe every Pokémon Ethan brought would be this strong. Even the Elite Four could not guarantee that all their Pokémon were trained to the same exceptional level. Surely an ordinary Trainer could not.

Metagross rose from the broken rock. Fire Punch had hurt it, but it still had plenty of strength. Better yet, the blow had not burned it. Tyson's eyes hardened. This battle had only just begun. All six of his Pokémon were still available. He would give everything he had, as if this were the final.

Across the field, Ethan knew nothing of Tyson's thoughts. But he saw the change. Tyson had been serious before; now his gaze was sharper and his bearing stronger. Dragonite's punch seemed to have shaken him fully awake. Perhaps he understood the danger and meant to fight at a hundred and twenty percent.

Ethan merely smiled.

Tyson pressed the attack. Under his commands, Metagross struck back fiercely and gave Dragonite real trouble. Dragonite answered in kind. They fought in the air, crashed among the rocks, and rose from the shattered stone to clash overhead again. Both took injuries, and the audience loved every moment.

This was what a main-tournament battle ought to look like. Yet it seemed almost too good for a round-of-thirty-two match. It felt more like a final. Other competitors watched open-mouthed, impressed—and relieved that they had not drawn either of these two.

The broadcast ratings climbed as the fight went on. John, the live director, ought to have been delighted. Instead, worry outweighed his satisfaction. He looked toward the small old man standing with his hands behind his back on a distant platform.

If the first round already offered a match of this quality, what would Goodshow do later? Suppose Ethan won and then faced opponents weaker than Tyson in successive rounds. Would the declining competition turn viewers and the press against the tournament? John sighed. The old president had led the League to its peak and possessed far more strength than his appearance suggested. John hoped he truly had a plan.

On the field, the battle raged on. Its shock waves churned the pool. Fortunately, both Pokémon could fight above the water. Had they needed the rocks underfoot, the footing across the Water Field might have collapsed. Even so, water surged out of the pool, changing the field's appearance.

The two Pokémon looked evenly matched, but the balance could change in an instant.

Tyson let his guard slip for a moment. Ethan seized it. At his command, Dragonite grabbed Metagross and dragged it beneath the water.

There, Metagross could barely resist. Its psychic power seemed useless underwater. Though it could normally hover, its dense, heavy body could not lift itself back to the surface.

Dragonite was at ease. It released Metagross, swam around behind it, and delivered a point-blank Thunder Punch. Metagross went limp.

The underwater cameras showed the exchange, but the water made it hard for spectators to tell whether Metagross was truly finished. They got their answer when Dragonite burst through the surface and tossed the unconscious Pokémon out of the battlefield.

The stadium erupted. The referee raised a colored flag and called, “Metagross is unable to battle! Dragonite wins this round!”

Moments earlier, the Pokémon had seemed to be trading blows evenly. No one had expected the result to come so suddenly. After their surprise, the spectators gave them thunderous applause.

Tyson could only shake his head. He had done everything he could. After a poor start, Metagross had fought back and appeared to match Dragonite. It was the best Tyson had seen it perform since he caught it.

Even that had not been enough. With Metagross underwater at the end, the pool had kept Tyson from recalling it; he could only watch it fall. Worse, looking closely now, he saw that Dragonite bore few marks. Their apparent equality had been his own wishful thinking.

It was almost unbelievable.
