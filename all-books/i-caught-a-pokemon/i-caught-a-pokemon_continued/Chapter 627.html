After losing two rounds, Wattson's hearty smile faded. Even Watt looked at him with concern. His boss was getting on in years; a strong emotional shock would do him no good. If he lost all three rounds and fell ill from it, the match would hardly be worth the cost.

Wattson appeared unaware of his assistant's worry. He recalled Magneton and took out his third Poké Ball with a grave expression. Blue light flashed, revealing his final Pokémon.

An Ampharos.

It resembled a large kangaroo. Its skin was yellow, with a white belly, flat hands and feet, and a single toe on each foot. A red orb sat on its forehead and another at the end of its tail. Black bands marked its neck, ears, and part of the tail.

The tip of that tail produced an intense light. After dark, its glow could be seen far out at sea, serving as a beacon for ships. In ancient times people had even used Ampharos's light to send signals to companions in the distance.

Ethan had battled an Ampharos in Johto, so it wasn't unfamiliar. His experience with the games told him something else that most challengers might not know: Ampharos was one of Wattson's ace Pokémon.

The old fox. Wattson had quietly raised the difficulty again. An ordinary challenger might never have realized it.

Ethan recalled Combusken and assessed Ampharos. Despite “dragon” in its Chinese name, it was purely an Electric-type Pokémon. Even so, it was versatile and knew moves of several types beyond Electric.

With Wattson using an ace, Ethan had to choose more carefully. His fingers passed over the Poké Balls at his waist. He considered Dragonite and Tyranitar, then stopped on Tyranitar's ball.

Wattson showed no surprise when Tyranitar appeared. “I knew you'd send it out! When it broke my Laughing Million-Volt No. 2, I could tell it was strong. I was sure you'd use it in this battle.”

Sure I'd use it? Ethan shook his head with a faint smile but didn't argue. Anyone who saw a challenger with a strong Pokémon would assume it was the greatest threat and keep watching for it. To Ethan, Tyranitar had merely been one of several choices.

Tyranitar gave its customary roar. Many of Ethan's more ferocious Pokémon seemed to have developed that habit.

Wattson's Ampharos was rather timid. The roar and Tyranitar's greater size made it instinctively take a step back. Wattson's heart sank.

“Don't be afraid!” he called quickly. “Fight the way you always do. Have confidence in yourself. You're the best!”

His encouragement worked. Ampharos calmed down and even met Tyranitar's gaze. Its nerves might be a little fragile, but it clearly had a close bond with Wattson and trusted him.

When both Pokémon were ready, Watt waved his flag again. The third round began. Each side had sent out a powerful partner, and the battle promised to be fiercer than the first two.

They opened with a clash of ranged attacks. Ampharos's Signal Beam and Tyranitar's Dark Pulse, two differently colored bursts of energy, met in the air and exploded violently.

Smoke spread over the middle of the field. Ampharos was preparing to fire through it when the ground began to tremble. Tyranitar burst from the smoke and charged. The tremors came from that armored tank of a Pokémon pounding toward it.

Startled, Ampharos fired a Thunder Shock. Electricity struck Tyranitar, but it roared and kept coming through the damage.

Ampharos was not incapable of close combat. With moves such as Fire Punch and Thunder Punch, and Thunder Wave to paralyze an enemy, it could stand up to most Pokémon. But something about Tyranitar's intimidating presence made it lose heart. It desperately wanted to stop Tyranitar at a distance.

Wattson sensed what his partner was feeling. The opponent was already coming close, though, and Ampharos knew how to fight there. It couldn't run from the exchange.

“Pull yourself together! Thunder Punch!”

Ampharos gritted its teeth and went forward. That was exactly what Ethan wanted.

As electricity flashed around Ampharos's fists, he smiled. “Tyranitar, keep going. Ram straight into it!”

Tyranitar set its armored shoulder and head forward and ran harder. Ampharos tried to sidestep the charge before striking with Thunder Punch, but Tyranitar was larger and could burst forward over a short distance. It hit before Ampharos could clear its path.

Bang!

Ampharos felt as though a truck had struck it and went flying. The impact looked tremendous, though it did less damage than its force suggested. Ethan was not finished. Before Ampharos could land, he sent Tyranitar after it.

“Now, let's make a mess of things! Thrash!”

Thrash was a familiar move: the user launched into a reckless flurry, striking without restraint and ending up confused afterward. Its power came with a cost, so it was best used when the target couldn't get away. Even if it did not finish the battle, it could cause serious damage.

Ampharos had just hit the ground and could not escape. It looked up to find Tyranitar towering above it. As Ampharos watched, Tyranitar's eyes turned bloodred. It raised both arms high and brought them down.
