It had not happened quite as suddenly as it seemed. May was still chatting with Ethan as they walked a mountain path, the Egg cradled in her arms, when she felt it tremble. She thought at first that her grip had slipped on the uneven trail. Then came a faint cracking sound.

“Ethan, look!” Her eyes lit up as she glanced down. “I think the Egg is about to hatch!”

“It certainly looks that way.” One glance told Ethan something was changing. He looked around them. They were halfway up a mountain, with no town ahead or village behind. Forest stretched in every direction, and there was no sign of anyone for miles, let alone a Pokémon Center.

“We'll have to welcome the little one ourselves. It's getting dark, so let's camp here tonight.”

A Pokémon Egg usually needed no help to hatch. Still, neither of them had done this before, and Ethan wanted to be as careful as he could in case something went wrong. He let several Pokémon out, set up their tent and a simple camp, and prepared hot food and water.

When everything was ready, Ethan, May, and the Pokémon gathered around the fire. Every eye turned toward the Egg. Gengar had slipped out of Ethan's shadow at some point and was watching with interest too.

“What?” Ethan asked with a smile. “You've lived all these years and never seen a Pokémon hatch?”

“I haven't.” Gengar shook its head. Its face held an unusually thoughtful expression. “I've even forgotten what it was like when I was born.”

The answer brought a weight to the camp. Ethan looked around. His Pokémon had lowered their heads, as if the Egg had made each of them think of its own beginnings.

Who remembered the moment they were born? Beyond that, how many of Ethan's Pokémon had ever known their parents? Someone had given him Gardevoir. Dragonite had been abandoned. Crystal Steelix had always been alone. There were many more such stories.

And Ethan himself was no different. Coming to this world had severed every tie he had on Earth—to his parents, relatives, and friends. He was adrift here without roots. He could not go back.

“Let's not dwell on that.” He gave a rueful shake of his head. “We're together now. That makes us a family. Whatever happens next, we'll face it together.”

They were bound closely by the Poké Balls and the lives they shared. They might not have been born into one family, but they had become one. With each other beside them, none had to be alone.

May listened quietly. She had parents and a younger brother, so she could not fully know what it felt like to be cut loose from everyone. The sadness in the circle still touched her.

Then the Egg moved again, drawing everyone's attention. A few soft cracks sounded. Before their eyes, its shape changed: the Egg itself transformed into an Eevee. Not a single piece of shell remained.

It was remarkable. The newborn Eevee was smaller than the ones they might meet in the wild, as anyone would expect of a baby. Yet as a Pokémon, it was already developed enough to move, see, hear, and cry. Apart from being more fragile, it looked much like any other Eevee.

“It's an Eevee! That's wonderful!” May had not known what was inside the Egg, and she was delighted. Eevee was popular and adorable. She liked it on sight.

There was another reason to be happy. As with her Combusken and Ethan's Blaziken, they now had another Pokémon line in common. She hoped to learn from Ethan's Eevee and grow as a Trainer herself.

The baby looked curiously at the people and Pokémon around the fire. Before it could take everything in, a large, fluffy, foxlike head appeared at its side. Ethan's Eevee sniffed its new relative, tail wagging excitedly.

Except for May, no one was happier to see an Eevee hatch. The other Pokémon came close too, each eager to examine and welcome the newcomer. They all gave the new life their warmest care.

May set out the food and water she had prepared. “Let me introduce myself. I'm May,” she said with a tilted smile. “Welcome to the family.”

…

Eevee's arrival added to May's team and kept her smiling for a long time. After a night's rest on the mountainside, though, it was time to travel on. Ethan and May set out the next morning, now with a lively little Eevee at May's side.

Seeing how eager it was to explore, Ethan released his own Eevee to guide the newborn and let it discover the countryside. They went along at an easy pace and eventually reached a large city called Commerce City.

Ethan had never visited it and had not even heard its name. That was a gap in his own knowledge, he realized when he saw the many tall buildings. The city looked more prosperous than Viridian or Pewter. Without an official Gym of its own, however, it had little fame among traveling Trainers.

May's first concern on arriving was to ask whether Commerce City held a Pokémon Contest. She soon received good news and bad. It did host one, and its scale and popularity were second only to the Saffron Contest. Unfortunately, the Commerce City Contest had ended two weeks earlier.

May could only laugh ruefully. They had reached the city too late.

Before she could dwell on her disappointment, she heard another piece of local news: the legend of Red Lightning.

People said a strange figure draped from head to toe in a red cloak was attacking Trainers around town. It moved like lightning and could even use Pokémon moves. That was how it had earned its name. It challenged Trainers it met on the street whether they agreed or not. When it won, it covered their Pokémon in graffiti. It had beaten so many that people were growing uneasy.

May told Ethan. He rubbed his chin. “A stranger who can use Pokémon moves and only draws on the Pokémon it defeats? Sounds like a Pokémon in disguise.”

Red Lightning. Had that character appeared in the anime? Ethan could not remember at all.

“I don't think so,” May said with a frown. “People say it can talk. If that's true…”

She had been about to say it could not be a Pokémon. Then she remembered Gengar, and Team Rocket's Meowth. Another talking Pokémon was hardly impossible.

“We should be careful, whoever it is,” she said. “We're Trainers too. It would be troublesome if it decided to target us.”

To her surprise, Ethan laughed. “Wouldn't that be convenient? Human or Pokémon, it's already caused enough trouble. If we run into it, we'll stop it.”

May had thought of Red Lightning as an urban legend. She had briefly forgotten that the man beside her was more legendary than the tale itself. Commerce City was enormous, anyway. The chances of meeting this figure were slim. There was no reason to worry.

Just as she reached that conclusion, a sudden laugh sounded above them while they crossed a park. Both looked up. A figure entirely wrapped in a red cloak stood in a large tree, staring down at them.

“A red cloak… Could it be—” May began.

“Heh, heh, heh.” The figure looked down on them, and a deep man's voice issued from beneath the cloak. “So you've heard my name. That's right. I am Red Lightning!”

It extended a red pincer and pointed at Ethan's Eevee. “You and I will battle!”

Without waiting to see whether Eevee agreed, it jumped down in front of them.

At this range Ethan could make out the red-cloaked figure. His guess had been right: the theatrical stranger was a Scizor. He owned one himself, so the body shape and the portion of its head that showed were easy for him to recognize.

There was something unusual about its left eye. It was a different color, as though the Scizor had mismatched eyes—or someone had fitted something over it. Whatever the reason, the cloak did look impressive. Ethan almost wanted one for his own Scizor.

May was still uncertain. “You want to battle Eevee? Does that mean you're a Pokémon?”

Scizor's humanlike shape, its concealing cloak, and the popularity of Pokémon masks made it hard for her to be sure. Her lack of experience did not help.

Red Lightning gave no answer. It raised its right pincer and attacked. Hot energy gathered there, then a Hyper Beam shot toward Ethan's Eevee.

The beam's path also included Ethan and May. Ethan's brows drew together.

Keeping Trainers out of an attack was an accepted rule of Pokémon battles. A Pokémon living with people in a city, able to understand their speech, could hardly be ignorant of it. They had only just met and had no quarrel. This attack was deliberate.

Ethan's opinion of Red Lightning turned sharply against it. He no longer felt any need to handle the matter gently.

“Good. Since we've met, we'll put an end to your troublemaking today.” He stared coldly at the Scizor and paid no heed to the approaching beam. He knew someone would act the moment a Trainer was threatened.

A huge ball of dark energy flew from the shadow at his feet and struck the Hyper Beam with perfect aim.

Boom!

The attacks collided in an explosion. Gengar appeared in front of Ethan and May and used its power to shield them from the blast. Across the park, Red Lightning crossed its great pincers to block the shock wave, but the red cloak blew away.

May finally saw what stood beneath it. “Scizor! It really is a Pokémon!”

Now that its disguise was gone, she noticed an odd device hanging at its neck. It looked like a speaker.

“Looks like we've uncovered Red Lightning's real identity,” Ethan said.

As the smoke cleared, he walked forward and picked up an object that resembled a false eye. He tossed it lightly in his hand. It had been covering Scizor's left eye and had fallen off in the blast, landing near him.

“A camera,” he said, “and a speaker under your neck. So you aren't a talking Pokémon at all. The so-called Red Lightning is just a puppet someone is controlling.”
