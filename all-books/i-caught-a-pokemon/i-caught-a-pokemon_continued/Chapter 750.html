Phoebe looked much as she had when Ethan and Misty last saw her. A few days had changed nothing.

“Phoebe, you startled me!” Misty said, pressing a hand to her chest.

They had been through a great deal together and had traveled side by side for more than a day. The surprise made Misty forget to speak formally to a member of the Elite Four. Phoebe felt more like an ordinary friend now.

Phoebe rubbed her nose and grinned. “Like a Ghost-type Pokémon, I come and go without a trace!”

Ethan had not expected her to appear just then. He removed the two pouches from behind him and handed them over.

“Keep them safe. Don't let anyone take them again.”

Phoebe accepted the pouches and bowed deeply. “Anabel told me what happened, Ethan. Thank you so much. I'll guard them properly this time, I promise. And without ambitious people like Maxie and Archie around, I don't think anyone will come after them again.”

Ethan and Misty exchanged a look. Phoebe clearly did not know that Necrozma had taken Maxie and Archie. Anabel must have kept the news quiet to prevent panic, even withholding it from a member of the Elite Four.

That was understandable, though Ethan saw little reason to fear another immediate attempt on the orbs. The two men might have escaped custody, but they were hardly free to act as they pleased. And if Necrozma chose to help them seize Groudon and Kyogre, no hiding place for the orbs would stop it.

Anabel had not told Phoebe. Ethan and Misty did not tell her either.

“How are your grandparents?” Ethan asked.

“They're settled,” Phoebe said with a smile. “The League is building a new house for them where the old one stood. It isn't finished yet, but they should soon be able to live quietly again.”

Ethan nodded. With that, their business here was done. They could continue their journey without worrying about the orbs.

They talked a little longer, declined Phoebe's invitation to a meal, and waved goodbye. Phoebe watched them leave, gripping the two pouches tightly and holding on to her gratitude.

...

The Hoenn Grand Festival was held in Slateport City. Ethan and Misty knew the city well. On an earlier visit, they had toured the Oceanic Museum, met Drew, and watched May compete in her first Contest. Returning felt like revisiting an old stop on their journey.

After two days of travel, they reached Slateport. Ash and his friends had already been there for some time. Rather than staying in the city, they had moved into the Grand Festival's competitors' village.

The Pokémon League treated Contests almost as seriously as its battling tournaments. It had built a splendid venue, along with a Pokémon Center, dormitories, dining halls, and training facilities. The complex stood beside Slateport's waterfront, divided between a harbor and the competition area. From above, the latter formed a pentagon. Tall buildings and open-air spaces surrounded a grand five-sided arena at its center.

Crowds had filled the grounds already. Contests might not quite rival League tournaments in popularity, but the anticipation here came close.

At the dormitory, Misty and May grabbed each other's hands and talked excitedly.

“May! You really did it!”

“I know. Honestly, I didn't think I'd win five Ribbons and make it to the Grand Festival!”

“You worked for them!”

While the girls celebrated, Ethan looked toward Ash. Ash happened to look back.

“You've got all eight Badges too?” Ethan asked.

“Of course!” Ash's eyes shone with the same excitement as May's. “I can't wait for the Conference!”

“Neither can I. The Ever Grande Conference should be something.”

A thought seemed to strike Ash. “Ethan, now that you've got your Badges, can we all travel together for the next few weeks?”

“Unless something comes up. Why?”

“Yes!” Ash jumped. “That means more big meals!”

Ethan stared at him. That was what he had been so eager to ask? Ash really did love food.

Yet the request also showed how close Ash felt to him. He would not speak so freely to just any friend. Ethan had become both a rival Ash wanted to catch up with and someone he could rely on.

Ethan also met May's mother, Caroline. He had expected her to attend such an important event and cheer for her daughter. May was a little disappointed that her father, Norman, could not leave his Gym. He would watch her on television from home.

In May's room at the elaborately decorated dormitory, Ethan learned the Festival's schedule. It had a preliminary round and a main competition. Tomorrow, 247 registered Coordinators would perform on the Appeal Stage. Sixty-four would advance, then compete for the final victory.

It sounded much like a League tournament. With the preliminary round only a day away, May was understandably nervous. After they talked for a while, Misty suggested one last practice session. Everyone except Ethan went to the nearby outdoor training field.

Ethan was only here to watch. He was glad of the free time. As the others hurried away, he headed toward the snack street beside the venue to browse.

When he competed, he had to prepare seriously. It was pleasant to experience a tournament without that pressure.

Snack streets sold much the same foods and trinkets wherever he went. The pleasure lay in the bustle. Ordinary treats and goods became tempting among the crowds and bright stalls.

He let Gardevoir out and bought a few snacks he liked. Together they moved slowly with the flow of people.

It had been a long time since Ethan and Gardevoir had spent time alone. Their telepathic connection meant they needed few words to understand one another, but that did not make these quiet moments any less valuable.

Gardevoir was not unheard of in Hoenn, yet Ethan's stood out in bearing and appearance. Passersby kept turning to admire it. One stranger even approached and asked Ethan to trade Gardevoir away. Ethan had no patience for someone who would make such a presumptuous request. He waved the person off and continued.

A little farther along, he saw three familiar figures. Despite their disguises, he recognized Jessie, James, and Meowth at once. Wherever Ash went, they were rarely far behind. They had drawn a crowd and were calling out around some stall, apparently promoting a new scheme.

Ethan did not care to find out. He was about to pass when all three seemed to sense him and turned at once. Their eyes met his. They shivered and went rigid.

Ethan looked away and walked past with Gardevoir. His pace was unremarkable, but to Team Rocket's trio, every footstep sounded as though it landed on their hearts.

They did not move until he had vanished into the crowd.

Jessie's back was soaked with sweat. Still frozen in place, she whispered, “H-he's gone, right?”

There was no one in the world they feared more.

“I think so.” James breathed out, then glanced at Meowth. “Hey, did you wet yourself?”

“That's sweat! Sweat!” Meowth wanted to swipe at him, but Ethan might still be close enough to return. “That was too close. I never expected to see him here!”

“I thought he was going to finish us,” Jessie said, recovering slowly. “Just being near him is terrifying.”

James hesitated. “Have you noticed? This isn't the first time he's ignored us. He never seems to go out of his way to bother us.”

“Wouldn't it be more accurate to say he doesn't consider us worth noticing?” Meowth ventured.

“Because we're no threat to him,” Jessie said, showing a rare flash of insight. Then she smiled. “Fine by me! As long as we don't start trouble with him, we'll be safe. We can keep our attention on the twerp and Pikachu.”

“That's true!”

They laughed together. Their fear faded as quickly as it had come, and they returned to their work.

Outside the snack street, Ethan found a bench. He laid out the small trinkets he had bought and divided them into groups. His visit had not been aimless. Since he had found gifts here, he wanted to send some home. Sabrina, Erika, and Chloe, who kept helping care for his Pokémon, all deserved thanks.

He was sorting the last of them before finding a delivery service when a petite girl stopped in front of him. She wore a cap, sunglasses, and a face mask.

“Hehe. I knew it was you!”

The mask muffled her voice, but it sounded familiar. Ethan studied her hair, figure, and rather bold, showy short skirt.

“Lisia? What are you doing here?”

It was the young Contest idol he had met in Sootopolis. She had wrapped herself up as though desperate not to be recognized, though Ethan had identified her quickly enough.

Lisia took off her mask and gave him a broad smile. “They invited me for the Festival. I got bored in my room, so I came out in disguise for a walk. And then I found you! I'm so lucky!”

She looked from side to side. Misty was nowhere nearby. “Where's the girl who's always with you?”

Ethan knew they did not get along particularly well. “She's practicing with her friends at the training field.”

Lisia's smile grew brighter. She boldly slipped both hands around Ethan's arm and held it close. “Wonderful! Then let me keep you company while we look around.”

Ethan felt her press against him and tried to free his arm, but she held on too tightly. He let it be, privately glad Misty was elsewhere. If she saw this, she and Lisia might argue for hours.

“Sorry. I just finished browsing and was about to go back and rest.”

He meant it as a refusal. Lisia turned it into an invitation. “That's perfect! I've been out for ages and I'm tired too. Let's go back to the dormitories together!”
