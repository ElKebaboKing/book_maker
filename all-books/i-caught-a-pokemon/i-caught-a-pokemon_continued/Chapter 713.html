Night had fallen by the time Dragonite carried Ethan, Shelly, and Courtney down to an unfamiliar stretch of the Hoenn coast.

They had left the island after Kyogre's departure. More than an hour of flying had finally brought them back to the mainland.

Shelly jumped off first. Once Ethan and Courtney had climbed down, Ethan patted his panting Pokémon. “Well done. You really worked hard today.”

The journey had exhausted Dragonite. It had fought at Mt. Pyre, carried them to Team Magma's base, then flown them from the base to the island for another battle. Afterward, it had brought them back to shore. Between the fighting and the travel, it had barely had a chance to rest. Even Dragonite's stamina had its limits.

They had little choice. Team Magma's and Team Aqua's submarines had sunk, and the force of Precipice Blades and Origin Pulse had destroyed Shelly's submarine as well. Dragonite was their only way back to land.

It gave Ethan a broad, cheerful smile and a thumbs-up. Tired as it was, it was happy to have helped its Trainer.

Shelly dropped onto a nearby rock, rubbed her numb backside, and asked, “What do we do now?”

They had calmed this crisis, but she knew what Ethan had said was true. As long as Maxie and Archie remained free, their ambitions for the two ancient Legendary Pokémon would never disappear.

Ethan looked at the two women and thought for a moment. “We need to watch their movements closely. Kyogre and Groudon are secondary. The real danger comes from Archie and Maxie. If we know what either of them plans to do next, we can step in before things get worse.”

“That's easier said than done.” Shelly sighed. “There are only three of us. We can't watch them across the whole sea.”

Courtney lowered her head in silence, apparently considering the problem too.

“It isn't as complicated as you think,” Ethan said. “Groudon and Kyogre might be hard to find, but we don't need to follow them. We only need to keep an eye on Maxie and Archie.”

Shelly's expression turned thoughtful. She seemed to see where he was going. “You mean...?”

“I think they're probably safe, and I expect the orbs will end up in their hands again,” Ethan explained. “So we prepare for the worst. Even with an orb, neither man can control and catch Groudon or Kyogre on his own. They'll need a lot of people. Watch the bases around here for sudden movements of personnel, and we should be able to find them.”

“That would save us some effort.” Shelly's eyes brightened. “We could work out their general plans by tracking their members. If we get detailed enough reports, we might even know every move Maxie and Archie make next.”

She looked at Ethan with a small smile. “You're quick, aren't you? You came up with that almost at once.”

Courtney gazed at him just as intently.

Most people would still be celebrating. Ethan had thwarted Maxie's plot and driven off Kyogre. Even if he didn't boast about it, surely he deserved to feel pleased for a while.

Instead, he was already thinking ahead, frowning over the worst outcome and making plans to counter his opponents' next move. No wonder he had come so far. Few Trainers could match that composure.

Of course, Courtney was seeing him through a generous filter.

Was Ethan really that extraordinary? Perhaps he was, perhaps he wasn't. He had certainly dealt with more difficult incidents on his journey than most people encountered in a lifetime.

But ever since the exchange incident, Courtney had come completely over to his side. She admired him so much that she instinctively imagined him in an even better light.

That was what a filter could do. Ethan had no idea what she was thinking. Her gaze merely seemed familiar, as though he had seen someone look at him that way before.

Facing the women's very different expressions, he said seriously, “I'll have to leave the next part to you two.”

Shelly understood at once and laughed. “So you want us to do the watching?”

“Yes. You're the only ones suited to it.” Ethan sighed. “No one knows Team Aqua and Team Magma better than you do. You must know which of their bases are hidden nearby.”

“Of course.” Shelly nodded proudly. “I remember all Team Aqua's secrets and deployments. I only left a few days ago. Even if they wanted to relocate, they couldn't do it overnight. Finding the nearest secret base will be easy.”

Time was a strange thing. Shelly's resistance and pain had gradually given way to acceptance, changing how she thought and felt. People said time was the best medicine: eventually, even a deep hurt could ease.

She had plainly accepted her new identity and life. She still thought about Team Aqua now and then, but she saw herself as an outsider and could speak of it with some detachment.

Ethan nodded to Shelly and turned to Courtney. She nodded back without hesitation.

“The same goes for me. A lot of Team Magma's members don't even know I've left. If I'm careful, I could turn some of them and make them my eyes inside the organization.”

“I can do that too,” Shelly said before Ethan could answer. She glanced at Courtney. “Plenty of my old followers are still in Team Aqua. If I ask, I'm sure they'll quietly watch for Archie.”

Though Ethan's intervention had brought both women out of their organizations and made them teammates, they were still wary of each other. They had fought for years before he came along. Such hostility would not vanish in a few days.

Only Ethan standing between them kept the peace. Without him, they would probably go their separate ways in no time.

He sighed inwardly. “Then it's settled. Each of you find people you can bring around. They don't have to leave their organizations. They only need to watch Maxie and Archie. Let me know as soon as you hear anything.”

“Understood,” Shelly said. “We're both doing as you ask. What will you be doing?”

“Me?” Ethan shook his head. “Saving the world is just a hobby. Challenging Gyms and the League is my real job. I'm going to keep traveling, of course.”

“How leisurely.” Shelly folded her arms and rolled her eyes. “At this rate, we'll end up working for you.”

“If that's how you see it, I can't stop you.” Ethan gave her a long look, then smiled. “You're free to give up. You can let Archie control Kyogre and put the whole world at risk.”

Shelly opened her mouth but said nothing. She turned away rather than meet his eyes.

When he saw she had yielded, Ethan spoke frankly. “Don't worry. My next task won't be easy either. I'm still a little short of the strength needed to stop Primal Groudon and Primal Kyogre, and I might have to face Rayquaza too. Deoxys is the only one of my Pokémon that could fight at that level, and even then there's a lot of uncertainty. So I'm going to find help.”

“Oh?” Courtney's interest stirred. “Who are you going to ask? Have you found another Pokémon you want to catch?”

Shelly stole a glance at him.

“There are two,” Ethan said openly. “One is very powerful and promised to help if I ever ran into trouble. Unfortunately, it's all the way over in Kanto. There isn't time to make the trip.”

Anyone who knew Ethan well would understand whom he meant. But as he said, going to Kanto and back would take too long.

“And the other?” Courtney asked.

Ethan raised his eyes to the star-filled sky and sighed. “For the other, I'll have to go to Sky Pillar and see if there's even the slightest chance.”

“Sky Pillar?” Courtney paused before remembering what the place was. The top of Sky Pillar was said to be Rayquaza's home.

But...

“You know where Sky Pillar is?” Shelly asked. “As far as I know, it's only a legend. People have talked about it for years, but no one has ever found its location.”

Ethan shrugged. “I'll have to ask the Draconid people. They've always guarded that place, and as it happens, I have a small connection with one of them.”

...

Under the stars, Ethan made his way toward Lilycove City alone. He would meet Misty there before setting out to look for Sky Pillar.

After their short rest, Shelly and Courtney had gone to carry out their assignments. If things went well, word of Archie and Maxie would reach him soon.

The sea breeze brushed against him, and he pulled his clothes tighter. After everything that had happened, he was hungry and tired.

To be honest, he had not traveled alone for a long time. He found himself missing the feeling.

Fortunately, they had landed close to Lilycove. He did not need to call on Dragonite again. Half an hour on foot brought the lights of the great city into view.

At nine that night, Ethan finally pushed open the doors of the Lilycove Pokémon Center.

Misty and Phoebe had heard he was coming and were waiting anxiously in the dining area. Both stood when they saw him.

To Ethan's surprise, Ash, May, Brock, and Max were there too.

“Ethan! You're finally back!” Misty hurried over. Up close, she saw how disheveled he was. His clothes were creased and his hair was a mess. He must have been through a great deal.

She went straight to his side, patiently straightened his clothes, and picked a tiny shell off his back.

Ethan patted her head, nodded to Phoebe, who looked as if she wanted to say something, then turned to Ash and the others. “What are you doing here?”

Ash scratched his head. “We came with May for the Lilycove Contest. We ran into Misty, and she told us what happened. How did it go, Ethan?”

“Let me sit down first.” Ethan sank onto a dining-room sofa and leaned back. Only then did he realize how thoroughly worn out he was; even his bones seemed ready to fall apart. The others were the only people left in the dining area. “Is there anything to eat? I'm starving.”

“I'll ask Nurse Joy to make you dinner!”

Misty hurried to the counter. She was so attentive that anyone who did not know them might have mistaken her for his girlfriend or little sister.

Ethan nodded and took a sip of the warm water May handed him. After soothing his dry throat, he told them what had happened since he left Mt. Pyre.

He left Shelly and Courtney out of it. He said only that Team Aqua had attacked while he was pursuing Maxie, that Groudon and Kyogre had then fought, and that both orbs had been lost in the battle.

Although she had prepared herself for bad news, Phoebe sighed when she heard it.
