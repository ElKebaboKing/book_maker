The Trainers on the river and in the air had no choice but to fight the hundreds of Deoxys copies that came at them.

They soon discovered that, for all their numbers, the copies were fragile. Even a slight hit broke one apart into a fading shadow. That was a relief. The pressure on the Trainers eased at once.

If every copy had been as strong as the original, there would have been no point fighting. They might as well have fled. The technique looked terrifying, but its numbers came at the cost of durability.

The battle grew fierce. Ash, Rafe, and Sid were caught in it like everyone else.

Ethan's Deoxys had been with him only a short time and did not know Ash. It had been ordered to steer Rayquaza toward the Trainers and wear them down. Even if it had known Ash was Ethan's friend, it would have treated him like anyone else without special instructions.

Still, Deoxys was not trying to kill anyone. The Trainers had room to fight back. Ash and the others sent out Pokémon to counterattack and hurried toward the bank, knowing the river put them at a severe disadvantage.

The greater danger came from Rayquaza's fight with the wild Deoxys. Neither meant to strike the humans below, but the force spilling from their battle was more than many Trainers could bear.

Powerful attacks burst against the water, battering the Pokémon crossing it. Rayquaza's long, massive body swept through the sky and knocked Flying-type Pokémon down. Some Trainers finally understood that they lacked the strength even to approach such creatures.

The copies ignored Rayquaza and focused on the Trainers' Pokémon, adding to the confusion. Most of the crowd, however, had lost its caution at the sight of Legendary Pokémon. Even a one-in-ten-thousand chance of catching one kept them in the fight.

Cold river water and one defeat after another did not break their determination. If anything, their fixation on Rayquaza and the Deoxys grew stronger.

Amid the chaos, Ash, Rafe, and Sid fought their way across the river and reached the far bank first.

“Whew, we're finally out.” Sid climbed off Blastoise and wrung out his clothes. He looked back at the fight on the water. “If we'd stayed in the river with those copies attacking and Rayquaza firing overhead, we might as well have been targets.”

“They're not quite copies,” Rafe said. Ethan's first impression of him had been right: he thought carefully about what he saw. “They move independently and can hit things. Splitting off pieces of itself is a better description.”

“Splitting?” Sid sighed. “Whatever you call them, what can we do about so many shadows?”

He had a point. They could knock the creatures down one at a time, but there were too many to clear. To reach the Pokémon they wanted to catch, they first had to get past the split-off forms. It seemed like a deadlock.

“You're right,” Rafe admitted after a pause. “We'll have to find a way to eliminate them before we get close.”

He knew a simpler answer existed: bring a Pokémon strong enough to match Deoxys. But neither he, Sid, nor Ash had one.

“Don't be so gloomy,” Ash said with a grin. “We made it ashore. That's the first step. If we keep trying, we'll find a way.”

Sid agreed. “Exactly! Why overthink it? Let's give it everything we've got.”

Seeing that neither intended to back down, Rafe nodded. “Then let's work together for now.”

They were rivals in their hunt, but the opponent before them had put them on the same side.

From the forest, Ethan watched the Trainers fight harder instead of retreating. They seemed to be growing more determined with every attack. It gave him a headache, especially when he saw Ash at the edge of the trees.

Why couldn't Ash stay with Tory and help him get over his fear? Why did he have to join this mess?

Very well. If they would not leave, Ethan would raise the stakes. He fixed his gaze on the Trainers and sent another order to Deoxys.

“Let them see what you can really do.”

...

“Pikachu, Thunderbolt! Corphish, Bubble Beam!”

“Blaziken, Flamethrower! Quagsire, Water Gun!”

“Blastoise, Hydro Pump! Magneton, Thunder Shock!”

On the open ground before the forest, Ash, Rafe, and Sid directed their Pokémon against the Deoxys copies overhead. Their combined attacks broke apart more than a dozen. It barely changed the wider battle, but it cleared a patch of sky and made the space around them safer.

Then a figure flashed before their eyes. A Deoxys descended until it hovered only a meter off the ground in front of them.

Its colors differed sharply from the copies. This was the original.

“What?” Sid froze. Before he could react, Blastoise flew backward as if struck by an invisible force. It skimmed past him and slammed into a tree.

Thud! The trunk splintered. Blastoise crashed through several more thick trees before finally stopping. Its shell had protected it from the worst of the impact, and after struggling for a moment, it slowly got up.

“How did it get here?” Ash cried.

He had no time to move. The same unseen power hurled Pikachu into another tree. “Pikachu!” Ash could only watch as his partner struck the trunk and fell to the ground.

Rafe reacted faster. He snatched a Poké Ball from his belt and shouted to the other two, “Don't just stand there! This is our chance. We have to bring it down together!”

Ash and Sid knew the opportunity might not come again. They left their injured Pokémon for the moment and released everything else they had. Blue flashes filled the clearing, and more than a dozen Pokémon surrounded Deoxys.

Up close, the three Trainers could sense how unusual it was. Deoxys made a short, strange sound, like a signal none of them could understand. Its metallic, alien face showed no expression. It seemed cold and indifferent.

“Everyone, attack!” Sid called. His Pokémon rushed forward.

Deoxys raised both arms and released a pulse of power. Sid's Pokémon flew backward before they could touch it.

“It's too strong!” Sid stared as several of his Pokémon went down from that one blow.

Ash's and Rafe's Pokémon closed in from either side.

“Swellow, Wing Attack! Snorunt, Icy Wind!” Ash commanded.

Both moves struck toward Deoxys, but neither broke through its protective barrier. Rafe fared no better. His Pokémon failed to breach the defense and fell to Deoxys's counterattack.

His confidence took a heavy blow. He had expected a gap in strength, but not one this vast. They had barely begun, and it already felt impossible to cross. Could any human truly catch such a Pokémon?

A yellow shape flashed past him. Pikachu, hurt but back on its feet, ran low to the ground, leaped, and swung its shining tail at Deoxys.

What was it doing? Rafe had fought Pikachu at the Battle Tower earlier and remembered its poor performance. He expected this charge to end badly.

Instead, Pikachu's Iron Tail shattered Deoxys's energy barrier.

How could such a little Pokémon hold that much power? Why had it looked so weak in the tower?

Deoxys flung Pikachu aside with psychic force before it could cause any injury. But the broken barrier gave Rafe a moment of hope.

“Now! Hit it with everything you have!” he shouted.

The usually composed boy sounded fired up. His fallen Pokémon forced themselves upright despite the pain and launched their strongest attacks together. Five or six blasts of different colors converged on Deoxys.

Boom!

They struck almost at once, raising a cloud of smoke. It worked! Rafe's face brightened.

The smoke thinned. Deoxys still hovered in the same spot, unharmed. Its shape had changed: its body looked thicker, as though it wore protective armor.

Rafe's smile vanished. Why had it changed? His Pokémon had attacked with all their strength. Even a Legendary Pokémon ought to have felt that. Yet this one had not moved or suffered so much as a scratch.

What kind of monster was it?

As he stared, Deoxys changed back to its earlier form, raised its arms, and counterattacked. Rafe's face drained of color.

He had never encountered Deoxys before, but he understood now: it could take different forms. One excelled at attacking; another had extraordinary defense.

He did not have time to order a dodge. Black light flashed, and an explosion tore through the clearing, turning trees to ash and scouring the earth. His Pokémon fell, as did Ash's and Sid's.

After only two direct attacks, Deoxys had knocked out every Pokémon they had on the field.

“You!” Sid cried. The hopelessness was too much for him.

In desperation, he pulled out a Poké Ball and threw it at Deoxys. The time to make a capture attempt had clearly not come, but Rafe understood. With no Pokémon left able to fight, this was the only thing Sid could try.

Neither Rafe nor Ash expected it to work, yet both followed the ball with their eyes as it reached Deoxys.

Click. It dropped to the ground without reacting at all.

“Huh?” Ash and Sid stared. Had the Poké Ball broken?

Rafe was startled too. Then his face changed. Poké Balls rarely failed unless damaged. Even thrown at a healthy wild Pokémon, one should at least flash red.

There was only one explanation.

“Run!” he shouted at the others. “Somebody has already caught this Pokémon! It has a Trainer!”
