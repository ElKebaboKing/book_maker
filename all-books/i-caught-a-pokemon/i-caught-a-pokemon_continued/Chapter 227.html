Now that Ethan had arrived, Rudy had to abandon his plans for lunch and take him to the battlefield.

Before they left, Ethan heard lively music coming from somewhere inside the Gym. He looked toward a room with an open door. A dozen or so Pokémon of different species were moving to the beat, twisting their bodies in strange steps that looked like a dance.

"What are they doing?"

Rudy could not be bothered to explain, but his sister answered eagerly, as though showing off. "They're dancing!"

"Dancing?" Ethan paused. He had assumed they were doing some sort of special training, but apparently they really were just dancing. They looked happy, though human dance steps performed by Pokémon had an odd, comical quality.

Mahri nodded hard. "That's right! Every Pokémon at our Gym learns to dance. It's what makes this place special!"

Perhaps Mahri's explanation was too simple, or perhaps calling it a specialty made Rudy's method sound less serious than he liked. Although he did not want to speak to Ethan, he added, "Hmph. The days of teaching Pokémon nothing but moves are over. I invented this training method myself. It might not look related to battling, but it greatly improves a Pokémon's natural abilities!"

Greatly improves them? Ethan watched the Pokémon more closely, but saw nothing unusual in their movements. He supposed dance could be another way to develop coordination and flexibility.

He could understand making Hitmonchan, Electabuzz, or even Ninetales dance. But what was Starmie or Golem supposed to gain from it? One had a body as hard as a gem, and the other was heavy and awkward. Asking them to dance and bend their bodies seemed rather unreasonable.

Still, Ethan kept his thoughts to himself. Everyone had a training method of their own. He had no reason to interfere with Rudy's.

Rudy and Mahri never did get their lunch. Each grabbed a piece of bread to tide them over.

They left the Gym, with Ethan and Sabrina following Rudy. Surrounded by his many attendants and Mahri, the group took a path along the coast and came to a cliff.

The clifftop was barren, with hardly any vegetation. Marks left in the ground showed where, centuries earlier, the islanders had cut stone for the pillars. To protect their descendants, they had quarried away more than half a hill right where they stood. It must have been an enormous undertaking.

Ethan looked beyond the cliff. There stood the remarkable formation of dozens of huge stone pillars. Water rushed and roared below. When he stepped to the edge and looked down, he could see scores of whirlpools spinning at once, drawing seawater into them.

Anyone swimming there, or traveling in an ordinary small boat, would be in grave danger if caught in those currents. The whirlpools could wreck a vessel and drag its passengers into the depths.

Rudy pointed among the stone pillars. "That's where we'll battle."

Ethan followed his finger and saw what looked like a small mountain rising out of the sea, its top cut flat. The surface was about the size of a soccer field, and a regulation battlefield had been marked on it.

He had not fought a formal match in a long time. The sight of the field stirred an excitement he had missed.

Rudy cast him a sidelong glance and snorted. "First you have to get there. Consider that the final test. It isn't mandatory, though. If you can't make it on your own, just say so. My hot-air balloon can take you across."

He raised his chin toward a clearing in the distance. A balloon with a hanging basket stood there, ready to fly.

The battlefield was at least three hundred meters from the cliff in a straight line. Without a large Flying-type Pokémon, reaching it safely would be difficult. Crossing the water was out of the question. Challengers with no other way across would have to accept a ride in Rudy's balloon, putting them at a psychological disadvantage before the match even began.

Ethan had no such problem. He took a Poké Ball from his belt, released Charizard, and climbed onto its back. Rudy watched with a trace of disappointment.

Sabrina was about to join Ethan, but Rudy stopped her.

"I'm sorry, miss," he said, shaking his head. "Only the Trainers taking part in the match may go onto the field. You'll have to watch with my sister and the others."

With no choice, Sabrina boarded the hot-air balloon with Mahri and Rudy's attendants to watch from above.

Charizard beat its wings and carried Ethan to the battlefield first. Rudy released a Pidgeot and followed close behind.

Standing on the platform, buffeted by cold wind and listening to the unending roar of the sea below, Ethan could finally feel how dangerous this place was, both for him and for the Pokémon.

The two Trainers took their places on opposite sides.

Rudy brushed back his bangs. "All right. Beat me and you'll earn the Spike Shell Badge. Which Pokémon will you send out first?"

By custom, the Gym Leader should send out the first Pokémon. Trovita Gym, however, required opponents of matching types, so the challenger had to choose first. Rudy would then select a Pokémon of the same type. It might make the battle a little less varied, but it also prevented either side from gaining an advantage through type matchups.

Ethan did not need to think long. Charizard was already out, so it might as well take the first round.

"Charizard, you're up first!"

"Roar!"

Charizard landed, spread its wings, and roared at Rudy. Rudy took out a Poké Ball and released his first Pokémon in answer.
