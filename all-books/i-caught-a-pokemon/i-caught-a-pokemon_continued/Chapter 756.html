A dazzling fireworks display opened the Grand Festival's main competition. As the crowd cheered, special guest Lisia took the stage and sang “Charm☆Love,” warming up the audience before the matches began.

“Ladies and gentlemen!” Once Lisia had left, Vivian walked to center stage in a pink dress. Her voice was as sweet and clear as ever. Smiling, she waved to the stands. “The main competition of the Pokémon Contest Grand Festival, every Coordinator's dream, is finally beginning! These Coordinators earned five Ribbons in Contests across the region, then placed among the top 64 on the preliminary Appeal Stage. Now they'll face each other in spectacular competition for the title of Top Coordinator. And the winner will receive this brilliant Ribbon Cup behind me!”

The preliminaries had been modest by comparison. This was a full-scale event before the arena crowd, broadcast live on television and online to homes across Hoenn. The main competition was the Festival's true beginning. Even people who chose not to watch would hear how popular it was.

“Now, let me introduce our judges!” Vivian gestured behind her.

The panel had grown for the main competition, another sign of how seriously the organizers took it. Five judges sat there: Mr. Sukizo, president of the Pokémon Fan Club; Mr. Contesta, head of the Contest committee; and three Nurse Joys. Ethan had found it odd not to see Sukizo and Contesta in the preliminaries. Given their positions, it made sense that they had been reserved for the main event.

The three Nurse Joys looked identical, down to their clothes. Standing side by side, they made an impressive sight. They were neither twins nor triplets, but members of one enormous family. Perhaps only Brock could tell them apart. Ethan wondered where the organizers had found them, and how they distinguished them. Were the slightly different colors of their hats enough?

Each judge greeted the audience. Some ardent Lisia fans sat beside Ethan, still enthralled by her lively song. They shouted so loudly he could not hear a word from the panel. Presumably the judges wished the Festival well.

May was not in the stands with them today. She waited in the preparation room with the other contestants.

Under the rules, each Coordinator needed two Pokémon over the course of the main competition. The first round was another appeal: one Pokémon per contestant, with three minutes to perform. The judges would score the acts, with each judge awarding up to twenty points. Only the top 32 would advance to the next round of Contest battles.

With both appeals and battles, the format offered something for viewers of all ages. That variety was one reason Contests were so popular. Some girls and older spectators even preferred the Grand Festival to a League tournament.

The day before, Ethan and Ash had done basic training. Ash had focused on the accuracy of his Pokémon's moves. Ethan had taken a more hands-off approach and let his Pokémon join Ash's group to experience a different training style. They had gained something from it.

Ash had wanted a private battle with Ethan, but Ethan refused. “Save the main attraction for the Ever Grande Conference,” he had said. “Why enjoy it early?”

That had persuaded Ash. He stopped asking and looked forward to their eventual meeting even more.

Misty, meanwhile, felt she had wasted the entire day guarding Ethan. Lisia had not appeared once, even by bedtime. Misty was pleased, but frustrated too. A persistent rival she could confront. Lisia's unpredictable appearances were harder to defend against. Misty never knew when or where she might strike again.

Vivian was still speaking. At last, she raised her voice. “Now, please welcome our 64 Coordinators to the Appeal Stage!”

Applause and cheers filled the arena. Ethan waved his light stick gently. Amid that sea of excitement, the first round began.

A boy in his teens ran to center stage, leaped high, and released Ninetales. It breathed flames as soon as it appeared, producing a striking opening appeal that drew applause.

Going first was difficult. The audience had not yet settled into the show, and many minds were still elsewhere. Even an excellent act could lose some of its effect. The boy handled the pressure and gave the Festival a strong start.

Unexpectedly, Harley came out second. He emerged from the competitors' passage smiling, blowing kisses and waving to the stands. Nothing in his manner recalled his humiliating retreat the previous day. That defeat had done nothing to shake his composure; in one respect, his resilience was formidable.

Perhaps Gengar had hurt Cacturne too badly for it to recover overnight. Harley had brought a Banette instead.

Banette greeted the arena with a chilling laugh and a frightening expression. The air seemed to grow colder. The audience went quiet. People whispered to one another, wondering what Harley meant to do.

He soon showed them. At his command, Banette conjured a ring of Will-O-Wisps and then used Thunder, calling a powerful bolt down onto its own body. It kept laughing within the golden lightning. The glare made its grin more eerie still.

As Vivian exclaimed, it was a dark, unsettling performance with an explosive impact. The crowd stared in astonishment.

The act had little to do with conventional beauty, yet its strange style expanded their idea of what an appeal could be. Harley earned a high score: 89 points, just one short of the next ten-point mark.

“That...” Ash stared for a while before managing, “I've never seen an appeal like that.”

“Neither have I,” Ethan said. “It's an unusual direction, but it works. In a way, Harley's personality suits this style. I have to give him credit.”

Though of course Harley would put his creativity into something unsettling.

More than a dozen Coordinators followed. Their performances varied. Strong acts scored over eighty, and one entrant with a Ledyba even reached ninety. Others landed in the seventies; the lowest earned only 72, all but ending that contestant's run even before everyone had performed.

It was both splendid and unforgiving. Whatever each entrant's result, the atmosphere in the arena grew steadily livelier.

Soon it was May's turn. Her pretty, cheerful appearance earned an immediate round of applause. Instead of Beautifly, she released Skitty, which came out full of energy and used Assist at once. To everyone's surprise, it produced Silver Wind.

White light shone around Skitty's paws. A spiraling wind thick with glittering scales swept upward from them.

Assist, sometimes called Cat's Paw, was a Normal-type move available to only a few feline Pokémon. It borrowed a move at random from one of the user's teammates. Regardless of the borrowed move's type or how unsuitable the user's body might seem, a strange power let it perform the move once.

That unpredictability made Assist a clever technique in battle, but also a risky one. A borrowed move might create trouble for its user. This time, Skitty had drawn Silver Wind from May's Beautifly. A cat performing a Bug-type move was surprising enough; the sparkling scales drifting overhead made the sight especially delightful.

May looked pleased and ordered Assist again. Skitty borrowed Bulbasaur's Razor Leaf, sending a wind full of leaves across the stage.

Whether that was her plan from the start or she had been encouraged by her luck, May kept using Assist. Combusken's Flamethrower, Bulbasaur's Vine Whip, Beautifly's String Shot and Gust—one little cat performed move after move that was not its own. The audience loved it. May and Skitty worked in perfect step, giving them an exciting appeal.

But Assist's uncertainty could work against them.

On the next use, Skitty produced Solar Beam, a powerful Grass-type attack. It should have been a chance for an especially brilliant display. Instead, as the move began, Skitty froze. May's commands stopped too.

The big screen caught even a moment's hesitation. Their pause was far longer than a moment. The audience frowned, wondering what had happened. Ethan and Misty were just as puzzled.

“Why isn't she continuing?” Misty asked.

“Well...” Brock hesitated. “As far as I know, none of May's current Pokémon can use Solar Beam.”

He did not need to finish. Ethan understood. A move neither May nor Skitty expected had broken the rhythm of the act, and any mistake was magnified on a stage like this. But if none of May's partners knew Solar Beam, how had Skitty borrowed it?

There was no time to consider that question. May recovered from her confusion and ordered Assist again. This time Skitty used Petal Dance. Pink petals filled the air, drawing the audience back into the performance and easing the effect of the earlier pause.

Then another problem appeared. Skitty's eyes turned into spirals, and it staggered around the stage as though drunk.

Ethan recognized the side effect of Petal Dance at once: the move could leave its user confused. One accident had followed another before May could recover from the first.

Most spectators could not tell. They thought Skitty was spinning about on purpose, showing off its playful sense of humor. Laughter spread through the stands at the adorable Pokémon's antics.

Only Ethan and the others who knew what they were seeing understood how alarmed May must be. Fortunately, she had grown a great deal. Her face betrayed none of her panic, and in a moment she thought of a way to save the act.
