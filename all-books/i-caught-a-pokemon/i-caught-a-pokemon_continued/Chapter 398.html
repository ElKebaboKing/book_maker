After waiting a long time without seeing Banette escape, Morty raised a hand to his forehead.

"Stop... Uh... Open your mouth. I surrender."

Only after the referee raised his arm did Ethan snap his fingers, signaling Crystal Steelix to spit Banette out.

At his command, Crystal Steelix opened its mouth and spat forcefully as though expelling a mouthful of saliva. The next instant, the battered Banette flew from its jaws and landed on the ground.

It rolled several times before finally coming to rest flat on its back, limp and completely unresponsive. It had clearly lost consciousness.

Fortunately, its body was as soft as a doll and seemed to contain no bones. Even after being crushed inside Crystal Steelix's mouth for so long, it had suffered no life-threatening injuries.

It had simply fainted from the pain.

Of course, being swallowed was a terrible experience. Hopefully Banette would not be traumatized by it in the future.

Morty recalled Banette with a complicated expression, took a deep breath, and fixed Ethan with a burning gaze.

"You're even stronger than I imagined. You actually defeated my Banette. It seems I can't hold anything else back. It's time to send out my strongest Pokémon!"

Truthfully, before the battle, he had expected an ordinary Gym challenge. He had never imagined matters would develop like this, much less that he would need to use his ace.

But the man before him had won two matches with ease. He was clearly no ordinary Trainer!

Morty had to win at least one round!

Taking a deep breath, he solemnly drew his third Poké Ball.

"Go, my ace!"

Like an expert pitcher, Morty hurled the Poké Ball onto the field. Light flashed, and a strange Pokémon more than two meters tall appeared before Crystal Steelix, floating in the air as though it wore an iron helmet.

Ethan recognized it at once.

"A Dusknoir..."

Dusknoir's body was almost entirely black. It possessed a ghostly tail instead of feet and a gray head shaped like a medieval knight's helmet, though it had only one red eye. Its large, round belly appeared to bear an eerie face!

Like Banette, Dusknoir was not native to the Johto region.

Ethan took out his Pokédex and aimed it at Dusknoir.

The Pokédex announced, "Beep. Dusknoir, the Gripper Pokémon. A Ghost type. Dusknoir is said to travel between this world and the afterlife, though no one knows whether it possesses a will of its own. The antenna on its head receives radio waves from the spirit world, and it follows those instructions to carry people there. It also appears beside those approaching the end of their lives and guides their souls into the underworld. Occasionally, however, it mistakenly takes the souls of people whose time has not yet come."

Judging by that description, Dusknoir possessed an undeniably intimidating mystique.

Guiding souls made it sound just like the Grim Reaper!

Whether that was merely excessive speculation or an ability it truly possessed, Ethan could not say.

Whatever the truth, this was Morty's ace Pokémon. Its strength could not be underestimated!

Ethan recalled Crystal Steelix. As he hesitated over which Pokémon to send out next, Gengar suddenly poked its head from his shadow.

"Let me handle this battle!"

"You?"

Ethan gave it a surprised look, unsure why it had volunteered.

Gengar loved pranks and enjoyed watching excitement unfold, but it usually disliked trouble—or, more accurately, it was rather lazy. Unless Ethan asked, it rarely offered to battle anyone.

Seeing Ethan's expression, Gengar grinned. "Don't look at me like that. I'm just itching for a fight. Besides, now that I think about it, it's been a long time since my last Gym battle!"

Since Gengar wanted to fight, Ethan naturally had no reason to refuse.

"All right, but be careful. That's the Gym Leader's ace, so it must be very strong!"

Gengar cackled. "His ace? Aren't I your ace too? Besides, the ace of a Champion sounds far more impressive than the ace of a mere Gym Leader!"

Now that was the art of speaking!

Those words sounded wonderful. Ethan thoroughly enjoyed Gengar's flattery and gave it a large thumbs-up.

"You're right. Good luck, then. You're the best!"

Gengar grinned and floated onto the battlefield.

On the opposite side, Morty had not heard what Gengar and Ethan said to each other, but he had clearly seen Gengar open its mouth and speak!

Suppressing his astonishment, he studied Gengar with great interest, his gaze traveling repeatedly up and down its body.

"Incredible! A Gengar that can speak? How did you accomplish that?"

Morty was clearly fascinated by Gengar!

In this world, aside from a handful of unusually oblivious people, anyone who saw a Pokémon speak human language would react with the same astonishment as Morty!

"What's so strange about it? Isn't speaking easy?" Gengar replied carelessly. "Besides me, I've met a Meowth and a Slowking that could speak human languages too."

"Wait, how could that possibly be easy!?" Morty did not believe it at all and continued questioning Gengar. "When did you learn to speak?"

"Kid, why do you have so many questions? Are we fighting or not?" Gengar frowned impatiently, apparently finding the man far too talkative. To stop him from asking question after question, it continued, "Since you insist on knowing, listen closely. I learned to speak several centuries before you were even a fertilized egg!"

Morty fell silent.

Hah. This Gengar really knows how to talk...

Ignoring it, Morty turned toward Ethan.

"As you can see, I'm a Ghost-type Trainer, and I love all kinds of Ghost-type Pokémon. Your Gengar is fascinating and unique. I realize this is rather forward, but would you consider trading it to me?"

Trade away my Gengar?

Dream on...

Ethan shook his head coldly. "Sorry. This Gengar is an important partner of mine. I would never trade it away!"

Seeing Ethan's firm attitude and complete unwillingness to negotiate, Morty sighed in disappointment.

"What a shame."

On the field, Gengar listened to Morty ramble on and finally urged him, "Hey, kid, are we fighting or not? I didn't come out here to listen to you discuss me!"

Morty raised his eyebrows. Seeing Gengar's impatient expression, he realized they had indeed delayed for too long. He nodded and grew serious.

"You're right. We've talked long enough. Let's begin!"
