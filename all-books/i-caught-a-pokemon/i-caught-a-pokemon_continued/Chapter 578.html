Both Pokémon vanished beneath the water. Underwater cameras gave the audience a fixed view of what happened below, but the Trainers could no longer see their partners directly. Their commands could not reach through the pool, either.

Now the battle depended on the Pokémon's own judgment.

On the stadium screens, Golduck's streamlined body, long tail, and webbed feet made it look swift in the water.

Blastoise was no less capable.

It had helped Ethan win the racing competition in Altomare. A straight sprint might not be its strength, but its powerful muscles gave it tremendous force and bursts of speed.

Glub... glub...

The crowd could not hear the fighting underwater. The images still showed how fierce it was.

Golduck used its agility to circle Blastoise, darting in from one side and then another to rake at it.

Blastoise had its hard shell for protection. It needed only to face Golduck's attacks. Whenever it turned a little, Golduck had to swim a much wider arc and spend more energy finding another opening.

As the fight continued, Jon noticed something was wrong.

Blastoise drew its head and limbs into its shell. The great shell began spinning, faster and faster. Soon the water in the pool was turning with it.

Oh no! "Golduck, get away from there!"

Jon shouted toward the bottom of the pool, but Golduck could not hear him.

Fortunately, it sensed the danger for itself and swam for the shore. It was already too late. A whirlpool had formed underwater.

Golduck was not large enough to fight the powerful current. It had barely made any progress before the suction pulled it back toward the center.

The whirlpool's effect spread to the surface. Even without the screens, spectators could see a huge spiral opening in the water and guess what was happening below.

Then water roared upward. A waterspout rose from the pool and climbed high into the air.

Golduck was caught at its top, battered by the rushing water.

It was an underwater tactic Ethan had used to win many battles. Now Jon was experiencing its power.

Jon's face changed. He took out Golduck's Poké Ball to recall it, but the waterspout rose beyond the ball's range.

When the column finally dissolved and Golduck fell, it dropped too fast for Jon to aim the recall beam. He tried several times and missed his chance.

Bang!

Golduck struck the ground hard. As the dust cleared, it lay unconscious.

Splash!

Blastoise jumped from the pool and landed on the central ground.

The referee raised his red flag. "Golduck is unable to battle! Blastoise wins the second round!"

Applause filled the stands. After only two rounds, many spectators felt Ethan's championship was becoming inevitable.

Jon recalled Golduck, his expression grave. While Ethan seemed at ease, Jon's spirits sank.

He looked across at Ethan, clenched his teeth, and reached for his ace.

"Go, Rapidash!"

"Oh? Jon is sending out his strongest Pokémon already!" the announcer exclaimed. "Rapidash has been unbeaten in his earlier matches and carried him through this tournament. But we're only at the third round, still in the first half. Is this too soon?"

Too soon? Jon could only smile bitterly.

Against another Trainer, he might not have needed Rapidash at all. His other Pokémon could have carried him through.

Now he had to stop this run of defeats.

If he kept losing, what good would a win in the final round do? He would have nothing left to face Ethan's remaining team. Better to stake everything on a fight now.

Ethan knew Rapidash was Jon's strongest Pokémon, even though it had not been his first partner.

He could leave Blastoise on the field and take advantage of the type matchup. That would be straightforward. Such an easy choice no longer interested him much.

What should he choose instead?

"This round is yours." Ethan recalled Blastoise and threw another Poké Ball. "Go, Dragonite!"

Dragonite, Tyranitar, and Scizor were the newer generation of his team. They would carry his next regional journey, so he wanted them to gain as much battle experience as possible.

Jon breathed easier when he saw Dragonite.

From Ethan's matches, he knew that Dragonite and some of the other newer Pokémon were a little less formidable than veterans like Gardevoir, Jolteon, Crystal Steelix, and Gengar. He had a better chance against one of them.

If he could turn the match around, it had to begin here.

Both Trainers looked satisfied with their choices. At the signal, they ordered their Pokémon forward.

Rapidash was a Fire type; Dragonite was Dragon and Flying. Neither held an obvious type advantage.

That did not tell the whole story. Their individual abilities mattered too.

Rapidash, a running Pokémon, had an impressive speed in the Pokédex. Dragonite's listed speed was lower, but its wings let it fly fast enough to keep pace with Rapidash.

Rapidash relied mainly on Fire- and Normal-type attacks. Dragonite had a wider range: Dragon and Flying moves, along with some Electric and Water moves. Even a single Aqua Tail could surprise Rapidash at close range.

Both had paths to victory. The uncertainty made the round more exciting.

Dragonite twisted aside in midair and dodged Rapidash's Fire Blast. Then it dived.

Rapidash ran, trying to leave it behind.

They chased and clashed all around the field. Dragonite did not always have the initiative. Rapidash lived up to its reputation as Jon's ace, finding chances to strike back.

When Dragonite missed one dive, Rapidash kicked it with its front hooves.

The struggle remained close. Both had taken damage after only a short while.

Then one of Rapidash's Fire attacks burned Dragonite. Every movement brought it fresh pain.

Another Trainer might have recalled it until the burn subsided.

Ethan hesitated when he saw the determination in Dragonite's eyes. He shook his head and let it continue.

Dragonite wanted to win. Recalling it now would hurt its confidence.

It had a way to ease the pain. Dragonite dived into the pool and leaped out again. Water clinging to its body soothed the burn.

As with rinsing a burn under clean water, the relief was immediate.

"A clever choice!" the announcer said. "The water won't remove the burn completely, but it has eased the pain!"

Dragonite returned to the fight with even greater ferocity. It ignored its injuries and closed the distance instead of attacking from afar. The burn had already taken hold; it might as well press the attack.

Thunder Punch met Rapidash's Flame Wheel, and an unexpected Aqua Tail lashed across Rapidash's body.

Perhaps Dragonite's relentless assault rattled Rapidash. It could neither outrun its pursuer nor break away. In the end, both Pokémon exhausted each other and fell together.

The audience gasped as they collapsed.

A draw? Rapidash was Jon's strongest Pokémon, while Dragonite was considered one of Ethan's less experienced partners. Few had expected them to bring each other down.

Ethan was surprised too. He had already been ready to send out his next Pokémon, only for Dragonite to give him this welcome result.

It really was growing fast through this tournament.

Jon shook his head helplessly. Losing his ace took much of the fight out of him. He knew it would be harder than ever to stop Ethan.

So it proved. After halftime, Jon lost his earlier aggression. His Quagsire, Hitmontop, and Marowak fought cautiously, defending far more often than they attacked. Jon's commands became cautious too—a complete reversal of his usual style.

Ethan answered those three Pokémon with Scizor, Tyranitar, and Gardevoir. Each won its round. Three successive victories ended the match.

Marowak had barely fallen when salutes thundered around Ethan. Before he fully realized he had won, cheers rose from the stands and ribbons flew through Silver Stadium.

"We have our champion!" the announcer shouted. "The favorite has delivered! Congratulations to Ethan, winner of this year's Silver Conference. After the Indigo League, he has now won two championships in a row!"

Ethan stood still for a moment. He was happy, though not as excited as he had been after his first title.

He had won already?

The rounds had passed almost routinely. The match had held few surprises.

Jon simply had not challenged him enough. Ethan judged him to be roughly as strong as Harrison, and neither had been especially difficult to face.

Kanto and Johto shared a continent and had close ties with little cultural difference. In Ethan's view, they scarcely needed separate League tournaments. One combined competition could bring all their strongest Trainers together.

They even shared the Elite Four. A combined tournament would be more exciting than one in which, apart from a few standouts, the competitors varied so widely in strength.

On the other hand, the Indigo and Silver Conferences had been good first stages for Ethan. They had given him experience and helped train his Pokémon for harder battles ahead.

He came back to himself, smiled broadly, and waved to the cheering crowd.

Jon shook his hand with visible disappointment. Then the referee directed Ethan to the awards platform.

League President Charles Goodshow was waiting. He placed a gold medal around Ethan's neck, took a shining trophy from an attendant, and handed it to him.

"Congratulations, young man," Goodshow said with a kindly smile. Ethan thought he detected a trace of bitterness beneath it. "You've won the Silver Conference. That's your second championship. Quite an achievement!"

Goodshow's words were not entirely wholehearted. He sincerely congratulated Ethan, but part of him was disappointed.

As League president, he had to think about the competition as a whole. Two consecutive titles were a great honor for one Trainer. For the others, watching the same person win again could be discouraging.

"Thank you, President Goodshow." Ethan had not noticed his conflicted mood. "Don't worry. I'll keep working hard on my travels and try to win another trophy!"

Goodshow's smile faltered. That was not what he had meant. He could hardly say so aloud, though. "Then... good luck. I'm sure you'll do well."
