“Let you go?” Ethan looked Shelly up and down. “That depends on what you can offer me in return. Start by telling me where you're going.”

He looked threatening enough that Shelly, remembering his strength, had no thought of resisting. But she hesitated over her purpose, plainly reluctant to speak.

Ethan shook his head. “You still don't understand your position. If you won't tell me, I won't waste time arguing. Once I've handed you over, you can explain everything to the International Police from a cell.”

He drew two Poké Balls and threw them. Dragonite and Tyranitar appeared on opposite sides of Shelly, trapping her between them.

“W-wait! I'll tell you!” she said quickly.

She knew he was serious. After another moment's hesitation, she seemed to make a difficult choice.

“After our last operation against you—no, after we fled from you—I took a group to the Weather Institute near Fortree City. We occupied it, and I used its equipment to investigate and simulate the ocean's effect on Hoenn. Only then did I realize how ignorant and absurd the goal Team Aqua has pursued since its founding really is.”

She meant the vision promoted by Team Aqua's leader, Archie: to make the world an ideal home for Pokémon by awakening and controlling Kyogre through Primal Reversion.

Team Aqua had thrown itself into that supposedly noble cause even though the world saw it as an evil organization. Hearing one of its commanders reject the goal now surprised Misty. Ethan, who knew the games' story, had long understood the danger.

“What did you discover?” he asked.

Shelly shuddered. “Archie was naive. Primal Kyogre would never obey anyone. It would keep raising the sea and creating extreme weather. Its whirlpools could swallow everything!”

Ethan sighed. “Wasn't that obvious? How did it take you this long to see it?”

Shelly looked embarrassed. Archie was openhearted and had won his followers' trust. None of them had questioned his dream. If she had not investigated it herself a few days ago, she might still believe in it.

“So you're going back to stop Archie?” Ethan asked.

She nodded. “I left the group and hurried here. I have to stop him before he wakes Kyogre.”

Ethan caught the implication. “Archie knows where Kyogre is?”

Shelly hesitated again. Ethan's grave expression made her answer. “Yes. And some time ago, he obtained a blue orb with a strange power from Mt. Pyre. They say it can awaken Kyogre and cause its Primal Reversion.”

That complicates things. Ethan rubbed his chin.

Mt. Pyre was a resting place for departed Pokémon, much like Pokémon Tower in Kanto, Lost Tower in Sinnoh, or Celestial Tower in Unova. Mist, the wishes of the dead, and many Ghost-type Pokémon kept most people away. Those same qualities made it a fitting place to guard objects that should not fall into human hands.

Ethan did not know how the Blue Orb had been made or who had placed it there. He knew only that it had a counterpart, the Red Orb, and that their powers could awaken Kyogre and Groudon and drive them into their primal forms.

With both the Blue Orb and Kyogre's location in Archie's hands, awakening Kyogre was only a matter of time. If the Pokémon broke free and threw all Hoenn into chaos, who could stop it?

“How do you plan to stop Archie?” Ethan asked.

“I...I'll try to talk him out of it.” Shelly looked troubled.

“Talk him out of it? Do you really think he'll listen?”

She fell silent. Archie was charismatic but stubborn. He had pursued this dream for years and was now close to achieving it. A few words from her were unlikely to change his mind.

Her expression told Ethan she had no confidence in her plan. He sighed, signaled Dragonite and Tyranitar to step back, and made an offer.

“I'd like to be rid of Team Aqua's interference for good, but this concerns all of Hoenn. Let's make a deal.”

“A deal?” Shelly stared at him. She had always treated Ethan as Team Aqua's greatest threat. She had never imagined working with him, though she could guess what he wanted.

“What kind of cooperation?” she asked cautiously.

“I'll pretend I never saw you and let you go. In return, you take me to Archie.”

“And what will you do when you find him?”

“What I do best. Defeat his Pokémon, put an end to this fantasy, and return the orb to Mt. Pyre.”

Shelly's brow tightened as she wrestled with the choice. Bringing Ethan to Archie would betray her leader and Team Aqua. Even if it stopped him, she would have no place in the organization afterward; her comrades would despise her.

Yet without Ethan, she had no real way to stop Archie. Letting him proceed could doom Hoenn and betray the reason she had joined Team Aqua in the first place.

Ethan gave her time to decide. He looked toward the distant sea. He did not suspect a trap. He knew the broad course of these events from the games and what might happen if no one intervened.

As a traveling Trainer, he could have left the matter to the authorities, the Pokémon League, or the Elite Four. He was not obliged to handle every crisis himself.

But could he, someone who thought of himself as a decent person, truly ignore a danger unfolding in front of him? He might be closer than anyone else to the heart of it—and best placed to stop it.

There was another reason, too. He wanted to see Kyogre for himself, to learn how it compared with the Pokémon of the games and anime, and how overwhelming it really was.

Shelly took a deep breath and nodded firmly. “All right. I'll take you to Archie. But promise me one thing: don't attack the other members—”

“You still don't understand your position,” Ethan cut in coldly. “Are you so used to being a commander that you think you can give me orders? What gives you the standing to set terms? I'm the one holding the advantage.”

Shelly went still. She remembered that she had nothing with which to bargain. After a long breath, she said dejectedly, “I understand. Can we leave now?”

She had faced the situation and made her choice. Ethan was satisfied, though he quietly told Gengar to watch her in case she changed her mind or tried to escape.

After discussing their next steps, they agreed to follow Shelly's original plan: go to the cave where Kyogre was hiding, find Archie, and decide how to act from there.

Before they left, Ethan sent Anabel a message outlining the danger. He could not guarantee success. If Kyogre did cause a tsunami, advance warning might at least give people at sea time to get clear.

The three of them continued through the cargo warehouses toward a secret Team Aqua facility. Only senior members knew of the small base. It held supplies and, Shelly said, a craft they could use for the journey.

As Shelly led the way, Misty fell back beside Ethan. “Do you really trust her? Something about this feels wrong.”

“Don't worry.” Ethan patted her head. “I think she's telling the truth. Even if it is a trap, I can get you out safely.”

“That's not what I mean. She changed her mind so quickly. Is Kyogre really powerful enough to drown all of Hoenn?”

“Don't underestimate Legendary Pokémon.” Ethan glanced at Shelly, who seemed too preoccupied to hear, and lowered his voice. “You saw Rayquaza fight Deoxys. Rayquaza was incredibly strong, but it wouldn't be enough against Primal Kyogre without taking on a stronger form itself. Kyogre could raise the sea and create enormous whirlpools with a flick of its tail.”

Misty stuck out her tongue in alarm. “That strong? Can we really stop it? Your strongest Pokémon aren't with you right now.”

“That's what worries me too. But if we take the orb before Archie makes his mistake, I don't think Kyogre will undergo Primal Reversion.”

“I hope we can stop him.” Misty paused. “Wait—you aren't doing all this because you want to catch Kyogre, are you?”

“Of course not. I know which Pokémon I can catch and which I shouldn't. Kyogre and Groudon should be left undisturbed in their corners of the world.”

“What about Rayquaza? You looked very interested in the one we saw.”

“That's different. Having one more or one less Rayquaza wouldn't change the world. But I'm not after the one we met. I want a cooler-looking one.” Ethan waved the subject away. “That's for another day. We need to focus on this crisis.”

They reached a warehouse at the far end of the cargo district. Shelly entered a code, and its heavy door opened automatically.

In the center lay a pool connected to the sea. A small submarine bearing Team Aqua's emblem rested in the water.

Shelly did not start the submarine yet. She went to a shelf, took down two sets of clothes, and handed them to Ethan and Misty.

“To avoid unnecessary trouble and get close to Archie more easily, put these uniforms on first.”

Ethan had not expected her to think of that, but he had no objection. Reaching Archie quickly mattered most. Team Aqua's uniforms looked ugly to him, yet the thought of infiltrating their ranks in disguise gave him an odd thrill.
