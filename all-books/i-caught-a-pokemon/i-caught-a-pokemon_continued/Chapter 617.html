Flannery's third Pokémon was a Camerupt.

Ethan had battled Courtney's Camerupt not long ago. Its abilities were still fresh in his mind, so this time he felt better prepared. Having faced one before did not, of course, guarantee a victory.

He sent Scizor against it.

Frankly, it was a terrible matchup. Scizor was both Bug and Steel type, and Fire-type attacks were four times as effective against it. One blast of flame could leave it in a dreadful state, if it didn't knock it out outright. Even Ash, for all his enthusiasm for battling at a type disadvantage, would rarely choose a Pokémon with a fourfold weakness to its opponent.

Flannery frowned when she saw Ethan's choice. She wanted to ask whether he'd lost his senses or grown overconfident. But his eyes were as calm as ever. He must have done this deliberately.

Either he had tremendous faith in Scizor, or he had some other plan. After seeing Tyranitar and Dragonite fight, Flannery preferred the first explanation. Was Scizor his strongest Pokémon?

Once they began, she realized she had overestimated it. Scizor was certainly strong. Its attacks and mobility were impressive, especially against a slow Pokémon like Camerupt. It twisted and darted around the field, finding plenty of openings. Double Team and Agility helped it avoid attack after attack. It was giving everything it had.

But it could not overcome the type disadvantage. Scizor could succeed a thousand times; it couldn't afford a single mistake.

Camerupt was not one of Flannery's aces, but it was no weakling. Even under Ethan's careful direction, Scizor could hardly fight an opponent like this without leaving an opening. As it kept moving, its stamina fell and its speed began to drop.

Flannery seized the chance. She predicted where Scizor would land and ordered Camerupt to unleash a wide-ranging burst of flame over that part of the field.

Fire swept across it. Scizor had nowhere to go. The flames engulfed it, and it cried out in pain. The hit was devastating.

After finally finding a path to victory, Flannery held on to it. Before Scizor could escape, she had Camerupt pour out more fire. Another wave of searing heat brought Scizor down.

She had won!

Ethan could only shrug helplessly. He hadn't thrown the round on purpose. After Tyranitar and Dragonite, Scizor had simply been next in the order he'd chosen. He had also wanted to see whether it could win despite a fourfold weakness. Evidently it still needed more training.

Flannery jumped with excitement. She was so openly delighted to have won a round that she seemed not to consider her opponent's feelings at all. Ethan couldn't help smiling.

She really did act exactly as she pleased. That could be troublesome for the people around her, but it was also appealing. He meant that as simple appreciation, not romantic interest.

He recalled Scizor and looked again at Camerupt. It seemed every bit as strong as Courtney's; the two were probably evenly matched. That said something about the resources behind Team Magma and Team Aqua. If one of their commanders could command a Pokémon at a Gym Leader's level, it was no wonder those organizations had operated so brazenly in Hoenn for so long.

Ethan's fingers hovered between Tyranitar's and Dragonite's Poké Balls. At last he chose Dragonite, the more exhausted of the two.

Yes, the more exhausted Dragonite. The less favorable the situation, the more he wanted his Pokémon to fight through it. That was how he could build their strength.

Flannery was startled to see Dragonite return. This was the last battle, and Tyranitar was in better condition. Why send out Dragonite? After a moment's thought, she finally understood: Ethan was using their Gym battle to train.

She had only been a Gym Leader for a short while, but she knew how most challengers prepared. They chose Pokémon to exploit the Leader's type, made sure their team was rested, and came to the Gym only when everything was ready. Ethan seemed to feel none of that pressure. He treated a Gym challenge like an ordinary battle he might find anywhere—and was even looking for a chance to train.

What nerve.

Another Gym Leader might have felt looked down on. A proud one might have taken offense. Flannery felt only more curious about him. No wonder his Pokémon were so strong: he never wasted a chance to improve them.

Curiosity would not win the match for her, though. When Dragonite was ready, she put the thought aside and focused on the field.

This round was different. Ethan no longer had to worry about a disastrous type matchup, and he attacked without restraint. Flannery's good mood quickly vanished as the battle turned against her.

Dragonite's advantage over a ground-bound opponent was obvious. It had been injured earlier, but Scizor had worn Camerupt down as well. Dragonite had also rested for a while in its Poké Ball. Neither Pokémon entered this round in much better shape than the other.

Unlike Magmortar, Camerupt couldn't keep Dragonite at bay with overwhelming firepower. Under Dragonite's fierce assault, it belched thick smoke and sprayed great quantities of magma in resistance, but neither was enough.

It did not last long. Dragon Rage struck Camerupt. Its massive body wavered, then toppled.

Thud! It landed against a huge rock with a dull crash.

Ethan could feel the difference Flannery had described. This Camerupt really was a step below Torkoal and Magmortar. The round had been less intense than the first two, and he had won it more easily.

“I lost. You're really good.”

Flannery recalled Camerupt and strode over to Ethan with an open smile. She had lost the match, but accepted it wholeheartedly. That alone set her apart from many Gym Leaders. Some pretended not to mind when a challenger beat them, yet their tone and movements betrayed their discomfort. Flannery showed none of it.

“Your Pokémon were impressive too. I got lucky this time,” Ethan replied politely.

Then the two stood looking at each other, each apparently waiting for the other to speak. An awkward silence settled over them.

Misty finally cleared her throat. “Um, Miss Flannery? Aren't you forgetting something?”

“Forgetting what?” Flannery turned to her in confusion. Only when Misty said “badge” did realization dawn.

“Oh! I forgot!” She slapped her forehead and hurriedly searched her jeans pockets.

Ethan and Misty exchanged speechless looks. After spending this long with Flannery, they should have expected it. There was little they could do about having met such a forgetful new Gym Leader.

After searching for a while, Flannery realized she didn't have the badge on her. “Sorry! Please wait here. I must have put it somewhere else. I'll go look...”

Before either of them could answer, she dashed out of the room.

“Oh...” Misty sighed at her retreating back. She turned to Ethan, worry in her voice. “Can she really handle being a Gym Leader?”

“Probably... maybe... I hope so.” Ethan couldn't be sure. Her enthusiasm was precious, at least. He hoped she would keep it.

They waited long enough for Misty to grow impatient and consider going to check on her. At last Flannery came running back, out of breath.

She stopped before Ethan and, without waiting to catch her breath, held out an orange-red badge shaped like a flame.

“Here! The Lavaridge Gym's Heat Badge!”

Ethan took a closer look. Its colors shifted from a bright red circle at the bottom to a flame rising above it. It was striking—perhaps the best-looking of all the badges he had collected.

“Thanks!”

He placed the Heat Badge in his badge case. Then he and Misty said goodbye and left the Lavaridge Gym. Flannery accompanied them all the way to the entrance, waved, and went back inside.

With his second Hoenn badge won, Ethan was one step closer to the Ever Grande Conference. It was already late, so he and Misty decided to spend the night in Lavaridge Town.

Ethan saw the anticipation on Misty's face and knew what she had in mind. He smiled. “I know, I know. We're in a town famous for its hot springs. It would be a shame not to have a soak.”

“Yes! Hooray!” Misty cheered. Her delight lifted Ethan's mood too, and he laughed.

They went first to the Pokémon Center and left their injured Pokémon for treatment. Then they headed to the hot spring district and checked into a guesthouse that looked especially clean.

The guesthouse had its own hot spring—and, as it happened, it was a mixed bath. Misty was thrilled. Whatever she was imagining made her cheeks turn red, and she kept stealing glances at Ethan.

Ethan had no idea what she was thinking. After a generous dinner, they washed and wrapped themselves in towels before going out to the open-air spring behind the guesthouse.

Lavaridge had so many hot springs that, despite the crowds of tourists in town, the two of them appeared to be the guesthouse's only visitors that night.

Ethan tested the water, stepped in, and sank until only his neck and head remained above the surface. He leaned back against a natural stone and gazed at the stars.

Misty took a deep breath to gather her courage. She slowly entered the spring and sat beside him. Neither spoke. She edged a little closer, then followed his example and looked up at the night sky.

What a beautiful night.

The scene reminded her of the hot spring inn on Cinnabar Island. She had shared a spring with Ethan there too, sitting almost this close. Close enough that she could have touched him simply by reaching out.

What had happened after that? She thought back and suddenly remembered the pink demon, Jigglypuff. It had appeared beside the spring and sung until both of them fell fast asleep. When they woke, their faces had been covered in drawings, exactly as one might expect.

Ah, those were good memories. How had Jigglypuff's song gone again?

Something like...

“Jigglypuff, Jigglypuff... Jigglypuff...”

Yes, that was it! The song sounded just as comforting as she remembered, enough to make her want to drift straight to sleep. Misty nodded as the fading memory came back to her.

“Jigglypuff, Jigglypuff... Jigglypuff...”

The singing came again. Misty's eyelids grew heavier, and her thoughts began to blur. Through the haze, she heard a sudden splash beside her.

Startled, she turned and saw Ethan plunge headfirst into the water. Before she could understand what he was doing, he surfaced again, looking around with a grave expression.

“Misty, get up. Listen! I think that's Jigglypuff's voice!”
