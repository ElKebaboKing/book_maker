With the crowd cheering and their friends worried, Ash and Katie's match began.

Watching a great tournament from the stands was nothing like competing in one. Under the spotlights, tens of thousands of eyes followed two Trainers and two Pokémon. The crowd's mood rose with every fine move and fell with every mistake. Its cheers rolled around Ethan, and he felt his own excitement grow.

This was the atmosphere of the Ever Grande Conference. This was the world's favorite sport. Under its lights, every action seemed larger, every movement more vivid. Even an ordinary move could draw cheers. A remarkable performance could make a Trainer known everywhere. A newcomer might grow through the experience and, with enough luck, leave as a star.

Torkoal and Venomoth had already begun fighting. Ethan ate some popcorn, leaned back, and watched.

Katie shared her Chinese name with Tate of the Mossdeep Gym. Leaving their Pokémon's strength aside, the poised woman on the field made a far more striking impression than the younger Gym Leader. She had earned eight Badges and reached the final tournament, so her battling skill would be no small matter either.

Katie opened with Venomoth's Stun Spore. Torkoal withdrew into its shell to avoid the powder. Katie seemed to have expected that: she ordered Venomoth to use Disable, preventing Torkoal from using Withdraw again.

It was a clever move. A turtle Pokémon relied on its shell for defense and sometimes for attack. Disable sealed the last move its target had used, taking away one of Torkoal's most valuable options.

Ash did not appear concerned. He ordered Flamethrower. Venomoth dodged the flames, and Katie immediately recalled it.

The crowd was confused. Why switch Pokémon so soon after the match began? Ethan understood. Katie was a measured Trainer who gathered information before committing herself. She had led with a Flying Pokémon to probe Ash's lineup. Now she had seen Torkoal and could choose a better matchup.

Blue light flashed. Golduck appeared before Torkoal, just as Ethan expected.

Ash recovered from his surprise and ordered Torkoal to attack before Golduck had settled in. Golduck reacted first. Its Water Gun struck Torkoal and lifted it into the air. Then Golduck ran beneath its landing point and used Fury Swipes the moment it came down.

Claws raked Torkoal again and again. In a few seconds, it had taken dozens of hits. Golduck's final swipe sent it tumbling back beside Ash. Its eyes had turned to spirals. The referee raised the flag and announced the first result.

Max and Morrison exchanged uneasy looks. “She's strong,” Max said. “Is Ash going to lose?” Katie had defeated his first Pokémon with alarming ease.

“One loss doesn't decide a match,” Ethan said calmly. “Katie's style gives Ash trouble, but he'll adjust. It may still be a rough fight. Unless something unexpected happens, I think he'll win.”

His steady voice settled their nerves. Ethan had no proof he could offer, but they trusted the judgment of a two-time Conference champion.

On the big screen, Ash looked more nervous now. Sweat ran down his forehead. To recover a round and gain a type advantage, he sent out Pikachu against Golduck.

Katie gave him no such chance. She recalled Golduck in front of him and sent out Dugtrio instead. She understood type matchups very well.

Misty glanced at Ethan. “Doesn't she fight like you did at the Indigo Conference?”

“A little.” Ethan remembered how he had once needed to plan carefully around type advantages because his Pokémon were not yet overwhelmingly strong. By the Silver Conference he relied on that less. Now he could often ignore matchups entirely. Travel had changed him. Katie fought much as he had in those earlier days.

Type advantage, however, did not always settle a battle with Ash. Dugtrio's Ground type threatened Pikachu and ignored Electric attacks. Its three dark heads crowded together in the center of the field, a formidable answer to an Electric Pokémon. But Pikachu had beaten Ground types in memorable ways before. Ash had earned his reputation for winning against bad matchups.

Katie knew none of that, or did not think it mattered. She smiled confidently. Ash did not retreat either.

At the start of the round, he sent Pikachu forward with Quick Attack. Katie ordered Dugtrio to use Dig. It vanished underground, leaving Pikachu to stop and watch for its return.

Dugtrio did not surface. From below, it used Sand Tomb. The center of the Rock Field collapsed into a widening pit of flowing sand. It reached Pikachu and pulled it in. With the ground slipping beneath its feet, Pikachu could not escape with Quick Attack. Ash told it to use Thunderbolt instead.

The Electric attack did nothing to Dugtrio. Murmurs passed through the stands. Max and the others sighed. Ash could devise wonderfully inventive tactics, then make a basic mistake as if he had forgotten how types worked.

Katie smiled faintly. She ordered Dugtrio to use Double-Edge through the sand. Ash, equally rashly, told Pikachu to meet it with Iron Tail.

The collision exploded. Smoke filled the pit, and Pikachu shot high out of the Sand Tomb toward Ash. For a moment Max and the others were relieved: at least it had escaped.

Ethan narrowed his eyes. Pikachu's posture was wrong. He shook his head. The master of winning bad matchups seemed to have gone missing for now.

“What's wrong?” Misty asked.

Before Ethan could answer, Pikachu hit the ground beside Ash and lay motionless. Its eyes were open, but it could no longer move.

“What?” Max stared. “Pikachu lost?”

They had known Pikachu could lose. They had not expected it to fall so quickly or so completely.

“Ash rushed,” Ethan said. “If he'd focused on escaping Sand Tomb before trying to fight Dugtrio head-on, he could have worked his way back into the round.”

The others agreed, though they knew that a patient retreat was hardly Ash's style. Temperament shaped the way a Trainer fought. With Katie winning two rounds using Dugtrio and Golduck, Ash was in a dangerous position. Ethan's confidence kept his friends from giving up hope.

Ash lifted Pikachu and set it beside him. Then he sent out his third Pokémon: Glalie.

Ethan had never fought or watched Ash's Glalie in person, but he knew its strength from the anime and from Max's accounts. Even without Pikachu and Torkoal, Ash could still turn the match around.

Katie left Dugtrio in. Perhaps she had no clear counter to Glalie, or perhaps she had another plan.

She tried to repeat her underground tactic. As soon as Dugtrio used Dig, Glalie drove a Headbutt into the ground. The impact forced Dugtrio back out. While it was stunned, Glalie hit it with Ice Beam.

Ice was strong against Ground. Together with Headbutt, the attack knocked Dugtrio out almost at once. The crowd was shocked, and Katie was too. Glalie had hit too hard for her to switch Dugtrio away. She should have changed Pokémon before the round began. Two easy victories had made her careless, and now she had paid for it.

Cheers filled the stadium. After watching Katie defeat two of Ash's Pokémon so easily, some spectators had written him off. Glalie's immediate win gave them a contest again. Not everyone wanted the one-sided victories Ethan had delivered; a match that swung back and forth was exciting too.

“At last, a win,” Max said. He and the others exhaled. They had believed Ethan's prediction, but two losses had tested that belief. Now they could see a path back.

Katie recalled Dugtrio and sent out Misdreavus. Neither Pokémon had a type advantage over the other. A Trainer could bring only six Pokémon, after all; no team could cover every matchup.

Glalie had taken no damage in the previous round, and Misdreavus was fresh. Ash opened with Ice Beam. Katie ordered Misdreavus to advance straight through the attack toward Glalie.

The crowd could not understand her choice. Ethan saw it and guessed her plan at once.

So that's what she's doing. She's decisive enough to be ruthless.
