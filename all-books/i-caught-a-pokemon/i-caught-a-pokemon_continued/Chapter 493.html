"A Rainbow Wing and a Silver Wing?"

Kurt frowned at the names. As a master Poké Ball maker, he knew exactly what they were.

Professor Elm and Erika knew less, but their knowledge still gave them a fair idea of where the feathers came from.

Only young Maizie looked to her grandfather in confusion.

"Grandpa, what are those? Are they a Pokémon's feathers?"

Erika liked the girl's innocent, open manner. She answered in Kurt's place. "A Rainbow Wing is a feather from Ho-Oh. A Silver Wing comes from Lugia, the God of the Sea. Both are Legendary Pokémon, so even their feathers hold extraordinary power."

"Ho-Oh... Lugia..."

Maizie murmured the familiar names, her eyes full of longing as her thoughts drifted away.

She had grown up in Johto hearing stories about those Legendary Pokémon and knew how deeply people here revered them.

A Poké Ball made from their feathers must be extraordinary!

Kurt and Professor Elm were far less carefree.

Rare and precious materials did not necessarily make a good Poké Ball. The materials also had to work together. If they clashed, the ball might fail to function even if it could be made at all, and it could pose a safety risk.

Kurt sighed. "One feather represents Ho-Oh's fire, the other Lugia's sea. They're opposing forces. Whoever managed to combine them must be an exceptionally skilled craftsman."

There was one thing he kept to himself: for all his reputation as a master of handmade Poké Balls, he could not have made a ball like this.

Professor Elm turned to Ethan. "Is the ball you heard about this GS Ball?"

Ethan did not answer directly. "I heard that ball has two letters on it. One stands for gold, and the other for silver."

Professor Elm and Kurt looked at the GS Ball again, their eyes resting on its two letters.

It did seem to be the very ball Ethan meant!

"Gold... silver..." Kurt pondered. "But why? Are those the maker's initials, or do they describe the materials?"

Ethan shook his head without committing himself.

He could hardly tell them that the letters referred to the Gold and Silver versions of a game, or that they themselves were characters in games and an animated series!

Professor Elm frowned. "Why go to such lengths to make a single Poké Ball?"

"To catch a powerful Pokémon, of course," Kurt said. "Isn't that why humans make Poké Balls? But Ho-Oh and Lugia stand at the very peak of Pokémon. Obtaining their feathers would already be difficult. What Pokémon could be worth all this effort?"

Professor Elm noticed Ethan's silence and had a sudden thought. "You already have an answer in mind, don't you?"

Ethan nodded. "It's a ball meant to catch time."

"Catch time!?" Kurt and Professor Elm exclaimed together.

"Yes," Ethan explained. "It uses the feathers of two Legendary Pokémon to capture a Pokémon with power over time."

Everyone present except Maizie knew enough about Pokémon to realize which one he meant.

"Celebi..."

Maizie looked from face to face, then asked the beautiful older woman, "What is Celebi?"

Erika gathered what she knew before answering. "Celebi is a Mythical Pokémon. It's said to travel through time and wander from place to place. People also call it the God of the Forest. It can heal wounds and make plants grow thick and strong."

"A Mythical Pokémon?"

Maizie caught on to the unfamiliar term and looked up, her little eyes full of questions.

At her age, she still knew very little of the world. She had heard of Legendary Pokémon, but never of Mythical Pokémon.

Professor Elm nodded. "Mythical Pokémon are extremely rare. Sometimes they're spoken of alongside Legendary Pokémon as a separate group; sometimes they're included among them."

His words left the room heavy with thought once more.

Who would make a ball like this to capture a god of time?

After a long silence, Kurt sighed. "That... makes things rather difficult."

The others turned to him.

Kurt gave a bitter smile. "If it were an ordinary Poké Ball, we could just set it aside if we couldn't understand it. But if the GS Ball has such a history, there could be trouble when people learn about it!"

Professor Elm fell silent.

He was right. Once word spread that such a Poké Ball existed, some people would surely want it for themselves. If someone like that came here...

Elm hesitated. "Perhaps... we should give it to the police."

Kurt shook his head.

The police?

He had lived long enough to know how ugly the world could be. If they handed the GS Ball over, a word from someone in power might have it delivered straight to them.

As soon as he made the suggestion, Professor Elm realized the same thing and sighed inwardly.

Yet keeping it at Kurt's house would bring danger to Kurt.

Even if everyone here kept the secret, the ball had already passed through many hands. Anyone who looked into its trail could discover where it was now.

What a problem...

Elm glanced at Kurt. His face was troubled too; clearly, he had reached the same conclusion.

With a sigh, Elm shook his head. Then his gaze happened to pass over Ethan, and an idea struck him.

"Mr. Kurt, I have a favor to ask..."

"Oh?" Kurt looked at him. "Go ahead, Elm. What is it?"

"Now that we know where it came from, further study may not be necessary. And it isn't safe to keep a ball like this here. I think we should entrust it to the person best suited to guard it."

Kurt had lived long enough to understand Professor Elm's meaning at once.

He followed Elm's gaze, and his eyes came to rest on Ethan.
