Wednesday dawned beneath a cloudless sky.

Bright sunlight spread across the whole Indigo Stadium.

Amid wave after wave of cheers, the morning matches had already produced the first two winners in Group A. Now that the lunch break was over, everyone's attention had turned to the afternoon's two battles.

All fifty thousand seats in the main stadium were filled, and voices rang from every direction.

Compared with the smaller preliminary arenas, the scale here was overwhelming. Wherever one looked, people were moving and cheering. Streamers, horns, balloons, and cheering sticks seemed to be everywhere, filling the stadium with a festival atmosphere.

People drank soda and ate popcorn as they lost themselves in the year's biggest tournament.

Ash's match was scheduled for one o'clock in the afternoon.

Ethan, Chloe, Misty, Brock, Delia, and Professor Oak had all taken their seats early to support him.

The commentator's rousing voice warmed up the crowd and introduced both competitors' records. Then, to a roar of applause, Richie entered the field.

But when the commentator called Ash's name, nobody appeared.

He sounded surprised, then called again and again. Still no Ash. Neither the spectators nor the Pokémon League staff had expected this. Staff hurried to the waiting room, but he wasn't there either.

"What's going on?" people asked each other.

"Where did that trainer Ash go?" one spectator wondered.

"Who knows? Maybe he's forfeiting."

The crowd grumbled, but under the tournament rules, Ash could not be disqualified before the match's official start. However impatient they were, the spectators had to keep waiting.

In the stands, Delia looked worried.

"He didn't oversleep again, did he?"

"He couldn't have," Misty said. "He had breakfast with us this morning. After that, I think he got a phone call and rushed out with Pikachu."

"And he still isn't back?" Professor Oak frowned. "Could something have happened to him?"

Misty and Brock were about to get up and search for Ash when Ethan shook his head.

"Let's wait a little longer. Something must have held him up. Whatever it is, I believe he'll make it here at the last moment."

Ethan did not need to guess very hard. Team Rocket had almost certainly caused trouble for Ash.

Even so, he had no intention of intervening. Everyone grew through different experiences, and this was one of the setbacks Ash needed to face. If he overcame it, he would be stronger and more mature for it.

Time passed. The spectators grew more restless, and Misty and Brock became increasingly anxious.

Just as the referee approached Richie to award him a win by default, Richie stopped him. He earnestly asked for the start to be delayed. Seeing how sincere he was, the referee consulted the tournament officials, who agreed.

After all, the League and the audience both wanted an exciting match. Nobody stood to gain much from seeing a trainer advance without a battle because his opponent was late.

The referee allowed another ten minutes. They waited through nearly all of them. Just as the new deadline approached, Ash finally appeared.

"Wait!"

At the sound of his voice, everyone in the stadium looked toward the distant sky.

A Pidgeotto was flying rapidly toward them, pulling a Meowth-shaped hot-air balloon behind it. A damaged bicycle hung beneath the balloon. Ash stood on it with Pikachu, gripping a rope in one hand and looking frantic.

The balloon settled, and Ash jumped down onto the field.

His dramatic entrance caught the crowd's attention and seemed to ease some of their irritation.

"He made it!"

Misty and the others breathed a sigh of relief. But Ethan noticed something else. Just as he remembered from the anime, Ash's Pidgeotto was panting after hauling the balloon here in such a hurry. It did not look fit to battle.

That left Ash at a clear disadvantage.

Because the match had already been delayed, there was no time for him to rest. As soon as both trainers were ready, the referee announced the start.

Ash and Richie opened their Poké Balls at the same time.

In flashes of blue light, Richie sent out Butterfree, while Ash chose Squirtle.

From the fifth round onward, the tournament still used three-on-three battles, but the field was now an ordinary dirt arena.

It looked plain beside the preliminary fields, with fewer surprises and unusual terrain features. That also meant the match would better show each trainer's ability to react in the moment, on fairer ground.

Under their trainers' commands, Butterfree and Squirtle traded attacks. Both took some damage, and for a while neither gained an advantage.

Ethan rubbed his chin, looking from Ash to Richie and back again. The two might have become friends, but neither appeared to be holding back.

That made sense. Knowing Ash, the more he cared about someone, the more determined he became to give them a real fight.

The Pokémon kept grappling and pulling at each other. Compared with the fierce preliminary matches, their struggle looked almost small in this enormous stadium. Spectators shook their heads, unimpressed, and an occasional boo sounded from the stands.

Ash and Richie were too focused to notice.

After another exchange, Richie gave his command. Butterfree released Sleep Powder, catching Squirtle by surprise and covering its face.

Squirtle swayed from side to side. It lasted only a few seconds before toppling over and falling fast asleep.
