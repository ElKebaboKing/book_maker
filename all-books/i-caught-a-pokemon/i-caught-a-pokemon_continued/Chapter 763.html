Ash had chosen his most trusted partner, Pikachu, for the preliminary match. His opponent wore a white martial arts uniform and a red headband. That outfit practically announced a Fighting-type Pokémon. Sure enough, a Hitmonlee stood before him.

Ash had made a good choice: Pikachu was not at a type disadvantage. Then again, Ash might not have cared even if it were. His reputation for battling against unfavorable matchups was well earned.

Ethan and Misty arrived just as the match began. Hitmonlee rushed at Pikachu, trying to fight up close. It swept one leg across the field, but Pikachu leaped high and brought an Iron Tail down on its round head. Hitmonlee struck the ground.

That first exchange said a great deal. Pikachu had taken the initiative, and Ethan doubted this would be a hard fight for Ash.

He was right. Hitmonlee rose and attacked with High Jump Kick. Pikachu avoided it with Quick Attack, then used another Iron Tail to knock Hitmonlee out. It had won without a single Electric-type move.

Ash had grown tremendously. Ethan could no longer think of him as the careless, clumsy child who had started traveling so long ago. Ash had become a difficult opponent.

After the match, they met outside the competitors' passage. Ash's friends introduced the two people with them.

One was a stocky boy with orange hair tied in a ponytail. He wore traditional clothes and spoke in a rustic style, referring to himself with a rough, folksy pronoun. He looked intimidating but was actually rather absentminded. His name was Morrison.

Ash had met him during his recent travels. They had much in common: both fought with spirit and refused to give up. They competed over nearly everything, becoming friends and rivals at once.

Even without the introduction, Ethan knew something about Morrison from the anime. He was one of Ash's Hoenn rivals. He was capable, but fate would pair him with Ash at this Conference, where he would lose and finish in the top sixteen.

The other newcomer was an older man, perhaps two or three years older than Ethan, whose performance at this Conference would outshine nearly everyone else's. Tyson was the torch runner with the hat-wearing Meowth whom May's group had met at the ramen shop and the relay.

Ethan knew his future in the anime: Tyson would win the Ever Grande Conference. That made him Ethan's most serious expected rival here.

He did not know either man personally, so he greeted them with a polite nod.

All the preliminary matches finished within the day, narrowing the field to 256. At sunset, the qualifiers gathered in the main arena for the opening ceremony. Before contestants and spectators, the ceremonial flame reached the raised platform and lit the cauldron. Only then did the Ever Grande Conference truly begin.

After a rest day, the preliminary tournaments began. First came the draw.

All 256 contestants assembled in a hall, giving Ethan his first look at many potential opponents. Besides Morrison and Tyson, he recognized quite a few faces from his travels. Their presence here was a surprise.

The room was crowded and noisy even though everyone spoke quietly. Nearly four hundred others had already been eliminated. Had they all been packed in here too, there would scarcely have been room to breathe.

Nurse Joy explained the rules again. Unlike the opening preliminaries, this stage consisted of three Double Battles per contestant. A Trainer had to win all three to advance; one loss meant elimination. The 32 winners would enter the final tournament, where full six-on-six battles would decide the champion.

Most had read the guidebook, though a few had not learned the format until now. Everyone who had reached this stage was eager to see what came next.

The draw appeared on the big screen. All 256 portraits flashed into place at once. Ethan searched for his own, but Ash called out first.

“Found it! I'm in Arena C this afternoon!”

Morrison seemed determined to be louder. “I've got Arena E this morning!”

They were competing even over who could announce a schedule more forcefully. Ethan shook his head and looked back at the screen. He found his portrait at the lower right. His first match was fourth in the afternoon at Arena F.

He had not been drawn against Ash. That was good news—not because he feared Ash, but because he did not want to send his friend home early. As for Ash beating him? Ethan smiled to himself.

Then he checked his opponent and found another familiar face. Anthony, the man who had operated an unofficial Gym and claimed to own the world's strongest Pokémon, had entered. His supposed marvel had been a Pelipper carrying several Poké Balls in its mouth.

Across the hall, Anthony saw Ethan's portrait too. He remembered the man who had beaten him and tightened his fists, eager for a rematch. “Wait and see. I'll show you how much I've grown since then. I'll avenge that loss!”

They were not truly enemies, but Anthony wanted to settle the score. He also had to defeat Ethan to reach his second match. Either way, he could not afford to lose.

As the group left for the dining hall, a woman stepped into Ethan's path. He was startled for a moment, then recognized her and sighed. Alexa had interviewed him at both the Indigo League and the Silver Conference. She was dressed as a reporter again, with her equipment-laden Gogoat at her side.

“We meet again.” She smiled. “I came early just to get the first interview with you.”

“It's been a while, Alexa,” Ethan said with a wry smile. “You give me too much credit. I'm not going anywhere. There was no need to rush.”

Alexa planted a hand on her hip. “You're a sought-after story. Hoenn hasn't recognized you yet because people here don't know what you can do. Once you advance, reporters will be queuing up. I'd rather get here before them.”

May and Max knew Ethan had won two regional tournaments. Even they were surprised a reporter had traveled to Hoenn specifically for him. Tyson and Morrison were more stunned. They had never heard Ethan's name before today. Why would a journalist seek him out when the preliminary tournaments had not even begun?

Why was no one interviewing them?

Tyson had some fame in Hoenn and had been selected as a torch runner. Surely that made him a better subject. Both men quietly adjusted their clothes and posture, hoping to draw Alexa's eye. She never looked away from Ethan. Their hopes sank.

Hurried footsteps approached. A woman with a microphone and a man carrying a camera ran toward them, breathing hard.

Morrison and Tyson brightened. At last, reporters for them! They prepared to greet the pair. The two ran straight past without a glance and stopped in front of Ethan.

What?

Morrison and Tyson could only stare. The man was taller, better-looking, and in better shape than they were, but what else set him apart?

Ethan recognized the newcomers: the TV Mauville reporter and her cameraman whom he had met in Mauville Park. He tried to recall their names.

“You're... Emma and Ida, right?”

“Gabby and Ty,” they corrected with embarrassed smiles.

“Ah. Sorry.” Ethan had remembered both names wrong.

“It's fine,” Gabby said. She glanced warily at Alexa, who was studying her with interest. “Ethan, do you remember promising me an exclusive interview?”

Ethan searched his memory. He had wanted to escape their persistence in Mauville and had put them off until the Ever Grande Conference. They had remembered and come to collect on the promise.

Now both Alexa and Gabby wanted an interview. Ethan looked between them, unsure whose request to accept first. Alexa understood why Gabby was here, and her expression grew serious. Coming early had been the right decision. Hoenn's reporters were already beginning to notice Ethan.

Morrison finally turned to Ash. “Who is he? Why do all these reporters want him?”

Tyson quietly leaned closer to hear.

“Him...” Ash looked toward the sky, complicated feelings in his voice. “His name is Ethan. He's the person I've spent my life trying to surpass.”

That only confused Morrison and Tyson further. They had spent enough time with Ash to know how direct he usually was. Why answer with a riddle now? Apart from the man's name, they still knew nothing.

But Ash's words and the reporters' attention made one thing clear: Ethan had to be formidable. For the first time, both men studied him seriously.
