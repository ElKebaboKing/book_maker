Beside the road rose a low, bare hill of rock. It was only a dozen or so meters high. Halfway up, behind several boulders, a narrow black opening hid in the stone. Only one person could pass through at a time.

The three climbed the hill in the downpour and hurried inside one after another.

The cave was small, oval-shaped, and had no other entrance. Ethan estimated its floor space at little more than a dozen square meters.

It might once have been a wild Pokémon's den. Its occupant had apparently moved elsewhere long ago. Thick, dry grass remained, but there were no recent signs of habitation and no unpleasant smell.

Small though it was, the cave could hold all three of them. Best of all, its entrance faced away from the typhoon. The wind couldn't reach inside, and even the torrential rain ran downhill past the opening.

The cave mouth felt like an invisible barrier separating them from the storm. Out of the wind and rain at last, all three let out relieved breaths.

Ethan and Misty looked at each other's bedraggled appearance and burst out laughing. Their laughter was contagious. Even the corners of Bea's mouth lifted slightly. They had escaped danger, and for a moment they could simply enjoy that.

Then Misty shivered. They were soaked from head to toe. If they didn't warm up, they would soon be in trouble.

Ethan looked around. “There are plenty of dry twigs and grass here. Let's get a fire going.”

Misty immediately gathered the grass and arranged it with the twigs. Bea hesitated, but before she could kneel to help, Misty had finished. The ease of her movements told Bea that Ethan and Misty were accustomed to camping outdoors.

Ethan took out a Poké Ball and released Combusken. “Combusken, use Ember.”

Combusken breathed a burst of fire into the kindling. Flames leaped up, lighting the cave and filling it with warmth.

The fire was a start, but they needed to dry their clothes. Wearing wet fabric would be miserable and could leave them chilled and ill.

There was an awkward problem: Ethan was sharing the cave with two girls. He had a way around it.

He released Dragonite. The large Pokémon sat down in the middle of the cave, forming a living screen. More than two meters tall with a broad belly, Dragonite separated Ethan from Misty and Bea.

Someone could still see around it by leaning forward, but as long as everyone stayed in place, it blocked most of their view of one another.

“All right. Take off your wet clothes and lay them on the rocks by the fire to dry.”

Ethan went first. He removed his clothes, shoes, and socks, keeping his shorts on, and spread the wet things near the flames. Then he leaned against Dragonite. Dragonite wrapped an arm around him and warmed him with its own body heat.

That was why he had chosen Dragonite rather than the equally large Tyranitar. Tyranitar's armor was hard and cold. Dragonite was soft, comfortable, and warm.

On the other side, Misty followed his example. She set her clothes by the fire, stayed in her underclothes, and leaned against Dragonite's opposite flank. She felt no need to make a fuss. She and Ethan had visited hot springs together more than once. She liked him, and she had never been shy by nature. She could treat it like a trip to the swimming pool.

Bea was the only one who hesitated. She knew drying her clothes was sensible, but a man she had met only a few times was just beyond Dragonite. After making sure he couldn't see her, she changed out of her wet clothes too and kept her underclothes on.

Misty glanced at her without thinking. Bea was older and more developed than she was, though not nearly as much as Erika. Bea's firm muscles and taut stomach caught her attention as well. Her skin had the healthy tone of someone who exercised regularly.

Misty wondered why Bea trained her own body so hard. Weren't Trainers supposed to train their Pokémon? Up close, she could also see that Bea was quite pretty, with delicate features, even if she wasn't the sort of beauty who stunned a room at first sight.

“I'm Misty,” she said, setting those thoughts aside. “That's Ethan over there. I don't think we know your name yet.”

Bea looked at the smiling girl. They had met a few times now and had gone through the storm together. They were no longer complete strangers.

“I'm Bea.” She offered nothing more.

“From your accent, I'd guess you're not from Hoenn?” Misty tried again.

Bea nodded. “I'm from Stow-on-Side.”

“Stow-on-Side?” Misty searched her memory. “Where's that?”

“Galar.”

“And where's Galar?”

“Far to the west.”

“What brought you all the way here?”

“I came to challenge the Fighting-type Gym.”

Misty was doing all the work in the conversation. Bea answered each question and never opened a new topic. It was exhausting.

They had known each other only briefly, but Misty could already tell Bea was quiet and rarely spoke more than she had to. After a few dry exchanges, Misty gave up and turned toward Ethan.

“How long do you think the typhoon will last?”

Ethan had heard the whole exchange and found it funny. Bea was exactly the sort of person Misty struggled to talk to. He didn't tease her about it.

“We'll have to wait and see. It's only just begun, so it won't end right away. But don't worry. The sky will clear eventually.”

“I know, but I wish it would clear sooner,” Misty sighed.

Crackle, crackle. The fire went on burning. The cave was shallow, so Ethan wasn't concerned about carbon dioxide building up inside.

After another half hour by the fire, their hair and skin were dry. The clothes on the rocks were warm too. They dressed again and gathered around the flames.

Misty checked her phone. There was no signal.

Rain continued to roar outside, without any sign of easing. With little else to do, Ethan and Misty talked to pass the time.

Bea sat apart from them, making no attempt to join in. Misty had to admire how comfortable she seemed with silence. But Misty and Ethan traveled together every day. They had already discussed most of the things that came to mind, and before long the conversation ran dry.

For someone as restless as Misty, sitting in the cave became torture. She got up and stretched. After moving around for a while, she looked back. Ethan was grooming Dragonite's and Combusken's feathers, while Bea was doing push-ups. Both had found something to occupy themselves.

Those two might actually get along. Misty shook the thought away at once. What was she thinking? Ethan was hers!

She wandered around some more. When she checked the time, it was only noon. The storm still hadn't weakened, and now she was thirsty.

Where could she find water? She had expected to challenge the Gym and return promptly to the Pokémon Center. Apart from her phone, wallet, and Poké Balls, she had brought nothing.

Ethan noticed her discomfort and took a lidded water bottle from the pouch at his waist. He was in the habit of carrying water, which had come in handy.

He poured a little into the lid. Then he looked at Bea and offered it to her first. “You must be thirsty too. Have some.”

Bea licked her dry lips. She thanked him, accepted the lid, and drank.

Misty had already reached out for it and pouted when Ethan handed it to Bea instead. Her smile returned when he passed her the bottle itself.

She took it carefully and tried a small sip. The water was lukewarm, but her heart felt light. Ethan used this bottle himself. Did drinking from it count as an indirect kiss?

Under his gaze, she pushed the thought aside, drank a little more, and handed it back. Ethan took the bottle and, without looking, drank deeply from it.

That was the spot where my lips touched... Misty's face flushed. Now she was certain it counted. Fortunately, the orange firelight flickered over her face, hiding her embarrassment.

Then her stomach growled. Ethan glanced at it. He was hungry himself.

Bea returned the empty lid and set her small backpack in front of them. She unzipped it and took out a surprising amount of food. Keeping some for herself, she pushed more than half the snacks toward Ethan and Misty.

“Oh!” Misty realized the food was for them. “Thank you so much!” Her opinion of Bea, previously no more than lukewarm, rose at once.

Ethan hadn't expected her to carry food or share so generously. He had water, Bea had snacks, and Misty had brought only her appetite. Between them, they were managing nicely.

He examined the packages: egg rolls, pudding, chocolate bars, strawberry cake. Almost everything was sweet. The food would supply plenty of energy, but that was a lot of sugar. Then he remembered that Bea in the anime loved sweets. No wonder she carried so many.

He glanced unobtrusively at her lean waist. With the amount she ate, her daily training must be what kept her fit.

Neither he nor Misty worried about sugar just then. They opened the packages and quickly ate their share. The snacks were a little cloying, but they filled them up. The rush of sweetness lifted their spirits too. Even the rain outside seemed less gloomy.

Sharing food brought the three closer. Misty tried talking to Bea again. Bea still answered as briefly as before, but Misty no longer minded. It simply seemed natural to her now.

The fire began to die. They had burned all the dry wood in the cave, leaving only embers. The air cooled quickly, and dampness crept inside.

Misty edged toward Ethan. He called Dragonite over and had it spread its arms around all three of them.

Misty sat in the middle, with Ethan on her left, Bea on her right, and Dragonite's soft, warm belly behind them. The chill vanished at once.

Before today, sitting so close to a near stranger like Bea might have made Misty uncomfortable. After what they had shared, it didn't trouble her at all.
