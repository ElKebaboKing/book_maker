“Milotic, use Water Gun!”

“Altaria, use Sing!”

Sing was a Normal-type move that could lull its target to sleep. In effect, it was much like Hypnosis or any other sleep-inducing move; the difference lay in its type.

From those first commands, the difference between Ethan's approach and Lisia's was already clear. Most Trainers preferred a direct attack. If they could defeat an opponent with sheer force, they would. Only when a battle turned against them, or an opponent proved difficult to break down, did they tend to vary their tactics.

Coordinators gave more thought to how a battle looked. A simple attack held less appeal than a complicated, dazzling move. Neither approach was inherently right or wrong. They were different ways to battle, and only a match between them would show how they fared together.

Milotic opened its mouth and sent a thick jet of water at Altaria. With a quick beat of its cloudlike wings, Altaria barely dodged the Water Gun and began to sing.

But Lisia's Altaria did more than sing. As its lovely voice filled the park, it traced graceful circles through the air, almost dancing.

Years of Pokémon Contests had likely made that movement a habit. Lisia encouraged it: judges and spectators enjoyed a performance that gave their eyes as much to follow as their ears. Here, however, those extra movements gave Ethan openings.

The two younger boys watched openmouthed. They had only seen performances like this on television. Now a beautiful idol's Pokémon was dancing directly above them. At last they understood why people found such performances so thrilling.

Ethan could appreciate its beauty too, but the elaborate display also gave him ample time to react. Milotic's eyes began to droop under the soothing music.

“Underwater,” Ethan ordered.

Milotic plunged into the pool. Cold water closed around its body, washing the drowsiness away. When it surfaced again, Altaria was still dancing.

Much too slow.

Lisia did not seem concerned. Sing had only been a test. The main performance was still to come.

She spun in place like a cheerful sprite, struck a cute pose, and smiled. “Now our blue-green moves and our grand performance can begin in dazzling style!”

...

Splash!

Altaria, soaked through, fell from the sky like a stone and struck the pool. It did not resurface. Their park battle had ended in Milotic's victory.

Victory had cost Milotic dearly. Scratches marred its beautiful scales, its strength was nearly spent, and it panted so hard that it clearly could not battle again.

For all Lisia's elaborate style, her Altaria had been formidable. Ethan would put her near Drew, the strong young Coordinator they had met recently. Both were far ahead of most people their age.

As the exchange grew fiercer, the Pokémon had fought almost evenly. For a while neither could gain a decisive advantage. Ethan's sharp commands let Milotic attack or defend at just the right moment, but Lisia had a strength of her own.

Altaria's experience had grown through countless battles. It could read a situation, dodge, and counterattack without waiting for an order, often faster than Milotic. An instinctive response took less time than a Trainer seeing a move, deciding what to do, and calling out a command.

Each side had fought to its own rhythm, which was why the contest remained close until the end. As Ethan had expected, though, repeated Water-type attacks soaked Altaria's feathers. Their growing weight hindered its flight. Once it lost its advantage in the air, Ethan found his chance and finished the match with Water Gun.

Those cloudlike feathers gave Altaria its distinctive beauty and helped it in the air. This time they had also brought it down.

“That's impossible. Lisia lost...?”

Kinta spoke before either Trainer did. He had swallowed his pride and brought Lisia here to avenge his defeats. He had placed all his hopes in her and never considered that Ethan might beat her too.

Lisia was a Contest idol and a skilled Coordinator. Kinta had battled her many times without winning once. Even some adult Trainers on Sootopolis Island could not beat her, especially when she used Ali, her strongest Pokémon. Could this man really be that good?

Ethan, meanwhile, was pleased with Milotic. It looked battered, but a narrow victory was still a victory. It had only recently evolved and did not know many moves yet. Beating an opponent like Altaria, even with Ethan's guidance bringing out everything it had and more, was a great step forward.

Lisia hurried to the edge of the pool and peered into the water. Ethan signaled Milotic to dive after Altaria and bring it up.

Milotic carried Ali to the bank. Altaria was in a sorry state. Its fluffy feathers hung flat and wet, and it looked nothing like the beautiful Pokémon that had taken to the sky at the start of the match.

“Ali...” Lisia knelt to check on it. Finding it conscious and in no serious danger, she finally relaxed. Then she looked at Ethan with open admiration. “You're really good. I lost this battle.”

“Let's call it a draw.” Ethan smiled and shook his head. “I know you were holding something back. If you'd used that, my Milotic would have lost.”

He tilted his head toward the Key Stone in her hair. Misty, Kinta, and the two younger boys followed his gaze and noticed it at last.

“So you do know what it is!” Lisia's eyes brightened. “Have you been to Kalos or another region? Or do you have one too, like I do?”

To Kinta and the boys, their conversation made no sense. Only Misty understood. Kinta scratched his head. Wasn't the thing in Lisia's hair just an ornament?

“I have one,” Ethan said. “I've even used it. It worked rather well.”

Lisia opened her mouth to reply, but hurried footsteps sounded nearby. A woman in her twenties was rushing toward them.

“Oh, that's my manager!” Lisia stuck out her tongue at the others. “She must have found out I slipped away. I have to get back, and I need to have Ali treated too.”

She said goodbye, recalled Altaria, and went to meet the woman.

With Lisia gone, the rest had little reason to stay. Kinta and his friends had now seen even the person they had called for help defeated. They exchanged glances and slunk off in Lisia's direction without saying whether they admitted defeat.

Ethan was still watching them leave when Misty spoke in a sour voice. “Still looking? She's out of sight already.”

“What are you thinking?” Ethan gave her a light knock on the head.

“Am I wrong?” Misty pouted. “She's pretty, her skin's fair, and she's an idol. Everyone likes her. Did you see Kinta and those two boys? They practically wanted to put her on a pedestal.”

“Come on.” Ethan rubbed her hair. “Do you think I'm interested in her? She's about your age. Why would I fall for little girls who haven't grown up yet?”

That struck Misty harder than he realized. She huffed, straightened her back, and glanced down at her chest. Then her shoulders sagged.

Just wait. I'll grow up someday!

Milotic was injured too badly to keep training. Ethan took it to the Pokémon Center for Nurse Joy to treat, bringing the day's plan to an end.

He had managed only five battles, fewer than he had hoped. Still, each opponent had been stronger than the last. Milotic had gained a great deal and was beginning to catch up with the rest of his Pokémon.

...

The Sootopolis Gym stood in the north of the city at the foot of an extinct volcano. Its height gave it one of the city's finest views; from there, nearly all of Sootopolis could be seen.

Juan, the official Gym Leader, specialized in Water-type Pokémon. Defeating him would earn a Challenger the Rain Badge.

After a night's rest, Ethan went there with Misty early the next morning, hoping to find Juan. As they had heard, he was a busy man. With the festival approaching, he spent most days away from the Gym. A visitor either had to arrange an appointment or hope to catch him by chance.

The Gym resembled a grand estate. Neatly tended gardens surrounded its handsome main building, making it look more like a nobleman's home than a place for Pokémon battles.

Before entering, Ethan noticed several middle-aged women lingering outside. What were they waiting for? Was Juan truly so charming that women their age gathered wherever he went?

Curious, Ethan and Misty went inside. The interior was just as elegant: statues, framed art, fine chandeliers, and polished marble floors. Whoever had decorated it had excellent taste.

But they saw no one. In the hall, their footsteps were the only sound. There was not even a staff member in sight. Had they come all this way for nothing?

“Can I help you?” a man called from above.

They looked up. A man with blue-green hair stood by the railing of the second-floor gallery, looking down at them. He wore a round white hat, a white outfit, and two long green drapes over his shoulders.

Ethan recognized him at once: Wallace, Hoenn's former Champion and the former Leader of the Sootopolis Gym. He was one of the people of Sootopolis, a distinguished Pokémon Trainer and top Coordinator, the elegant man known as the Water Prince.

What a surprise. Ethan had come looking for Juan, only to find Juan's even more accomplished student.

“Sorry to disturb you,” Ethan said with a smile. “I'm here to challenge the Sootopolis Gym. Is the Gym Leader in?”

Wallace was about to answer when light footsteps approached him. A familiar girl appeared at his side.

She followed Wallace's gaze down to the hall. The moment she saw Ethan, she pointed at him in surprise. “Oh! It's you!”
