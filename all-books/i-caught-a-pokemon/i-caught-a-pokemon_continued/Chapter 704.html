Courtney looked at Deoxys floating beside Ethan and knew she could not beat him. This Pokémon had fought Rayquaza to a standstill. Even without it, Ethan's usual team was probably beyond her.

She would not have felt that way against most people. She would have challenged even Phoebe of the Elite Four and judged the outcome after they fought. Ethan was different. Courtney knew too well how frightening he could be.

Her purpose in staying was to hold him here—or die with him. She would give her life for Team Magma's dream, and to make up for her own mistakes.

It's my fault, just as the boss said. She had failed to keep Ethan from rescuing Professor Cozmo. She had failed to defeat him and stop him from catching Deoxys. Her lookouts had missed him on Mt. Pyre, almost ruining the operation. She put every setback on her own shoulders.

She had made so many mistakes. Now she had one chance, perhaps her only chance, to redeem them. She would not let Ethan interfere with Maxie again. She gripped the controller Maxie had given her.

Ethan could not hear her thoughts. He approached and asked in an even voice, “I doubt you'll tell me, but I have to ask. Where is Maxie going?”

Courtney had attacked him more than once. In the battle over Deoxys, she had even ignored the Legendary Pokémon to aim at Ethan himself. Yet he thought somewhat better of her than of Shelly. Courtney was less cunning, but her determination had value, even if it sometimes became stubbornness. She was beautiful, too, and Ethan knew he was more inclined to forgive a beautiful woman.

As he expected, she did not answer. A strange half-smile appeared on her face, touched with something frantic. Light seemed to flicker in her amber eyes.

“Hee hee...” Her laugh sounded unlike her usual voice and made the air feel colder. “You're too late. The boss is taking the orb to Groudon. I won't let you go any farther. You can't stop him now. You and your Pokémon will be buried here with me.”

Ethan glanced at the object in her hand and shook his head. “Maxie left you here. No, he abandoned you. Do you really think you can stop me by yourself? Or are you counting on that thing?”

He seemed to understand both her position and what she was about to do, yet remained calm. Courtney was surprised. Was he not afraid? She decided he must be pretending. No one could survive the explosion she could trigger. Everything here would be crushed in an instant.

“A great plan requires sacrifices,” she said. “You're too dangerous. To us, you're the greatest threat and the greatest uncertainty.”

Ethan understood that she meant to kill them both. He was glad he had sent Gengar ahead to disable the base's destructive systems. As a further precaution, he recalled every Pokémon except Deoxys and Gengar. Too many partners around him would make a sudden emergency harder to manage. Those two could protect him.

“I know what you're thinking of doing. Don't.” He held her gaze. “We're not the only ones in this base. There are members of your own team who are unconscious or haven't escaped yet. Their Pokémon are here too. Do all of them need to die for this dream?”

Courtney's smile froze. She had been thinking only of dying with Ethan. She had forgotten her teammates and friends who might still be inside. Was she going to take them with her? The thought struck like cold water.

Ethan kept talking because he needed information. Maxie was already at sea; searching its vast expanse blindly would be nearly impossible. Courtney could tell him where the submarine was headed. And after seeing Shelly leave Team Aqua, he wanted to help Courtney step away from Team Magma as well.

Both organizations would fall sooner or later. Courtney had ability, despite her failures against him, and he did not think she had a cruel heart. Helping her change course would be a good deed.

That's the reason, he assured himself. It certainly isn't because she's pretty.

Seeing that his words had reached her, he stepped closer. Courtney reacted like a startled rabbit and raised the controller.

“You're right. I can't drag the others into this. It will be enough if I'm the only one who dies with you!”

She pressed a small button.

Gengar smiled. She doesn't know I cut the wires in their control room.

Explosions sounded one after another. Before Gengar could understand what had happened, the ceiling above the underground harbor collapsed. Rock, soil, and broken equipment poured down.

Ethan's heart lurched. He had not expected Courtney to press the button so quickly, or for the blast to work after Gengar's sabotage. But he had prepared for the worst when he recalled his other Pokémon. Gengar was a Pokémon, not an engineer. It could break machinery; it could not guarantee that every device in the base had been disabled.

“You lunatic!” Ethan shouted. How much did she hate him to choose this?

The ceiling fell rapidly. The lights went out as a broad section of the harbor roof gave way. Emergency lamps snapped on. In the same instant, Deoxys used psychic power to hold a barrier above them. Gengar floated over Ethan, ready to catch anything that broke through.

Courtney had closed her eyes to await death. Ethan cursed and pulled her under the barrier. Darkness fell around them as rubble struck its surface with a series of heavy crashes.

The ground shook, but the blasts were limited to this part of the base. Courtney had detonated the underground harbor, not the entire facility. Its destruction system must be separate from the one Gengar had sabotaged.

Deoxys had stopped the first fall, though stones and soil kept dropping. The mountain was solid. Two enormous slabs had lodged against each other in an inverted V, temporarily holding back more of the collapse. They were sliding downward, however. In seconds they could give way.

Courtney opened her eyes and saw that Ethan had saved her. “Why... why did you do that?”

“You wanted to die. I couldn't stand there and watch it happen, even after you tried to take me with you.”

She stared at him, then shook her head. “It's pointless. The exit is blocked. We'll be buried alive anyway.”

Ethan looked back. Rubble filled the entrance through which he had come. There was no gap.

Gengar knocked an incoming stone aside and saw Ethan still talking. “Hey! Stop trying to charm her and find a way out. This place is coming down!”

Gengar could sink through the ground and escape. Its panic was for Ethan. But charm her? Ethan shot it a glare. This was hardly the moment. Then he looked toward the water.

“There is another way. We go through the sea.” He pulled Courtney toward the edge.

She peered into the black water below. “What are you doing? You're taking me with you?”

“Of course. Did you think I'd leave you here to die?”

“You've got to be joking. I just tried to kill us both. And I've half succeeded.”

“Then let's make sure the other half fails. Besides, I've always been good at turning enemies into friends.” Ethan urged her closer. “We have no time. This is the only way out.”

“Wait!” Courtney held back. “I can't leave. And... I can't swim.”

“You can't swim? You were ready to die, but you're afraid of this? Or do you not want to die after all?”

The words struck her. She had made her decision and pressed the button. She had thought herself unafraid of death. Why, then, was her heart pounding and her body trembling?

Ethan would not give her more time to argue. He took Milotic's Poké Ball from his belt, drew a deep breath, and jumped, pulling her with him.

Splash!

The harbor water was icy. Ethan shivered. Milotic emerged from its ball and swam to them.

Courtney had managed one breath before he pulled her in. Now she squeezed her eyes shut, held that breath, and flailed for something to grasp. Ethan released her hand. Panic surged through her, but his arm immediately caught her around the waist. Her hands found Milotic's scales, and the Pokémon swept them forward.

Ethan kept his eyes open underwater. One hand held Milotic, the other kept Courtney securely against him. Few creatures could carry them through water as fast as a Water-type Pokémon. Milotic was their best chance to leave before the harbor collapsed completely.

Since receiving Maya's blessing, Ethan had felt far less discomfort underwater. There was even something strange and almost familiar about the sensation now.

Only Deoxys remained beside him. Gengar disliked the water and had slipped back into his shadow the moment Ethan jumped.

Rocks continued to splash into the harbor and sink toward the seabed. Deoxys followed behind them, holding up another psychic barrier to keep the falling debris away.

Milotic drove through the passage like a sea serpent at the greatest speed it could manage, carrying both humans toward the far end.
