Ethan took out a Poké Ball, tossed it lightly in his hand, and threw it onto the field.

Blue light flashed, and Gardevoir appeared.

From yesterday's review, Ethan knew Ash had around twelve Pokémon available, leaving aside his thirty Tauros. Any of them might appear today. Ethan had always treated Ash like a younger brother, but he could not underestimate his strength. One careless choice could cost Ethan the match.

He had to be cautious.

So he opened with the partner he trusted most, aiming for a steady first round.

Ash had spent all of yesterday studying Ethan's team with Brock. He knew most of Ethan's Pokémon were formidable. Gengar and the red Gyarados were particularly terrifying. He could not afford to let his guard down.

At the sight of Gardevoir, Ash drew a deep breath. Following the plan he and Brock had made, he took out a Poké Ball.

"Go, Donphan!"

Ash threw hard. A sturdy Pokémon resembling a small tusked elephant appeared before Gardevoir.

Donphan? Ethan had not expected that.

On reflection, though, it made sense. Neither Pokémon had a type advantage over the other, and Ash had few better answers to Gardevoir.

With the crowd watching, the battle began at once.

At Ash's command, Donphan curled into a ball and rolled straight at Gardevoir with tremendous momentum.

"Gardevoir, stop it!"

Gardevoir reached out with its psychic power, trying to restrain Donphan. But Donphan moved quickly while rolling, and rocks of different sizes blocked its path from view. Catching it was not so simple.

Rumble, rumble...

The ground trembled beneath Donphan. It rolled straight up the sloping side of a huge boulder, launched into the air, and dropped toward Gardevoir.

Surprising an ordinary Pokémon might have made the attack work, but Gardevoir saw it coming. A single Teleport carried it safely out of Donphan's path.

Donphan had barely landed and had not yet turned around. Gardevoir seized the opening, raised a hand, and used Psychic to hurl it into a distant rock.

Crash!

The impact shattered the rock. Donphan's ringed armor protected it, however, and it took little damage.

In Ash's section of the stands, Brock sighed helplessly.

He and Ash had worked hard on their plans yesterday. Of all Ethan's Pokémon, they agreed that Gardevoir would be the hardest to face apart from Gengar.

Gengar was absurdly strong, but they knew Ethan rarely sent it into tournament matches unless the situation was desperate. That offered some reassurance.

Gardevoir was another matter. They could not expect to avoid it. It stood like a mountain between Ash and the semifinals.

After considering Ash's Pokémon one by one, they had chosen Donphan to face Gardevoir if it appeared. There was no type disadvantage between them, which improved Ash's chances a little. Gardevoir's psychic attacks also tended to lift opponents and slam them into the ground. Donphan could curl up to soften those impacts.

In theory, it was a sound plan. Brock had been especially relieved when today's battlefield was drawn as the rock field.

The boulders might limit Donphan's movement, but they also blocked Gardevoir's sight. That should hinder its psychic attacks even more.

Now that the battle was underway, the plan had helped somewhat, but the reality was far from what Brock had expected.

All he could do was hope Ash would adapt.

He hoped Ash could survive this round.

Misty sat in the same section, but her two good friends were on opposite sides of the field. She tried to remain neutral and did not cheer especially hard for either one.

In a small, casual match, she might have teased Ash without harm. At an important tournament like the Silver Conference, she did not want to unsettle him and affect his focus.

She had even stayed out of Ash and Brock's planning yesterday so there would be no question of taking sides.

She could not help having a preference in her heart. When she saw Ethan take the lead, she felt glad for him.

On the field, Gardevoir went on the offensive at Ethan's command.

Waiting passively had never been his style.

The great boulders did restrict Gardevoir, but an opportunity could be found if they watched for one.

Donphan rolled forward again, trying the same launch from a boulder. Gardevoir timed its move before Donphan could land. A forceful burst of Psychic sent it flying out of the battlefield.

Donphan sailed past the referee and struck the wall behind him before finally stopping.

The referee raised his flag. "Donphan is out of bounds! Ethan wins the first round!"

"How did that happen...?"

Ash scratched his head as he looked at Donphan beyond the boundary. The round had ended before he was ready. Donphan was hardly injured, yet it had been eliminated like this. It was frustrating.

He recovered quickly, recalled Donphan, and drew a second Poké Ball.

"Go, Noctowl!"

Blue light flashed, and Ash's golden Shiny Noctowl appeared over the field. Ethan could not help feeling envious all over again.

He wanted a Shiny Pokémon too.

Ethan shook off the thought, recalled Gardevoir, and chose his second Poké Ball.

As before, he was fighting cautiously but still wanted to train his team before the final. He tried to give every Pokémon some time on the field.

Jolteon would face the Shiny Noctowl. Now the terrain could work in Ethan's favor.

As the next round began, Jolteon darted through the rock field with nimble steps. Noctowl had the advantage of flight and was fast in its own right, but it could not pin Jolteon down.

Each time Noctowl dived, Jolteon sprang from a boulder and changed direction sharply. It opened the gap again before Noctowl could catch it, leaving the bird to sail past.

"Ash..."

In the stands, Delia watched her son struggle and grew worried. She had brought a professional camera to photograph his best moments, but so far his Pokémon had scarcely had a chance to show what they could do.

"Trust him," Professor Oak said beside her. "Remember the match the day before yesterday? Ash fell behind early, then came back and beat Gary. I believe he can do it today too."

Delia nodded, but the worry did not leave her face.

She had not known Ethan for long, yet she liked him. And she could see that Gary, in both his Pokémon's strength and his own composure, was not Ethan's equal. Could Ash truly beat a Trainer like this?
