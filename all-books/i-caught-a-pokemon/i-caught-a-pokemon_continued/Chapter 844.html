Whoever was controlling Scizor had probably never expected its disguise to be exposed so quickly. They could not have expected someone to stop its Hyper Beam so easily, either.

Surprised or not, Scizor kept attacking. It changed targets from the Eevee it had picked out to the ominous-looking Gengar in front of it.

Red Lightning rushed in and swung both great pincers at Gengar. Its fists struck as fast as bullets, dozens of blows in the space of moments. Gengar remained calm. Wearing an insolent grin and letting out its familiar cackling laugh, it vanished and reappeared, easily avoiding the assault.

Scizor abruptly changed its rhythm, slipping a Metal Claw into its string of Bullet Punches to alter the angle of attack. Gengar slipped past that too, almost casually.

Red Lightning had ability. It could never have defeated so many Trainers or caused such a stir otherwise. Against Gengar, though, it could do nothing.

It could be as strong and fierce as it liked. Unless it was an exceptionally powerful Legendary Pokémon, had broken past its body's limits, or had allies to gang up on Gengar, Ethan's two-thousand-year-old partner would put it down. Gengar's moves hit hard, but raw power was not its greatest weapon. Its endlessly varied ways of attacking were almost impossible to guard against. Treating it like an ordinary Pokémon was a sure way to suffer.

Red Lightning had never learned that lesson. Perhaps it had met no worthy opponent before. It fought bravely, but its pincers could not touch even Gengar's shadow. Gengar toyed with it until its body was covered in wounds.

At last Gengar turned toward Ethan. “What should I do with this one? Give it a thorough beating or catch it?”

Ethan considered it. Scizor was acting on someone's orders. Punishing the Pokémon alone would solve nothing; they needed the person behind it. He gave Gengar a look, and his partner understood.

Gengar formed two Shadow Balls and sent them at Scizor one after the other. While Scizor concentrated on blocking them, Gengar disappeared. It reappeared behind its opponent.

Perhaps it was a Bug-type's instincts that warned Scizor. It felt danger at once. Each pincer smashed a Shadow Ball apart; then Scizor spun and slashed downward with its right claw. It was too late to stop what came next.

A wave of Dark Pulse swept through its guard and struck its body. Scizor crouched in pain, trembling from head to foot as though the blow had become unbearable.

The person controlling it was shocked. “Scizor! What's wrong?” They had not expected a single hit to leave it almost unable to battle.

Ethan listened for anything revealing in the voice. All he could hear was something like a man in his thirties or forties, and the sound had plainly been synthesized electronically. It gave him no other clue.

Whoever was speaking had evidently realized they had found a dangerous opponent. They did not want to lose Scizor here. As soon as it could stand again, the voice ordered it to retreat. Scizor leaped into the treetop and glided away.

“Scizor is flying?” May stared. “How? It has wings, but I thought it couldn't fly!”

“It can't.” Ethan raised a palm to feel the air. “It's the wind. The buildings make drafts all through this city. Scizor is using them to glide over long distances, so it looks as if it's flying. See? It just landed and jumped again.”

Before May could reply, Ethan signaled Gengar once more. Grinning, Gengar rose into the air and followed the fleeing Scizor.

“Come on.” Once Gengar was out of sight, Ethan sent out Gardevoir to guard them. “Let's find out what's really behind this—and meet the person in charge.”

With Gengar tracking Scizor, there was no risk of losing it and no need to hurry. Ethan and May walked under Gardevoir's guidance. At its current strength, Gardevoir could pinpoint Gengar anywhere in the city, even among crowds and complicated streets. Gengar was cooperating, of course. If it chose to hide its presence, even a Legendary Pokémon would struggle to detect it.

They passed through several narrow streets and came onto a main road leading toward the center of Commerce City. Scizor's refuge had to be somewhere ahead.

Ethan had not expected that, but it made sense. Red Lightning operated throughout the city. A place in the center offered quick routes in every direction.

Still, something puzzled him. The attacks had been going on for some time. How had Officer Jenny and the other Trainers failed to find anything? A red-cloaked figure stood out, even here, and so many assaults should have left traces. Were Scizor and its controller that good at hiding, or was there another reason?

It no longer mattered much. Red Lightning had come after him. Whatever sort of urban legend it had become, Ethan meant to put an end to it today.

People and cars grew more numerous as they entered the city center. Passersby stared curiously at Gardevoir, a Pokémon unfamiliar to many in Kanto. Ethan and May slowed down. Any conspicuous move under so many eyes would attract more attention and complicate what they planned to do.

He began to understand a little of what Officer Jenny and her colleagues might have been dealing with. Perhaps they too had reason to be cautious.

The crowds soon fell behind them. They turned into another alley and could move freely again. Gardevoir stopped and pointed toward the top of the tallest building nearby. At that moment Gengar floated down from above.

“You took your time,” it said quietly. “Red Lightning's nest is up there. I have to say, rich people live well.”

Ethan did not understand the remark until Gardevoir used Teleport to take him and May to the roof.

On top of a building nearly thirty stories tall stood an entire estate. Its main house was a huge three-story villa with a tower. A fountain, lawns, and landscaped gardens spread out before it. The estate occupied the whole rooftop.

“This really is how rich people live,” May said, staring. “I've only seen houses like this on television.”

Ethan was impressed by the owner's wealth too. Surely they owned the building; who else could build an estate on its roof?

“So that's how they stayed hidden,” he said. “Up here, ordinary people would have trouble spotting them. Even the police might hesitate to act if they suspected the owner. And attacking Trainers only to draw faces on their Pokémon—that sounds like the whim of someone with too much money. The person behind it might even be a child.”

Gengar stared at him. “How did you know it really is a child? I followed Scizor up here and saw it talking to a little girl.”

“A girl?” Ethan nodded. “I expected a boy, but that doesn't matter. I guessed from the pranks. Who but a child would spend their time drawing on Pokémon's faces?”

There was no reason to hide now. Ethan led May and their Pokémon openly toward the house. After the trouble its owner had caused across the city, she could be the one to worry.

They were noticed almost at once. As they reached the villa's entrance, a little girl appeared with the same Scizor.

She looked younger than May. Her brown hair was tied into two ponytails, and she wore a red dress. She was pretty and carried herself with the confidence of a child raised in a wealthy home. That confidence faltered visibly when she saw them.

“You followed me here? How did you find this place?”

Her voice sounded nothing like the synthesized man's voice from Scizor's speaker, but the tone was familiar. Ethan knew she had been controlling Red Lightning.

“Was it supposed to be difficult?” he asked. “You just hadn't met a capable Trainer before. Picking on beginners proves nothing.”

Whether it was his tone or the discovery of her hiding place, the girl grew angry. “What? You don't like my pranks?”

“Pranks?” Ethan shook his head. “I haven't seen all of them, but people in this city have suffered because of you. More Pokémon than I can count have needed treatment at the Pokémon Center after your attacks. You went far beyond a prank. And you tried to hit me a few minutes ago. Would calling that deliberate harm be unfair?”

Her face flushed, whether from fear or anger Ethan could not tell. “This is none of your business! You came into my home without permission. I won't let you get away with that! Red Lightning, defeat them!”

Scizor stepped in front of her again. Gengar had driven it off once, but it stood bravely now to protect its Trainer—or perhaps its owner.

Someone else came running toward them. “Please, wait!”

Ethan turned. An elderly man dressed as a butler hurried up, breathless. Without even taking time to recover, he addressed Ethan.

“Sir, please wait.” When Ethan did not attack, the man exhaled and bowed. “Please forgive Miss Luna's rash behavior. I know these pranks have gone too far. Could you be generous and let the matter rest?”

Ethan raised an eyebrow. “So you knew she was attacking Pokémon around the city?”

“Well…” The butler hesitated at his tone. “The master and mistress know nothing of it. I alone knew that Miss Luna—”

“Stop talking. I don't need you meddling in my affairs!” Luna interrupted.

May could hold back no longer. “Why are you doing this?”

Luna lifted her chin. “Because it's fun, of course!”

May stared. “That's…that's your only reason?”

“Of course. How many times do I have to say it? Nobody gets to tell me what to do. Now you've invaded my home, so be ready for what happens next. Red Lightning, use Hyper Beam!”
