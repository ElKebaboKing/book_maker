Venusaur and Meowth took their places on opposite halves of the Grass Field.

Apart from Ethan, Tyson, and those who knew them well, such as Ash and Morrison, no one realized that this hat-wearing Meowth was Tyson's strongest Pokémon—and his last hope. The crowd noticed its almost human outfit without sensing the danger beneath it.

Ethan studied Meowth more carefully than he ever had before. He had dealt with Team Rocket's Meowth many times, but its comic presence had led him to overlook the species. This Meowth made the familiar Pokémon seem strange again.

Its shape resembled a small housecat or a beckoning cat. Most Meowth walked on four legs, though a few learned to walk like people through sheer determination. Pale yellow fur covered most of its body, turning brown at the end of its tail and on its hind paws. Its oval face held large eyes and a wide mouth but no visible nose. A bright gold coin gleamed on its forehead.

Meowth could sleep through the day and become energetic after sunset. They moved silently on careful paws and washed their faces often to keep the coins on their foreheads clean. They loved shiny things, especially coins, which they hid when they found them. Around garbage dumps, they were even known to fight Murkrow over glittering objects.

Meowth and Venusaur had no type advantage over one another. Of all Ethan and Tyson's exchanges, this was the fairest matchup yet.

Tyson's earlier losses had not shaken Meowth. It wore a confident smile and raised one sharp claw toward Venusaur.

The referee lifted the flag and began the round. Before Tyson could give an order, Meowth charged, its claws glinting. It meant to settle the fight at close range.

Ethan did not fear close combat, but he would not give up Venusaur's reach and let Meowth approach freely. “Venusaur, Razor Leaf.”

A dozen or more sharp leaves spun toward Meowth. It twisted its body and slipped past most of them. Those it could not avoid, it sliced apart with its claws. By the time the leaves had passed, it had reached the middle of the field.

“Vine Whip,” Ethan ordered.

Two long vines emerged from the flower on Venusaur's back and lashed at Meowth. Unlike a volley of leaves, the vines were part of Venusaur's body and could change direction at will. Their reach was shorter, but they were harder to evade.

The vines cracked through the air. Meowth jumped over the first strike. Before it landed, the vines sprang up from where they had hit the ground and curled toward it again.

Meowth's eyes narrowed. It twisted sharply in midair and escaped, only for the vines to turn and pursue it once more. It could not dodge a third time. Calmly, it slashed ahead with both claws and cut the vines apart.

That stopped the attack, but it stopped Meowth's advance too. It landed where it was, unable to close the remaining distance.

The exchange took only moments. Neither Pokémon had gained much, yet the crowd leaned forward. Meowth had clearly earned its place in this match, and the battle promised to be worth watching.

From his command position, Tyson was impressed by how Venusaur had kept his ace away. He called, “Meowth, try again!” Meowth had reached halfway across the field; it was a promising start.

Meowth raised its claws and rushed forward. Ethan answered at nearly the same instant.

“Venusaur, Sleep Powder!”

A thick cloud of green powder poured from the flower on Venusaur's back, spreading around it. In the sunlight the cloud glowed an ominous green. Meowth knew better than to let a sleeping powder settle on it.

Instead of retreating, it accelerated. Before the powder could fall, it sprinted right up to Venusaur and lifted a claw. Eyes widened across the stadium. Tyson had found one of his few openings in the match.

Even Ethan had not expected such determination. Meowth had risked being put to sleep to get close. None of Tyson's other Pokémon had shown that kind of nerve. This was an ace.

Venusaur's vines snapped out again. At this distance, though, Vine Whip was less useful. The vines needed room to extend and turn. Tyson had driven Meowth in close for precisely that reason.

Meowth slipped past the vines and raked Venusaur with both claws.

Slash! Slash!

The crowd cheered. The announcer joined them. “Meowth has hit Venusaur! That blow should—oh. Without a type advantage, it hasn't done much damage. And the Sleep Powder is falling now. How will Meowth get out?”

Many spectators had begun favoring Tyson. Ethan looked too strong, they wanted a closer match, and Tyson was a local Hoenn competitor. His first clean hit excited them almost as much as it excited him.

Ash and Morrison clenched their fists along with the crowd. Misty looked at them in distaste, crossed her legs and arms, and leaned back in her seat. “It was just two scratches. The battle's barely begun. What is everyone so excited about?”

Her companions realized they had annoyed her. They sat up straight and pretended nothing had happened.

On the field, Meowth faced the danger the announcer had spotted. No matter how fast it was, it could not simply run clear while Sleep Powder fell around it. Its small body and hat kept the powder from landing on it immediately, but green particles drifted ever closer. The whole stadium watched to see what it would do.

A flash of electricity burst from Meowth and cracked through the air.

“Thunderbolt!” the announcer cried. “This Meowth knows Thunderbolt!”

The discharge ignited the surrounding Sleep Powder and burned it away. The current spread farther, but the Pokémon it ought to have struck was gone.

Where was Venusaur?

When the glare faded, the crowd saw it well beyond the electric attack's reach. Supported by its vines, Venusaur had pulled itself backward in an instant, reopening the distance between them.

How had it known to move? Had Ethan anticipated Thunderbolt?

Tyson was the most startled of all. Thunderbolt was one of Meowth's hidden strengths. It had used the move only once in the preliminaries, and Ethan had not watched that match. Had Ash or someone else told him?

Ethan read the question on Tyson's face. No one had warned him. He had seen Tyson's Meowth in the anime long ago, but could not remember exactly what it could do. As far as he recalled, it was simply an unusually strong Meowth.

He had judged from its behavior. Meowth had seen Sleep Powder and rushed closer anyway. It must have had some way to protect itself. Ethan did not need to know what that way was to see that Venusaur should keep its distance. While everyone watched Meowth, he had ordered Venusaur back. That was how it escaped Thunderbolt.

Tyson, however, assumed Ethan had learned the move in advance—either from a recording of his earlier match or from someone who had seen it.

As Tyson searched for an explanation, Ethan attacked. Meowth was strong, but Venusaur had traveled with Ethan since it was a Bulbasaur. It had grown and evolved beside him and helped him win Conference titles. It had worked as hard as any Pokémon on his team.

A hat-wearing Meowth was not going to intimidate it. Even a Persian in that hat would have been another opponent to face.

A huge seed shot from Venusaur's flower toward Meowth. Meowth dodged the seed itself. Then it exploded, and the blast engulfed it.

“That's bad news,” the announcer said. “Seed Bomb fires a large seed with a hard shell. Meowth avoided the direct hit but couldn't escape the explosion.”

Meowth burst from the smoke and leaped to safe ground, breathing hard. It had run from the center of the blast, but the explosion covered a wide area. It had still been caught. Fortunately, the distance spared it a critical wound.

Burn marks stung its body and slowed it, yet its resolve to help Tyson win was greater than the pain.

Tyson exhaled when he saw Meowth escape. He blamed himself for losing focus. Had he noticed Venusaur's attack sooner, he might have spared Meowth those injuries. Regret would not help it now. He fixed his attention on the battle.

Meowth was still fighting with everything it had. Tyson would do no less.
