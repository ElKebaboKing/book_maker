Ethan had seen this black, catlike Pokémon more than once. It was Sneasel, a Dark- and Ice-type Pokémon.

Sneasel's appearance might have been inspired by the Japanese kamaitachi, or perhaps by a mix of a kamaitachi and a cat. Its body was mostly black, with a red tail, silvery white claws, and small gold markings on its forehead and chest. Sharp claws hid in its fingers.

Although Ethan had encountered Sneasel many times, he had never really learned much about it.

Now one had leaped in front of him with a vicious look. He raised his Pokédex and aimed it at the Pokémon.

The Pokédex chimed. "Sneasel, the Sharp Claw Pokémon. Dark and Ice type. Sneasel generally live in cold places and are cunning and vicious. They suddenly extend their sharp claws to intimidate attackers. They often hide in the dark to stay inconspicuous and hunt cooperatively at night. One draws the parents away while another steals an egg, though they may quarrel endlessly over who gets to eat it. In Alola, they have even been recorded preying on Sandshrew."

Ethan put the Pokédex away and looked at the Sneasel again.

It was plainly wild. It had its mouth open and seemed to be saying something to him, while Machoke and Machop flanked it like bodyguards or underlings.

Ethan glanced down at his feet. "Hey, Gengar, what's it saying?"

Gengar poked half its head out of his shadow. "It says this place belongs to it, and you aren't allowed to trespass."

"Your territory?" Ethan stared at Sneasel in surprise. "Isn't this a temple? How did it become yours? Are you one of the temple's Pokémon?"

The question seemed to provoke Sneasel. With a sudden roar, it sprang at Ethan and slashed at his face.

Ethan dodged backward, avoiding the attack. He frowned.

"What are you doing?"

Sneasel spoke again, and Gengar translated. "It says it's no Pokémon of you despicable humans, and you should leave at once."

Hearing the disturbance, a young man in black training clothes ran out of a nearby building and hurried over.

"Are you all right?" he asked Ethan. He glanced at Sneasel too, his face full of worry.

He appeared to work at the temple, so Ethan asked, "What's the story with this Sneasel?"

The man sighed. "It's a wild Pokémon from around here. It moved in and won't leave. We haven't been able to do anything about it."

Ethan was astonished. "Why don't you drive it away?"

"Well..." The man scratched his cheek awkwardly. "We've tried several times, but we can't beat it. Even the Trainers we asked for help lost to it. Recently it made that Machop and Machoke its followers, so now there's even less we can do!"

Erika spoke up. "So it has taken over the place?"

"It's causing us a lot of trouble," the man said with a sigh. "The Silver Conference is coming soon. With it occupying the temple, we can't even get close, let alone bring out the sacred flame!"

"I see." Ethan nodded. "Since we're here, I'll help. I just need to drive it off, right?"

"...I'd appreciate it."

The temple attendant gave a polite smile, but he did not look hopeful. As he had said, other Trainers had offered to help and made bold promises. In the end, they had accomplished nothing and only gotten their Pokémon hurt. Perhaps this would end the same way.

Ethan took several Poké Balls from his belt pouch and studied Sneasel again.

It had driven off that many Trainers, so it clearly had some ability. He would not underestimate it.

Besides, this was a good chance to train his own Pokémon.

He threw the Poké Balls. Blue light flashed, and Dragonite, Tyranitar, Scizor, and Eevee appeared before Sneasel.

Against Machop, Machoke, and Sneasel, each of the four faced at least some disadvantage in type matchups.

Ethan had chosen them deliberately. He wanted them to practice turning a battle around when the types were against them.

Of course, he did not want to lose. Sending out four Pokémon against three gave them a better chance of winning.

At the sight of Ethan's team, Sneasel roared and charged before they could make a move.

Machoke and Machop rushed in with it, closing on Ethan's Pokémon from both sides.

No wonder it had beaten so many Trainers. Even outnumbered, Sneasel dared to attack first.

Ethan thought a little more highly of it.

"Tyranitar, Scizor, keep Machoke and Machop busy! Eevee, help Dragonite deal with Sneasel!"

At his command, the four split up and faced their assigned opponents.

As Fighting types, Machop and Machoke had a decisive type advantage over Rock- and Dark-type Tyranitar. Scizor's Bug typing, at least, offset some of the weakness its Steel typing had to their Fighting-type attacks.

Dragonite and Eevee closed in on Sneasel together. Eevee, a Normal type, was in better shape. Against a Dark- and Ice-type like Sneasel, Dragonite's Dragon- and Flying-type combination put it at a severe disadvantage, just as Tyranitar was against the Fighting types.

Erika surveyed the matchups with concern. "Are you sure this is a good idea?"

"If they win, they win," Ethan said casually. "It isn't a tournament match. If they can't manage it, I have other Pokémon to back them up. Think of it as training."

Erika had nothing more to say, though she still worried. They were Ethan's Pokémon, and because she cared about him, she did not want any of them hurt.

Fortunately, his Pokémon were strong, while Machoke and Machop were not especially formidable. Tyranitar and Scizor soon gained the upper hand.

Dragonite, however, was in trouble.

It had failed to dodge one of Sneasel's Ice-type attacks, and the hit left it in poor condition. If Eevee had not kept drawing Sneasel's attention away, Dragonite might already have been defeated!
