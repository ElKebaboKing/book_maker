Confused, Anabel looked again at the far-right figure, the one her first instinct had marked as Scizor. It had vanished under Espeon's first Psychic attack too.

What? That one was wrong as well?

She had been certain she saw through the move. How could both of her guesses have failed? There was no time to work it out. The four remaining red figures were nearly upon Espeon, too close for it to run clear.

Acting on instinct, Espeon struck at the two in the middle. Like Anabel, it chose wrong. They had tested four of the six figures without finding the real Scizor.

Now the danger was immediate. Both Anabel and Espeon knew that if Scizor reached close range, escaping would be difficult and fighting it directly would be worse. Everything they had gained with Espeon's speed would disappear.

They had chosen to spend those first moments attacking rather than opening more distance. If their guesses had succeeded, the choice would have kept Espeon safe. Because they had failed, a heavy price followed.

Of the two figures left, the one on the left reached Espeon and raised both pincers. Scizor used X-Scissor.

Espeon, dodge! Anabel cried through their bond.

Espeon did not know Teleport. At this range its speed was no use. The Bug-type move struck before it could escape.

Everyone could see the real Scizor now. Knowing its identity after the hit did little good.

Like Anabel's Alakazam and Metagross, Espeon could fight an opponent that came close. It used Iron Tail, Take Down, and Quick Attack to hold its ground against most foes. None of those moves worked nearly as well against Ethan's Scizor, and the type matchup was against it.

After only a few more exchanges, Scizor's rapid attacks drained Espeon's remaining strength. It sank to the floor and could not rise.

“Espeon is unable to battle! The winner is challenger Ethan Vale!”

The green-haired referee shook his head, disappointed for Anabel, but raised his hand impartially. Apart from the draw between the two Metagross, Ethan had won this challenge by a wide margin. It had to sting for her.

Anabel recalled Espeon and hurried over to Ethan. Instead of congratulating him, she asked the question that had been troubling her.

“Mr. Vale, how did you do that with the copies? I thought I could see the real one. Why was I wrong so many times?”

That misjudgment had turned a manageable situation into a collapse. May joined them, looking just as curious. Ethan smiled. He waited for Scizor to reach his side, patted its shoulder, and answered.

“It's simple. I had Scizor use Double Team twice.”

“Twice?” May and Anabel asked together.

“The instant you saw through the first set, it used Double Team again over the original copies and changed its position.”

“You can do that?” Anabel stared at him. No wonder her successive guesses had failed.

The idea sounded straightforward, but she knew how demanding it was. A Pokémon needed great command of Double Team to cover the first set without exposing the trick. During the battle she had sensed that something was off, but she had not thought of a second use of the move. Scizor had concealed it exceptionally well.

Why tell her the method? Wasn't he worried about revealing a secret?

Then she considered it further. With enough practice, many Pokémon that knew Double Team might be able to use it twice. That was theory. Doing it mid-battle, at precisely the moment an opponent recognized the original, without letting a capable Trainer notice, was much harder. Too early or too late, and the trick failed. The Trainer had to read the situation sharply enough to choose the right instant. Simply knowing the principle would not let most people reproduce it.

The explanation left Anabel even more impressed with Ethan.

“That was an excellent battle,” she said. There was no dejection in her voice, only a little excitement. “I've never faced a Trainer as strong as you. Losing is frustrating, but I learned a great deal. I'd love to battle you again someday.”

Ethan nodded to himself. Even a decisive loss had not broken her confidence. She had taken the experience and grown more eager to fight. Anabel was not easily defeated in spirit.

Before he could answer, she spoke again. “Mr. Vale, may I ask you something?”

“What is it?”

She hesitated. “Have we met somewhere before? You look…familiar.”

Ethan's heart jumped. Had she noticed? He had told May nothing about the two Anabels and had been careful not to act as if he knew this one. Where had that feeling come from? Was it her ability to sense others through her bond with Pokémon?

This was why he disliked dealing with Trainers who specialized in Ghost or Psychic types. Ordinary Trainers were one thing, but Gym Leaders, Frontier Brains, and Elite Four members of those specialties often possessed unusual abilities themselves. A careless word around them could give away a secret.

Still, this Anabel and the Anabel of the International Police were separate people here. However closely related they were, they shared no memories. Why should Ethan seem familiar to her?

While he searched for an answer, May laughed.

“Of course he looks familiar,” she told Anabel. “Ethan won the Indigo League. You probably saw him on television.”

May knew nothing of Ethan's real worry, but she had given him an escape.

“The Indigo League champion?” Anabel looked surprised. It made sense: she must have seen his face on a broadcast. “So that's why…”

She accepted the explanation, though a faint feeling that something was still off remained. She chose not to pursue it. Instead she took a gold-colored Symbol from her pocket and handed it to Ethan.

“Mr. Vale, this is my Ability Symbol. It's proof that you defeated me. Please accept it.”

It looked almost like a gold coin or a bottle cap. Ethan accepted it carefully and put it in his Symbol case. Seeing all six places filled, Anabel nodded in understanding.

“So you've collected six Symbols. You'll be challenging Mr. Brandon next.”

Brandon was the Frontier Brain of the Battle Pyramid, the seventh and final facility. Challengers needed all six earlier Symbols before they could learn its location and seek him out. As the final test, Brandon was widely regarded as the strongest of the seven Frontier Brains.

“Yes. The time's gone quickly,” Ethan said. “Will you tell me where the Pyramid is, or should I ask Scott?”

“Scott will tell you.” Anabel smiled. “Even if I wanted to, I don't know its exact location. The Frontier Brains know of one another, of course, but we look after different areas and don't often meet. I know even less about the Battle Pyramid.”

That was mostly true, though not absolute. Some Frontier Brains were friends despite their infrequent contact. Lucy, for example, had heard from Greta that Ethan was traveling between the facilities.

Ethan waved goodbye to Anabel and left the forest with May. At the nearest Pokémon Center, they entrusted the Pokémon that had battled to Nurse Joy. Then they went to the dining area. While they waited for dinner, Ethan called Scott.

He answered quickly and guessed the reason before Ethan spoke. “Mr. Vale, a call from you now must mean you've collected all six Frontier Symbols.”

“I have. I'm calling to ask where the final facility is.”

Scott sounded wistful. “Already? I still remember the first time we met. I didn't think you'd finish this quickly. Well, I've sent its location to your phone. Take a look!”

Ethan thanked him and ended the call. As he went to open the message, he noticed May staring at him.

“What? Is there something on my face?”

She shook her head. “I don't understand why every challenger has to ask for the Battle Pyramid's address after getting six Symbols. Isn't it always in the same place?”

“That's exactly it.” Ethan smiled. “It may be built like a pyramid, but it isn't fixed in one place. It moves.”

“Moves? Does it have wheels underneath, like a camper? Or does it travel by sea?”

“Neither. It's more extravagant than that.” He pointed upward.

May understood and gasped. “It's in the sky?”

“Yes. Brandon fitted the entire Pyramid with a flight system. He can move it wherever he wants. That's why challengers have to get its current position from Scott.” Ethan opened the message and turned his phone toward her. “Right now it's near Viridian City. By tomorrow it could be somewhere else.”

“Viridian City…” May sighed. “Then we'd better hurry there. We're at the eastern edge of Kanto. That's a long way to walk.”

“That works out well.” Ethan put his phone away and slid her dinner toward her. “You still need one more Ribbon for the Grand Festival. The Festival itself is on our way back, so we can handle both things along the route. Besides, as I said, the Pyramid moves every day. By the time we're done with the Festival, it might turn up right in front of us.”
