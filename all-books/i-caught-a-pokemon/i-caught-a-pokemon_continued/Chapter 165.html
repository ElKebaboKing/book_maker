On the field, Jolteon's Quick Attack struck Scyther and sent it flying more than ten meters. Scyther rolled across the ground again and again before collapsing, battered, at Logan's feet.

On the green trainer's platform, Logan stared at his fourth fallen Pokémon, his heart racing.

Four to zero.

If this kept up, he would be swept out of the final and remembered only as a miserable backdrop to Ethan's triumph. The difference in strength, and Ethan's relentless use of type advantages, left him unsure how to prevent it.

At the referee's prompting, Logan recalled Scyther and sent out Sandslash. Its Ground typing made it immune to Jolteon's Electric-type moves. Logan knew that would not be enough. Ethan would simply switch Pokémon again.

Sure enough, Ethan recalled Jolteon as soon as he saw Sandslash and brought out Blastoise.

Water against Ground. Again.

Logan sighed. Ever since losing the first bout, he had been forced to choose his next Pokémon before Ethan chose his. He did not know how to turn the match around.

But battles could still surprise him. As the fifth bout began, Sandslash did something that made him look up with hope.

It dug into the ground and used Sand Tomb. A round pit formed at the center of the field, and the packed earth around it broke into fine sand that poured toward the hole.

Blastoise was caught off guard. It slid toward the pit, rocking its body in an effort to get clear, but its own weight drove its feet deep into the sand.

Against all expectations, Sandslash had found an opening. Logan glimpsed a ray of hope in what had felt like complete darkness.

Was there still a chance?

Watching Blastoise sink farther, Ethan thought for a moment and chose a solution worth trying.

"Blastoise, aim at the center of the pit! Use Water Gun!"

Both cannons on Blastoise's back fired streams of water into the hole. The sand around its feet quickly soaked through. Wet grains clung together and turned the shifting ground into heavy mud.

That mud no longer flowed so easily toward the pit. Sand Tomb's pull was broken. Blastoise swam a short distance through the mess and climbed back onto safe ground, covered in muck.

Sandslash had been hiding beneath the dry sand, waiting to attack. Once water flooded in, it took in a mouthful and had to scramble to the surface.

The crowd and commentator marveled at Ethan's inventive answer. Logan watched his brief hope vanish, his mood sinking again.

So even that wasn't enough.

The fight continued. Sandslash's long claws raked across Blastoise's shell, throwing off sparks, but Blastoise answered with Water Pulse and left it reeling.

Then both Pokémon began to spin. Blastoise withdrew into its shell; Sandslash curled into a ball. Like two tops, they gathered speed and slammed into each other.

Bang!

They separated and unfolded. Blastoise had taken only a little damage. Sandslash staggered forward a few steps, lost its balance, and fell.

The referee raised the flag.

"Incredible!" the commentator shouted. "Ethan Vale wins another bout! It's five to zero! One more Pokémon stands between him and the championship. Is he about to set a record beyond anything this tournament has seen?"

Cheering filled the stands. Someone began a chant, and the scattered shouts gradually joined into a single voice.

"Champion! Champion! Champion!"

The spectators had started it themselves. To them, Ethan had earned the title already. Ash, Brock, and the others rose and joined in.

Logan's face darkened. The match wasn't over. He still had one Pokémon left. Yet the crowd had stopped expecting anything from him.

It was as good as finished.

He glanced toward the referee and caught a flicker of sympathy in the man's eyes.

Even the referee pitied him? That stung more than Logan could bear.

"Champion! Champion! Champion!"

The chant went on. Ethan, now the focus of the entire stadium, had not expected anything like it. He was usually composed, but his heart surged with excitement.

Win one more bout, and he could lift the Indigo League trophy.

At the commentary booth, Lance watched him thoughtfully. He had spent years among the Elite Four and seen countless trainers, at both the Indigo and Silver Conferences. He had never seen an audience embrace a competitor quite like this.

No wonder the International Police had once asked Ethan for help.

Under enormous pressure, Logan sent out his last Pokémon: Hitmonlee, a Fighting-type.

Ethan recalled Blastoise and chose his sixth Pokémon, Gardevoir.

Hitmonlee had won plenty of battles for Logan. But by now, Logan had lost the will to fight. Facing a Psychic-type that held the advantage over Hitmonlee, he almost wanted the match to end quickly.

One side was riding a wave of confidence. The other had nearly given up.

The result was inevitable. Gardevoir struck Hitmonlee down with Psychic.

After more than two weeks of competition, the Indigo League tournament was over.

Colorful streamers burst from both sides of the field and filled the air. The commentator leaped to his feet.

"We have a new Indigo League champion! Congratulations to Ethan Vale, the winner of this year's tournament! And almost unbelievably, from the preliminaries through the final, he never lost a single bout. He's set a record that may be nearly impossible to break! Now let's call out his name together!"
