As they slowly finished their coffee and a few pieces of dessert, the initial excitement of their reunion settled. Conversation became easier, and they talked about why May had come to Slateport.

There was no Gym in the city, but Ash's group had not arrived without a purpose. May had begun her travels unsure of what she wanted. After some time on the road, she had found her direction and resolved to become a top Pokémon Coordinator.

Coordinators took a different path from conventional Trainers like Ethan. They caught and raised Pokémon for Pokémon Contests, traveling to events in different cities to earn Ribbons rather than focusing on ordinary battles.

They cared about their Pokémon's appearance and condition. They groomed them and gave them Berries, Pokéblocks, and Poffins to improve their condition. They trained moves too, paying attention to how beautiful and polished each performance was. It was a different road, but every bit as demanding.

“There's a Pokémon Contest here?” Misty's eyes lit up. She was still new to Hoenn and curious about these performances. She had regretted missing the last one; now, so soon afterward, another opportunity had appeared.

“Yes,” May said. “It's tomorrow, and I've entered.”

Misty grew even more excited. She turned to Ethan with shining eyes. “Can we go watch?”

“Of course.” Traveling meant seeing all sorts of people and events. They had arrived at the right time, so why miss this one? Ethan also wanted to see what a real Pokémon Contest was like—and how May, the future “Princess of Hoenn,” would perform.

He looked at May again. She was the girl who traveled with Ash in the third generation of the anime, and she was as pretty as Misty, though in a very different way. They had one thing in common: both were still flat-chested. At their current age, they were still growing up. They might be more mature in thought than boys their age, but neither looked like an adult yet.

May would earn the title “Princess of Hoenn” and a following after entering only a few Contests. Her choice of path was clearly right for her. She must have remarkable talent.

May noticed Ethan's gaze from across the table. He wasn't embarrassed to be caught looking. He met her eyes, gave her a friendly nod and smile, then turned away as though nothing had happened.

Though young, May could tell he had been studying her. She didn't read anything into it and went back to talking with Misty.

She felt an immediate connection with the other girl. Misty could be rather forceful, but their views were much alike. May had a feeling that the girl who called herself the world's most beautiful young woman might one day become a close friend.

There was a reason May wanted that friendship. She had only recently settled on her goal. She was eager to pursue it but not yet confident, and the thought of her first Pokémon Contest made her anxious.

Her traveling companions were all boys. Ash was oblivious, Brock lost his head whenever a beautiful older woman appeared, and Max, though her own brother, was a bookworm. None quite understood what troubled her. She had kept worries she couldn't sort out to herself. Now she had met an outgoing girl her own age and finally had someone to confide in.

Misty felt much the same. She had three older sisters, but their age difference and different dreams left them little to talk about. Most days they argued instead. She had kept her own private feelings to herself. Meeting May—the girl who had inherited the hardships of traveling with Ash—felt like meeting an old friend.

They talked eagerly about their interests, hobbies, and shopping. By the time everyone left the café, May and Misty were walking arm in arm.

The boys, Ethan included, were too slow to understand how the friendship had formed. They could hardly believe two girls meeting for the first time had become so close, so quickly.

They needed somewhere to stay, so after leaving the café's street, the group headed for the Pokémon Center.

On the way, Ash excitedly told Ethan about the Pokémon he had caught. When Ethan mentioned that his Torchic had evolved into Combusken, Ash could hardly wait to battle him.

Ethan shook his head at the boy's eagerness. “What's the rush? Even if we're going to battle, shouldn't we check in first?”

“Heh.” Ash rubbed his nose, suddenly realizing how impatient he sounded. “Right. We can do it after we get to the Pokémon Center. Go, go, go!”

Saying that did little to calm his desire for a match. He hurried the group across much of the city until they finally reached the Pokémon Center.

But when they opened the door, Ash stopped short. The lobby was packed with people of every age, height, and appearance. All of them had a Pokémon at their side.

“Why are there so many people?” he murmured.

“Isn't it obvious?” Ethan walked past him toward the counter. “They must be here for the Pokémon Contest. Come on, let's check in before every room is taken.”
