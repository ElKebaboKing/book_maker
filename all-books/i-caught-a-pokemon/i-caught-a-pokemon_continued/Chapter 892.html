Perhaps Ho-Oh had changed Ethan's constitution. Although he had gone to bed late, he woke early the next morning. He had slept only a few hours, yet he felt full of energy, without a trace of fatigue from yesterday's long flight on Charizard.

He had said it many times already, but Ho-Oh's blessing really was powerful.

Eevee's evolution had settled another concern. With one day left before the Grand Festival began, Ethan could relax and look around the venue, remembering how it had felt when he competed in the Indigo League.

It was barely six in the morning. The others were still asleep, so he left them undisturbed and ate breakfast. As activity picked up outside, he took his newly evolved Umbreon for a walk beyond the Pokémon Center.

He had hardly stepped out when he saw an interview playing on a distant outdoor screen. The woman being filmed wore glasses and a disguise, but Ethan recognized Jessie at once. He knew her too well not to.

Team Rocket's disguises could be impressive—James could even pass for a busty woman—but familiar details gave them away to Ethan. Ash and people who did not know the trio might remain fooled.

Still, seeing Jessie's face first thing in the morning put a dent in his mood. It was a beautiful day, and he had hoped for a pleasant surprise. Instead, the moment he stepped outside, he found Team Rocket.

He rounded the screen and spotted the trio filming in an open space nearby. Jessie had her hair in two long pigtails and wore large glasses, a short, ornate dress with a big red bow, knee-high white stockings, and orange shoes. She smiled professionally at the camera, trying very hard to look cute.

James, Meowth, and Wobbuffet were dressed as her camera operator and crew. Ethan moved closer through the onlookers to hear her report.

“Hello, everyone!” Jessie said in an affectedly sweet voice, holding a microphone. “Tomorrow, the long-awaited Pokémon Grand Festival begins! I'm the adorable Jessadia, bringing you beautiful live reports from the scene!”

She stretched her words and played up every cute gesture. Ethan could see how hard she was trying to win people over. To him, the costume and voice sat awkwardly on her. She had rather outgrown this sort of act.

Jessadia continued, “Coordinators who earned five Ribbons across Kanto have been arriving at the Indigo Plateau! Excitement outside the venue has reached its peak. Everyone's busy checking their Pokémon's health before tomorrow's preliminary round!”

James turned the camera to show the surrounding activity, which appeared on the big screen. Ethan stepped back into the crowd to stay out of the shot.

For all their failures at stealing Pokémon, the trio were good at this kind of work. When the short segment ended, Contest host Lilian walked up to Jessie.

“Well done, Miss Jessadia! You volunteered for the job, and you're proving you have talent.”

“Thank you!” Jessie bowed with a smile. “I'll keep working hard!”

Lilian had not recognized her. She had met Jessie many times and had even seen her posing as the Contest entrant Jessibella. Yet another name and a new outfit were enough to fool her. Perhaps she simply had no reason to expect Jessie to return as a reporter.

“Then I'll leave it to you to keep the crowd excited,” Lilian said. “See you later!” She waved and went on her way.

Ethan had no intention of exposing Team Rocket. If they did not cause trouble in front of him, he saw no reason to interfere. He shook his head and walked on with Umbreon.

After making a circuit of the venue, he put the encounter out of his mind. When he thought May and the others would have woken and eaten, he returned to the Pokémon Center. Team Rocket had never noticed him. If they had, their fine morning might have soured as quickly as his.

In the dining area, May was on a video call. As Ethan came closer, he saw her mother Caroline on the screen.

“I'm sorry, May,” Caroline was saying. “I really can't get away. I can't come to Kanto to cheer for you in person.”

Before May answered, Max let out a disappointed sound. Like his sister, he had hoped to see their mother after so long apart, rather than talk through a screen.

May shook her head understandingly. “It's all right. You and Dad are busy.”

“Don't worry!” Caroline said brightly. “I'll send you all my cheering energy from Hoenn!” She pushed both hands toward the camera as though sending a wave of power.

May and Max stared, their expressions flattening at their mother's playfulness. “You don't need to do that. We know you're cheering for us...”

Ethan stepped into view and greeted Caroline.

“Oh, Ethan! Thank you for looking after May. She hasn't caused you trouble, has she?”

“Mom!” May protested.

“Not at all,” Ethan said. “May's helped me a great deal. Traveling with her has made things easier for me.”

“What a sweet thing to say!” Caroline laughed. “Her father and I can't be there, so please cheer for her in person.”

“Of course.”

Ash leaned toward the screen and thumped his chest. “Leave it to us, Aunt Caroline!”

“Thank you! Max, you cheer for your sister too. Bye, everyone!”

The call had just ended, and May was about to speak when cries rose from the other end of the lobby.

An Aipom had appeared from somewhere and was snatching people's hats, stacking them on its head. When anyone tried to catch it, it darted through the crowd and stole still more hats. The commotion grew.

“Is that...?” Ash's eyebrows rose when he saw it.

“Do you know that Aipom?” May asked.

Ash gave an awkward smile. Brock explained. On their way here, after Ash lost to Brandon, they had come across a group of Aipom fleeing an angry farmer. One had taken Ash's hat. The effort to get it back had led to several more encounters with that particular Aipom.

Now it had followed them to the Indigo Plateau and was stealing hats again. May shook her head at their luck. Ethan, recognizing the Pokémon, smiled.

Anyone familiar with Ash's future team would know this Aipom. Ash would soon catch it and take it through Sinnoh on his Gym journey. Later he would trade it to Dawn, who would use it in Contests. Eventually it would leave them both to train with a Pokémon Ping-Pong player. Some fans would jokingly call it the “ungrateful monkey.”

It was capable in both battles and Contest performances. Its restless temperament and tendency toward jealousy, and the ease with which it later left its companions, made some viewers dislike it. Others jokingly called it a Pokémon of three masters.

Ethan was less bothered. Each departure had given Ash or Dawn a useful new partner, and Aipom had contributed plenty while traveling with them. They had parted amicably; it was the viewers who felt slighted.

Whether because Aipom was uncommon in Hoenn or because May found it cute, she raised her Pokédex. It supplied an entry.

“Aipom, the Long Tail Pokémon. Normal type. Most of its body is purple, with cream-colored areas and a single tuft of hair on its head. Females have a longer tuft than males. It lives high in trees and can hang upside down from branches by its strong tail. Its tail also helps it balance as it leaps between branches. Aipom stays lively and energetic throughout the day.”

By then Aipom had piled more than a dozen hats on its head. It came to a stop in front of Ash and wiggled about, plainly proud of itself. He had no doubt it was the same one.

“Why are you stealing people's hats again?” he asked helplessly.

To everyone nearby, that sounded as though Ash was its Trainer. Faces turned toward him.

“Is that your Aipom?”

“Tell it to give our hats back!”

“Stop it from causing trouble!”

With so many people speaking at once, Ash could hardly explain. He turned back to Aipom. “Come on. Give everyone their hats back.”

Perhaps Aipom listened. It twisted and sent the stack of hats flying off its head. Then it sprang forward, yanked Ash's cap away, and fled into the crowd without looking back.

That cap was one of Ash's most treasured possessions. He chased Aipom at once, but even he could not catch an agile monkey in such a crowded place. Pikachu joined in; its speed was little help against Aipom's leaps from one height to another.

Nurse Joy hurried over to investigate the noise. As Aipom passed her, it stole her cap too. That was enough to draw Brock into the chase on her behalf.

May stood holding her Pokédex, stunned. A moment ago everything had been peaceful. Now the lobby had turned into a contest of people and Pokémon chasing hats.

Then again, trouble seemed to follow Ash wherever he went. She almost felt nostalgic. She glanced at Ethan, about to ask whether they should intervene.

Before she could, Roselia sprang into the fray. Its Petal Dance formed a small whirlwind of petals around Aipom, trapping it. The crowd turned to see a green-haired boy in purple, hands tucked coolly in his pockets.

“Drew?” Ash and the others recognized him at once. None had expected to find him here, much less see him step in to stop the disturbance.

A woman's voice called from the crowd. “Slowbro, use Psychic!”

Psychic power wrapped around Aipom. It floated out of Roselia's swirling petals and drifted slowly back toward Ash.
