The two Deoxys made strange sounds Ethan could not understand. Gardevoir's telepathic link had been released for the moment, but the Pokémon appeared to be speaking amicably. Neither made a threatening move.

After a long exchange, they descended together and landed in front of him.

They looked identical, but Ethan could tell them apart. One regarded him with familiar, calm eyes. The other carried a cool, distant pressure. His own Deoxys nodded to him, and Ethan knew the first part of his plan had worked.

If he could persuade the wild Deoxys, he might avert the destruction it had caused in the movie. Then he could quietly help it find its companion.

He asked Gardevoir to connect him to the wild Deoxys through telepathy.

“I know where your companion is, and I know it's safe. Please don't worry. Wait a little longer, and we'll find it without hurting anyone.”

“...I understand... My companion trusts you... I'll trust you too...”

Ethan let out a breath. He had feared it might refuse to communicate, but the conversation had gone smoothly.

His Deoxys had also met another of its kind. It now knew it was not alone on this planet. That was Ethan's greatest reason for coming to LaRousse, and perhaps the best assurance that Deoxys would want to stay with him.

Then both Pokémon looked toward the distant sky and went on guard.

Ethan followed their gaze. A black speck was approaching fast. He understood at once.

Rayquaza was coming.

The auroras had attracted more than human attention. They had drawn the top predator from the ozone layer. Rayquaza held a grudge, and if Deoxys had hurt it before, it would want revenge.

So much for avoiding a confrontation.

Compared with Groudon and Kyogre's battle, the two Deoxys had done nothing dangerous. Their auroras carried no force. But viewed from the ozone layer, that display had been far more conspicuous than the battle, enough to catch Rayquaza's eye.

At least Ethan had thought to move out of the city. For now, LaRousse should be safe.

Within moments, Rayquaza reached the forest. The instant it spotted the Deoxys, it fired a blast of energy without hesitation. Both Deoxys rose into the air, dodged, and flew to meet it.

The attack missed them and kept descending toward the ground. Gardevoir reacted at once, wrapping Ethan in psychic power just as the scorching energy washed over him.

For a moment, he could see nothing but orange-yellow light. The psychic barrier kept him from being hurt, but the heat was nearly unbearable. A few seconds later, the attack passed.

Now Ethan was angry. Rayquaza clearly had no concern for whether the human below lived or died.

Then again, it lived high in the ozone layer, far above everyone else. With that great serpentine body, it seemed cold-blooded and indifferent to life. The Draconids worshiped it as a savior, but to Ethan it was a Pokémon that treated the whole planet as its territory. It had stopped Groudon and Kyogre's earlier battle only because their fight had disturbed it.

Rayquaza had seen two Deoxys waiting here and attacked anyway. It certainly had nerve. At a certain point, though, courage became stupidity.

Ethan had Gardevoir take him to a safer spot and settled in to watch.

From what he remembered of the movie and from their relative strength, Rayquaza had struggled against one Deoxys. What gave it confidence against two?

High above, the Deoxys moved in perfect accord, one before Rayquaza and one behind. They blocked its path and attacked together. Black beams shot from their cores. Rayquaza dodged the first but the second struck it, drawing a cry of pain.

The blow was still within the limits of what one of this planet's greatest predators could endure. Rayquaza twisted toward the Deoxys behind it and spat another burst of energy, which its target nimbly evaded.

Ethan had heard tales of two dragons playing with a pearl. Today he was watching two “pearls” toy with a dragon.

While the three Pokémon fought, Professor Lund and Yuko were scrambling in the LaRousse laboratory. They had noticed the first aurora over the city but thought little of it. In a world of Pokémon, unusual lights could have any number of causes.

It had been four years since their Antarctic expedition, when they saw Rayquaza fight Deoxys and brought the other Deoxys's core home. The scene had faded in their memories. They had not expected the Deoxys Rayquaza defeated to travel all this way in search of the core.

When blue light appeared over the forest and the red aurora answered it from the city, they understood. Deoxys had come. Its purpose was obvious: it wanted its companion back.

By rights, they ought to return the core. But they had spent four years pouring time, equipment, and effort into it, and their experiments were finally nearing completion. Letting go would be difficult.

Keeping it was dangerous too. A searching Deoxys could turn the city upside down.

Yuko immediately offered a suggestion for the residents' safety, but after considering it, Lund did not act on it. Instead, he called Officer Jenny by video and asked her to order a citywide evacuation.

An evacuation would affect hundreds of thousands of people. Any mistake would bring public criticism, so Jenny could not issue the order on one man's word alone. She needed to understand the threat.

“Professor Lund,” she said from a screen in the high-tech police station, “why is Deoxys here? What is it looking for?”

Lund would not reveal the core in his laboratory. “What it's looking for isn't the immediate issue. Rayquaza may come after it. If those two fight near the city, we can't afford even a fraction of the damage they could cause.”

Jenny could sense that he was withholding something. But this was no time to press him. If the Pokémon fought in LaRousse, the destruction could be catastrophic.

“Understood. I'll issue the evacuation order now. Contact me at once if anything changes.”

She ended the call and pressed a red button on her console. Across LaRousse, the city's robots sprang into action. Their screens changed from the green that signaled safety to yellow. They went from street to street and door to door, telling residents and visitors to evacuate. With the moving walkways, they directed people to the nearest shelters.

...

Elsewhere, Ash and the others had gathered in an enclosed park. Tory stood nearby, speaking quietly to a translucent, diamond-shaped figure that glowed cyan.

After leaving the café, Ash had found Tory there with surprising ease. He had seen the strange glowing figure beside him. Tory initially tried to hide it, but Ash's friendly persistence soon won him over. They became friends, and Tory introduced him to the figure.

Brock and Max arrived soon afterward. Misty and May followed. With the exception of Ethan, the group was together again.

They were laughing and talking when the evacuation alarm sounded. Misty alone understood what it might mean. The others looked around in confusion.

“What's happening?” Ash asked. He looked outside the park but saw only people evacuating.

“Could it be connected to that aurora?” Brock wondered. He had identified the most likely clue, though he could not be certain.

May looked at Misty. Misty had reacted strangely to the aurora, called Ethan, and then brought May straight to the others. She must know something.

Misty would normally have told her friends, but Tory was there. She shook her head slightly, asking May to stay quiet. May answered with a look. The two girls had become close enough to understand each other without speaking.

Max missed the exchange. He glanced at the people outside. “Shouldn't we evacuate too?”

Tory stood and pointed to a small gate at the other side of the park. “I know a better place to shelter. That way leads to my father's laboratory. It's well protected.”

But Ash shook his head. “No. I'm going outside to see what's happening.”

“But the announcement says everyone has to take shelter,” Tory protested.

“That's exactly why we should look!” Ash gave him a bright smile. “We can't help if we don't know what's going on.”

The idea shook Tory. He would never have thought of responding that way. But Ash and the others had already run out of the park. Tory gritted his teeth and followed.

Outside, people were evacuating faster, and a few frightened screams rose above the noise.

What was happening?

They looked across the river. Above the forest in the distance, a long green Pokémon twisted through the sky while two humanoid Pokémon attacked it. Explosions and bursts of fire flashed around them. The fighting seemed to be moving closer to LaRousse.

“What Pokémon are those?” Ash had never seen any of the three.

Max adjusted his glasses, eyes shining. “I know! I read about the long green one. It's the Legendary Pokémon Rayquaza!”

“Rayquaza?” Ash committed the name to memory and looked at the other two. “Then who are those Pokémon?”
