Jolteon and Sceptile began their battle.

As Tyson had suspected, Ethan had not chosen Jolteon for some special strategy. He looked as though he had reached into his belt pouch at random and sent out whichever Pokémon's ball his hand found, regardless of whether it suited the matchup. Even he could not predict his next choice, much less anyone else.

Almost no other Trainer at the Ever Grande Conference would dare fight that way. Random choice could hand an opponent a type advantage and make a difficult battle worse. Ethan's willingness to do it showed how little he feared such disadvantages. He trusted his Pokémon's strength.

In the main grandstand, Goodshow watched helplessly. He had arranged for Ethan to face one of the strongest performers from the preliminary tournament, yet Tyson could not even make Ethan take the matchup seriously. The round of thirty-two was unlikely to stop him. Goodshow could only hope Tyson forced Ethan to reveal more of his team so later opponents would know what to expect and perhaps find a weakness.

On the field, Sceptile went straight on the offensive. It had already fought through half a match and was warmed up; the rest had restored much of its energy.

Jolteon, however, had been with Ethan since early in his travels and had distinguished itself at both the Indigo and Silver Conferences. In strength, composure, and experience, it surpassed Sceptile. A few leaps across the rocks were enough to find its rhythm. After several probing attacks, it took the advantage.

It needed no Electric-type moves. Its Normal-type attacks could trouble Sceptile, and Pin Missile gave it a Bug-type move that struck Grass types hard.

Its speed was Tyson's greatest problem. Sceptile's powerful legs let it cross the rocks easily, but Jolteon was quicker and lighter. Four paws gave it surer footing, and Sceptile could rarely catch up.

The two Pokémon darted back and forth across the Water Field, first ahead, then behind, then left and right. They touched down on almost every cluster of rocks. To the naked eye, they were little more than passing shadows; the audience needed the screen's slow-motion replays to follow them.

That could not last. Their attacks cracked the rocks and sent pieces sinking into the pool. As their room to move shrank, the battle grew more intense. At last, both Pokémon were forced into a direct exchange on a single flat rock—the only foothold left.

Now that they could no longer run, the audience could finally see Sceptile's desperate struggle. Jolteon was superior in too many ways. However much rest Sceptile had received, it was being driven to the brink.

Tyson gripped a Poké Ball. He thought for two seconds, then decided not to recall Sceptile. That instant of distraction was enough for the battle to reach its end.

Jolteon fired Pin Missile. Sceptile, covered in injuries, had nowhere to dodge the dense swarm of sharp needles. It tried to answer with Leaf Storm, but Pin Missile came first. The needles were short-ranged, yet they launched with such speed that Sceptile was struck again and again before the storm of leaves could fully form.

Sceptile gritted its teeth and tried to hold on. Jolteon's shape flashed before it. By the time Sceptile lifted its head, Jolteon was already lunging with Quick Attack.

The hit caught Sceptile squarely. It fell and lost consciousness.

“Sceptile is unable to battle! Jolteon wins!” the referee announced.

Applause came from the stands, though it was not especially enthusiastic. After Metagross and Dragonite's brief, thrilling contest, Tyson's later Pokémon had spent most of their time under pressure. The crowd could see the gap between him and Ethan. It was also clear that his team had not been comfortable on the Water Field.

With Tyson's third Pokémon defeated, at least the field would change. Perhaps he would fare better on new ground.

Ethan did not look at Tyson's expression. He recalled Jolteon, left his command position, and went to the nearby rest room. Under the final tournament rules, the fall of a third Pokémon on either side brought a brief intermission. Spectators could visit the facilities; Trainers could rest and plan their next moves.

For Ethan, there was little to plan. Facing Tyson had been no more difficult than facing Harrison in Johto. Tyson's performance in these rounds had even fallen short of Harrison's.

Of course, Ethan had used longtime partners. Milotic and Metagross, which he had caught more recently, would have had a harder time. But they had fought in the preliminaries, and his rotation now favored the older members of the team. Tyson had been unlucky to meet him in this round. If they had met one round later, Ethan might have needed to work much harder.

Tyson still had his formidable Meowth. Ethan hoped it would give him a better surprise.

Elsewhere in the stands, Team Rocket's Meowth stopped in the middle of selling rice crackers and drinks. The trio were in disguise, and it looked toward Tyson's side of the battlefield.

Jessie noticed first. “What's wrong, Meowth? Don't just stand there. This is our chance to make a fortune! Get moving!”

“I know, meow.” Meowth looked at Tyson once more, shook its head, and followed Jessie away. They were the same species and had lived somewhat similar lives. It had said what it could the night before. Against that man, warnings would do little good.

While the Trainers took their break, the roulette wheel appeared on the stadium screen again. The Water Field symbol was gone, leaving rock, grass, and ice. The pointer spun, slowed, and came to rest on grass.

Tyson breathed more easily. The Water Field had kept his Pokémon from fighting at their best. Now they could finally move freely.

The intermission passed quickly. At the referee's signal, the Trainers returned to their positions with new Poké Balls. The entire Water Field descended. Gears rumbled, and a Grass Field rose in its place, covered with thick grass, small hills, and several trees.

Tyson was more urgent than before. He released his Pokémon as soon as the field locked into place. Blue light flashed, and Donphan appeared.

Ethan could see Tyson's determination to recover from his losses. Three of his Pokémon were down without a single knockout in return. Ethan would have been anxious in his place too.

He smiled and threw his own Poké Ball. A roar shook the field before the crowd saw what had emerged.

“Ethan has sent out Tyranitar!” the announcer called. “Good news for Tyson! Donphan's Ground type has an advantage against the Rock-type Tyranitar. This could be his chance to pull a round back!”

Tyson heard him. He disliked the suggestion that only a favorable type matchup gave him a chance, but the announcer was right about one thing: this was an opportunity. Donphan had the type advantage, and the open Grass Field suited it. Tyson could not imagine losing this exchange.

His smile disappeared a moment later.

Both Pokémon reached their starting positions, but before the referee could begin, Donphan looked into Tyranitar's cold eyes. The force of its presence made Donphan step back in fear.

“What?!” Tyson frowned. The announcer cried out too. “An unexpected development! Tyranitar's sheer presence has frightened Donphan. What will that mean for this battle?”

After Ethan's gigantic red Gyarados and Crystal Steelix, Tyranitar was his third ferocious-looking Pokémon. It had not yet reached the heights of those two, but an ordinary Pokémon could still be shaken by it. It needed no Intimidate Ability. Its fierce face and gaze were enough to inspire fear.

“Donphan, stay calm! Don't let it scare you!”

Tyson had to encourage his Pokémon before the fight even started. Fortunately, Donphan's long travels had given it some nerve. The sight had merely caught it off guard, and Tyson's voice helped it recover.

Better now than in the middle of the match. Ethan would surely have attacked if Donphan had faltered then. Tyson took what comfort he could, though the incident left a shadow over his hopes.

Once both Pokémon were ready again, the referee raised the flag to begin the first exchange of the second half.

“Donphan, Sandstorm!” Tyson ordered.

Yellow sand rolled across more than half the field. Ethan lost sight of Tyranitar inside it. So did the audience, the referee, and even the cameras.

From the other side, Tyson gave his next command. “Donphan, use Rollout!”

This was clearly a combination he trusted. Sandstorm cut off the opposing Trainer's view and made effective orders difficult. Donphan could then roll through the obscured field and crash into its target before the other side knew where the attack was coming from. Against many Trainers, it might work.

Yet Sandstorm was a Rock-type move, and Tyranitar was part Rock type. The blowing sand also hid the battle from Tyson. If Rollout failed, he would not know what was happening inside his own cover.

Most of all, Tyranitar could keep fighting and tearing apart whatever stood before it without Ethan's guidance. With its towering body and heavy armor, how much damage could a rolling Donphan really do? Tyson had considered that risk, but his order was given. He could only trust his Pokémon now.

Donphan curled into a wheel and charged into the sandstorm like a runaway tire.
