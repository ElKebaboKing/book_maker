Having accepted Foster's request, Ethan and Sabrina entered the ruins together.

They began with the nearest stone chamber. Although it was still daytime outside, the interior was dim. With no electricity or lights, Ethan had to turn on a flashlight before he could see the chamber clearly.

The bright beam swept back and forth across the stone walls.

With its help, Ethan looked around. The chamber was completely empty. Even the writing-like patterns on its walls had faded, weathered until they were barely visible.

He approached one wall and slowly traced a finger across the patterns, as though he could still feel the passage of time within them.

He turned to Sabrina. "Do you sense anything strange nearby?"

Sabrina closed her eyes and extended her psychic power through the surroundings. After a long while, she shook her head.

"No. This place feels only peaceful, like a desert or a stone. It's entirely ordinary. Nothing feels out of place."

Her answer did not particularly surprise Ethan.

Foster and the other researchers had probably surveyed these chambers countless times. If anything could be found through ordinary means, they would have discovered it long ago. There would have been nothing left for Ethan.

Foster clearly had not asked him to explore in the same conventional way.

They left the first chamber and searched a second, then a third. Both were just as empty.

This won't work. Searching blindly like this will only waste our time!

Ethan stopped and carefully reviewed everything he remembered about the ruins from the games. At last, he decided to try another method.

He strode out of the chamber and approached Foster.

"Do you have a radio?"

"A radio?" Foster stared at him. "What do you need one of those for?"

Ethan could hardly explain the truth. "I have an idea that I need a radio to test."

Has he discovered something?

Foster's eyes brightened. He had never truly expected Ethan to find anything. He had merely wondered whether a Champion might notice something ordinary people could not and had invited him to try his luck.

Now Ethan apparently had a clue. Of course Foster would offer all the support he could.

But a radio...

Foster considered the request.

Few people other than the elderly still used radios these days. Besides, the ruins were far from any city. Even if they found one, there were no nearby broadcasting stations and hardly any channels it could receive.

"Wait here. I'll have someone look for one!"

Foster immediately took out his phone, called a number, and issued hurried instructions.

They waited for more than half an hour. At last, they saw a researcher running toward them from the distance, panting as he carried a relatively modern radio.

"I... found one!"

Ethan accepted the radio with some apprehension. In truth, he did not know whether this method would work. But trying was better than giving up.

He carried it back into the ruins. Foster and several other researchers followed, curious to see what would happen.

Ignoring the audience, Ethan switched on the radio. With no station nearby, it naturally received no signal. Only harsh static crackled from the speaker.

Enduring the noise, he slowly turned the tuning dial from left to right.

Everyone but Ethan watched in breathless silence. Even Gengar floated curiously in the air, eager to see what Ethan intended to do.

10.5... 12.1... 12.8... 13...

After a long stretch of static, Ethan finally tuned the radio to 13.5. A different sound came through the speaker.

Crackle... beep-beep... crackle... ooo...

The sound was faint, broken, and buried beneath heavy interference, but it immediately caught everyone's attention.

Foster stared in shock. "Is that..."

"Shh!"

Ethan motioned for silence. Without changing the frequency, he raised the volume to its maximum and walked toward the nearest chamber.

At its entrance, the sound became slightly clearer. Ethan immediately changed direction, and the transmission grew clearer still.

By the time he reached one particular chamber among the many ruins, the sound came through perfectly, without a trace of static.

"Incredible!"

Foster's eyes widened. Listening carefully, he thought he could distinguish the melody of a flute mixed with the cry of a bird Pokémon.

The researchers became intensely excited. They took out paper and pens and hurried to record everything. Yet when they looked inside the chamber, they saw the same emptiness as before. Nothing seemed unusual.

"What do we do now?"

Ethan approached one of the stone walls and studied it.

"Gengar, pass through and see what's behind it!"

"Got it!"

Under everyone's gaze, Gengar passed through the wall. Barely a second later, it returned.

"There's another room behind the wall!"

"What!?"

Foster and the others were astonished. This was a major discovery. They had searched for hidden chambers before, but there were so many stone rooms that they had abandoned the effort after checking more than a dozen.

Who could have imagined that an ordinary chamber like this concealed another room behind its wall?

Ethan felt across the stones for a while but found no switch. He turned to Foster.

"Do you trust me?"

"Of course!" Foster said excitedly. "Without you, who knows how long it would have taken us to discover this secret? This is an enormous find. I can't imagine what new discoveries might be waiting behind that wall!"

"Then break it open."

"What? But..."

Foster hesitated. These were ancient ruins. Every stone was precious, and smashing through the wall would damage the site's integrity.

Yet without disturbing it, they had no way to enter the chamber beyond.

After only a moment's thought, Foster made his decision. To prevent unnecessary damage, he did not call upon any Pokémon. Instead, he ordered the workers to excavate carefully with hammers and chisels, removing the stones little by little.

Without machinery, the work would inevitably be long, painstaking, and time-consuming.

While the workers labored, Ethan and Sabrina returned to the research center and ate lunch in its cafeteria. By the time they came back, the team had opened a passage through the wall.
