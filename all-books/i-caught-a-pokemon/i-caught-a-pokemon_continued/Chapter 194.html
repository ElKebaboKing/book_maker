"Charmeleon, use Flamethrower!"

The Crystal Onix sent rocks toward it. Charmeleon dodged and opened its mouth, breathing a powerful stream of fire over the Onix.

A Fire-type move should have done only limited damage to a Rock-type Pokémon. Yet the Crystal Onix cried out in pain. Mateo stared. It was not an overwhelming hit, but unlike Cloyster's Water Gun, the flames had clearly affected it.

Why? The Onix could use Rock-type moves and seemed immune to water, yet fire did not meet the resistance Mateo expected.

When Flamethrower faded, the sight startled him further. Heat had turned the Crystal Onix's body the red of a flame.

Was it truly an embodiment of the god of glass, vulnerable to fire as glass was to the furnace?

He had no time to puzzle it out. The Onix lunged at Charmeleon.

"Dodge, then use Fire Spin!" Mateo called.

He was not a practiced battler, and his command came too late for an easy escape. Charmeleon reacted on its own, leaping clear of the powerful charge. Then it puffed out its cheeks and sent a spinning rush of flame toward the Onix.

The fire grew faster and broader until it became a twisting column that swallowed the Crystal Onix. It roared in pain.

When the flames dispersed, the Onix was still upright. Its crystal body glowed a brighter red than before. Sunlight caught its facets and reflected the fire within them, making it dazzling to behold.

Mateo went still. He stared at the Pokémon, struck by a sudden realization.

No one moved or spoke. Ethan and Sabrina did not want to interrupt him, and they too were caught by the rare sight.

At last Mateo came back to himself. The weariness vanished from his face. "That's it. That's the feeling I was missing. I think I've found what I came for!"

He turned to Ethan and bowed deeply. "Thank you, Mr. Ethan. I can't tell you how much you've helped me."

"You don't need to thank me. I hardly did anything, and you made Articuno's ornament for me. We're even."

Mateo shook his head. "What I've learned today will stay with me for the rest of my life. It may change everything I do. I can't compare it with the ornament I made."

Ethan saw he meant it and accepted the bow without further argument.

Mateo offered his hand. "Please excuse me. My head is full of ideas, and I want to get back to the shop and start working."

Ethan shook it firmly. "Go ahead. I hope you become the master glassmaker you want to be."

"Thank you. And I hope you become an outstanding Pokémon trainer."

They exchanged smiles. Mateo hurried away from the guesthouse, and Ethan waited until he was out of sight before turning to Sabrina.

"That's settled. We should get moving too."

Sabrina nodded and went to pack. When she returned to the courtyard, she looked back once at their rooms.

Nothing remarkable had happened during their days there, but she had been happy. The small things they had shared each day had made the time feel full. Whatever journeys lay ahead, she knew she would remember these days.

Under white clouds, Charizard flew swiftly across the sky with two travelers and their packs on its back. Sunlight struck its body, leaving a small, blurred shadow on the sea hundreds of meters below.

They had spent several days on Sunburst Island. To make up time, Ethan stopped exploring at random. He and Sabrina followed the map straight toward the next Gym island.

After hours of flight, land finally came into view at dusk. A great volcano rose through the forest and reached toward the clouds. It covered nearly three quarters of the island. The whole place looked as if it had been formed by an eruption.

"Is this Navel Island?" Sabrina asked with a knowing half smile. "I wonder whether that volcano is active. It looks dangerous."

Ethan knew she was thinking of Mt. Ember. He pretended not to understand. The more he tried to explain that eruption, the more guilty he might sound. He was not going to accept a reputation as a volcano killer.

The mountain looked larger as they drew near. Charizard landed on a beach, and they walked into the island's only village.

Compared with Mikan or Sunburst Island, few people lived here. Even the island's sole settlement had not grown into a proper town. Its houses stood apart, each with its own little yard and tiled roof. The quiet streets seemed empty. After a long walk, they finally saw a few older residents tending vegetable gardens.

Sabrina looked around doubtfully. "Did we come to the right place? Would the Orange League really have an official Gym somewhere this remote?"

"We're in the right place," Ethan said. "Many Gyms are built in busy cities and help bring people there. Some Leaders prefer to work somewhere quiet, whether because of their personalities or for other reasons. They don't want constant interruptions."
