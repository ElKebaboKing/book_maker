"You want to challenge my Gyarados?" Ethan was surprised. "Why that one in particular?"

"No special reason," Clair said, shaking her head. "I watched it fight in the Dragon Holy Land yesterday. Its opponents weren't strong enough to bring out everything it could do, but its scars and the way it fought left a deep impression on me. I want my Gyarados to face it and see which one is stronger."

A match between two of the same species... Neither Pokémon would have a type advantage. It would come down to their strength and their Trainers' decisions. In a fair contest, it might be Clair's only chance to recover the match.

But did she truly want this?

Ethan considered it. Among the Pokémon he currently owned, Gyarados was one of his strongest, behind only Gengar and the three Legendary birds. It had grown through battle after battle. Jolteon's type advantage made it a difficult opponent for Gyarados, but even Gardevoir and Charizard could not beat it.

Clair, you've chosen the wrong opponent.

Hoping to spare the Dragon-type Gym Leader some embarrassment, he asked once more. "I don't mind, but are you sure?"

Clair answered with a solemn nod.

"All right." Ethan shrugged. "If it's what you want, I have no reason to refuse."

He recalled Crystal Steelix and took out Gyarados's Poké Ball. They released their Pokémon together. Two flashes of blue light brought a pair of Gyarados onto the field.

Clair's was clean and unmarked. Ethan's body was covered in scars, giving it a far more fearsome appearance.

Roar!

As soon as it appeared, Ethan's Gyarados threw back its head. Its piercing cry echoed through Blackthorn Gym. Lance and Clair both frowned, taken aback by the sheer force of its presence. Having grown up among powerful Pokémon, they knew that a Gyarados's bearing often reflected its strength. This one was clearly exceptional.

Kabu was older and closer to the field. The roar hurt his ears enough that he covered them at once.

Clair's Gyarados stiffened under the other's display. It roared back, but its voice lacked the same force. In presence alone, it had already fallen well behind.

Kabu rubbed his ears. Seeing both sides ready, he signaled the start of the third round.

Clair began to give orders. Ethan, however, stood with his hands in his pockets. He said nothing and simply watched, as if the match had nothing to do with him.

Why isn't he commanding his Pokémon? Clair barely had time to wonder before Ethan's Gyarados charged on its own.

She focused on the field. "Gyarados, Dragon Rage!"

A blue-violet wave burst from her Gyarados's mouth. Ethan's ducked under it, closed the distance, and opened its jaws to bite.

Clair's Gyarados fought back. The two Pokémon bit and slammed into each other, wrestling from the ground into the pool and back onto the ground. Water sprayed across the field, and the force of their struggle made the Gym tremble.

Before long, Clair's Gyarados began to falter.

The one before it seemed unable to feel pain. Whatever blows Clair's Pokémon landed, Ethan's ignored them and answered more savagely, striking vulnerable spots with frightening precision. Scales fell away. Blood ran. In moments, Clair's Gyarados was covered in wounds.

"This..."

Clair and Kabu stared. Even Lance, watching from above, looked grave after his first shock. Ethan's Gyarados was extraordinarily strong.

It fought with raw instinct, as though engaged in a fight for survival. It scarcely seemed like the same creature Lance had watched the day before. He glanced at Ethan. The young Trainer remained calm; clearly, this was not the first time his Gyarados had fought this way.

But why was it not using moves? Was this its usual style?

Clair's Gyarados could barely fight back now. It struggled under one attack after another. Clair could not bear to watch it take any more punishment.

She clenched her teeth. "Stop. I concede..."

The match had been one-sided. Ethan's Gyarados had not knocked its opponent out in a single blow, but its easy dominance over another of its kind showed the difference between them. Blood from Clair's Gyarados was close to staining the pool red.

Only now did Clair understand why Ethan had seemed reluctant to use his Gyarados. It was too late to change her request.

Normally she would never concede in the middle of a round. This fight had forced her to set aside her pride as a Dragon Tamer and bow her head.

Kabu raised the green flag high. "The Gym challenge is over! Clair has conceded. Ethan Vale is the winner!"

Ethan recalled Gyarados at once. He feared that the sight of blood might drive it into a frenzy and make it attack Clair's Pokémon again.

Lance came down the stairs and joined them. "Well, Clair? Was that defeat convincing enough for you?"

His teasing earned only silence. For Clair, the loss had been complete. After the last two rounds, she thought she would have trouble watching Pokémon bite one another for some time. Both Altaria and Gyarados had fallen beneath an opponent's enormous jaws.

Lance turned to Ethan. "How did you raise that Gyarados? It's powerful, but more than that, its presence and ferocity are unlike any Gyarados I've seen."

Ethan had wondered the same thing. Had he somehow struck its head too hard when it evolved? Was there something loose in its mind that left it eager to grapple and bite instead of fighting at a distance?

"Maybe it kept more of its wild nature," he said with a smile after a moment. "Pokémon raised by Trainers often come to focus on the power of their moves. They don't like trading injury for injury, and they rely less on instinct."

There was something else Ethan did not say. To many Pokémon, a battle was a contest fought by rules. To his Gyarados, it was always a struggle for survival.

"I see." Lance's eyes lit up. "Well, you have an Elite Four member standing right here. Why not make the most of it and battle me? I'm getting impatient to try!"

"No, thanks." Ethan shook his head without hesitation. "I'm not planning on that yet. Let's wait until after the Silver Conference."
