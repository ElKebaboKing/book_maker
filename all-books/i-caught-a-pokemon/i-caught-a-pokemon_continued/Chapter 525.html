Colored streamers showered Ethan. He smiled and waved to the crowd around him.

Behind him, Misty crouched on her board, a little dejected. She stroked Corsola gently and glanced toward Ethan.

She and Corsola had given everything they had, only to lose to him by the narrowest margin. Of course she was disappointed.

Even so, she was happy for Ethan. Losing to him was something she could accept. If anyone else had beaten her so closely, she would already be pouting and complaining.

Farther behind them, Ross looked thoroughly glum. He had hoped to win for a second year in a row. Instead, he had lost to a younger, more handsome man, and even the girl beside that man had beaten him. Third place was a bitter fall from his expectations.

Speaking of that girl, she really was pretty.

An idea occurred to him. He turned to Misty. "You're visiting from elsewhere, aren't you? To celebrate your second-place finish, how about coming aboard my boat? I could show you around the city."

His enthusiasm made the offer difficult to refuse. Misty thought about it. They had come to an unfamiliar city and could use someone to show them its sights. Ross had volunteered at just the right moment.

"Sure," she said after a pause. "But my friends are coming too."

"Of course!" Ross agreed at once. "And your friends are...?"

Misty smiled brightly and pointed ahead, her gaze softening as it settled on Ethan.

"He's one of them!"

Ross's eyes narrowed. The two were together?

That man was fortunate. He had won the race and had such a pretty girl keeping him company.

The medal presentation, Ethan's remarks, and the photographs took about half an hour. When he found Erika and the others afterward, Ash had already rejoined them, though he had vanished before the finish line.

Ethan immediately noticed Ross beside the group.

Ross was talking animatedly, but apart from Ash, no one seemed especially interested.

Erika and Misty were too focused on Ethan to pay much attention to another man. Brock, for his part, cared more about pretty older women than listening to a man tell stories.

When Ethan returned, Ross approached before anyone else could speak and held out his hand with a broad smile.

"Congratulations on winning the race!"

He was clearly trying to present himself as gracious and good-humored.

Ethan knew Ross's name but had no idea why this stranger was with his friends. He shook Ross's hand, then looked toward Erika in confusion.

What's going on?

Erika shook her head. She did not know either.

Ross looked about twenty-five or twenty-six. Years in the sun had given him a slight tan. He wore his medium-length brown hair parted in the middle and had the easy air of someone who spent his days surfing by the sea.

Misty came over and explained. "Ethan, Mr. Ross has offered to show us around the city."

"I see. That's very kind of you," Ethan said.

"Oh, right!" Ash said excitedly. "Let us see the winner's medal!"

Everyone but Ross looked at what Ethan held. It was a round medallion of clear glass. Sunlight refracted into colors through it. The shapes of two Pokémon were faintly visible inside, along with a green gem set into the glass.

"So that's the Altomare glass winner's trophy?" Misty said. "It's beautiful!"

Ethan smiled and handed it to Erika. For sheer beauty, it surpassed the trophies he had won at the Indigo League, in the Orange League, and at the Whirl Cup.

Then he remembered Ash's disappearance. "Where did you go during the race? How did you end up off the course?"

Ash was baffled himself. He scratched his head and gave an embarrassed laugh. "I don't really know. Maybe Totodile got nervous and took the wrong way."

Latios and Latias had evidently not shown themselves to Ash. Perhaps their interference had been nothing more than a sudden whim.

Misty raised her chin, looking vindicated. "See? I said finishing anywhere but last would be a great result for Ash. Instead, he was the only racer who didn't finish at all!"

Ash blushed at the memory of his boast before the race and could not bring himself to meet her sharp look.

Ross could not get a word in. When their banter finally stopped, he said, "Why don't we talk on the boat?"

Ethan had pictured a speedboat. At the canal bank, however, Ross led them to a wooden boat—more of a narrow skiff. Here it had a proper name: a gondola.

Ross boarded first and took up the long oar at the stern. Ash and Brock climbed in. Ethan helped Erika and Misty aboard one after the other, then jumped in last.

The small gondola was immediately full.

Ross gave the oar a gentle stroke, and the boat drifted along the canal.

The scenery slipped past on either side. Seeing the city at leisure from the water had a different charm from walking its streets.

Brock sighed contentedly. "I've always dreamed of riding an Altomare gondola."

They circled much of the city before returning to the two stone pillars commemorating Latias and Latios, which they had seen when they first arrived.

"They're our guardians," Ross said when he saw them looking. "Legend says they saved Altomare from a disaster. Even now, some people believe they're still hidden somewhere in the city, watching over the City of Water."
