“Of course it isn't real,” Captain Stern said with a smile. “It's only a replica. It is somewhat larger than an ordinary Wailord, though. That's why it makes such an overwhelming impression.”

They left the main hall and entered the next gallery. Unlike the entrance area, which served mainly as a place for visitors to gather and pass through, this was a proper exhibition space.

Display cases stood in rows on either side. Inside were models of dozens of Pokémon that lived in the sea.

They weren't limited to Hoenn species. The collection included sea Pokémon from Johto, Kanto, Sinnoh, and Unova as well. Every model was so lifelike that it might have been a real Pokémon standing still.

“This is wonderful!” Misty exclaimed. No one in the group loved Water-type Pokémon more than she did. Seeing so many of them, even as models, delighted her more than winning a battle.

But the fact that they were models made her curious. “Captain Stern, why don't you keep real Pokémon here? Wouldn't that be better to look at?”

“This is a museum, not an aquarium,” Stern said. “I also study marine Pokémon. With models, I can examine their body structures more closely and do it safely.”

For a researcher like Stern, living Pokémon were unpredictable and difficult to control. Even so, could studying models alone truly teach him much?

They passed through the gallery and went deeper into the museum. In Exhibition Hall No. 2, they found an orange submarine model.

Before anyone could ask about it, Stern took on the role of guide. “This is my multipurpose submarine. I designed it myself and built it at my shipyard. With it, I can go down to the seafloor and observe wild Pokémon firsthand!”

His tone held a touch of pride. He was clearly pleased with his design.

Max had been excited the entire way. After praising the submarine, he turned to the others. “It's such a shame May and Brock didn't come! They're missing so much!”

Misty gave him a look. “May has something more important to do, and Brock specializes in Rock- and Ground-type Pokémon. He might not be interested in a marine museum. You shouldn't assume everyone sees things the way you do.”

Max fell silent as soon as Misty spoke. Perhaps it was the punch she'd thrown at Drew, or perhaps it was her occasional fierce manner. Either way, he was a little afraid of this pretty older girl. He wouldn't argue with her as he did with his sister; he barely dared meet her eyes.

As they talked, they reached the room where Stern had first heard them outside the window. Only now did they see it was his office.

Stern went to his desk, picked up an elegant small leather case, and set it before them. “This is the reason I closed the museum today. I needed to study it.”

He opened the case. Everyone leaned in, only to find that it contained a single black oval stone.

It was no larger than a palm and looked thoroughly charred. Apart from that, there seemed to be nothing special about it.

“What's that?” Ash asked. “Some kind of rare gem?”

“No.” Stern shook his head. “It's an unidentified stone discovered by chance on the seafloor. It may not look like much, but it isn't ordinary. Those scorched marks come from heat inside the stone. Even in the deepest water, it continues to give off heat.”

Intrigued, Ethan and the others reached toward it. They felt a fierce warmth before their hands even came close. In the short time the case had been open, the room itself seemed to grow a few degrees hotter.

Ethan looked more closely at the case. It had a thick insulating lining. No wonder they hadn't felt anything while it was closed.

Stern turned on a nearby computer and showed them a scan of the stone. At its center, the image revealed an unidentified source steadily radiating heat.

“That doesn't make scientific sense...” Max pushed up his glasses and leaned toward the screen. “Why would a stone give off heat? It should be inert...”

“I've considered many possibilities,” Stern said. “I still don't know how it formed, but I don't believe it formed naturally. I think a Pokémon's power created it.”

“A Pokémon made it?” Ash's eyes widened. An ordinary Pokémon couldn't create something that defied common sense like this. Only a Legendary seemed capable of it.

But Ash didn't know much about Hoenn yet. He couldn't even identify all its ordinary Pokémon, let alone its Legendaries.

Fortunately, Stern didn't make them guess. “Do you know Groudon?”

Groudon? Ash was about to ask what that was, but Ethan looked perfectly calm. Ash caught himself and nodded. “Of course. It's a Legendary Pokémon, right?”

Before Stern could answer, Max eagerly displayed what he knew. “Groudon is one of the ancient Pokémon! They say it's the embodiment of the land, the Pokémon that created land and expanded the continents!”

“You know a great deal,” Stern said approvingly. “He's right. And I believe Groudon's power may have formed this stone.”

Ethan rubbed his chin. He hadn't expected an ordinary museum visit to lead back to Groudon.

And Groudon inevitably brought Team Magma to mind. Stern's discovery might not stay secret from them. Judging by their usual methods, they could come to steal it at any moment.

It seemed Ethan and his companions might soon cross paths with Team Magma again.
