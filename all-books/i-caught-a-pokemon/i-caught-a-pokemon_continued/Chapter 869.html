With all three Legendary beasts in his care, Ethan's team had become far stronger. It was a surprise he could never have expected before coming back to Johto.

He was still looking at Entei's Great Ball when May hurried over, picked it up, and carried it to him as carefully as if it were a precious treasure.

Her caution was understandable. While traveling with Ash, she had glimpsed Legendary Pokémon a few times, but usually from a distance. Even when she met one, she had approached it warily. This was different. Ethan had caught Entei by his own strength, and now she was holding its Poké Ball in her hands.

Ethan smiled at her expression and remembered himself when he first caught Zapdos. He had probably looked much the same.

Beside them, a flash of light swept over Charizard. When it faded, Charizard had returned to its usual form. May watched in fascination.

“I thought it would stay like that,” she said, a little disappointed. “Does it have to evolve all over again for every battle?”

“Yes.” Ethan called his other Pokémon back from their positions around the valley. “Mega Evolution isn't permanent. It makes a Pokémon stronger by temporarily bringing out hidden power, but it needs both a Key Stone and a Mega Stone. It can't maintain that form indefinitely. Knowing when to use it and when to let it end matters.”

His Pokémon gathered around him and looked at the Great Ball in his hand. Gengar seemed about to say something, but it and Gardevoir turned toward the same part of the valley.

“Human footsteps,” Gengar said quietly. “Coming this way fast.”

That was hardly surprising. People traveled through all kinds of places, especially Trainers who liked exploring mountains and caves. Ethan had expected the chance of an interruption from the moment he decided to catch Entei. One reason for stationing Pokémon around the fight had been to keep strangers from interfering before the capture was over.

At least whoever this was had waited until Entei was safely caught.

Ethan recalled several of his Pokémon, put Entei's Great Ball in his pouch, and was about to leave with May on Charizard when two voices carried toward them.

“Hurry! The battle sounds came from this direction. Those had to be two rare Pokémon!”

“But it's quiet now. What if they've already gone?”

“Then we'd better hurry even more!”

Ethan paused. One of the voices sounded familiar. Had he met its owner before? He slowed his steps and waited.

The answer soon came around a great rock in the valley: a teenage boy and girl.

The girl was pretty, with pale blue hair tied into two long pigtails that curled upward at improbable angles. Her fitted top and shorts suited her bright, energetic bearing.

The boy wore a cap backward, a red sweatshirt, and yellow shorts. A lock of hair stuck defiantly through a gap in the cap.

They all recognized one another at once.

“You two?” Ethan said.

“Mr. Vale?” the pair exclaimed.

His memory caught up. “Jimmy and Marina?”

They were the two Trainers Ethan had met while traveling through Johto. They had shared an adventure and met again at the Silver Conference. They were hardly strangers.

“It's us!” Jimmy said excitedly. “It's been ages. I never thought we'd run into you here!”

“Neither did I,” Marina said. “Mr. Vale, what brings you here?”

Ethan shook his head. “I was about to ask you the same thing. Why are you two in this valley?”

“The weather changed, and we heard a battle,” Jimmy said. “Of course we came to see what was happening.”

He glanced around at the battered ground. “Wait. Were you the one fighting here?”

“I was.” Ethan gave an answer that was true, if incomplete. “I found a fairly rare Pokémon and caught it.”

Entei certainly qualified as fairly rare.

“Oh…” Jimmy's excitement visibly faded. He had clearly come hoping to find the battlers, only to discover that someone else had reached them first.

“Where's your other friend?” Ethan asked. “The one from your hometown. His name was…?”

“Vincent?” Marina laughed softly. “He's traveling on his own somewhere else. You've met him plenty of times, Mr. Vale. How can you still forget his name?”

It was strange. Ethan had met countless people on his journey, old friends and new faces alike. Yet the name of that round-faced boy never seemed to stay in his memory.

Jimmy and Marina turned their attention to May. “And who is this?”

“I'm May,” she said with an easy smile. “I'm traveling with Ethan, and I'm a Coordinator.”

“A Coordinator?” Jimmy instinctively looked at Marina.

“What a coincidence,” Marina said, smiling. “I switched paths recently. I'm a Coordinator now too!”

“Really?” May brightened. “That is a coincidence.”

Since becoming a Coordinator, May had met many others, from friendly rivals to people as spiteful as Harley. The more she spoke with fellow Coordinators, the more she learned. Meeting a friend of Ethan's made the conversation easier still.

With midday approaching, the four found a clean, open place nearby and sat on the rocks. Ethan and Jimmy prepared lunch while May and Marina talked about Contests.

May soon discovered that although Marina had entered the field only recently, she regularly placed first in the preliminary judging and had already won several Ribbons. She had striking insights into the stage and the Coordinator's craft as well.

It was astonishing. Marina looked about May's age, yet she already knew so much and performed so well.

Ethan noticed May's admiring look and smiled to himself as he worked on lunch. She could not know that Marina would one day win a Grand Festival and become one of Johto's leading Coordinators. Someone destined for that achievement naturally had her own way of seeing the art.

Once the food was ready, they spread out a picnic cloth and ate a hot, practical meal in the valley. It was not particularly tasty, but Ethan had done what he could. In the kitchen, his greatest success was cooking something thoroughly enough that no one would go hungry.

Marina raised her eyebrows a little at the first bite. Jimmy and May, on the other hand, ate with evident enjoyment.

Running into the pair had been a pleasant interruption. After lunch and a little more conversation, May and Marina exchanged contact details. Then the two groups said goodbye and continued toward their separate destinations.

Neither Ethan nor May mentioned meeting and catching Entei. They shared only a few stories from the road.

Once Jimmy and Marina had gone, Ethan and May climbed onto Charizard again and flew toward the Whirl Islands. They would have preferred to walk and explore, but the time before the Grand Festival was slipping away. Another unexpected delay might come at any point, so they needed to leave room for it.

Fortunately, the rest of the flight passed without incident. After many hours in the air, they sighted the Whirl Islands in the distance as night fell.

Ethan felt a trace of uncertainty, as he had when returning to Kanto. He had been away a long time and had not contacted Maya. What if her feelings toward him had changed? Would this trip have been for nothing?

Then he dismissed the thought. If he lacked even this much confidence in himself, that would be a poor showing. He held League titles from three regions and traveled with so many Legendary Pokémon. Surely he could face one conversation.

He gently pressed his feet against Charizard's sides. It accelerated toward Blue Point Isle, where Maya lived.

…

Night covered the sea. Thin clouds veiled the moon and softened its light.

At the Temple of the Sea on Blue Point Isle, Maya had finished her duties for the day. The beautiful, graceful Sea Priestess stood on the broad terrace outside her room, gazing west across the water.

Far beyond her sight, people said, lay the Hoenn mainland.

For some time she had come here every night before bed to look in that direction. She had never explained why, but her attendants knew what occupied her thoughts. Her Sea Hero was somewhere across the sea.

A cool wind swept over the terrace, and Maya drew her long dress closer around herself.

She had stood there long enough. As she turned toward her room, the clouds began to drift away from the moon. A black speck appeared against its light.

Maya stopped and looked up. Was it a bird Pokémon?

The speck grew larger as it approached. It was no bird. A dragonlike Pokémon was flying toward the temple, with someone apparently riding on its back. It seemed to be coming straight for her.

Her heartbeat quickened. She suspected who it might be, but the dim light kept her from seeing clearly.

A minute or two later, the approaching Pokémon entered the glow from her room. It was a Charizard. And the figure riding it was her Sea Hero.

Had the God of the Sea finally answered her prayers?

Maya's heart settled. She resumed the composed bearing of the priestess everyone knew.

Charizard's wings stirred the air as it landed on the terrace.

From its back, Ethan looked down at the beautiful woman before him. Without her scepter, some of the distance of her sacred office seemed to fall away. She looked simply like a woman, as she had that night.

He found the answer he hoped for in her eyes.

Ethan swung down onto the terrace and smiled. “It's been a long time, Sea Priestess.”

Maya smiled back. She was about to speak when another figure—a young girl—climbed down from Charizard's back.
