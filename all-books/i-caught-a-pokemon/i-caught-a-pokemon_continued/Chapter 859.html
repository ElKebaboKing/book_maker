“Apart from breaking the meteorite, I don't think the three of us have a better solution.” Ethan smiled at May's concern for Deoxys. “The stone has no use to it now. It can't ride it back into space, and staying with it is hurting it. Given the choice, Deoxys would probably destroy the meteorite itself.”

He had his Deoxys ask the wild one. It agreed. The only reason it had not destroyed the stone already was that the geomagnetic anomaly weakened its powers. Trapped inside the meteorite, it could not break free without help from outside.

With that settled, Ethan led May and Solana back to a safer distance and signaled to his Deoxys. It rose high into the air and began gathering energy.

The wild Deoxys returned inside the meteorite. It changed to Defense Forme and used the power it could still summon to raise a barrier before itself, ready to withstand the blast.

Ethan's Deoxys was ready too. An energy sphere swelled before it until it was nearly two meters across. It pushed the enormous ball toward the meteorite.

The sphere struck almost at once. A blinding white flash filled Ethan's vision. Before he even felt the force of the explosion, an immense boom sounded in his ears. The shock wave followed.

They were more than fifty meters away, but Ethan had underestimated the blast—or, more precisely, what happened when the meteorite exploded.

Its unusual material burst into a spray of brilliant colors. Fragments flew in every direction, tearing up the surrounding ground. Gardevoir reacted in time, wrapping Ethan, May, and Solana in psychic power to protect them from the shards.

The shock wave still swept over them. Wind whipped their hair and clothes, made it hard to open their eyes, and nearly knocked them off their feet. Fortunately, no one was hurt.

When the blast passed, they looked ahead. The meteorite was gone. In its place lay an enormous crater.

The wild Deoxys stood inside it in Defense Forme, beginning to stretch. Its barrier had broken at some point, and its body bore scrapes, but none seemed life-threatening. The wounds were already healing. In the short time they watched, most had closed.

Ethan kicked a meteorite fragment away from his feet and nodded. “That worked.”

It certainly did. Solana surveyed the area. The meteorite was gone, but so was the landscape as it had been. The crater looked like a chunk scooped out of level ground. Still, freeing Deoxys was worth it.

Remembering the original problem, she hurriedly took out her geomagnetic detector. She checked the readings and broke into a delighted smile.

“The anomaly's gone!”

Ethan checked his phone. Its signal had returned. May took out a Poké Ball and released Eevee.

“Everything works again. That's wonderful!”

The rescued Deoxys approached Ethan's partner. Multicolored ripples spread from both of their chests, each pattern shining into the other's body.

“What are they doing?” May asked.

“It's how they communicate,” Ethan said, still watching them. “The wild one is probably thanking mine.”

After a short exchange, the wild Deoxys shot into the sky and vanished. Its departure was sudden and decisive.

“Huh? It just left?”

“Why wouldn't it?” Ethan shook his head. “It said goodbye and thanked my Deoxys. Now it wants to get away from the place that held it captive.”

After all they had done, the incident seemed to end almost abruptly. In truth, that was what they had wanted. Ethan already had a Deoxys and did not need to catch another.

The effort had not been wasted. They had freed a trapped Mythical Pokémon, resolved the geomagnetic anomaly, and helped the Rangers. Most of all, Ethan's Deoxys had met another of its kind. Ethan had promised to help it find more companions, and this encounter honored that promise.

Solana thanked Ethan and May for their help. The three also exchanged contact information.

She wanted to accompany them back to the Pokémon Center, but Ethan declined. “Let's part here. This took quite a while, and we need to continue our journey.”

They had both come away with a better opinion of the other. Solana admired Ethan more than before, and he had begun to appreciate her in turn.

“Where are you going next?” she asked.

After their earlier disagreement, she would never have asked so easily. Now it felt natural. May briefly explained their plans.

“The Battle Pyramid and the Grand Festival?” Solana spent much of each year in the wild, but she was not ignorant of major competitions. She knew both Ethan and May had reached decisive points in their journeys. “Then I wish you both success. Where will you go once those are over?”

May turned to Ethan. “If nothing changes,” he said, “we'll probably travel through Sinnoh next.”

“Sinnoh?” Solana was surprised, then smiled. “What a coincidence. I'm being assigned there next. It looks like we'll have a chance to meet again.”

“Really?” Ethan smiled too. “Then perhaps we'll see each other there.”

…

Two days of travel later, Ethan and May finally reached Pewter City. For some reason, the Battle Pyramid had stayed near the city throughout those two days instead of moving again. Ethan did not know why it had stopped, but he was glad. They would not have to waste more time and effort chasing it elsewhere.

The map showed it somewhere outside the city. After such a long journey, though, they could afford a short rest before the challenge. The Pyramid did not seem about to fly away. They headed for Pewter's Pokémon Center first.

Ethan had been here before. It had been a long time, and he had forgotten some of the streets, but as he walked, old memories returned.

He guided May through an alley and onto the street where the Pewter Gym stood. He pointed ahead.

“Brock's Gym and home are just up there. I remember the front looking like a little house built beneath a rock—almost one huge boulder. It was distinctive. When you went around it, though, the rock was only decoration. The building looked more like a warehouse. See, it's just like—”

He stopped. The entrance that should have looked like stone was now covered in bright colors, as if someone had painted all over it. Flags, streamers, and strings of lights hung from the front. It was spectacularly gaudy.

Was this really the Pewter Gym he remembered? May was astonished too.

“This is Brock's Gym? It's so colorful. It doesn't match how I picture him at all.”

Other Gyms had changed over time as well, including the Celadon and Cerulean Gyms. That in itself was not surprising. Ethan was taken aback by how completely the Pewter Gym's style had changed since Brock led it.

“His parents must have wanted it this way. It doesn't suit Brock's serious face, but it does fit the passion underneath,” Ethan said.

May covered her mouth as she laughed. “Now I want to know what Brock would think if he saw his home like this.”

They stopped to look a little longer, then continued down the street. It was someone else's family matter, and Brock was not here to explain. They could ask him when they next met.

Pewter was a small northern city, far from Kanto's bustling center, but its streets were lively enough. Shops lined both sides of the road. Pedestrians, vehicles, and Trainers traveling with their Pokémon moved among them.

Ethan and May walked side by side, talking and laughing. As they passed a woman with dark purple bobbed hair and glasses, Ethan suddenly felt that something was wrong.

He frowned, stopped, and looked back. The woman turned at almost the same moment, their gazes crossing over the crowd.

May noticed his reaction and looked too. The woman appeared to be in her twenties and otherwise unremarkable. She wore a dark red trench coat and oval black-framed glasses, giving her the look of an office worker.

“What's wrong? Do you know her?”

Ethan did not answer immediately. He studied the woman. He was certain he had never met her, yet she felt strangely familiar. Who could she be?

While he tried to place her, she turned away without showing any alarm and went on as though nothing had happened. Even after she disappeared, he could not think of where he might have seen her. He let it go.

“I can't remember. It probably isn't important. Let's keep going.”

Once they had left, the woman peered out from a shadowed alley nearby. Seeing that Ethan was truly gone, she let out a long breath.

A large man in ordinary clothes, with an eye patch, came up beside her. He frowned at her expression.

“Miss Matori, what's wrong?”

The bob-haired woman was Matori, Giovanni's secretary—the “melon-rind glasses” Jessie liked to call her. The man beside her was Gozu, captain of the Matori Matrix, Team Rocket's special unit under her command.

Matori's low, measured voice suited her composed appearance. “I just saw that man.”

“That man?” Gozu's face changed. Within Team Rocket, only one person needed no name when spoken of that way: Ethan Vale, the threat that weighed on all of them. “Did he recognize you?”

“No.” Matori shook her head. “But even though we'd never met, he seemed to sense something about me.”

“How could he? You've never appeared before him. Even the International Police's files contain nothing about you. What could he have noticed?”

“I don't know. That's what bothers me.” She sighed. “Fortunately he only suspected something. If he'd known who I was, we'd be in serious trouble.”

Gozu had faced Ethan before. The encounter had left a deep impression, and he knew how difficult the man was to deal with. Since then, Ethan had caught even more powerful Pokémon. Facing him again, Gozu felt helpless.

It was not that Team Rocket had failed to prepare. Ethan had simply grown too strong. Even the whole organization could no longer do much against him. Gozu hated admitting it, but he knew the gulf between them.

“Why is he here?” he asked. “Could he have discovered we're in the city?”

“Impossible. No information about our presence has leaked.” Matori shook her head, then added cautiously, “Still, we should report this to Boss Giovanni and let him decide what to do.”
