At dusk, Ethan's group stood outside the Battle Arena. Greta had offered to host them for dinner, but Ethan, Misty, May, and Bea politely declined. With Scott accompanying them, they said goodbye.

Greta wished Ethan could stay longer. Every extra day might bring another chance to spar with him and improve. But he had achieved what he came for, and he could not spend more time here. For one thing, he was eager to reach Saffron City. For another…

“I'd better not,” Ethan said. He looked past Greta at her students and smiled. “If I stay much longer, my hand might go numb.”

After the match, he had tried to leave, only to be surrounded by students asking for photographs and autographs. It had taken him a long time to satisfy them. Who knew what they might ask for next if he stayed? The joke made them smile sheepishly.

Finally Greta and all her students bowed together in the manner of karate practitioners. “Have a safe journey!” they called.

Ethan waved. The group climbed into Scott's red sports car and drove away from the Battle Arena.

…

The car stopped smoothly in the open space outside the same Pokémon Center. Ethan and the others got out and waved to Scott.

“This is far enough,” Ethan said. “We can handle the rest of the way ourselves. Thank you for everything.”

“I should be thanking you,” Scott said with a smile. “Strong challengers like you push the Frontier Brains to improve. That makes my Battle Frontier stronger!”

He paused, then asked, “Which facility will you challenge next, Mr. Ethan?”

“I haven't settled on one, but probably the Battle Factory near Mt. Moon. Why?”

Scott's eyes lit up. “What a coincidence! I'm heading that way too. It seems I'll get to see you battle again.”

Scott was more of a battle enthusiast than Ethan had realized. Then again, without that passion, he would never have built a Battle Frontier this large.

“I'll see you there, then.”

“Until next time!”

They watched Scott's car disappear, then went into the Pokémon Center.

Ethan wanted to reach Saffron City before dark. But Misty and May were traveling with him, and he had to consider their stamina and comfort. They would spend the night here and leave in the morning.

After checking in, they put away their luggage. About fifteen minutes later, all four met in the dining room and ordered dinner. Ethan and Misty turned their attention to Bea.

“Where are you going next?” Misty asked her.

By now everyone understood that Bea sought out Fighting-type Gyms and renowned Fighting-type Trainers. She challenged one, then traveled on to the next. She glanced at Ethan before answering.

“I've heard Bruno of Kanto's Elite Four is near Mt. Silver. I plan to challenge him.”

“Bruno?” Misty exclaimed. “You're challenging a member of the Elite Four?”

Ethan looked at Bea in surprise. May listened closely too. She did not know Kanto's Elite Four well, but surely they were about as strong as Hoenn's. For this beautiful older girl to challenge one took courage. Then May remembered Bea's battle at the Arena and that she was an official Gym Leader. Perhaps it was not so unreasonable.

“Have you thought it through?” Ethan asked. “I don't know Bruno well, and I've never met him, but I know he's strong. Make sure you're prepared.”

He was not trying to discourage Bea. Bruno was formidable even among the Elite Four, and some called him a human Pokémon. Anyone who rushed into a challenge against him without enough preparation or strength could be soundly defeated.

Bea nodded. “I'll give it everything I have.”

Her decision was made. Ethan and Misty told her what little they knew about Bruno, hoping it would help her understand the opponent she had yet to meet. Most of their information was secondhand. Ethan did remember which Pokémon were considered Bruno's strongest, though, and offered a few warnings and thoughts on them.

Bea watched him help her for a long moment before quietly looking away.

Chansey arrived pushing a cart with their dinner. Once the plates were on the table, Ethan noticed how many desserts Bea and May had ordered. May loved all good food. Bea had a particular weakness for sweets. In this, at least, the two were alike.

…

They rested at the Pokémon Center overnight. Early the next morning, Ethan, Misty, and May said goodbye to Bea.

Ethan offered her his hand. “You're heading for Mt. Silver, and much of that area is in Johto. We really don't know when we'll meet again. Good luck with your challenge.”

“We'll be rooting for you,” Misty said with a smile. “Tell us how it goes!”

Bea looked at Ethan's hand, then took it and nodded. “Thank you.”

She swung her shoulder bag into place and set out west with determined steps, leaving them a striking view of her back.

Misty watched her solitary figure grow distant and sighed.

“What's wrong?” Ethan asked.

“Bea's amazing. She travels and trains all over the world by herself. I couldn't do that.”

“It's part of her training.” Ethan watched Bea go. “But even birds return to their nests. Her home is in Galar, and she's a Gym Leader there, so I'm sure she goes back often. Maybe traveling between her Gym duties helps her relax too.”

After parting from Bea, Ethan could finally set off for Saffron City. He grew quiet on the road. Misty and May's voices filled the air, but he rarely joined them. As the city drew nearer, his pace quickened. He could hardly wait to get home.

The distance was short enough that they reached Saffron City's outskirts by noon.

At the Saffron Gym, a challenge was about to begin. The challenger was a novice Trainer from Cerulean City, traveling to collect badges. His opponent was to be Sabrina, the beautiful Psychic-type Gym Leader.

The young Trainer was already waiting on the battlefield. Sabrina was walking there through a long corridor with her father when she abruptly stopped.

“Hm?” Her father looked at her. “What is it?”

Sabrina turned toward the south. He followed her gaze, but there was only a wall. Before he could ask again, she turned without a word and headed for the exit.

“Sabrina! Where are you going? Your challenger is waiting!”

She did not look back or explain. Her father could neither stop her nor call her back. He sighed, took out his own Poké Balls, and went to the battlefield alone.

What could have made Sabrina abandon a Gym match so suddenly? It was unlike her. And yet he had a vague feeling something similar had happened before.

Ethan arrived at the Saffron Gym alone. He looked up at the Pokémon League sign above it, feeling excited, uncertain, and a little nervous.

How was Sabrina? Had her feelings for him changed? Would she greet him as she had before?

He raised his head. The Gym doors opened, and Sabrina stood at the top of the steps, smiling down at him. The moment their eyes met, Ethan's mind went still. For that instant, neither saw anyone else.

He had only begun to lift a hand when Sabrina vanished from the steps. His vision blurred. Before he understood what had happened, her warm, fragrant body was in his arms. Her long black hair swept against his face.

Ethan held her tightly, and she held him just as tightly.

“Sabrina. I'm here…” he whispered.

She kissed him before he could say more. He answered her eagerness with his own. A long while passed before they drew apart.

Sabrina leaned back, and Ethan finally had a chance to look at her. Her black hair still fell softly to her shoulders. Her eyes, her expression, and her familiar bearing had not changed. She was still Sabrina.

As the first rush of emotion ebbed, Ethan remembered where they were. The Gym stood beside a street. Pedestrians passed by, and Trainers came to challenge it. Before anyone noticed them, he patted Sabrina's back.

“Let's go inside. If we stay out here, we'll end up in today's headlines.”

Sabrina did not care whether anyone saw them, but she had no objection when Ethan asked. She led him inside. At his insistent urging, they went straight to her room.

His longing for her was nearly overflowing. He needed another way to express it. Sabrina was his lover, and with her he held nothing back.

…

Meanwhile, in the Saffron Gym's battle hall, Sabrina's father finally defeated the novice challenger after three difficult rounds. He gave the disappointed young Trainer a few encouraging words and watched him leave. Then he shook his head and returned to the Gym's inner rooms.

He had not battled in some time and felt rusty, but he had managed to defend the badge. Young Trainers were getting stronger. This one had nearly beaten him.

He looked at his watch and frowned. Where had Sabrina gone? Why was she still not back?

Growing concerned, he went to the front entrance and looked outside. He saw no sign of her. A few students who practiced psychic abilities at the Gym happened to come in, and he stopped them.

“Have you seen Sabrina?”

“Miss Sabrina isn't in the Gym?” They shook their heads. “Sorry, we didn't see her on our way back. Could she have returned already?”

“No. If she had, she'd have come to the battlefield. Something must have delayed her.” He sighed. “Well, if the Gym Leader isn't here, we can't accept any more challenges today. Put up the closed sign. The Gym will take the rest of the day off.”

After giving the instructions, he decided not to worry further and left. Unlike Sabrina, he did not live at the Gym. He had a home nearby. Though their relationship as father and daughter had improved lately, they had long lived apart.

With Sabrina missing and these new Trainers becoming harder for him to handle, a day off sounded welcome. Normal business could resume when she returned. Hands behind his back, he went down the steps toward the street.

He had taken only a few steps when he faintly heard a woman's breath coming in short gasps. The sound seemed to come from a window on the side of Sabrina's bedroom. He almost laughed at himself.

Listen to that. It had been so long since he last battled that he was hearing things. Sabrina had gone out; how could there be a woman's voice in her room? Especially a voice like that?

He shook his head, thought no more of it, and walked away humming as he glanced up at the bright sky.
