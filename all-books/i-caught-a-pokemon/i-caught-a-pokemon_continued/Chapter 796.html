Hariyama's Arm Thrust missed again. Its Force Palm had not touched Blaziken either. Through all their exchanges, it had landed only one Vital Throw, hurling Blaziken hard onto the floor. Blaziken had immediately got up and escaped.

Greta could only sigh to herself. This was difficult—far too difficult. With Blaziken moving so fast, how was Hariyama supposed to catch it?

That did not necessarily mean Blaziken was stronger. Its speed simply left Hariyama's great power with nowhere to go. Against a strength-based opponent like Bea's Pangoro, Hariyama would at least have had a chance to answer each attack.

There was one hopeful thought. Moving at this pace would drain Blaziken's stamina quickly. If Hariyama could endure until it had to slow down, it could finally strike back.

But if Greta could see that, so could Ethan. He had no intention of letting the battle last that long. Blaziken might be a Fighting type, but Ethan had never planned to defeat a pure Fighting type by relying on Fighting moves alone. Fire remained Blaziken's best weapon.

Blaziken evaded another attack and slipped behind Hariyama. It clenched both claws. Flames sprang up around its fists, and it swung them hard.

Fire Punch!

An orange blur swept past. Both fists struck Hariyama in quick succession. With a roar, flames burst from the blows and spread across its back. The pain made Hariyama stagger again.

When the visible flames died away, Hariyama was still suffering from a burn. That was the worst possible news for Greta's plan to prolong the fight. Blaziken could afford to wait. Hariyama could not. Every movement and every punch now brought fresh pain from the burn.

Ethan saw that it was time to finish the round. At his command, fire engulfed Blaziken's body. It used Flare Blitz, charging like a blazing ball toward Hariyama.

For the first time, Blaziken attacked straight from the front. Greta would have preferred it to stay as far away as possible. She wanted Hariyama to dodge, but it could not match Blaziken's speed. The burn tormented it as well. It managed two steps before pain stopped it.

Flare Blitz drove Blaziken to its highest speed yet. It slammed into Hariyama's chest. Blaziken took damage from the collision too, but the attack brought Hariyama down. Its huge body fell backward and struck the floor with a thunderous crash.

Hariyama had spent the round almost helpless. Their different fighting styles had left it absorbing blows while landing very few of its own.

Ethan would have liked to beat Hariyama in a direct contest of strength, but he had no Pokémon that was solely a Fighting type and suited to that kind of struggle. He had chosen a fast, evasive approach instead. Whatever the method, a win was a win.

“I can't believe it…” A student rubbed his eyes. “Master Greta's Hariyama was beaten just like that?”

The Arena's students knew how strong it was. Hariyama had defeated countless challengers. Even Bea's Pangoro had needed a long fight to overcome it and had taken considerable damage. Watching this round, the students had felt only helplessness. Blaziken had given it no opening at all.

So this was what a championship-winning Trainer could do? Bea had fought in a completely different style. Still, Blaziken's performance showed its own strength. Another Trainer's Pokémon might have tried the same tactics and been knocked out before succeeding.

Greta shook her head without making excuses and recalled Hariyama. Ethan called Blaziken back as well. This was a two-on-two elimination match, and both teams had already been revealed. He was not going to make Blaziken fight both rounds. Scizor deserved a turn.

With a wave from Ethan, Scizor buzzed its wings and sprang into the center of the field before Greta could send out her second Pokémon. His eagerness suited her. She did not get an opportunity to face an opponent like Ethan every day.

She no longer felt like someone merely defending the Arena from a challenger. She wanted to learn from fighting him. The Blaziken round had exposed weaknesses in her Hariyama. What might this Scizor teach her?

At Greta's signal, Medicham entered the battle area.

The matchup was unfavorable for it. Medicham's Fighting and Psychic types faced Scizor's Bug and Steel types. Still, Scizor's advantage did not look overwhelming at first glance. No one doubted its ability, though. It had traveled with Ethan since Johto and performed well at the Conferences.

The students fell silent and watched intently. The older student refereeing signaled for the second round to begin.

This time Medicham attacked first. Its Fighting and Psychic moves might not do much damage to Scizor, but Greta was not about to stand still. Type matchups were not absolute. Battles had been turned around countless times through composure, experience, and quick decisions.

At Greta's direction, Medicham charged and prepared a Focus Punch. She clearly wanted to test Scizor at close range. Fighting-type Trainers could be stubborn about their specialty; unless forced otherwise, they preferred not to rely on attacks of other types.

White light wrapped Medicham's fist. It ran up to Scizor, leaped high, and drove the punch downward.

“Scizor, block it!”

Their speeds were comparable. Ethan did not try to evade. Unlike Hariyama, Medicham was not built around raw power, and Scizor had excellent defense and a favorable type matchup. There was little reason for another evasive battle.

Scizor used Metal Claw, slashing upward to meet the descending fist.

Greta was a Frontier Brain for a reason. Even after her battle with Bea, she still had a trick she had not shown. Just before the two attacks met, Medicham's figure flickered and vanished from Scizor's sight. It reappeared behind Scizor and struck from the rear.

Focus Punch landed with a heavy metallic thud, making Scizor stumble. It was a startling blow. Scizor stood about six feet tall, weighed well over two hundred pounds, and had a steel-hard body. To make it lose its footing, Medicham's punch had to carry exceptional force. It had shown nothing like this against Bea.

Even so, the unfavorable type matchup blunted the result. The surprise blow had landed, but it had done less than Greta hoped. Ethan had also seen the resilience of Frontier Brains and Gym Leaders again. At their level, anyone could produce a brilliant move when given the chance.

He was surprised, but not worried. The same trick would not catch him twice.

Greta tried it again, ordering another Focus Punch. Medicham leaped toward Scizor just as before. When it vanished and reappeared, a bright sheen flashed across Scizor's body.

Iron Defense.

The second Focus Punch hit with a deeper clang, as though Medicham had struck a thick iron door. Scizor stood perfectly still and took the blow outright. Medicham pulled its fist back in pain.

Greta winced as she watched it clutch its hand. Between Scizor's natural defense, Iron Defense, and the resistance from its typing, its body had become dauntingly hard.

Then Scizor counterattacked. Its huge pincers snapped forward in a series of punches as fast as bullets. Medicham rallied, dodging and blocking, but its flexible body could avoid only so many. One lapse was enough. Bullet Punch struck again and again, more than a dozen blows in the blink of an eye, leaving Medicham reeling.

Anyone who knew Ethan's battles recognized the danger. Once he found an opening, the outcome was usually settled. His eye for weaknesses was formidable. Without a decisive move to reverse the situation, an opponent could only endure the finish.

Scizor followed Bullet Punch with Metal Claw, then drove Medicham to the floor with Iron Head. Medicham tried one last counterattack, but Scizor evaded it with Double Team. Its chance to recover was gone.

Another Metal Claw landed hard and knocked Medicham unconscious. The challenge was over.

“Medicham is unable to battle! Scizor wins!” the referee announced.

Misty and the others applauded, and Greta's students joined them with equal enthusiasm. They were Arena pupils and might have mourned their teacher's defeat against anyone else. By now, Ethan had won them over completely.

Even Bea's beauty and skill had not earned quite this response from them. Ethan was a champion, and that changed how they saw the match. Besides, the Battle Arena stood in Kanto, and Ethan had won the Indigo Conference. From a regional point of view, he belonged to their side as much as Greta did. They could cheer for either of them without betraying anyone.

Greta recalled Medicham and approached Ethan, applauding. Against Bea, she had felt she might win a round or two. Against Ethan, she had never seen even the smallest path to victory.

He was far too strong. It was not merely the strength of his Pokémon. Under his direction, Trainer and Pokémon moved together, each supporting the other. Greta could find no weakness in them, while every tiny error she made seemed to grow the instant he noticed it.

Was this the skill of a man who had won three regional conferences? How had he achieved it? Greta had the unsettling feeling that even an Elite Four member might look as fragile as she had against him.

“That was eye-opening,” she said. “Those two aren't even your strongest Pokémon, are they? You beat my best partners so easily with a team below your full strength. I hardly know how to describe you.”

“Describe me normally.” Ethan smiled. “My Pokémon are the strong ones. Their own hard work brought them here. I'm just an ordinary person. How am I any different from the rest of you?”

It was the sort of thing a champion could say: humble on the surface, yet impossible to separate from the strength he had just displayed.

With a wry smile, Greta took another coin-like golden Symbol from her clothing and placed it in Ethan's hand.

“The Guts Symbol of the Battle Arena. It's proof that you defeated me. Please accept it. And thank you, truly. You showed me how much more there is to learn. From now on, I'll train even harder.”
