After a day of rest, Ethan returned to Indigo Stadium for his quarterfinal match.

From this round onward, every match was a full six-on-six battle. Following the Group A bracket, his opponent was Richie—the trainer who had eliminated Ash.

Richie brought back the Pikachu, Charmander, and Butterfree he had used against Ash, then completed his team with Wartortle, Oddish, and Primeape.

Ethan answered with Gardevoir, Charizard, Blastoise, Venusaur, Gyarados, and Jolteon.

Looking at Richie's team, it was hard not to notice how much it resembled Ash's early roster. Add their similar ages, clothes, faces, and instant friendship, and a person might jokingly wonder whether Richie was another son Ash's father had left somewhere along the way.

Of course, that was only a joke.

Delia and Professor Oak had left the Indigo Plateau for Pallet Town after Ash's match two days earlier. Ash had been eliminated, but he had stayed behind. He wanted to see how far Ethan could go.

Now Ash sat in the stands, watching Ethan's every move as if hoping to learn something from the battle.

He expected Richie to put up a stubborn fight. Instead, once the match began, Richie could barely answer Ethan's fully evolved Pokémon. None of his six caused Ethan much trouble. Ethan found opening after opening and defeated them one after another.

It was a complete defeat on every front.

Ethan won six to zero.

Ash rubbed his eyes. The trainer who had knocked him out of the tournament had looked almost helpless against Ethan. All six bouts together had taken less than thirty minutes.

The ruthless pace and the enormous gap in strength left Ash stunned. He even began to feel sorry for Richie.

That was brutal.

But if Ash had faced Ethan himself, would the result have been any different? The thought made his own elimination feel a little less painful.

After the match, Ethan compared Richie with the other trainers he had faced. Richie was not especially strong. He was weaker than Ash and perhaps a little behind Gary and Damian as well. At best, he was around Serena Yuto's level.

A bit of luck had helped him reach the quarterfinals. More than that, his earlier opponents simply had not been very strong.

Ethan was about to leave through the competitors' tunnel when the commentator's voice rang through the stadium.

"First, congratulations to Ethan Vale on reaching the semifinals! And with this six-to-zero victory, he has set a new Indigo League record!"

A record?

Ethan stopped and listened, curious despite himself.

A set of figures appeared on the big screen.

"Since the founding of the Indigo League tournament, no trainer has ever advanced from the preliminaries all the way to the semifinals without losing a single Pokémon! Ethan Vale has now done it with remarkable ease. A new record has been born!"

The spectators rose to applaud him, their admiration plain to see.

When the clapping finally faded, the commentator went on.

"And I believe Ethan will keep making history in the battles ahead. Perhaps he'll push that record even further!"

Had the League told him to promote Ethan? The commentator sounded more like an Ethan fan with every sentence.

Still, it was no surprise. Anything involving Ethan had become a popular subject.

On the field, Ethan shook his head and walked toward the exit. The record pleased him a little, but it did not prove much or bring him any practical benefit.

The overall level of competition at this Indigo League was lower than he expected at future tournaments such as the Lily of the Valley, Vertress, and Lumiose Conferences. Winning every bout here was not something he could let himself grow arrogant about.

He met up with Chloe and the others, returned to their lodging, and celebrated as usual with a large meal.

So many competitors had left that fewer than ten trainers still lived in the village. The residential streets felt deserted. One could walk a long way without seeing anyone.

Ash's room had been taken back after his elimination. With nowhere else to stay there, he, Misty, and Brock moved into Ethan's detached lodge.

It was not simply that the Pokémon League wanted to save on accommodation costs. Removing eliminated trainers from the village also protected those still competing from anyone bitter enough to bother them. To outsiders, though, it could look rather cold.

Another day passed.

Well rested, Ethan entered the semifinal and eliminated a trainer named Lily.

This time, unlike his earlier matches, he felt some real pressure. Lily's Rhydon and Venomoth were particularly formidable, performing beyond anything he had expected.

Fortunately, Ethan's Pokémon got through the first four bouts and defeated their opponents. For the fifth, he sent out Haunter. It beat both Rhydon and Venomoth in succession, leaving Lily no chance to recover.

Ethan won six to zero again and became the first trainer to reach the final.

After watching the whole match, the audience was ecstatic. The bouts had been hard-fought, and they had seen his record continue right before their eyes.

Even Ethan could not help feeling excited now. From the day he received his first Pokémon, he had come all this way. The Indigo League championship was only one step away.

He steadied himself and waited to learn who would emerge from Group B.

Unexpectedly, Damian had lost his quarterfinal and never reached the semifinals. His hope of meeting Ethan in the final was gone.

Logan, on the other hand, defeated Damian's opponent in the semifinal and advanced.

Ethan's final opponent would be the man he had met on New Island—one whose memories of that encounter Mewtwo had erased.
