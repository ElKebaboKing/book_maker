Only now did Solana realize how much the man in front of her had accomplished. It made little difference to her. She rarely followed news from outside the Ranger Union. Learning he had won three League Conferences told her he was capable, but it did not change how she treated him.

Solana and Lunick were the only Rangers here. With everyone together, they shared all they knew. They stopped to rest while Ethan listened, then he touched his chin.

“So since this began last night, you haven't found Celebi, but you have found ashes scattered through the forest?”

“Yes,” Lunick said. “Wood ash, as if trees had burned down. But there hasn't been a forest fire here recently. We can't explain where it came from.”

“Given what Celebi can do, it may have brought the ash with it while traveling through time,” Ethan said. As the Trainer of a Celebi, he felt qualified to offer an opinion. “That would explain its appearance. Did you follow the traces to look for Celebi?”

The Rangers exchanged a look. Solana answered. “We came this morning and noticed the ash. We searched around it, but the thorns and vines have changed the terrain. Some places are covered completely. There isn't a clear trail to follow. Celebi is small, too. If it decided to hide, finding it would be difficult.”

She was right. Celebi could slip into any hollow tree. In a forest this large, finding it without startling it would be like hunting for a needle in a haystack.

“Then there are two approaches left,” Ethan said, glancing at the Capture Stylers on their belts. “You could recruit more wild Pokémon to help search. Given enough time, they might find it. Or…”

“Or what?” Solana asked.

“Find a way to draw it out.”

Both Rangers frowned and shook their heads.

“I don't think that will work,” Solana said with a sigh. “Celebi is peaceful and likes forests, but it's naturally timid. Especially in its present condition, even wild Pokémon wouldn't be able to lure it out.”

It was not impossible, Ethan thought. They simply lacked the right Pokémon. A Pokémon important enough to catch Celebi's attention, or one carrying a familiar presence, could draw it out. Another Celebi, for example.

Neither Ranger could provide that. Ethan could. He could even use Solana's Styler to have his Celebi sent from Celadon Gym. But he did not want to reveal his own Celebi to two people he had only just met, even if they were Rangers trying to heal this forest.

It was a private secret. Unless there was a good reason, he would not tell outsiders. The women who tended the greenhouse at Celadon Gym were a different matter; they had to care for Celebi there.

Still, Ethan would not leave the wild Celebi to suffer. The Rangers had found no other danger, but what if Celebi itself was badly hurt? Every delay could reduce its chance of survival. Ethan loved Pokémon too much to ignore that possibility.

He needed a way to help while keeping his own secret.

After a moment's thought, he decided. “I can help you, but you must agree to one condition.”

Both Rangers looked at him. “We'd be glad of your help,” Lunick said. “What's the condition?”

Ethan's voice was calm and firm. “When I search for Celebi using my method, you must leave the forest. And you must not watch.”

“What?” The demand caught them both off guard.

“Why?” Solana asked, her brow furrowing. “Do you have a way to find it?” For a moment she wondered whether Ethan wanted them gone so he could secretly capture Celebi.

“I can't promise success, but there's a good chance,” Ethan said, leaning back. “You may keep trying your own way instead. I'll continue my journey and leave this to you. It isn't my assignment. I only stopped because a Celebi might be in danger.”

He had made the choice clear. If they wanted his help, they would have to let him take charge of the search and follow his directions. If they could not trust him, he would leave them to their work.

May felt the tension between them and held her breath. Solana considered the demand, then shook her head.

“No. This is a Ranger matter. Even if you work with the International Police, you can't simply—”

“I understand,” Lunick interrupted. He stopped her and turned to Ethan with a serious nod. “I'd rather trust you than waste more time searching without a direction.”

Solana stared at her partner. “Lunick…”

“It's all right. Trust me.” He gave her a reassuring look, then smiled at Ethan. “A man who has won three regional League Conferences and works with the International Police must have methods of his own.”

Solana hesitated again. In the end, she trusted Lunick and said nothing more, leaving the decision to him.

“We'll do as you ask, Mr. Vale,” Lunick said. “We'll watch the forest perimeter and won't look in.”

Ethan showed no change of expression. Then he added, “I'll need you to leave one Capture Styler with me.”

That sounded like pushing his luck. Lunick paused, but something seemed to click. He nodded. “Take mine.”

There was more respect in his voice now. Ethan regarded the younger man more highly as well. Lunick was outgoing and good-humored, but he could make a firm decision. Perhaps he had guessed that Ethan needed the Styler to transfer a Pokémon here and understood that Ethan was serious.

Rangers were not chosen lightly.

“If things go as expected, this should be resolved soon,” Ethan told him. “And I'll return your Styler intact.”

Lunick handed it over and left a way to contact him. Then he and Solana headed toward the edge of the forest.

They walked more than a hundred meters before looking back. Ethan and May had vanished from sight, and they were too far away to be overheard. Solana turned to Lunick.

“Are you sure about this? We left him in charge and gave him important equipment. He won't even let us watch. I don't like walking away from something so secretive.”

“Do we have a better choice?” Lunick asked as he walked. “We've spent all day trying. We searched from the air with Flying Pokémon and underground with Dugtrio. Every attempt failed. I'd rather give this man one chance than keep wandering without a plan. Three League victories don't come from nowhere. He may surprise us.”

As soon as they had gone, Ethan examined the Styler. A short time spent trying its controls was enough for him to understand the functions he needed. He asked May to stand watch in case the Rangers came back, then called Erika.

He had decided to bring his Celebi here and ask for its help after all. In the film about Celebi's journey through time, a group of Celebi from other times had revived one that had died. Ethan trusted that his own Celebi could help an injured member of its kind.

Erika answered quickly. Once Ethan explained, she went to the greenhouse to find Celebi.

When everything was ready, Ethan judged that Solana and Lunick had gone far enough. He changed the Styler's configuration and pressed its transfer button. A GS Ball appeared before him.

It was curious. According to Kurt, Professor Oak, and Professor Ivy, the GS Ball's materials had once prevented it from being sent through a transfer machine. After Ethan caught Celebi in it, the ball seemed to behave normally and could be transferred. He did not understand why. He had never mentioned the change to the professors, keeping it a secret until now.

May glanced over from where she stood guard and saw the unusual ball. So that was Celebi's Poké Ball. It certainly looked different.

Ethan picked it up and pressed its button. Blue light flashed, and an onion-headed creature appeared before him. Celebi, of course.

It immediately sensed how different the forest air was from the greenhouse and flew about with delight. Ethan called it back, mindful of the time, and explained what had happened.

Celebi understood him well. It recognized the seriousness of the situation, gave a soft cry, and flew up over the treetops.

Green light shone from its body. The glow spread rapidly across the forest, and the invasive vines and thorns began to vanish beneath it.

That light did more than clear the forest. It seemed to carry a message between Celebi.

Within half a minute, another Celebi came flying unsteadily from deeper among the trees. It approached Ethan's Celebi. As he had hoped, even hurt, it had sensed its own kind and come out to meet it.

Guided by Ethan's Celebi, the wild one descended in front of him. He examined it at once. Scratches and scrapes covered its body, but they were shallow. It had no obvious life-threatening wound. Its listlessness seemed more likely to come from exhaustion. Food, water, and rest ought to help.

“May!”

She ran over with the first-aid kit. The wild Celebi shrank back from them at first. Ethan's Celebi reassured it, and it finally accepted their help. Compared with most Celebi, this one even seemed a little bolder.

They cleaned its wounds, applied medicine, and dressed the scrapes. May had done such work many times on the road. Her gentle face helped the wild Celebi relax and accept her care.

When the treatment was done, Ethan produced his other reliable remedy: instant noodles. The smell made the wild Celebi's mouth water. Whatever caution it had left disappeared.
