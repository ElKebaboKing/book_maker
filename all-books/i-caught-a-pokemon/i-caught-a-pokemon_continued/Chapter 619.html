Eager to make progress, Ethan and Misty missed the next town where they could have stayed. That night they had to pitch their tents in the wilderness again.

Setting up camp, gathering firewood, boiling water, cooking, and laying out the dishes all went smoothly. They had done it so many times that each step came naturally.

When dinner was ready, Ethan let out Gardevoir, Gengar, Dragonite, Scizor, Tyranitar, Eevee, Combusken, and Beldum. They ate together around the campfire, enjoying one another's company until the meal ended.

Only then did Ethan release Jigglypuff.

It was still indignant, unable to believe it had been caught so easily. The moment it appeared, its eyes darted around and it bolted.

There was a reason Ethan had brought it out last. His other Pokémon were sitting all around it. Except for Beldum, Jigglypuff couldn't hope to beat any of them, and before it had gone far one of them blocked its path.

“Stop struggling,” Ethan said with a smile. “I've already caught you. Running won't change that, so you might as well accept it.”

Jigglypuff tried another direction and found a different Pokémon in its way. After several failed attempts, it plopped down on the ground and sulked, cheeks puffed out. Misty giggled.

Ethan, Misty, and the Pokémon teased it for a while longer. When it finally seemed to have given up trying to escape, they left it alone.

Jigglypuff, however, had only been waiting for them to lower their guard. While everyone was talking, it slipped over to their bags and began searching through them. It wanted its microphone back.

It turned both Ethan's and Misty's bags inside out without finding it. As it wondered where the human had hidden the microphone, a shadow fell across it.

Jigglypuff looked up with a start. Gengar stood there grinning, its microphone in one hand.

“Jiggly!”

Its eyes lit up and it reached for the microphone. Just as its short hands nearly touched it, Gengar lifted its arm. That tiny extra distance put the microphone out of reach. Jigglypuff jumped; Gengar jumped too. It still couldn't get it.

“Jiggly!”

Now it understood it was being toyed with. It glared up at Gengar, whose face wore the same infuriating smile as the human's.

Mauville City was a colorful, lively metropolis where past and present met. It was home to the Mauville Gym, the Mauville Game Corner, Mauville Park, and Mauville Food Court. Young visitors to the city rarely missed those well-known sights.

On the morning of the third day, Ethan and Misty finally reached Mauville. Rather than sightseeing, they went straight to the Gym.

The Mauville Gym was one of Hoenn's official Gyms. Its Leader, Wattson, specialized in Electric-type Pokémon. From a distance, the building looked five or six stories tall. Its outer walls were painted gold, several electrical towers rose from the roof, and an enormous lightning-bolt sign hung outside. It looked more like a power station than a Gym.

Unfortunately, when they arrived, they found it closed for the day. They had missed their chance to challenge it. At least the closure ended that night; the Gym would open for challenges again the following morning.

With no other choice, they looked at each other and set off to find somewhere to stay.

Following the street toward the Pokémon Center, they came to Mauville Park in the middle of the city. Outside the park stood a tall tower modeled on Lumiose City's Prism Tower. It was a symbol of friendship between Lumiose in Kalos and Mauville in Hoenn. According to local stories, the tower shone brightly whenever something went wrong at New Mauville, warning the city.

They had time to spare, so they changed plans and decided to walk through the park first.

Just inside, they saw a small boy arguing with a middle-aged man. Ethan and Misty glanced at them and kept going, but as they passed, the boy suddenly ran over.

“Excuse me, sir! Please wait!”

Ethan and Misty stopped and turned, eyebrows raised.

“What's the matter?” Ethan asked.

The boy came up to him, bowed politely, and asked in an almost pleading tone, “Are you a Pokémon Trainer?”

“Of course.”

As he answered, Ethan took a better look at the child. He seemed about ten, with thick, pale green hair and neat features. Dressed in long sleeves and trousers, he looked like a diligent student and was a little shy.

“Um...” The boy hesitated. “Would you have a battle with me?”

Ethan had never met a challenger who was so polite, even when asking for a battle. He looked toward the man, then asked before agreeing, “I could. But what was going on between you two just now?”

The boy relaxed at the possibility of a match. “I was arguing with my uncle. He thinks I'm not ready to challenge the Mauville Gym. I want to battle you in front of him and show him what I can do!”

So that was it. But why choose Ethan? How did the boy know he could win? If he lost, that would hardly prove he was ready. Unless the boy thought Ethan looked weak.

Ethan glanced from the uncle to the boy once more and finally nodded. “All right. I accept your challenge.”

The boy brightened and pointed nearby. “There's a battlefield over there. Let's go!”

He returned to his uncle and spoke quietly to him. Ethan exchanged a look with Misty, shook his head with a helpless smile, and led the way toward the open-air battlefield.
