The blessing ceremony followed immediately after the awards. Only after receiving the blessing would Ethan truly become this tournament's Sea Hero.

The four attendants withdrew, and Maya approached Ethan with her scepter in hand.

"Mr. Ethan Vale, please kneel here on one knee."

Knowing this was part of the ceremony, Ethan followed her instruction and knelt upon the dais. Maya then raised the golden scepter and rested it against his shoulder.

His shoulder suddenly sank under the weight, and Ethan examined the scepter from the corner of his eye.

The solid-gold staff shone brilliantly beneath the sunlight, while the Sea Spirit set at its tip glittered with an azure radiance!

The scepter was remarkably heavy. Ethan had to admire a woman as seemingly delicate as Maya for carrying it everywhere she went!

A gentle breeze stirred Maya's long purple hair. She held her head high, seemingly enveloped in sacred light, and looked very much like a Greek goddess.

She solemnly proclaimed, "O great God of the Sea! In my name as Sea Priestess, I offer this prayer and ask for your grace. Bestow your blessing upon the warrior before me. Grant him the freedom to travel across the sea and the ability to connect his heart with all Water-type Pokémon!"

The speakers carried Maya's voice across the entire arena, allowing everyone to hear her clearly.

Ethan knelt motionless with a focused expression, apparently accepting the blessing with complete seriousness. Inwardly, however, his thoughts had wandered elsewhere.

Does the God of the Sea mentioned in this ceremony refer to Lugia too?

That fellow's shadow appears everywhere. It isn't just the people of the Orange Islands. Even the people here like calling it the God of the Sea.

When it came to worshiping Legendary Pokémon, people were much the same whether they lived in Kanto, Johto, or any other region.

Still, Lugia was a Psychic- and Flying-type Pokémon. Calling it the God of the Sea did not entirely match what it actually was.

To be honest, Ethan believed another pure Water-type Legendary Pokémon, Kyogre, fit the definition of the God of the Sea much better.

As for why no one called Kyogre by that title, Ethan guessed it was largely because Kyogre usually lived in the deepest parts of the ocean. It appeared far less often than Lugia and was consequently much less familiar to outsiders.

While his imagination wandered, Maya's proclamation reached its end. The Sea Spirit atop the scepter once again released a dazzling light.

Just as during the opening ceremony of the main tournament, magnificent radiance shone forth. This time, however, the pale blue light illuminated Ethan alone!

As before, the Sea Spirit's glow was gentle rather than blinding and appeared no different from the light everyone had already seen.

Bathed in blue, Ethan felt exceptionally calm.

He knew perfectly well that this traditional ceremony had been passed down for several thousand years. Such rituals were almost entirely symbolic and possessed virtually no practical effect. The blessing amounted to a title, proof that by winning the tournament, Ethan had earned the Whirl Islands' highest honor.

Ethan was hardly alone in thinking so. Sea Priestess Maya and nearly every spectator present shared the same belief.

The ceremony proceeded according to tradition and soon approached its conclusion.

But just as the blue light slowly faded and Maya prepared to lift the scepter, the Sea Spirit released a different radiance.

This new azure light shone even more brightly, matching the color of the Sea Spirit itself!

It erupted from the gemstone and instantly wrapped around Ethan!

Under its influence, Ethan suddenly became weightless and floated directly into the air!

After he remained suspended for several seconds, the light abruptly entered his chest, and Ethan descended to the platform once more.

Hmm?

The sudden change left Ethan completely confused. He lowered his head in astonishment and examined his chest, then looked up at Maya, who appeared equally bewildered.

"Um... I have to ask. Is this supposed to happen?"

"Well..."

Maya remained stunned for a long while. She and Ethan were not the only ones confused. The attendants nearby also found the scene utterly incredible.

Four years had passed since Maya became Sea Priestess, and this was the second Whirl Cup held during her tenure. Yet she had never encountered anything like this!

Neither had her mother or grandmother, the previous two Sea Priestesses. Across more than a decade in office, neither woman had ever witnessed such an event!

Why had the Sea Spirit suddenly released another light and sent it into Ethan's heart?

It was completely unprecedented!

"This is extremely strange. Why would such a thing happen?" Maya frowned, plainly unable to understand it. After thinking briefly, she looked at Ethan and asked tentatively, "Do you feel any different?"

Ethan rose, shook his head, and moved each of his limbs. He felt no change whatsoever.

He asked in return, "Wasn't that an effect you created?"

"Of course not," Maya denied immediately. "The Sea Spirit is a sacred relic passed down from ancient times. The light you saw wasn't some artificial effect. It all came from the Sea Spirit itself, and nothing like this has ever happened throughout its entire history!"

"Nothing like this has ever happened?" Seeing that she did not know the cause either, Ethan smiled. "Then can I take it to mean I'm special? Wouldn't that mean that compared with every previous winner, I'm the one who truly received the God of the Sea's blessing?"

Maya froze at his words. Then something seemed to occur to her, and surprise filled her face as she nodded thoughtfully.

"That makes a great deal of sense. Perhaps... perhaps there truly is something special about you, and you accidentally awakened one of the Sea Spirit's powers. I think... that must be the reason!"

The unexpected incident delayed the ceremony only briefly. Since no one could determine the cause for now, they had no choice but to suppress their questions and continue.

With Maya's blessing and the applause of the spectators, this year's Whirl Cup finally came to an end.
