As the earth grew muddier, Milotic moved across it more easily. Its smooth scales helped it slide through the wet ground and pick up speed. It twisted away from each of Swellow's dives with increasing ease.

Swellow, meanwhile, dared not fly too close to the mud or attempt another close-range strike. It might coat its feathers and slow it down. The bird had strength to spare, but fewer ways to use it.

Winona's opinion of Ethan rose another step. His willingness to change the battlefield showed rare creativity. It reminded her of Ash, the challenger who had visited some time ago. Were the two alike?

Swellow could still attack from a distance. It sent an Air Slash toward Milotic. Milotic answered with Water Gun, but the cutting air broke through the stream and continued toward it. Milotic slid aside across the wet ground without panic.

They traded attacks, neither finding a reliable way to defeat the other. For a time, the battle stalled.

“Swellow, Aerial Ace!” Winona called, changing tactics first.

Swellow wheeled through the sky, gathering speed. Ethan watched closely. Aerial Ace used swift movement to throw an opponent off before the decisive slash. Ordinarily, it was an attack that would land. He needed to make the situation anything but ordinary.

“Milotic, Twister!”

A spiraling wind rose from the field, lifting muddy water into the air and flinging it in every direction. The battlefield became harder to read. Swellow lost sight of Milotic behind the twisting column, while the turbulent air and flying mud disrupted its path.

Winona had chosen Swellow for her final round because it was skilled and experienced. It had the patience of an aerial hunter. If the Twister blocked its attack, it could wait for the wind to fade.

That was the right choice. After only a dozen seconds, the Twister weakened. Before it had fully vanished, Swellow seized its chance. It dove at great speed, made a sharp right-angle turn, and struck Milotic with Aerial Ace.

Milotic stumbled and fell backward. By the time it rose to retaliate, Swellow was high above it again.

Ethan saw the wound, but remained calm. “Milotic, Recover.”

Milotic gave a soft cry. Pale light surrounded it, and most of the injury healed. The battle seemed to have returned to where it began.

Still, Milotic had spent more energy on Twister and Recover than Swellow had used for its attack. Winona saw a possible path to victory.

“Good! Swellow, let's do that again!”

The first Twister had failed to stop Swellow. Ethan knew it would be difficult to fight this bird by the same means. Yet when Winona prepared to repeat her tactic, another idea came to him. He ordered Milotic to use Twister again.

The same spiraling wind rose from the field. Misty frowned from the stands, and Winona did too. Why repeat something that had not worked? Was Ethan out of ideas, or hoping a second attempt would succeed?

He gave them their answer. As soon as the Twister formed, Milotic plunged into it. The wind swept it upward within seconds. As it began to fall, it twisted its body, changed direction in midair, and dropped toward Swellow.

Winona understood. Swellow had avoided close combat, so Ethan was carrying Milotic to it. But she thought the plan too simple. The air was a Flying-type Pokémon's domain. Without the wet earth beneath it, Milotic could not maneuver. It would be an easy target.

“Swellow, show it what you can do!”

Swellow spread its wings and charged. Ethan smiled. Winona had taken the bait.

“Now, Milotic—Surf!”

Winona, Misty, the referee, and even Swellow froze in surprise. Milotic alone obeyed without hesitation. A great wave formed beneath it in midair and crashed down with Milotic toward Swellow.

The wave spread like an enormous net. Swellow was too close. It had time only to look up before the water struck, knocked it out of the sky, and drove it to the ground.

Milotic, Swellow, and the wave landed together. Swellow, once so proud in flight, looked like a drenched bird now. Its soaked feathers clung to its body.

“What?” Winona's expression changed.

Milotic was already slithering toward Swellow. “Swellow, get away!” she called.

Swellow beat its wings desperately. Water flew from them, but after a dozen strokes it could not rise even a meter. Milotic reached it.

“Aqua Tail!”

Water wrapped around Milotic's tail. It lashed Swellow with a sharp smack, knocking it off balance. Milotic gave it no chance to recover. It closed in, coiled its tail tightly around the bird, and squeezed until Swellow fainted.

“Swellow is unable to battle! The winner of this Gym battle is the challenger, Ethan!”

The referee's raised flag ended the match. Winona had been defeated by a constricting hold more than once today.

“You win,” she said. “That was excellent strategy. I had a chance in the last round, but you outplayed me.”

“You were strong too. Without that last idea, I might not have beaten you.”

They had made a good impression on each other, and exchanged a few more compliments after the match. Misty came down from the stands and sighed at the display. Adults could make even a finished battle complicated.

Winona took a shining Badge from her clothes and placed it in Ethan's hand. “Here. The Feather Badge, proof that you've defeated me.”

Ethan thanked her and examined it. Misty leaned in beside him. The Badge resembled a silver-white wing, simple and clean in its design. Ethan liked it.

As he placed it in his Badge case, Winona spotted the seven others inside. “You've collected all eight? I'm honored that mine was the last.”

Trainers usually chose a Gym route by distance, but many still tried easier Gyms first and saved harder ones for later. Leaders knew that, and few liked being considered the weakest, first stop. Winona was pleased to be Ethan's final challenge.

Erika had told Ethan something about Gym Leaders' feelings on the subject. He could guess why Winona was smiling.

They said goodbye and left the Fortree Gym. With all eight Badges collected, Ethan's journey through Hoenn was nearing its end—but the Ever Grande Conference still awaited him. Afterward, he might visit the Battle Frontier. He had plenty left to do.

Once they were clear of the Gym, Misty asked eagerly, “Where next? Straight to Ever Grande City?”

“It's too early. There's at least another half month.” Ethan shook his head. “But you've reminded me: we can verify my eligibility at the Pokémon Center now.”

They followed a leafy path back into Fortree and went to the Pokémon Center. Nurse Joy was at the counter. Ethan explained what he needed.

“Of course. Let me check your Badges.”

He handed over the case. Nurse Joy confirmed all eight, entered something into the computer, and returned it.

“You're registered in advance. When the Conference begins, you'll still need to confirm your participation in person at the venue.”

Ethan had followed the same process at the Silver Conference and had no objection. “Thank you.”

They left the Center without stopping to rest. Ethan had planned to find a lively city where he and Misty could relax while he finished training for the Conference. They had barely stepped outside when a call from May disrupted that plan.

“What? It's starting already?” Misty exclaimed.

Ethan stood about a meter away, yet he could hear her clearly over the phone. The Hoenn Grand Festival was about to begin. May had earned her five Ribbons and was on her way to the host city. She had called to invite Misty to join her there.

Misty hesitated and looked back at Ethan. He nodded.

“Absolutely!” she told May, laughing. “Ethan just got his eighth Badge, so we've got time. Let's meet there!”

She ended the call and started off at once. Ethan raised a hand to stop her.

“Why are you so excited? You're not competing. And haven't you forgotten something?”

“Forgotten what?” Misty looked confused. “We've got our bags. Nurse Joy returned your Badge case. What else is there?”

Ethan shook the two pouches hanging behind him. Misty saw them move and stuck out her tongue.

“Oh! The orbs. We still have to give them back to Phoebe.”

As she said Phoebe's name, someone dropped from the tree canopy above and landed in front of her.

“Ah!” Misty jumped. Then she recognized Phoebe. What timing.

If this world had the saying about Cao Cao arriving the moment his name was spoken, Misty would certainly have used it.
