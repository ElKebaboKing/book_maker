By now, Lucy knew how difficult Ethan's Milotic was to fight. Compared with her own, it performed exceptionally well on both offense and defense. It left her few openings. Even when she gave the battle her full attention, keeping up was hard; the slightest lapse let it catch her out.

Then again, this Milotic had competed in regional tournaments and come through battles of the highest intensity. Its ability should not have surprised her.

What troubled her more was the man behind it. His precise judgments and his steady style, daring when it needed to be, were the real problem. Under his direction, Milotic fought beyond its usual limits. It was as though he had given it another pair of eyes and another mind. He showed exactly what a Trainer could contribute.

Before Lucy realized it, her Milotic had fallen behind. Same species, same type, even many of the same moves—yet Ethan kept finding small advantages in the details. By the time she noticed how they were adding up, it was too late to undo them.

Most Trainers in that position would gamble on a bolder strategy to recover. Lucy was no exception. She knew that if the battle continued as it was, she would lose.

“Milotic! Dive into the water!”

Her Milotic plunged beneath the surface without hesitation.

Ethan stared for a moment, then understood. Lucy had realized she could not win while he could direct every exchange. By hiding her Pokémon underwater, she meant to cut off his line of sight and break that connection, leaving the two Milotic to settle it between themselves.

It was a good idea. Unfortunately for her, as Greta had found out before, Ethan's Pokémon excelled at fighting without instructions. Lucy had chosen the wrong way to challenge him.

His Milotic dived in after hers. Soon the surface settled, leaving only widening rings of ripples. Then bubbles rose from the bottom, the water churned, and waves spread toward the banks. The two Pokémon were plainly fighting beneath the pool.

Without underwater cameras or a large screen, nobody could tell exactly what was happening. Lucy likely hoped her Milotic's familiarity with the arena would help it find an opening. Against an ordinary Trainer, denying them a view of the battle might have worked. For Ethan's Pokémon, independence was an advantage.

In the stands, Lucy's supporters clenched their fists and watched the water intently. Which Milotic would come out on top? Would they surface in a dead heat and continue the fight?

They did not have long to wait.

Water burst upward. One Milotic emerged with its tail wrapped tightly around the other and hauled it onto the bank. It tightened its coils. The captive struggled a few times, then went completely limp.

At first glance, the two looked alike. But the women who spent every day with Lucy's Milotic knew the little differences. They recognized the unconscious one immediately: it was Lucy's.

“The boss's Milotic lost?”

“I can't believe it. It looked so easy for him…”

“So the champion's Pokémon really is stronger?”

As they sighed, May's eyebrows lifted. That's what I thought. There is no such thing as an attack nobody can withstand. Ethan is as amazing as ever!

Ethan did not know what anyone in the stands was thinking. All his attention was on his Pokémon. A closer look showed small wounds on his Milotic's body. The underwater fight had been fierce and dangerous.

The victory might have looked effortless from outside the pool, but it had been nothing of the sort. The two Milotic were closely matched. His had fought a little better, but it could never have overwhelmed Lucy's through strength alone. If he had to put numbers on it, he would have called the balance 5.3 to 4.7.

Lucy had suffered for her final choice. It had not been a foolish order; she simply did not know how Ethan usually battled. His Pokémon often took part in sprawling free-for-alls where he could not direct each of them. They had learned to make their own decisions. Had Lucy known that, she would never have sent her Milotic underwater to fight on its own.

After this loss, she had clearly realized it. It was not too late to adjust.

Lucy recalled her unconscious Milotic and took out a second Poké Ball. Blue light flashed, and her ace, Seviper, appeared before the cheering women.

“You can do it, boss!”

“Seviper, show him what you've got!”

Ordinary staff might have held back out of consideration for the challenger rather than cheering so loudly for their Leader. These women had no such reservations. Their shouts echoed throughout the Battle Pike.

Ethan paid them no attention. He did not even glance their way. This was hardly enough to rattle him, and his one-round lead gave him no particular pleasure either. He recalled his Milotic and studied Seviper.

When he thought of that species, Jessie's Seviper came to mind first. It was strong, full of character, and one of her most recognizable Pokémon. But the Team Rocket trio never dared cause trouble in front of him, so she had not shown it to Ethan once since catching it.

He still knew plenty about Seviper. It resembled a venomous snake, perhaps a black pit viper. Its body was mostly black, with a golden lower jaw, gold armor on its head and back, and six small gold projections along its belly that helped it crawl. Its fangs were sharp and red; its tail was shaped like a blade.

Seviper honed that tail blade against rocks, making it razor-sharp and coating it with potent venom. It hid in grass, struck approaching prey with its poisonous fangs, then slashed with its tail to cut and poison the victim. It was a dangerous, cold-blooded hunter.

Lucy clearly liked battling with serpent-shaped Pokémon. Milotic and Seviper were only the ones she had shown so far; Steelix and Gyarados shared that long, snakelike build. She seemed as fond of them as she was of the narrow-eyed Pokémon she kept at home.

What should Ethan use against her ace?

After putting away Milotic's Ball, he weighed Blaziken against Eevee for a moment, then sent Eevee out.

It was not that he had no other Pokémon to train. Tyranitar, Metagross, or Scizor could all have matched up well against Seviper. But he did not want to choose one of them. He knew how troublesome Lucy's supporters could be. If he sent out a Pokémon that countered Seviper and won, they would never let him hear the end of it. He would rather avoid inviting that trouble. Eevee was his choice.

His calculations did not stop at the edge of the arena.

Because water covered more than half the unusual field, neither Pokémon could take the usual central starting position. They stood instead on small patches of open ground toward the sides of their respective halves. The slight shift in position made little difference.

Once both Pokémon were ready, the referee waved her hand again. The second round began.

Despite her first loss, Lucy attacked as aggressively as before. Seviper raced toward Eevee and snapped at it. Eevee sprang away, but Seviper immediately swung its bladed tail at the spot where Eevee would land.

Eevee reacted in time and jumped again, escaping the slash. The assault was not over. A snake's powerful muscles let it surge forward in an instant, and Seviper lunged with its fangs.

Another Pokémon might have been caught at such close range. Ethan's Eevee was lively and quick to react. At the last possible moment, it avoided the venom-sheened fangs, kicked off with both hind legs, and struck Seviper on the head.

“Ah, so close!” Several disappointed cries rose from the stands.

The women knew that little sequence was one of Seviper's signature tactics, unimpressive as it might look. Once its fangs landed and the venom entered its opponent's body, Seviper gained a commanding advantage. Comebacks after that were rare. Eevee had escaped, and now that it knew what to expect, the combination would be even harder to land.

They did not realize that Ethan had thought That was close as well. He was good at reading the field and noticing details, but this was his first fight against Lucy's Seviper. He could not have anticipated that sequence. Eevee's quick reactions had saved it from a dangerous hit. It had grown a great deal.

With Seviper's opening attack over, Eevee went on the offensive. It could not move as fast as Seviper, but its small body gave it nimble footwork and it was an excellent jumper. It circled the snake and harried it from every side.

Seviper's fangs made approaching too closely dangerous, however. Eevee kept trying to find a safe opening and achieved little. The fight settled into a stalemate.

Lucy's eyes narrowed. She gave another decisive order, and almost before her words ended, Seviper's tail flashed toward Eevee. The bladelike tip moved so fast it whistled through the air.

“Eevee, dodge!”

Eevee had been watching the tail and leaped backward, but the strike was too fast. It hit the ground and sent an explosion of soil and debris outward, knocking Eevee into the nearby pool.

Splash!

Eevee panicked at first, paddling wildly like a little dog. Once it adjusted, it began swimming for the far bank.

Lucy saw her chance. Seviper was no water snake, but it went into the pool without hesitation and swam after Eevee.

The situation had turned dangerous. Eevee was agile on land; in the water it could barely fight at all. At their present speeds and distances, Seviper would catch it before it reached the shore. Then Eevee would be at its mercy.

Ethan frowned and swept his gaze over the pool. An answer came to him.

“Eevee, get onto that log beside you!”

The enormous vortex in the first round had torn shrubs and small trees out of the ground. Some had blown beyond the arena, but many still floated in the pool. Now that debris offered Eevee a way out.
