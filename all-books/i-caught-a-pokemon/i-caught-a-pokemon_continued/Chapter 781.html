The reversal caught Morrison completely by surprise. He had never expected Ash to combine two moves, increase their power, and turn Ice Beam back on Metang. When Ash came up with an idea, it could be almost impossible to prepare for.

The two Ice-type attacks froze Metang where it hovered. Its body temperature was so low that the ice held it in place, and frost and snow coated its surface. Ash would not waste the opening. Glalie charged in with Headbutt and struck it squarely.

The blow toppled Metang and did considerable damage, though it also broke Metang free of the ice. Morrison saw it recover and immediately counterattacked.

Metang flew toward Glalie at high speed and landed a Meteor Mash from close range, taking back some of the ground it had lost. Ash refused to give way. He sent Glalie into a close-quarters fight.

The two Pokémon tangled, colliding head-on and trading blows like brawlers. Everyone knew the decisive exchange was close. The spectators gave the field their full attention.

Metang came in with another Meteor Mash, while Glalie charged with Headbutt. Just before they collided, Glalie shifted its course. Metang's fist missed, and Glalie struck it instead. Metang tipped backward and fell in front of Glalie.

“It's over,” Ethan said softly.

As he spoke, the referee raised a flag. “Metang is unable to battle! Glalie wins! The winner of this match is Ash!”

Ethan and the others stood with the rest of the crowd and applauded Ash. He had earned this victory the hard way.

Both he and Morrison were strong Trainers. If bad luck had not paired them against each other, each might have reached at least the final four. Fate had put them in the same group instead.

After the result, Ash and Morrison walked together and shook hands. Ethan could not hear what they said, and there was no need to recount it. When they left the field, they came up to the stands and joined his group to watch the other matches.

With Morrison present, the others kept their comments gentle. They congratulated Ash and offered Morrison a few words of comfort. Perhaps he had settled things with Ash, because he waved their concern away.

“You don't need to console me,” he said. “I'm disappointed I lost, but I didn't get my head straight in time. Ash beat me fair and square. We agreed he'll keep fighting for both of us now!”

There was no bitterness in his expression. He was his usual openhearted, easygoing self. Unlike Tyson, who had left as soon as Ethan eliminated him, Morrison stayed to enjoy the rest of the tournament. That was one of the better things about people like him.

Three more matches followed Ash and Morrison's. The competitors varied in ability. They had bright moments, but the battles were less exciting. The audience did not mind. So long as nothing strange interrupted the matches and the Trainers kept fighting, they were happy to watch.

At the noon break, Ethan's group left the stadium. They ate a simple lunch nearby and chatted about the strengths and weaknesses of the other competitors. Ash even offered a solemn assessment of several of them. After eating, they wandered around outside the stadium. At last, at three in the afternoon, Ethan's match arrived.

“Good luck, Brother Ethan!”

“You can do it!”

“The Trainers in this Conference are strong,” Ash called. “Watch out for an upset!”

They were gathered outside the competitors' tunnel to wish Ethan luck. At Ash's teasing, Misty made a face at him. “Brother Ethan isn't as inconsistent as you. You nearly lost! Just watch. He'll beat his opponent in no time.”

“All right. Wait for the good news.” Ethan smiled, waved, and walked into the tunnel, leaving them with a confident view of his back.

…

To the sound of cheering, Ethan emerged onto the field and took his place in the Trainer's area. Across from him stood the young woman with glasses. Their eyes met across the field, and they gave each other polite nods.

Her name was Shizuko. It sounded gentle, entirely unlike her fierce battling style. Though they had entered together, the crowd cheered noticeably louder for her.

The difference came from their earlier matches. Ethan had faced Tyson, arguably the strongest Trainer left apart from him. But Ethan had won so decisively that the audience had seen little of a contest. They knew both Trainers were strong without getting a clear sense of just how strong.

Shizuko was different. She had dispatched her opponent with startling force and efficiency. A young woman with a quiet appearance and a violent battling style was memorable. She had become more popular with the crowd, and many people betting on the match favored her.

The screen selected the Rock Field for the first half. At the referee's signal, both Trainers threw their opening Poké Balls. Ethan sent out Blaziken. Shizuko chose Primeape.

Primeape was a fitting partner for someone with her fiery temperament. With both Pokémon ready, the battle began at once.

Primeape bounded toward Blaziken, restless and furious, much like its Trainer. Blaziken, a Pokémon with a fiery fighting spirit, charged to meet it. Before they reached the middle of the field, Blaziken used Flamethrower without slowing its stride.

Primeape had been eager for a close-range fight. Blaziken had looked ready to give it one. Primeape did not expect a sudden blast of fire and was engulfed before it could prepare. While the flames still covered it, Blaziken accelerated and drove a high kick into it.

The match had barely started, and Primeape had already taken two attacks. Max laughed in the stands.

“Ethan uses more strategy than Ash does after all!”

“What do you mean?” Ash pouted. “Didn't you see me thinking things through earlier? I don't just rush in anymore!”

“Thinking and strategy aren't quite the same,” Misty said with a grin. “Max means you still have a long way to go before you catch up with Ethan.”

“Come on!” Ash muttered, his face hot with annoyance. “Just watch the match.”

Primeape got up and finally closed with Blaziken. Both were Fighting types, and their close-range exchange was more exciting than most. Shizuko's Primeape was strong. It used Low Kick, Karate Chop, Cross Chop, and even the Normal-type Scratch with practiced skill and boundless drive. Its fighting ability could stand beside that of Pokémon from a Fighting-type Gym.

Blaziken was not as specialized in pure Fighting techniques. It gained little advantage up close, though it could hold Primeape off without falling behind.

That was where the Trainer mattered. Ethan saw that a close-range contest alone would not win it. He changed tactics and had Blaziken use its fire. Blaze Kick punished Primeape when it got close; Flamethrower struck when it backed away.

Blaziken took control of the pace. After several fiery attacks, Primeape fell. A cheer broke out. It was not overwhelming, but the audience plainly approved of what they had seen.

The referee raised a flag. “Primeape is unable to battle! Blaziken wins!”

In the opposite Trainer's area, Shizuko frowned. She had studied recordings of Ethan and knew he was a formidable opponent. She was confident in her own strength too. She had believed she could beat him.

Despite a difficult opening, Primeape had seemed to hold the upper hand through much of the round. Shizuko had thought she would win. Then Ethan changed the pace, and everything turned against her in a matter of moments.

Looking back, she felt as though she might have found a way to turn it around. At the same time, perhaps she never had one. The uncertainty bothered her.

She kept the thought to herself and took out her second Poké Ball. Blue light flashed. Dodrio stood before Blaziken.

Ethan studied the three-headed, flightless bird and felt he understood Shizuko's tastes a little better. Primeape and Dodrio were both volatile Pokémon, well suited to her aggressive style. Or perhaps working with them had shaped that style.

He kept Blaziken in against the three-headed bird. In the stands, Ash and the others were surprised.

“Ethan isn't switching Pokémon?”

“That's unusual for him,” someone said.

“Maybe he wants to give the Pokémon he caught in Hoenn more experience,” Brock suggested, rubbing his chin. “His other partners are already extremely strong, and he's given them all a chance to battle. It would be like him to focus on the newer ones now.”

The others nodded. Few Trainers would use a final-sixteen match to train a Pokémon. Ash and Morrison certainly would not dare. Then again, Blaziken and Ethan's other Hoenn partners were already strong enough to contend for the championship. Calling this training was only relative to the strength of his older team. Anyone who mistook these Pokémon for weak would be a fool.

Blaziken and Dodrio began. Dodrio fought much like Primeape, rushing into range before attacking fiercely. It was faster, and its three heads gave it more ways to strike. After facing Primeape, however, Blaziken was ready for this style.

It did not need a Fire-type move. Using only Fighting techniques, Blaziken subdued Dodrio and gave the crowd a moment to remember.

Each of its claws seized one of Dodrio's outer heads. It pressed its right foot down on the middle head, holding all three in a finishing pin. Dodrio could not move.

Shizuko saw the danger and reached for her Poké Ball to recall it. Before she could lift her hand, Blaziken tightened its claws and foot. Dodrio fainted in its grip.

Camera flashes burst from every angle, preserving the sight. Two to zero. Blaziken alone had put Shizuko in a dangerous position.

Many spectators were stunned by Ethan's strength. Those who had bet on Shizuko were especially nervous. They had expected the more aggressive-looking Trainer to dominate; instead, she was the one being pushed back.

At least she still had four Pokémon she had not sent out. The rest of the match remained uncertain.
