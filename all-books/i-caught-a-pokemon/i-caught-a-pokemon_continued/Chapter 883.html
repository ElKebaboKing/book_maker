Zuki left the dining room. A long while later, she returned carrying a finely made box.

Everyone looked at it. It had plainly been cleaned and looked almost new. Zuki opened it under their gaze.

A bell about the size of a fist rested on a soft lining. Its shape was ordinary enough, but its material was not. Despite its earthy yellow color, it was partly transparent.

“Is that the Clear Bell?” Sayo asked.

Apart from Miki, Kuni, and Zuki, no one had seen it before. Even Naoko and Sayo, familiar with their family's history, had not known the bell was kept in their home.

Zuki nodded and began to tell Ethan and May a story from long ago.

Seven hundred years earlier, the people of Ecruteak had built two towers, one to the east and one to the west, as monuments to friendship between humans and Pokémon. Both commanded magnificent views. The eastern tower called Pokémon to wakefulness, while the western tower brought them sleep. Ho-Oh and Lugia lived atop them.

Then, a hundred and fifty years ago, lightning struck the western tower. It burned for three days and nights. Rain finally put out the fire, but little of the building remained. Three unknown Pokémon died in the blaze. Ho-Oh descended and used a mysterious power to restore them to life.

That was the familiar story of the three legendary beasts. Zuki had more to tell.

“Our ancestors built the towers,” she said. “For generations, our family served as guardians and attendants of Ho-Oh and Lugia. But after the fire, everything changed. People feared Pokémon that could control life and death. Some wanted to subdue them by force; others wanted that power for themselves. Ho-Oh, Lugia, and the three beasts left Ecruteak and disappeared.”

She continued, “With both Pokémon gone and one tower destroyed, our family lost heart. Our ancestors left the towers and moved to this estate. We've lived here ever since.”

Zuki took the Clear Bell from its box and placed it before Ethan.

“We no longer serve Ho-Oh and Lugia as our ancestors did, but we've kept this bell. We hoped it might one day call Ho-Oh back.”

“Yet I've never heard a story of Ho-Oh returning to Ecruteak,” Ethan said, touching his chin. “So no one in your family has managed to summon it?”

Kuni nodded ruefully. “They've tried countless times over a hundred and fifty years. Ho-Oh never answered. Eventually our family grew tired of trying. By our generation, no one made the attempt. That's why the bell was left in storage.”

It also explained why Naoko and Sayo had not known the family possessed it.

“I had no idea your family had such a history.” Ethan picked up the bell and turned it in his hands. “Is this what you meant when you said you could offer me something in return?”

The three older sisters exchanged a look. Miki answered.

“After so many failures, our family came to believe the bell needed something more to draw Ho-Oh's attention. You've brought the three legendary beasts. They have a connection to Ho-Oh. That connection may be what was missing.”

Ethan understood. With Entei, Raikou, and Suicune here, the sisters hoped the Clear Bell could summon Ho-Oh.

He set the bell back on the table and smiled. “Why do you think I want to meet Ho-Oh?”

The women stared at him.

“It's Ho-Oh,” Zuki said. “Don't you want to catch it?”

The three sisters' perspective had changed quickly. Before becoming close to Ethan, they would have scorned anyone who spoke of capturing Ho-Oh. Such a person would have seemed arrogant and unworthy of the legendary Pokémon.

Now they were encouraging Ethan to do it. Others might not be capable, but surely their partner was.

“Catch Ho-Oh?” Ethan shook his head.

If he fought it in earnest, could he capture it? He thought he probably could. Apart from Pokémon on Arceus's level, and a few with unusual forms or Mega Evolution, very little in this world could withstand Rayquaza and Deoxys together. He had other powerful Pokémon waiting besides.

But did he need Ho-Oh?

It was undeniably strong. Its power to restore life, its golden appearance, and its rarity made it a dream for many Trainers. Ethan saw the appeal.

He also saw the trouble. If word of its capture spread, Pokémon hunters and criminal organizations would target him. Ordinary Trainers would crowd around him to challenge him or seize it. Travel would become a struggle.

And Ho-Oh had lived for thousands of years. A Pokémon with its own ways would hardly sit quietly in a Poké Ball or willingly submit to a human. Even if captured, it might come out on its own and resume roaming the world.

That did not mean Ethan would refuse a truly excellent opportunity. At present, though, he saw no need to pursue it.

The sisters were surprised by his indifference.

“You aren't interested in Ho-Oh?” Miki asked.

“It's more complicated than that.” Ethan gave a short snort and told them how Ho-Oh had interfered when he first tried to catch the three beasts.

Only then did the sisters learn he had already crossed paths with it.

“So we don't need to summon Ho-Oh?” Kuni asked.

“Why not?” Ethan grinned. “Ho-Oh and I still have unfinished business. It escaped last time. If we have a chance to meet again, I won't waste it. I don't plan to catch it now, but I do want to show it what I can do.”

The sisters laughed. He could hold a grudge even against Ho-Oh. For all his strength, there was something boyish about his determination to fight it.

His answer also eased their minds. They had suggested capturing Ho-Oh for Ethan's sake, but it was a symbol of Johto. If possible, they would rather see it remain free.

“Let's try summoning it after midnight,” Miki said. “Most people will be asleep. There'll be less chance of drawing a crowd. And we'd love to see Ho-Oh for ourselves.”

With the time settled, they talked eagerly about the plan. After a while, Miki, Kuni, and Zuki each left to spend time with the legendary beast entrusted to her.

May took Naoko and Sayo back to her performance practice. In moments, Ethan was alone in the room.

He shook his head, drank a few more cups of tea, and watched the courtyard for a while. At last he stretched and returned to his guest room. After the last two days' exertion and a full breakfast, he was sleepy again. A nap would help him prepare for the meeting with Ho-Oh after midnight.

He had just spread his bedding when the sliding door opened. Miki came in.

“Weren't you getting to know Entei?” he asked, surprised. “Why are you here?”

Miki closed the door behind her and sat beside him, a faint blush on her cheeks.

“I fed it. Flareon is keeping it company now. Entei still seems wary of me, but I think time will help it trust me.”

“That's good. These things take time.”

Ethan did not ask why she had come. Their relationship had changed abruptly, and they hardly knew each other as partners. She probably wanted a private conversation. It would be strange to share a life with him without knowing where he came from, who his family was, or what his past had been like.

They began to talk. Miki asked about his life without making him feel interrogated, guiding the conversation naturally toward what she wanted to know. Ethan admired her tact.

He learned about her interests, preferences, and the years that had brought her here. Their understanding of each other deepened.

As they talked beside the bedding, Ethan found himself drawn to her. They were partners now, and he reached for her hand. Miki let him hold it. He drew her into his arms and kissed her.

It was the first time he had kissed Miki while fully clearheaded. She closed her eyes shyly and returned the kiss.

They lay down together.

…

Afterward, Ethan was no longer sleepy. He dressed, folded the bedding, and watched Miki leave with a shy face and unsteady steps.

I couldn't resist.

He shook his head at himself. Since accepting the three sisters as partners, his self-restraint seemed to be failing him.

What should he do now?

He heard another set of light footsteps outside. At first he thought Miki had forgotten something. These steps sounded softer. A moment later, Kuni slid the door open and came in, looking shy.

Ethan stared. The two sisters had missed each other by perfect timing.

There was little doubt why Kuni had come. Like Miki, she wanted time alone to know him better. Ethan spoke with her and shared much of what he had just told Miki.

Before long, their conversation led them back to the bedding. Ethan was young and had had a few minutes to rest. Compared with the last two days, that felt like plenty.

The poor bedding had barely been folded before he spread it again. The room looked much as it had earlier, though the woman beside him was different.

…

Kuni left on unsteady feet. Ethan was more awake than ever. He dressed again and folded the bedding once more.

He had only just finished when the door opened for a third time. Zuki stood there.

Before stepping inside, she sniffed the air. “What is that smell? It's strong.” She understood immediately and gave a sharp little snort. “My sisters were here before me, weren't they? And you had a fine time with both.”

Ethan could only shrug.

Despite her words, Zuki came in. Like Miki and Kuni, she closed the sliding door behind her.
