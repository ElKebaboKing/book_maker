Drake did not seem especially surprised that Ditto had lost. If Ethan had failed to defeat it, that might have surprised him more. A Trainer with such a powerful Gyarados deserved his championship title and had met Drake's expectations. The speed and brutality of Ditto's defeat, though, had caught him off guard.

Ethan answered his compliment politely. "Your Ditto is strong. Against most Trainers, it would be difficult to handle. It's unfortunate that it faced my Gyarados. It never had the chance to make full use of its abilities."

Drake fell silent. Ethan sounded sincere, but the words struck him as arrogant. Unfortunate that it faced your Gyarados? The man did not seem to know how to be modest.

Drake smiled faintly. Perhaps that was for the best. A strong, confident opponent rekindled the excitement he had not felt in a long time. After waiting so long for someone who might beat him, he had every reason to give this match his all.

He took another Poké Ball from inside his vest. "Then this is my next Pokémon!"

He threw the ball. Blue light flashed, and Onix appeared on the field.

"Oh! Drake's Onix!" the announcer cried. "It's given outstanding performances in past matches. Drake must be counting on it to even the score. Let's see if it can do it!"

The sight of a fresh Pokémon revived Drake's supporters. They shook off the disappointment of Ditto's loss and cheered for him again. Applause rolled through the stadium.

Whatever the announcer said about Onix, Ethan's path had become simpler after winning the first round. Drake had to send his next Pokémon out first. Ethan could then choose a favorable type. Against Onix, the decision was easy.

He threw another Poké Ball. "Venusaur, this one's yours!"

Venusaur landed with a heavy thud and fixed its gaze on Onix. Its broad body held its own against such a large opponent. Grass-type attacks would strike Onix, a Rock- and Ground-type, with four times their usual effectiveness. The matchup was straightforward.

"My, my," the announcer drawled. "Venusaur! It looks like Ethan intends to claim a second win with a type advantage. How convenient for him."

There was a pointed edge to his voice. As a Drake supporter, he seemed to think a champion from a major League should choose an even matchup instead of exploiting a weakness.

The speakers picked up the sound of pages turning at his desk. A few seconds later, he gave a small laugh. "According to these records, Ethan Vale made good use of type advantages throughout the Indigo League. He must have liked the results so much that he's brought the same style to the Orange Islands."

Ethan heard every word and remained calm. Going first or second, using a type advantage or fighting with the same type—these were ordinary parts of Pokémon battling and legitimate tactical choices. He would not complain if someone used them against him. The announcer was merely venting after Drake lost the first round.

Rather than fault Ethan, perhaps he ought to consider why Ditto had lost.

Ditto's picture dimmed on the scoreboard. Now Onix faced Venusaur. Ethan looked across at Drake, whose expression had not changed. Most of the spectators understood type matchups too and paid little attention to the announcer's complaint.

The referee saw that both Trainers were ready and waved the flag for the second round.

"Venusaur, use Vine Whip!" Ethan attacked first again.

Two vines rose from the center of Venusaur's flower and swept toward Onix.

"Onix, use Dig!" Drake ordered at once. He knew the matchup was unfavorable and did not intend to take a direct hit.

Whoosh! The vines cut through the air like green streaks. Before they could land, Onix broke through the ground and slipped down into a tunnel like an eel. It vanished so quickly that Ethan frowned. Pokémon that knew Dig were often fast underground, but this Onix had clearly practiced it. It disappeared before most eyes could follow.

Smack! Smack! The vines struck bare ground, kicking up dust and gravel without touching Onix's tail.

With his opponent out of sight, Ethan studied the battlefield again. Its rocky terrain suited Onix. It could travel beneath the ground and use the rocks, along with its similar coloring, to conceal its position. Venusaur would have trouble telling where it was.

Ethan had the type advantage; Onix had the terrain. For now he would have to wait for it to surface.

"Venusaur, don't stand still. Move toward the pool and watch the ground beneath you!"
