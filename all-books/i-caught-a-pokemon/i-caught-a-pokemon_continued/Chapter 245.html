The submarine's hatch opened. Under everyone's gaze, an elderly man emerged. He had a thick handlebar mustache, small round glasses, and dark skin. He was short and looked at least sixty or seventy, yet he was remarkably lively. His eyes shone with intelligence.

As soon as he climbed out, he produced an assortment of strange little devices. He measured the wind, received a signal with a small satellite dish, and kept himself so busy that he seemed not to notice anything else.

Then he glanced aside and saw the group nearby. "Oh! You young people over there, you'd better be careful!"

The warning came without explanation. Ethan and the others stared at him.

"Careful of what?" Ash asked.

The old man ignored the question. He straightened and spoke to himself. "Wind conditions good. Water temperature acceptable. No change in the current. Solar spectrum normal. They'll be here soon!"

None of it made sense to the others. They looked at one another, puzzled.

"They? Who's coming? Who is this man?" Misty whispered.

"I don't know," Tracey said. "It looks like he's doing scientific research."

Before they could ask more, the sea around them seemed to boil. Bubbles and spray burst from the surface. The waves grew rougher, and tens of thousands of Magikarp suddenly surged up from below.

"M-Magikarp?"

"Where did so many come from?"

Ash and his friends gaped. The Magikarp paid them no attention. Every one of them swam in the same direction, as though following an unseen command.

Only then did Ethan understand the old man's warning. The Magikarp formed a dense red wave. They meant no harm, but anyone who fell into the water now could be battered badly by the sheer mass of fish.

Lapras shivered beneath the group. The sight had frightened it, and it stopped swimming, bobbing with the current. Ash hurried to soothe it.

Ethan steadied himself. Once the first shock passed, he noticed that the old man on the submarine was not surprised at all. He looked excited.

Tracey had kept his attention on the Magikarp. After studying them for a while, he spotted something too. "What are those rings around their tails?"

Ethan looked away from the old man. Many Magikarp had colored bands around the base of their tails. Some were red, some yellow, and some blue. Apart from their colors, the rings looked alike. Who had put them there?

The old man watched through binoculars for a time, then spoke to himself in a voice far more excited than his years suggested. "Targets confirmed! Speed and direction exactly as predicted. Excellent. Emergency dive! The pursuit begins!"

He closed the hatch and started the submarine again, following the Magikarp. They watched it move away until it disappeared from view.

"What was that about?" Misty said. "He rushed in and rushed off. I don't understand any of it!"

Tracey frowned, as though something had come back to him. "Wait. That direction..."

He took a map from his bag and checked the coordinates. His finger stopped on a small island. "That's it! The Magikarp are headed this way, and Rind Island lies right along their route."

"Rind Island?"

The name was new to Ethan, Ash, and Misty. Tracey, who came from the Orange Islands, explained. "As far as I remember, it's small and uninhabited. Hardly anyone knows about it, much less visits. I only heard my grandparents mention it a few times when I was little."

Tens of thousands of Magikarp and a mysterious old man in a submarine had caught Ash's interest. "Something must be happening. Let's go and see!"

Misty and Tracey agreed. They wanted to know what was going on too.

Ethan thought for a moment and decided his own journey could wait. He told Sabrina what they were doing and asked her to follow on Charizard. He stayed on Lapras with Ash and the others as they headed toward Rind Island.

Spray flew around them. Lapras avoided the Magikarp and fought to keep its balance in the disturbed water. On its back, Ash and his friends argued excitedly over why so many Magikarp had gathered. Their guesses grew less convincing by the minute.

Ethan listened without joining in. He had known that meeting Ash made it likely he would stumble into another adventure. It seemed to have happened again.

He did not mind. To Ethan, travel was made of unfamiliar experiences. Taking part in strange events was how people tested themselves and grew.

The constant splashing interrupted his thoughts. Magikarp were hardly impressive in an ordinary battle, but in the sea they could swim at a respectable speed. Even carrying so many passengers, Lapras worked hard to keep up. One by one, the Magikarp overtook it until they were following at the rear of the vast school.

Fortunately, after ten or fifteen minutes they could already see Rind Island in the distance. They would not lose their way now.
