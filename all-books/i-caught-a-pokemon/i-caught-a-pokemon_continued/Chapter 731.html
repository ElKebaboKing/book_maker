The white light dazzled Tate, Liza, their mother Rachel, and Misty in the stands. All four raised their hands to shield their eyes. Seven or eight seconds passed before the glare from Flash Cannon faded enough for them to look at the arena.

Another hanging planet had been blasted from the ceiling. Solrock and Lunatone were covered in injuries. The pressure of Ethan's attack had not even given them time to put up a Light Screen. They looked ready to fall, but both were still conscious. Their meteorite-like bodies were remarkably hard. It was their physical structure, not their types, that had let them endure this much.

Tate and Liza frowned at Milotic and Metang. Ethan was strong. That was their clearest impression of him.

His Pokémon were not impossibly powerful. The twins could see they were still growing and were roughly equal to Solrock and Lunatone in raw strength. Ethan's tactics, commands, and eye for the battle were what made the difference. His Pokémon did not even seem particularly practiced as a pair, but they followed every order faithfully. Under his direction, together they performed far beyond what the twins had expected.

He was unlike any challenger they had faced, but the match was not over. They still had a chance to turn it around.

They called their battered Pokémon back to their half of the field and decided to attack from a distance. That would give Solrock and Lunatone time to react if Milotic used Surf or Twister again, instead of being trapped as they had been before.

It was a sensible plan. The trouble came from their coordination.

They were twins who had run the Gym together for years, supposedly able to understand each other's thoughts. But when they launched another attack, their Pokémon's moves collided with each other halfway across the arena. Neither came close to threatening Metang or Milotic.

The twins immediately began to argue.

“You got in my way!”

“No, you got in mine!”

“Nonsense! You aimed from the wrong angle!”

“What are you talking about? I was supposed to attack Milotic, and you were covering Metang. You changed targets!”

“You never said that! We should each attack whichever one is closest!”

“Are you kidding? We agreed on this ages ago!”

They went red in the face, arguing heedless of the ongoing match or their audience. Ethan and Misty stared. Rachel pressed a hand to her forehead and sighed.

“Not again. Will you two stop? Isn't this embarrassing enough? You're in the middle of a Gym battle!”

Their mother's sharp reminder quieted them for a moment. Then they ordered another attack, and once again their Pokémon's moves ran into each other.

Now Ethan understood. The twins had worked well together in the early part of the match. He had assumed their coordination would last. Instead, as the fight went against them, it fell apart. Their composure had gone with it. Rachel's reaction showed this was hardly the first time.

The stories about their bond were exaggerated. Once their familiar opening tactics failed, especially against a stronger challenger, Tate and Liza began to interfere with each other. In matches against inexperienced Trainers, the weakness might never show. Against someone like Ethan, it emerged quickly.

No wonder the Pokémon League had appointed them together. Psychic twins made an intriguing draw, and their talent surpassed that of most children their age. But they were still too young to run a large Gym individually. Together they had a better chance of handling skilled challengers.

Ethan had heard enough arguing. When the twins began bickering again, he sent Milotic and Metang forward. Focused on each other, Tate and Liza missed the movement in the arena. Their exhausted Pokémon were knocked down almost at once.

Rachel shook her head, looking weary, but raised her referee's flags.

“Solrock and Lunatone are unable to battle! The winner of this Gym match is the challenger, Ethan!”

Neither Ethan nor Misty felt much joy at the announcement. The match had promised far more than its abrupt ending delivered. The twins had fought well at first. Once they lost the advantage, their performance had collapsed. Ethan felt as though the battle had ended just when he was beginning to enjoy it.

Still, a win was a win. He had another Badge. Only one remained before the Ever Grande Conference, and then his journey through Hoenn would be nearly finished.

Tate and Liza finally noticed their unconscious Pokémon and stopped arguing. They stared in disbelief until Rachel cleared her throat. The reminder brought them back to their duties. They recalled Solrock and Lunatone and approached Ethan, their expressions complicated.

“That was incredible,” Liza said, admiration in her voice. “Beside your Pokémon, ours hardly managed to work together. We barely had a chance to fight back.”

“You're one of the strongest challengers we've ever faced,” Tate added. “How did you get so good?”

Ethan shook his head and answered with an almost maddening modesty. “Travel more. See more. That's all.”

The twins looked at each other. They had become Gym Leaders at ten, achieving something many Trainers never managed in a lifetime. From the start, they occupied a position most people could only hope to reach. And this man was telling them to go out and travel?

Ethan could tell they were not eager. As Gym Leaders, they were already respected and had work they could keep for years. Challengers came to their door every day, giving them opportunities to train without leaving home.

Yet Ethan believed they ought to spend their energetic youth seeing the wider world. They could return to run a Gym after gaining more experience, rather than spending their whole lives here. Of course, people wanted different things. Perhaps the stability of Gym life was exactly what the twins preferred.

It was not his business. They had met only today. He had answered their question, but it was not his place to lecture strangers.

His advice did not seem to reach them. They had expected a special training method from someone so capable. Travel? They walked around the Gym's neighborhood often enough, and that had not produced results.

Rachel opened her mouth as though she wanted to support Ethan's suggestion that they travel. She looked at her children and remained silent.

After a pause, Liza seemed to remember something. “Tate, the Badge!”

“Oh! I nearly forgot too.” Glad for something else to say, Tate took a Badge from inside his clothes and offered it to Ethan. “This is our Mind Badge. It proves you've defeated us. Please accept it.”

Ethan thanked him and examined it. The Badge was heart-shaped, pink across the top and yellow below, with a red cylinder at its center. It had a distinctly girlish look. Whoever had designed it must have expected it to appeal to girls.

After turning it in his hand for a moment, he placed it in his Badge case. Misty came down from the stands. They gave the twins a final nod and left the Gym.

...

The Mossdeep Gym had been a brief stop, and one of the easier Gyms Ethan had challenged. What came next would matter far more.

At last, Shelly called him. Almost at the same time, a message came from Courtney. Their contact could mean only one thing: Archie and Maxie were alive, and after all their preparation, they had begun to move again. Those old enemies had even chosen nearly the same time to act. Was this some special understanding between rival leaders?

Thick black clouds spread over Mossdeep City, seeming to cover the entire sky. People stopped what they were doing and looked up in surprise. Such weather was deeply unusual for residents accustomed to sun and mild winds.

Ethan knew what it meant. As though the sky itself anticipated what was coming, the calm before the storm was ending. This would be the final confrontation. Archie, Maxie, Kyogre, and Groudon—everything would be settled in this crisis.

He would not go in unprepared. He took Misty to the Pokémon Center and adjusted his team. Several Pokémon that still needed training went to Cerise Park. He contacted Sabrina, Erika, and Chloe, and his strongest Pokémon returned from their care.

Misty felt the gravity of it too. She wanted to lighten Ethan's mood, but after opening her mouth several times, she could think of nothing to say.

“Don't be nervous.” Ethan slipped the returned Poké Balls into his pouch and smiled at her. “Act as you usually do. Remember, I've got this.”

By coincidence, the coordinates Shelly and Courtney had sent were different but both lay near Mossdeep, in the surrounding sea. That was good news: he could reach them in a few hours rather than spend days traveling.

Before leaving, he looked at Misty, who was still tense. “Would you rather wait here for me?”

“No.” She shook her head firmly and managed a bright smile. “I'm a Water-type Trainer. The sea is my territory too. I can help.”

“That's the spirit. I wouldn't expect less from my traveling partner.”

Ethan patted her head and released Dragonite. They climbed onto its back. It took off from outside the Pokémon Center and reached the sky above Mossdeep in a few wingbeats.

A passing Trainer flinched as Dragonite launched beside him. He was about to complain, but it had already carried the pair hundreds of meters up and vanished toward the dark clouds.

“Honestly,” he muttered after them. “What's the rush? Off to save the world?”
