With the whole stadium cheering, Ethan left the field through the competitors' tunnel.

Chloe, Misty, and the others were waiting near the exit. As soon as he appeared, they gathered around him, applauding and offering their congratulations.

Farther away, the biker gang held back several reporters and fans, refusing to let anyone through to bother him.

"That was an amazing battle!" Brock said. "Congratulations on winning—and on advancing. Now you and Ash are both in the Top 16!"

Ethan broke into a broad smile. Of course he was delighted to have reached the fifth round.

Speaking of Ash, though...

Ethan looked around. Professor Oak and Delia were standing nearby, but Ash was nowhere to be seen.

"Where's Ash?"

Misty covered her mouth with a smile. "After Gary left looking so dejected, Ash followed him with a complicated look on his face. I think he's trying to cheer him up."

They were rivals and friends from the beginning. Ash couldn't just leave Gary alone after a loss like that.

At the mention of Gary, Ethan turned to Professor Oak. "I'm sorry, Professor. I hope Gary isn't too upset about losing to me."

To his surprise, the Professor laughed and waved the apology away.

"It's all right. Every tournament has its losers. In Gary's case, I think this loss might actually be good medicine."

"Good medicine?" Chloe looked at him in confusion. His grandson had just been defeated, yet he did not seem worried at all.

"Yes," Professor Oak said. "Gary has been arrogant for a long time. Things have gone his way so often that he's learned to underestimate his opponents. I think losing here will help him understand that there are always people stronger than he is. It could do him a great deal of good as he grows."

Ethan nodded. Professor Oak understood his grandson well. As a respected scholar, he could look at the result rationally instead of siding with Gary simply because they were family.

In fact, the future would unfold much as the Professor had said. After losing at both the Indigo and Silver Conferences, Gary would change enormously. He would learn to respect others, become more modest and courteous, and discover what he truly wanted to do.

Those two losses would force him to see himself clearly. They would change the course of his life.

...

With every preliminary match finished, the sixteen remaining trainers were ready to compete in the main tournament for the championship.

First, they would have a short two-day break. The coming battles would be more difficult, so the trainers had time to rest, adjust their condition, and show the public their best performances.

Ethan made good use of it. With Misty and Brock's help, he put his Pokémon through focused training, hoping to keep his results steady as the competition grew harder.

Ash rested too. While he was out one day, he happened to meet another competitor named Richie.

Jessie, James, and Meowth, still as determined as ever to steal Ash's Pikachu, planned another attack. After some initial friction, Ash and Richie worked together to drive them off. By the end of it, the two had become friends.

When Ash brought Richie over and introduced him to the group, Ethan fell silent for a moment. Richie was about Ash's age, and even his clothes looked remarkably similar.

Were some events simply bound to happen, no matter what?

"I'm Richie. It's nice to meet you all."

Brock and the others warmly welcomed the boy, who also traveled with a Pikachu. As they talked, it became clear that despite the resemblance, Richie was quite different from Ash.

He was more polite and more composed. From Ash's account of what the two had been through, Richie also seemed able to keep his head in a crisis and think his way through a problem.

While everyone was talking, Chloe studied Richie for a while. Then she leaned toward Ethan and whispered, "Why does he feel like... like..."

"Like an upgraded version of Ash?" Ethan supplied.

Chloe nodded. Ethan glanced around to make sure nobody else was listening, then said quietly, "Watch him. He'll be Ash's greatest opponent in this tournament. If Ash doesn't handle it well, his Indigo League run could end right here."

"Really?"

Chloe looked at Richie again in surprise. She did not know how Ethan could be so certain. But she trusted him too much to question where he had gotten the idea.

When the break ended, Ethan and the other fifteen trainers arrived at the draw hall feeling refreshed.

The five counters that had stood there before were gone. In their place sat a large pool. It was shallow enough that they could see sixteen Magikarp swimming beneath the clear surface.

A staff member came forward to explain.

"Welcome, competitors! Beginning with the fifth round, all matches will be held in the main Indigo Stadium. The advancement rules have changed as well. The sixteen trainers will be divided into Groups A and B. Each group of eight will compete until one winner remains, and those two winners will meet in the final!"

She gestured toward the pool.

"This pool will decide your groups and matchups. Each Magikarp bears a number. You'll each use a fishing rod, and the number on the Magikarp you catch will determine where you compete."

As he listened, Ethan glanced around at the other trainers.

Besides himself, Ash, and Richie, only two people he knew had made the Top 16: Damian, the Beast Tamer, and Logan, whom he had met on New Island. The rest were unfamiliar faces.

Damian noticed him too and came over to say hello.

"You know, after beating two championship favorites in a row, you're the one everyone expects to win. For the rest of us, you're the biggest rival here!"

"Am I?"

Ethan had heard something like that already.

Looking over the Indigo League field, though, he didn't think it was simply that he was overwhelmingly strong. There just weren't many truly formidable trainers among the entrants.

That was why his path through the tournament had been so smooth.
