The Snowman Snorlax had not expected Gardevoir to find it then. The Blizzard had robbed her of sight and hearing, yet she still knew where it was. It had never encountered anything like this, and the discovery came at the worst possible moment.

Its opponent was even more troublesome than it had realized. There was no time to change course, so it drove its Ice Punch toward Gardevoir's face with all its strength. This was the closest it had come to landing a blow in the entire fight. It would not retreat now, even though she had seen it.

For an instant, everything seemed to slow. Wind roared, snow fell thickly, and the Snorlax's fist crept toward Gardevoir.

Only then did it see the strange, pale red light shining in her eyes. It had never fought an opponent like her and did not understand what that light meant. It only felt that those eyes saw through everything it might do.

Ethan could not see through the Blizzard, but his senses were linked to Gardevoir's by telepathy. It was almost as though he stood beside her. He could see the Snowman Snorlax too.

Future Sight. Now.

He gave the command silently. At once, an overwhelming force unlike anything Gardevoir had shown so far erupted before her and struck the Snorlax.

Boom!

A shock wave burst outward from the Snorlax. Then came a violent explosion. Snow already on the ground, flakes still falling, and even the driving wind were swept away. In less than a second, the Blizzard across the battlefield vanished.

With her view restored, May looked toward the center of the field. The Snowman Snorlax had been flung several meters into the air. It fell heavily onto the ice.

Thud!

May had no idea what had happened. Gardevoir stood unharmed, the faint red glow fading from her eyes, while the Snorlax lay where it had landed.

“What just happened?” May asked. “What was wrong with Gardevoir's eyes?”

“She used Future Sight.”

“Future Sight?”

May had little experience with Psychic-type Pokémon. She had never even heard of this move. Its name sounded impressive, though. Could it really let Gardevoir see the future?

Ethan seemed to read her thoughts. “It's an advanced Psychic-type move. Despite its name, it doesn't actually predict events. It marks an opponent for an attack that lands later. When that delayed effect is triggered, it deals a great deal of damage.”

May understood. “So even when the Blizzard hid it, the mark let Gardevoir know where the Snorlax was?”

“Exactly.”

The Snorlax did not rise. It appeared to be completely unconscious. Knowing the fight was over, Ethan took an empty Poké Ball from his belt pouch and threw it.

The ball arced over the Snorlax and snapped open. Red light drew the fallen Pokémon inside. The ball did not shake once before confirming the capture.

May ran over, picked it up, and brought it back to Ethan. He took it and bounced it lightly in his hand, delighted.

With the Snowman Snorlax and Entei, whom he had caught a few days earlier, Ethan now had twenty-six Pokémon.

Collectors—and even some ordinary Trainers—had more. But twenty-six Pokémon acquired over three regions was a respectable number. Except for Jigglypuff, every one of his Pokémon was formidable. He had both quality and quantity.

And, from a certain point of view, even Jigglypuff was frighteningly powerful.

“Well, we've done what we came to do. It's time to leave.”

Ethan put away the ball and recalled Gardevoir. Then he released Charizard, and he and May climbed onto its back. He glanced one last time toward Luka's camp, but she was nowhere in sight.

They did not linger. Charizard beat its wings and rose away from the iceberg.

Ethan checked the map and fixed their direction. Charizard flew northwest. Within a matter of seconds, the strange iceberg and its surrounding waters were far behind them.

At that moment, Luka looked up from the camp she was pitching elsewhere on the ice. She spotted their retreating figures, smiled, and waved.

Ecruteak City was an old city in northern Johto.

It stood among the forests between Mt. Mortar and the great plain. Everywhere were white walls, blue roof tiles, and connected courtyards. There were no high rises, only century-old houses that gave the city a quiet elegance.

Even many of the people on its streets wore traditional kimonos and walked at an unhurried pace.

After a day's travel and a night spent outdoors, Ethan and May arrived the following afternoon.

Charizard landed on a low hill beyond the city. Between red blossoms and green leaves, they could see much of Ecruteak. The Burned Tower and Bell Tower were visible on the far side.

May had never visited a large city built in this style. She stood taking in the view for some time. Ethan was already partway down the hill before he turned and beckoned her to follow.

Ethan had come for one purpose: to bring his Eevee back for a visit.

But he could not simply appear at the Kimono Sisters' door without warning. That was why he had asked Charizard to land outside the city. He took out his phone and called Miki, the eldest sister.

Someone answered promptly, but it was her manager.

“Mr. Vale? I'm sorry, Miss Miki and the others are performing onstage right now. The show will end soon. I'll ask her to call you back as soon as she comes offstage.”

Ethan had been right to call first. The five sisters had busy schedules every day. Without warning, it was hard to find a time to see them. They often performed in the evenings as well. Their programs changed from show to show; the legend of the three legendary beasts that Ethan had watched on his last visit had not been performed in a long while.

He relayed the message to May. She shook her head in disappointment.

“What a shame. If their show had only just started, I could have watched it too. I guess I'll have to wait until next time.”

They came down the hill and walked into the city, heading toward the sisters' home. May looked around as they went.

“Everything looks so old-fashioned,” she said with wonder.

Ethan nodded and told her about Bell Tower, Burned Tower, Ho-Oh, and the three legendary beasts.

They were about halfway to the house when Miki called back.

“Mr. Vale? I'm sorry my sisters and I were performing when you called. You're almost at our home already? Wonderful! We've missed Eevee too. I understand. Please wait a little while. We'll get ready and come straight back.”

He could hear her sisters chattering and occasionally interrupting in the background. They seemed surprised and excited that he had come.

When the call ended, May covered her mouth and laughed. “I didn't know you were so popular with them.”

“What can I say?” Ethan smiled and shrugged. “Someone as outstanding as me is a rare find.”

He had visited once before and could roughly remember the way. Following his memory, he led May through the streets.

As they reached the sisters' expansive compound, two expensive sedans stopped at its gate. The doors opened, and all five Kimono Sisters stepped out. They had not even had time to change out of their elaborate costumes.

May stared at them. “Those are the Kimono Sisters? They're all so beautiful! And those kimonos look expensive. They're gorgeous!”

Hearing the envy in her voice, Ethan shook his head. “Don't let the costumes fool you. Once they take off the makeup and change clothes, you'll find that they're…still beautiful.”

May rolled her eyes at the little joke. Ethan laughed. “When you have a chance to dress up like that, you'll look just as beautiful. Come on, let's catch them before they go inside.”

They crossed at the crosswalk. The sisters soon noticed them.

Sayo, the youngest, had been looking around ever since she heard Ethan was coming. She was the first to spot them. “Sisters! Look! It's Mr. Vale!”

The other four turned together. Their different temperaments showed in their reactions.

Miki, the eldest, Kuni, the second, and Sayo walked over to greet Ethan. Zuki, the third sister who had challenged him to a battle on his first visit, stayed where she was. Perhaps she was naturally reserved, or perhaps she still felt dissatisfied about that encounter. Naoko, the shy fourth sister, remained beside her. The two watched from a distance.

Ethan greeted them all with May at his side. “Miss Miki, Miss Kuni, Miss Zuki, Miss Naoko, Miss Sayo—it's good to see you again.”

May nodded to them. “It's nice to meet you. I'm May, Ethan's traveling companion.”

Traveling companion?

The sisters exchanged glances. None of them spoiled the mood by asking where Erika had gone.

Ethan released Eevee. The moment it saw the five women, it cried out with delight and bounded toward them.

“Eevee!”

“It's so good to see you again!”

“You hardly seem to have changed at all!”

Four of the sisters crouched at once, stroking Eevee and looking it over with obvious joy. Zuki gave it a glance, then turned her attention back to Ethan.

“Mr. Vale, I heard you won another League championship in Hoenn?”

Her cool tone made the atmosphere awkward. Ethan met her gaze without flinching.

“That's right. And there's more. This time I'm back to challenge the Battle Frontier. I've already beaten six of the Frontier Brains. One more victory and I'll have another honor to add to my record.”

Miki sensed the tension and quickly rose. She gave Zuki a look, warning her to be quiet, then smiled at Ethan.

“You must be tired after such a long journey. I'll see to your rooms and dinner. Later, you must tell us how Eevee has been doing.”

At their invitation, Ethan and May entered the courtyard.

As on his first visit, the sisters' wealth impressed Ethan. There was a pond crossed by a small bridge, with a pavilion and other buildings along its banks. Covered walkways joined the large, Japanese-style house beneath its blue tiled roofs. Cherry trees grew in a corner of the grounds. Every detail showed the sisters' resources and taste.

Miki brought the two guests to a sitting room and made tea. Then she excused herself to remove her makeup and change clothes with her sisters.

For Eevee, this really was a homecoming. It ran about, exploring surroundings both familiar and a little strange after so much time away.

May released her own Eevee to keep Ethan's company. After watching them roam, she took a sip of tea and asked, “How did you get to know Sayo and her sisters?”

Her curiosity was understandable. Ethan was a Trainer; the five women were famous stage performers. It was hard to imagine how their paths had crossed.

Ethan's thoughts drifted back to their first meeting. He gave May a brief account of Eevee running wild in the Ecruteak Dance Theater and the events that followed.

As he spoke, he also remembered Erika traveling at his side back then. It felt as if it had been only yesterday.
