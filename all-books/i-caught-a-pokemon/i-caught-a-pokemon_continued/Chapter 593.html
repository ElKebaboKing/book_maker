“What are you talking about?” The boy did not seem to hear Ethan's mockery at all. “Do you know how hard I worked to get close to those Zigzagoon? Look at this outfit. It took me ages to make!”

Ethan had the impression that the boy was not terribly bright.

Who tried to approach Zigzagoon like this? He was not a Pokémon Watcher. Even if they failed to spot him, what would he do once he got close? He would still need to battle them to catch one.

Besides, it was hardly Ethan's fault. The Zigzagoon had moved away when he arrived. He had not driven them off.

If even one of them had tried to stay or defend the clearing, Ethan would not have pitched his tent here.

Shaking his head, Ethan said, “Instead of wasting time arguing with me, you could go after them. They may not have gone far.”

The boy did not leave. He looked at Torchic beside Ethan.

“You're a Trainer too, aren't you?” he called. “Then battle me!”

“Battle you?”

Ethan looked him over. The boy wore every thought on his face. His mood changed in an instant, and whatever came into his head came out of his mouth. He could hide nothing.

There was no point getting angry with someone like that. He would drag Ethan down to his level and beat him with experience. But words alone were unlikely to make him go away.

“You really are persistent.” Ethan rose from his folding chair and walked to an open patch of ground. “I suppose you won't leave until we've battled.”

The boy's irritation vanished the moment Ethan agreed. “Great! Let's start!”

He took out a Poké Ball, aimed it at the ground, and pressed the button. A flash of blue light released a Mudkip in front of Ethan.

Despite its fishlike appearance, Mudkip was no fish at all.

One of Hoenn's three starter Pokémon, it was every bit as popular as Torchic. It looked soft and slippery, like an amphibious mud salamander from Earth.

Mudkip had slick blue skin and a small, nimble body. An upright fin rose from its head, bright orange gills marked its cheeks, and its tail fin was enormous.

It usually rested buried in the mud of a riverbed. In water, it breathed through its cheek gills. The fin on its head was a sensitive radar, detecting movements in water and air without relying on sight.

It was remarkably strong. It could swim at great speed and push against the ground with all four legs. When threatened, its muscles alone could break a rock larger than its body.

This Mudkip looked as though it had not been traveling long. It was still young and had not evolved into Marshtomp.

Then again, like Ash's Pikachu, it might have chosen not to evolve despite having great strength.

Strong or weak, one battle would tell Ethan what he needed to know.

After a moment's thought, he took a Poké Ball from his belt pouch and sent out Eevee.

The opposing Pokémon did not look especially formidable. Eevee should be able to handle it.

The two Pokémon took their positions, with Ethan and the boy standing behind their partners.

Personality shaped battle style. At the start, the boy threw himself into commanding Mudkip. At his order, it charged Eevee headfirst.

“Eevee, dodge!”

Ethan knew Mudkip was strong and had no intention of letting it land that hit. Eevee slipped aside, then counterattacked.

It twisted clear of Mudkip's rush, bared two rows of sharp teeth, and bit into its opponent.

Ethan did not know what Mudkip tasted like, but from its pained expression he knew Eevee had bitten hard.

“Shake it off!” the boy shouted.

Mudkip thrashed, but Eevee held on. However much Mudkip twisted, it could not break free.

At last Eevee let go on its own, giving Mudkip a moment to breathe.

Seeing that Eevee was faster, the boy changed tactics. At his command, Mudkip fired a Water Gun from farther away.

The stream curved through the air toward Eevee.

Eevee was a little faster than Mudkip, but it could not normally move quickly enough to dodge a Water Gun. On its own, it would be hit.

Ethan had an answer.

“Eevee, Quick Attack!”

Eevee accelerated sharply, slipped past the water just before it struck, and rammed into Mudkip again.

Mudkip staggered backward. Without waiting for another command, it scooped up a clump of mud and hurled it at Eevee.

It had understood the problem too. To win, it needed to slow Eevee down.

But the movement was obvious. Ethan saw it coming and would not let the attack land.

He had Eevee dodge calmly, then use Swift. The stars struck Mudkip and knocked it down.

“Huh?” The boy rubbed his eyes at the sight of Mudkip fallen. He seemed unable to believe he had lost so easily.

For Ethan, after all the high-level tournaments he had entered and the earthshaking battles he had seen, this was a beginner's match.

It was not arrogance. The battle simply had not been hard. His opponent's attacks were straightforward, and Ethan could predict nearly every move.

The boy recalled Mudkip and studied Ethan with new admiration.

“You're amazing. What's your name?”

Ethan shook his head. By now he could see that the boy was simple and spoke without thinking, often offending people, but meant no harm.

“Shouldn't you introduce yourself before asking for someone else's name?”

The boy paused, then nodded vigorously. “Oh! You're right. Listen up, then! Sometimes I'm a Zigzagoon running through the forest! Sometimes I'm a Tentacruel with lots of tentacles! Other times I'm a Bellossom with magnificent dance steps! But my true self is Nicholai, the boy known as the Shorts Boy!”

Zigzagoon? Tentacruel? Bellossom? Shorts Boy?

As he spoke, Nicholai pulled one costume after another from the huge pack on his back and changed at astonishing speed. Ethan felt his temples begin to throb.

This was exactly the kind of person he least wanted to deal with.

He could feel his blood pressure rising.

He could not follow Nicholai's pace or way of thinking, much less understand what went on in his head.

Without answering, Ethan turned and walked toward the camp.

Nicholai stared after him for a moment, then called, “Wait! You still haven't told me your name!”

Ethan kept walking. “When did I say I would tell you? Now please leave us alone. Goodbye.”

“But...” Nicholai's mouth fell open. “But you said I should introduce myself before asking yours!”

“That's right.” Ethan glanced back with narrowed eyes. “I never said I'd introduce myself.”

“I...”

Nicholai started to reply, then thought about it. Ethan really had not promised anything.

He was about to follow, but Ethan's cold expression plainly said he wanted no company. Nicholai stopped awkwardly.

He was not so oblivious that he could miss such clear distance. He could hardly keep pestering Ethan now.

It was a shame, though. Someone that strong, and he would never learn his name.

Well, no use dwelling on it. He would think of another way to get close to the Zigzagoon.

By the time Ethan returned to camp, Erika had finished dinner.

The rich smell of curry rose from the metal pot. It made his mouth water and his stomach growl.

Erika smiled gently. “All done?”

“Mm.” Ethan sat beside her and sighed. “A simple-minded fool whose mouth keeps getting him into trouble. That's exactly the sort of person I hate dealing with. Better to keep my distance.”

Seeing how determined Ethan was to avoid Nicholai, Erika could not help laughing.

He feared no Pokémon, yet someone with that personality made him want to run away. She had not expected that.

...

It was noon the next day when they left Petalburg Woods.

While they were there, Erika had found a Seedot and a Shroomish. Both were Grass-type Pokémon with no habitats in Kanto, so she had caught them both.

In a short time in Hoenn, Erika too had added two Pokémon to her team. Both she and Ethan had gained plenty.

They continued north. Before long, Rustboro City came into view.

Rustboro was a city that sought to bring nature and science together.

A major city in western Hoenn, it was much larger than Petalburg in population, size, and economy. It had a Pokémon school, and several world-famous companies were based there.

Before they reached the outskirts, they saw three hooded figures in matching red uniforms kick a man in a suit to the ground, snatch his briefcase, and run down the road toward them.

Huh? A robbery?

One of them saw Ethan standing in their way. His eyes hardened. “Move if you don't want to die! Get out of our way!”

Behind them, the man in the suit shouted, “Catch them! They stole my briefcase!”

There was no time to think. The three men were already in front of Ethan. He silently took one step back.

They thought he had decided not to interfere. But as they congratulated themselves, his shadow rippled. A long arm emerged, stretching across their path like a rope.

Unable to stop, all three tripped over it and fell headfirst onto the road.

They scrambled up, furious, and glared at Ethan.

“You dare get in our way?”

One threw a Poké Ball. In a flash of light, a Houndoom appeared and immediately sprang at Ethan with its jaws open.

Ethan kept one hand in his pocket. He did not move.

The Houndoom leaped, its mouth wide and foul-smelling saliva dripping from its teeth. Just before it could bite him, Ethan's shadow rippled again. Two huge claws rose from it and caught Houndoom in midair.

“What?”

The three men finally looked at the ground beneath Ethan. Behind the claws, Gengar floated slowly out of the shadow with a mischievous grin.

Gengar cackled. “You thought it would be that easy to touch him? You're underestimating us.”

“A... a talking Pokémon?” The men were even more shocked.

The suited man had caught up, panting. Apparently wary of the three men's strength, he stayed a short distance away and called to Ethan. “Sir, I work for the Devon Corporation. These people grabbed my briefcase without warning. There's something important inside. Please help me get it back!”

Devon Corporation?

Ethan nodded in understanding. He studied the three men again, his gaze lingering on their red uniforms and hoods.

He knew which story this was.

Those matching, attention-grabbing clothes belonged to one of Hoenn's two famous criminal organizations: Team Magma.

Though it was called a criminal organization, Team Magma was not evil in quite the way Team Rocket was.

Its members operated mainly in Hoenn. They believed humanity's development was tied to the happiness of the world as a whole, and that living alongside Pokémon hindered human progress. Their goal was to expand the land and give people more room to live.

Finding the Legendary Pokémon Groudon was one of their most important aims.
