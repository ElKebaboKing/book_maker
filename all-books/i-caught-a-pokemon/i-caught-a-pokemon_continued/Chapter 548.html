Because they had chosen their Pokémon without knowing what the other Trainer would send out, neither side could predict the matchup.

No one knew what kind of opponent they would face.

For Travis, the problem was that Kingler was a pure Water type. Dragonite did not have a type advantage over it, but Kingler's Water-type attacks would deal only half their usual damage to a Dragon type.

That was not good news.

As the battle began, Kingler hurried toward Dragonite. Dragonite took off and rose into the air.

Kingler could not reach it, so Travis called, "Kingler, use Water Gun!"

He knew a Water-type move would do little to Dragonite. But Water Gun was one of Kingler's few ranged attacks, and on an open field it was one of the few options Travis could rely on.

Hitting a flying target was difficult. Dragonite rolled lightly aside, and the long stream of water missed.

"Dragonite, use Dragon Rush!"

At Ethan's command, Dragonite glowed faintly blue-green. It circled in the air a few times, then dived toward Kingler.

"Protect!"

Travis shouted at once. He knew Kingler was not fast enough to dodge Dragon Rush, but he had another way to meet it.

Kingler raised both claws before its chest. A strange light surrounded its body just as Dragonite slammed into it with tremendous force. Kingler skidded several meters, yet when it stopped, it did not have a scratch.

That was the power of Protect. Ethan had not expected one blow to bring Kingler down, however.

Before Kingler could steady itself, Ethan pressed the attack.

"Don't give it time to recover. Use Thunder Punch!"

Dragonite was versatile at close range. Alongside the Normal-type Slam and Water-type Aqua Tail, it could use both Fire Punch and Thunder Punch. Ethan usually favored Thunder Punch over Fire Punch.

The two punches were equally powerful, but Thunder Punch could paralyze its target. Ethan liked that extra effect.

A moment's paralysis could change the course of a battle.

"Kingler, use Crabhammer!"

Kingler was strong in close combat too. Seeing Dragonite come in rather than retreat, Travis did not hesitate.

Kingler stepped forward and swung its two uneven claws. They collided with Dragonite's electrically charged fists, and the Pokémon traded blows.

In raw power, Crabhammer surpassed Thunder Punch. Kingler's enormous claw could even land a critical hit.

But Ethan's plan paid off. After the two Pokémon had exchanged several attacks, Thunder Punch paralyzed Kingler. An electric jolt ran through its body, and just as it tried to move, it froze.

That was the opening Ethan had been waiting for.

"Dragonite, Aqua Tail, then Hyper Beam!"

Water wrapped around Dragonite's tail. It spun and struck Kingler, knocking it into the air. At the same time, energy gathered in Dragonite's mouth. The instant Kingler landed, Dragonite opened wide and fired a thick beam that hit it squarely.

The smooth combination knocked Kingler to the ground.

The referee raised a colored flag. Only then did Travis seem to realize what had happened.

A moment ago, the two Pokémon had looked evenly matched as they traded blows on the ground. How had Kingler lost so suddenly?

After his surprise faded, Travis sighed, returned Kingler, and walked over with a wry smile to shake Ethan's hand.

"I lost..."

"You fought well." Ethan patted his shoulder. "But like I told you, my Dragonite is strong too."

Ethan could have kept Dragonite in the air and beaten Travis easily with ranged attacks. But he had met Luana and knew Travis had traveled far from home to pursue his dream. He liked the boy and did not want to crush his confidence, so he had met Kingler head-on instead.

It had carried some risk, but the result was good enough.

With his first match won, Ethan returned Dragonite and left the field.

Erika and Misty came over to meet him.

"Congratulations, Ethan! Only two matches left before you advance," Misty said first.

As a Gym Leader, Erika had noticed more about the battle. She looked toward Travis and said approvingly, "That boy is strong too."

Ethan nodded. She was right.

Travis had real skill. Ethan had seemed to hold a large advantage and win with relative ease, but the battle had shown him that his own strength made the difference. Against another Trainer, Travis might well have won.

If Travis maintained that level in his remaining matches, he should have no trouble advancing.

Ethan sat on a nearby bench to collect himself and wait for his second battle.

He had drawn Group A's fourth match. After watching the third, he was called back to the field.

This opponent was an older man who looked like an employee at a large company. He wore thick round glasses and had a dull expression, as though life had worn him down and he hoped a Pokémon tournament might revive his lost enthusiasm.

The man sent out Houndoom. Ethan chose Tyranitar.

But...

Ethan had no idea how long ago the man had earned his badges. The moment they began, it was clear that he had not trained his Pokémon in a long time. His Houndoom was nowhere near Travis's Kingler in strength; it was below even the standard of an ordinary Trainer's Pokémon.

Ethan won easily. Once he understood the difference between them, he barely gave any commands for the rest of the match.

Tyranitar was merciless. It showed its fiercest side, battering Houndoom until it howled again and again.

In the end, even Ethan could not bear to watch and turned his head away.

Only after facing this office worker did Ethan truly understand why the Pokémon League held the screening trials before the main tournament. They filtered out entrants who were only making up the numbers, leaving more exciting battles for the spectators.
