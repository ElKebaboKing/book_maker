May had not expected a place as ancient-looking as the Battle Palace to have such advanced technology. Still, the referee robot would make things convenient.

“If that's how we watch the battle,” she asked, “how do we give our Pokémon commands?”

Spenser stroked his long beard with a chuckle. “We don't. In a forest this vast, they must win without their Trainers directing them. Their own abilities will decide the battle!”

No commands? The Pokémon would fight as they pleased?

Ethan touched his chin. Why did that sound so familiar? Wait. Wasn't that exactly what he was good at?

With May's questions answered, Spenser was about to head for the Trainers' area on the other side of the arena. Ethan stopped him.

“One moment, Mr. Spenser.”

Spenser turned. “What is it?”

“It's rare to find a battlefield this large. Could we change the rules a little?” Ethan asked with a smile.

Spenser and May both looked at him in surprise. Change the rules? Did Ethan think the challenge was too difficult? May could hardly believe that.

“Of course,” Spenser said. “What did you have in mind?”

Ethan glanced at the broad expanse below the mountain. “On such a large, complicated battlefield, sending out only one Pokémon apiece would be dull. Why play three separate rounds? Let's send out all three at once and let them fight throughout the forest. Whichever side still has Pokémon standing at the end wins the challenge.”

Some people would have recognized the idea immediately: the famous survival format. The teams would battle across difficult terrain until one side was left standing. It suited this setting perfectly.

Spenser blinked in surprise. “But that means committing everything at once. You'd have no chance to change your lineup.”

“All the better.” Ethan spread his hands. “It would test the Pokémon both individually and as a team. Isn't that fitting for a battle in the wild?”

Spenser considered the proposal, then nodded. “If you've thought it through, I have no objection.”

With the rules settled, they took their places in the opposing Trainers' areas.

As the Frontier Brain, Spenser released his Pokémon first. He drew three Poké Balls from his clothes and threw them together. Blue light flashed, and Shiftry, Claydol, and Venusaur appeared on the arena.

Only a moment later, Ethan threw his own three Poké Balls. Scizor, Metagross, and Dragonite emerged.

Dragonite and Scizor had long been mainstays of Ethan's teams in Johto and Hoenn. They might not have reached the very pinnacle of strength, but they could compete at the level of League champions. Metagross had grown quickly too and was still improving. By sending all three out together, Ethan made clear his determination to win this forest battle.

Metagross remained as cold and impassive as a stone. Dragonite and Scizor, however, took on a fiercer air the instant they appeared and glared at their opponents.

Shiftry and Venusaur glared back. Even so, beside Dragonite's enormous eyes and comically intense expression, they seemed to lose something in the contest of intimidation.

The flying referee robot moved to the center of the arena. Once both sides were ready, its starting light changed from red to green.

All six Pokémon moved at once.

With so many combatants and so much ground to cover, there was little use calculating type advantages now.

None of the Pokémon was particularly fast, but Dragonite had wings. It reached Spenser's side of the arena first, dodged two lashes of Venusaur's Vine Whip, and swooped low into Shiftry, knocking it toward the cliff.

Shiftry managed to grab Dragonite as it was sent flying. Instead of tumbling over the edge, it was carried with Dragonite out above the distant forest.

Scizor and Venusaur clashed next. Neither Razor Leaf nor Vine Whip could get past Scizor's huge pincers. The closer Scizor came, the more trouble Venusaur was in.

Claydol moved to help, but Metagross struck with psychic power before it could intervene. Claydol had to deal with its own opponent.

As promised, neither Trainer interfered. Ethan, Spenser, and May could only watch.

They did not all watch in the same way. Ethan stood with his hands in his pockets, calmly observing as if the battle had nothing to do with him. May and Spenser followed it with fierce concentration.

Ethan had asked May to watch from his point of view and see what she could learn. She kept her eyes wide, unwilling to miss a detail that might help her in her own battles.

Spenser had never presided over a battle quite like this one. Six Pokémon were fighting across an enormous arena. Energetic as he was, it was difficult for a man his age to keep up with all of them at once.

Ethan watched with easy trust. May and Spenser watched with tension.

With no boundary to keep it on the hilltop, Venusaur chose not to stand and struggle against Scizor. It turned and ran down the mountain into the forest. That was its own territory; a Grass-type Pokémon could draw more strength from fighting among the trees.

Scizor pursued it without hesitation.

Only two or three minutes after the start, Metagross and Claydol were the last Pokémon left in the starting arena. Before long, their own fight carried them off the summit too.

Ethan, May, and Spenser stood alone. From the forest below came the occasional sound of battle.

Now the referee robot came into its own. It flew down into the trees, and a separate screen beside the Trainers showed the view from its camera.

There was only one robot. It could not follow every fight, so the screen showed only Metagross and Claydol, the last pair to leave the hilltop. At least the three spectators could focus on one contest.

Onscreen, the two Pokémon floated among the trees, weaving through the forest and trading attacks as they went.

Metagross was Steel and Psychic type; Claydol was Ground and Psychic type. Neither pairing gave an obvious overall advantage. Either could win on paper. In a real battle, though, their condition, composure, and experience mattered more than a calculation of types.

Spenser's Claydol was strong. So was Ethan's Metagross, hardened by Pokémon League competition. Without either Trainer directing them, they fought to a standstill. Of the three clashes taking place across the forest, this was the closest.

Ethan could not see Dragonite or Scizor. But explosions from their moves rang through the trees, followed now and then by the crash of falling trunks. He was confident both of his Pokémon had the upper hand. He knew what they could do.

Then an explosion thundered across almost the entire island. They turned to see a brilliant light flare in one part of the forest, bathing a great stretch of trees in green.

None of the three was new to battling. They could guess what had happened.

“Was that two moves colliding?” May asked.

“Probably Venusaur's Solar Beam meeting Scizor's X-Scissor,” Ethan said.

Solar Beam and X-Scissor? May knew both moves, and knew how far apart their raw power was. That Scizor could use X-Scissor to cancel a Solar Beam astonished her. Bug-type moves were strong against Grass types, but the matchup alone could not account for what she had seen. Scizor's own strength was unmistakable.

Spenser's eyes widened slightly too. He looked at Ethan again. He did not follow news from elsewhere closely and knew nothing of the young man's past. He could still sense the tremendous power of Ethan's Pokémon.

What a remarkable young Trainer. No wonder he had been confident enough to send all three Pokémon out at once and leave them to fight without orders. The challengers Spenser had met before seemed to be on another level entirely.

The area where the light had flared fell silent. One Pokémon must have been defeated. Most likely it was Spenser's Venusaur.

It had happened quickly. Only a dozen or so minutes had passed. In an ordinary arena, that might have been a fight of average length. On this sprawling battlefield, with all the travel through difficult terrain, it felt as though the battle had barely begun.

Before Spenser could reflect further, another great blast sounded. The three looked toward the coast, where Dragonite and Shiftry had been fighting.

That clash had been every bit as fierce as Scizor's. A wide swath of trees had fallen, and spray rose from the sea. Then, just as before, the noise stopped after the explosion. From the hilltop, no one could see what had happened.

Ethan expected Dragonite had won. He was not underestimating Spenser's Pokémon. He simply knew that Dragonite and Scizor had earned his trust.

May's delight showed on her face. Spenser quietly looked away and returned his attention to the screen. Metagross and Claydol were now the only pair still fighting.

Perhaps their struggle was too loud, or perhaps they had crossed into another Pokémon's territory. In the midst of their battle, a swarm of Beedrill burst from the trees and attacked both of them indiscriminately.

The interruption took all three spectators by surprise. Spenser shook his head with a rueful smile.

“Well, that's unfortunate. The wild Pokémon here are very territorial. Usually they're an obstacle for challengers. Today I've brought the trouble on myself, and my own Pokémon is their target. There's some irony in that.”

Ethan could picture how it normally worked. Beedrill lived throughout the forest and attacked intruders, adding another unpredictable hazard to an already complicated battlefield. Today three fights were taking place at once, with explosions powerful enough to anger the swarms. They had come out to find whoever was responsible, regardless of whose territory the fighters were in.

The battle had become more difficult. Individually, the Beedrill were not especially strong, but there were enough of them to make the swarm alarming.

Metagross and Claydol might have looked more like stone and clay than ordinary animals or people, but they were not foolish. Neither stayed to face so many Beedrill. Both broke away and fled back along the route they had taken through the forest.
