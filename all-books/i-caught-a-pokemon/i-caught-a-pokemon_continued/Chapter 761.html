Misty and May left the party and followed a stone path to the shore. Dim streetlights stretched their shadows long across the ground. Behind them, more fireworks rose and lit the night sky. Waves broke against the rocks with a steady rush.

May could not keep her concern to herself any longer. “What's wrong today? You seem unhappy.”

Misty stopped and faced her seriously. “May, what will you do after the Grand Festival?”

May did not know why she was asking. “I haven't decided exactly,” she said after a moment, “but I think I'll keep working toward becoming a better Coordinator.”

Misty looked as though that was the answer she had expected. “Why do you ask?” May said.

“Could you do me a favor?” Misty's solemn voice made May's smile fade.

“We're friends. Whatever it is, tell me.”

Misty took a deep breath, as if she were entrusting May with a dream of her own. “After the Ever Grande Conference, will you keep traveling with Ethan in my place?”

“W-what!?” May could hardly believe her ears. “You want me to travel with Ethan? Why?”

Misty turned toward the sea, looking forlorn. Night hid the water from view; only the sound of the waves reached them.

“I don't have much choice. When the Conference ends, I have to return to Kanto and take over the Cerulean Gym. You know how popular Ethan is. People like Lisia are always finding ways to get close to him. I can't stop worrying.”

May understood at once. Misty wanted her to keep an eye on Ethan.

“Well... but...”

May instinctively felt it was inappropriate. She and Ethan were good friends, nothing more. Watching him on Misty's behalf and keeping other girls away went beyond the place a friend ought to claim in his life. Traveling with him normally would be one thing; going with that purpose was another. What if Ethan misunderstood her? What if he came to dislike her?

Her better judgment told her to refuse. Yet when she met Misty's earnest, hopeful gaze, the words would not come.

Misty knew what troubled her. Still, May was the only person she could ask. Without Misty there, she could all too easily picture what would happen the next time Ethan met Lisia. She could not bear to watch Lisia take him away.

“May, this is my once-in-a-lifetime request. You're my best friend. Please say yes.”

May held her gaze for a long while. At last she nodded. “All right. I'll travel with Ethan, and I'll look out for you.”

Relief flooded Misty's face. She smiled again. “Thank you, May! I knew I could count on you!”

By the time they returned arm in arm, the party had ended. Ethan and the others were waiting for them, surprised to see the two girls so close together.

“May,” Max asked, “where did you go? What took so long?”

Misty lifted her chin before May could answer. “I'm not telling you!”

“What's with the secrecy?” Ash muttered. “It's probably just girl talk. I don't want to hear it anyway.”

At the words girl talk, May glanced guiltily at Ethan. He caught the look and wondered why she was watching him. Had they really been talking about him?

...

They had spent several days in Slateport City. With the Grand Festival over, it was time to leave for Ever Grande City.

Ever Grande lay to the east of Hoenn's mainland, a scenic coastal town on a large island covered in flowers. Its greatest claim to fame was the Ever Grande Conference and the Hoenn Pokémon League, both held there. Like Silver Town, it had grown and prospered around a major tournament.

Flying from Slateport would have been quickest, but Ethan was the only one in the group who could easily afford it. The others were young, and there was no need to spend the money. A passenger ship sailed directly to Ever Grande. It would take longer, but the Conference was still more than ten days away.

When they gathered at the docks the next morning, Jessie and James appeared disguised as salespeople, apparently preparing some new trick for Ash. They spotted Ethan with him, startled, and scurried off. Ash and his friends failed to recognize the pair and wondered why the salespeople had run away.

Ethan only shook his head. He had become a monster in Team Rocket's eyes.

After about two days at sea, they reached Ever Grande City. The excitement was palpable as soon as they disembarked. Visitors and competitors had arrived in great numbers despite the days remaining before the opening ceremony. Even Ethan and Ash felt their nerves tighten. The Conference was finally close.

The city occupied little land, but its modern buildings rose high and stood close together. In that respect, it could hold its own against much larger cities.

Ethan and Ash needed to confirm their eligibility at the registration center. At May's suggestion, Misty and the others headed instead to the Ever Grande Noodle Nosh, a ramen shop she was eager to visit. May said its ramen ranked among the three best bowls in Hoenn. That was enough to interest the group.

Ethan remembered that May loved food. “Food enthusiast” was the polite phrase. If a person or Pokémon stole something from her plate, though, her anger could transform her. Good food mattered that much to her.

Ash wanted to join the ramen outing, but registration came first. He watched the others leave, humming happily, then trudged after Ethan toward the Conference grounds.

The venue stood in the hills beyond the city, arranged around a lake. Facilities occupied the shore and connected by walkways to the central arena, forming a web of paths across the water. The grounds were broad, and people crowded nearly every part of them. Ethan and Ash wondered why so many had come early.

They went straight to registration. Entries remained open until just before the opening ceremony, but most Trainers registered well ahead of that deadline unless something delayed them.

Ethan had entered in advance at the Fortree Pokémon Center, so he only needed to verify his qualifications. He waited while Ash registered, then they accepted Conference guidebooks from Nurse Joy.

Despite the name, the booklets offered little help with battling beyond the schedule. Most pages covered food and restaurants. May would probably appreciate them.

Like the Indigo League and Silver Conference, this tournament would have a ceremonial flame relay. Nurse Joy told them it was happening today. Ash forgot all about ramen and wanted to see it at once. Ethan was curious too. He sent Misty a message, and the two left registration for the relay route.

Before they reached the venue gate, they saw a short elderly man step out of a black car. Ethan recognized him. Ash was already hurrying over.

“President Goodshow! It's been a long time!”

Charles Goodshow turned at the sound of Ash's voice and spotted Ethan behind him. “Ash! Ethan! Are you entering the Ever Grande Conference too?”

“That's right!” Ash said.

Ethan came up beside him. “Good to see you again, President Goodshow.”

Goodshow nodded. “I'm glad you're both here. I'm sure you'll give us splendid matches. Ethan, I expect you're aiming for another championship?”

“Who isn't?” Ethan smiled. “I'll give it my best. Whatever result that brings, I'll accept it.”

It was a careful answer. He had made his ambition clear without saying anything that would annoy the other competitors if repeated. Goodshow gave him a knowing look.

“Then I wish you both good results. I have business to attend to, so I'll leave you here.”

They knew how busy he was. After saying goodbye, they watched him go and continued toward the flame relay.

Goodshow entered an office building at the venue and went to a small conference room. Smoke hung in the air. Every organizer responsible for the tournament had gathered, smoking in gloomy silence. They rose when he arrived.

“Sit down.” Goodshow took the chair at the head of the table. His usual jovial manner vanished. “Tell me. What are we going to do?”

Almost everyone looked past him at the screen on the wall. It showed Ethan lifting the trophy at the previous Silver Conference. Silence gave way to uneasy discussion.

“So he really entered Ever Grande?”

“With his strength, won't he win the whole thing again?”

“I heard he challenged Hoenn's Gyms with a different team. Will he still be as strong as before?”

“Who knows? But if he wins with that team too, we might as well hand him the trophy from now on and forget running the tournament!”

None of it answered Goodshow's question. He rapped the table, silencing the room. “I asked for solutions, not complaints.”

The officials looked at one another. At last one spoke timidly. “There isn't much we can do. We can't forbid him from competing.”

Arguments broke out at once.

“Of course not! We've never barred someone like that since the tournaments began!”

“Our credibility would collapse if people found out!”

“Then what can stop him?”

Only one man remained silent, smoking with a faintly mocking smile. He was John, the live broadcast director at both the Indigo League and the Silver Conference.

John looked around the table and sneered inwardly. No one here understood ratings and audience demand better than he did. Keep Ethan out? John would be the first to object. Even Goodshow's wish to find a way to prevent another title was misguided.

These officials talked endlessly about the tournament's development. They overlooked ratings and reputation. A competitor like Ethan drew viewers by himself. Those viewers made the broadcast—and the Conference—a success. Let the others rack their brains; they would find no sensible way to stop him.

John shook his head, stubbed out his cigarette, crossed one leg over the other, and looked back up at the photograph.

It really was a good picture of Ethan: confidence, a little rebelliousness, and just a hint of arrogance. He seemed to be looking down on everyone in the room.
