Forretress, the evolved form of Pineco, looked as though it were wrapped in hard armor. It had no limbs. A narrow gap ran along the middle of its red shell, revealing its eyes, while four tube-like projections extended from its sides.

It floated in midair. With such an odd shape and no obvious features of a Bug-type Pokémon, it looked more like a mixture of rock and steel.

Wesley had the type advantage. Venusaur's Grass-type moves, in particular, would do very little to Forretress. That gave him the mistaken impression that victory was already his.

Once the battle began, though, he gradually realized something was wrong.

Under Ethan's direction, Venusaur opened with Tackle and followed it with Sleep Powder. The powder did not put Forretress to sleep, but it made the Pokémon falter and left an opening.

Venusaur seized the chance to use Leech Seed and Growth, strengthening itself while drawing energy from its opponent.

When Ethan saw the right moment, he ordered Double-Edge. Venusaur knocked Forretress down, bound it with Vine Whip, swung it through the air more than a dozen times, and slammed it into the ice. Leech Seed took effect again then, draining what little strength Forretress had left.

Boom!

Forretress punched a great hole in the ice and fell unconscious.

Wesley's eyes widened. "What?!"

To Ethan, it had been an imperfect win against a poor type matchup. The injuries covering Venusaur showed just how much damage Wesley's Forretress had dealt with its advantage. Still, even while at a disadvantage, Ethan had won.

He shrugged. "See? I told you to send out your strongest Pokémon."

It was an unkind thing to say after the fight, and Wesley did not answer. In fact, Forretress was his strongest Pokémon. He had chosen it to teach Ethan a lesson for his rude warning.

What made the loss especially embarrassing was that he had been beaten decisively despite such a favorable type matchup. Ethan was simply the better Trainer. Wesley had no reply.

Seeing that Wesley accepted his defeat, Ethan turned to Tuscany. The Week Siblings might have a big reputation, but perhaps their natural talent had its limits. They were capable Trainers, yet there was still a wide gap between them and a tournament champion like Ethan. Neither sibling seemed to recognize him, and he did not volunteer his identity.

"I'll take this one," Tuscany said.

She patted her brother's shoulder and motioned for him to step aside. Then she chose her own strongest Pokémon.

"Be careful, Tuscany," Wesley warned. "This guy isn't ordinary."

"I know."

Tuscany was more than a year older than Wesley, and stronger as well. Ethan recalled his injured Venusaur and took out a second Poké Ball.

"As you asked, this is my strongest Pokémon," Tuscany said. "I hope you're satisfied. Go, Lombre!"

They threw their Poké Balls together. Two flashes of blue light brought Lombre and Jolteon onto the icy field.

Lombre was a bipedal Water- and Grass-type Pokémon with a resemblance to a kappa. A broad lily pad covered its head. Its body was green, its fingers and toes were red, and it had short, rounded ears and a red mouth shaped like a beak.

This time, the situation was reversed: Ethan had the type advantage.

Jolteon nearly fried Lombre. One electric blast followed another until Lombre collapsed, smoke rising from its body. Even the icy field beneath it cracked apart under the shocks and melted into water from the heat.

Tuscany lost faster than her brother had.

"We lost," she admitted.

The siblings exchanged a look, then quietly came to Ethan. Each took a badge from their clothes and held it out to him.

"Here," Wesley muttered. "This is my Disappointment Badge."

"And this is my Heartbeat Badge," Tuscany said. "Take it, mysterious Trainer. They're proof that you beat us."

Heartbeat and Disappointment? Ethan accepted the two badges and inspected them. One was red and the other blue, but otherwise they were identical. They looked much like the ordinary badges he had seen before coming to this world.

Even so, they marked one more step in his growth. He put both away carefully and thanked the siblings. He had taken only a couple of steps toward the Pokémon Center when Tuscany called after him.

"Mysterious Trainer! Our other brothers and sisters are traveling around Johto. If you meet them, you can challenge them too. I promise you won't be disappointed. I'll tell them what happened tonight, and they'll avenge Wesley and me!"

The Indigo League champion yawned. Without turning around, he waved a hand at them. "Got it. I'll take them on anytime!"

After a night's rest in the Ice Path, Ethan and Sabrina set out again, refreshed and full of energy.

To Ethan, the battles with Tuscany and Wesley had been only a small episode in his life. He soon forgot about them.

The second half of the Ice Path was just as easy to follow. As the map had promised, when they emerged from the frozen cave and descended a mountainside, Blackthorn City was close at hand.

Blackthorn was a quiet city nestled among the mountains. Somewhere in those mountains, people said, lay a mysterious Dragon's Den inhabited by many Dragon-type Pokémon. Trainers who came from Blackthorn tended to specialize in Dragon types.

Blackthorn Gym stood in the north of the city. Its Gym Leader and keeper of the Rising Badge was Clair, a master of Dragon-type Pokémon. And speaking of Clair inevitably brought up her cousin, the famous Elite Four member Lance.

Both belonged to the local clan of Dragon Tamers. Clair's accomplishments were hardly insignificant beside Lance's, and the clan had held firm control of Blackthorn Gym since its founding.

Clair had trained in the Dragon's Den from childhood. Ever since becoming a Trainer, she had been supremely confident in her abilities. She even called herself the world's greatest Dragon-type master and dared to take on members of the Elite Four.

Her record gave that confidence some basis. In her years as Gym Leader, she and her powerful Pokémon had defeated many Trainers who came to challenge her. Even among Johto's Gym Leaders, she was one of the very strongest.
