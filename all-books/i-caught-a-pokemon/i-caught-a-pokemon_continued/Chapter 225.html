They untied the motorboat, and Ethan, Sabrina, and Rudy climbed aboard. Rudy would drive and supervise Ethan's attempt, so he had to come along. His attendants had gone ahead to take up positions and operate the targets.

After a short wait, Rudy heard over his radio that everything was ready. He started the boat.

The diesel engine rumbled and belched smoke as it carried the three of them upriver, toward the forest in Trovita Island's interior. The boat sped across the water, leaving ripples in its wake. Fortunately, the river was calm, and the ride was less rough than Ethan had expected.

The banks flashed past. After a little over a minute of silence, Rudy spoke again. "Another hundred meters and we'll reach the course I prepared. Are you ready?"

Ethan nodded. After a moment's thought, he opened a Poké Ball and sent out Jolteon.

Jolteon rubbed affectionately against him. Ethan explained the task, and it gave a soft cry, turned around, and fixed its attention on the river ahead, alert to the slightest movement.

"Jolteon?" Rudy sounded surprised as he looked at the spiky Pokémon. "On the water, with the boat moving underneath you, I thought you'd choose a Water-type like the other challengers."

"Don't worry," Ethan said, his face expressionless. "My Jolteon is strong. It can handle a small test like this."

In fact, he had considered using Blastoise. As Rudy said, Blastoise had spent so much time in the water that the rocking of a boat would hardly bother it. Its Water Gun was fast and reliable, and powerful enough to smash an ordinary wooden target.

But Blastoise had one obvious drawback: its size and weight. Summoning it onto the motorboat would surely sink them.

A round target suddenly dropped from the trunk of a large tree. Ethan turned toward it. At the same instant, Jolteon's stiff fur bristled, and a bright current shot out.

Bang!

The electricity struck dead center. The target burst into countless fragments that fell into the river.

Ethan gauged the strength of the attack from the wreckage, then crouched beside Jolteon. "Nice work. But save your energy from here on. That was more power than you needed. Ease up a little on the next one."

Jolteon cried in reply. Another target rose from behind a boulder, and without waiting for Ethan's command, it fired a second current. The target shattered too, though the damage was clearly less extensive this time.

"Good!" Ethan rubbed its head. "Keep it there."

The boat continued forward. Targets appeared more and more often. Some moved, and at times two or three appeared together. Despite the rising difficulty, Jolteon performed brilliantly, sending out controlled bursts of electricity and destroying every target.

Bang! Bang! Bang, bang!

The cracks echoed over the quiet river.

At the back of the boat, Rudy watched Jolteon in astonishment. The sun had set, though the sky was not yet completely dark. In the fading twilight, visibility was getting worse by the minute.

The Pokémon brought by previous challengers had varied in ability, so Rudy had set a passing mark: a hit rate of at least eighty percent. He knew Ethan must have some skill to have beaten the first two Gym Leaders. Still, he had expected the light to throw Ethan's Pokémon off enough to miss a few targets and perform below its usual standard.

Then Rudy could make a few cutting remarks while Ethan squirmed, before making an "exception" and graciously allowing the challenge anyway. It would have repaid Ethan for the insults earlier.

Instead, Jolteon had done so well. How disappointing.

On second thought, it had to be Ethan's strongest Pokémon. Of course it was! He had exposed his trump card just to pass the test. How naive.

The more Rudy thought about it, the more certain he became.

After completing a circuit, the motorboat returned to the dock. Once it had stopped, Rudy acknowledged Ethan's skill, disappointed though he was.

"You passed," he said haughtily, wearing the look of a man who had seen through everything. "Under the rules, you may come to the Gym and challenge me tomorrow. But now that you've shown me your best Pokémon so early, what will you do in the actual match?"

Ethan stared at him, baffled. His best Pokémon? Did Rudy mean Jolteon?

When Rudy had spoken of a course he had carefully prepared, Ethan had expected something difficult. One circuit had proved surprisingly easy. Perhaps Rudy's view of the world was simply too narrow. Having never ventured farther afield, he probably assumed every Trainer was on the level of those in the Orange League.

Rudy mistook Ethan's silence for embarrassment at having his plan exposed. He smiled. "Tomorrow, I'll use Pokémon whose types match yours. We'll fight three one-on-one rounds. Win two, and you'll have cleared my Gym challenge."

Then he approached Sabrina again and offered his hand with exaggerated courtesy. He evidently had not given up. "Since it's so late, beautiful lady, would you care to have dinner with me?"

Sabrina ignored him. She looked at Ethan and tilted her head, as though asking whether she could teach this arrogant man a lesson.

Ethan gave her a small smile and turned to leave. Sabrina followed without sparing Rudy another glance.

Rudy stood there, embarrassed, and slowly lowered the hand he had held out to her.
