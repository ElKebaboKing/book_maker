After lunch, they set out at once to look for traces of Team Rocket.

Before Ethan arrived, Lorelei had already searched One Island on her own and found nothing. Now she had set her sights on nearby Two Island.

Lorelei had no Flying-type Pokémon, so the three of them took her speedboat across the water.

The boat cut through the waves. Ethan paid little attention to the scenery; a question had been on his mind since they left. As the next island drew closer, he finally asked it.

"Miss Lorelei, if you're sure Moltres is here, why haven't you tried to catch it?"

Lorelei glanced at him as she steered. "I'm a master of Ice-type Pokémon. Catching a Fire type would be rather off-brand, wouldn't it? Besides, Moltres has an advantage over my whole team. Why would I go to all that trouble?"

That was her reason?

Ethan had imagined she wanted to protect a legend of her homeland and had quietly taken it upon herself to guard Moltres. The truth was much simpler.

Like other trainers who had mastered a single type, Lorelei had gained recognition through her specialty, but it also limited the paths she could take.

Spray from the speedboat's wake splashed their faces. After a little over ten minutes, they reached Two Island.

The island's formal name was Friendship Island. Its two peaks stood side by side like brothers with their shoulders together, giving it the name.

Once they stepped off the dock, Ethan noticed that it was a little more populated than One Island. It served as a trading center for the Sevii Islands. On certain days, an open-air market let islanders exchange supplies and sell local goods to visitors.

Today was not a market day. The stalls were nearly empty, apart from a few elderly women idly selling everyday necessities.

Ethan looked away, then noticed a newly opened Game Corner in the distance.

After what had happened in Celadon City, Sabrina immediately grew wary. "Is that Game Corner new? Could Team Rocket own it?"

"I wondered the same thing," Lorelei said, "but I checked its owners. They have no connection to Team Rocket. It has attracted a gang of bikers, though. If no one deals with them, they'll cause trouble sooner or later."

A biker gang? Sabrina was surprised to find one on such a remote island. Why would people ride motorcycles all day on an island this small, without even proper roads? Were they racing to see who could plunge into the sea first?

With no immediate lead in town, they headed inland.

The place Lorelei most suspected on Two Island, and the best place to hide something, was Cape Brink. They searched it carefully, but found no sign of Team Rocket.

"It seems they aren't on Two Island either," she said. "Let's try the next one."

The search had cost them energy, but at least they could be reasonably sure the island was safe. Now they had to walk back to the dock and take the speedboat to Three Island.

Three Island was also called Mother-and-Child Island. It consisted of one large island and one small one, linked by a rope bridge that looked perilous from a distance.

They found no trace of Team Rocket there, either. But the visit was not entirely wasted. In a dense Berry Forest, branches hung heavy with ripe wild fruit. Ethan let out his Pokémon and gave them the chance to eat their fill.

By then it was late. The long search had worn them all out, so they accepted Lorelei's invitation to stay at her home on Four Island.

Much of the Pokémon world remained untouched by people. In its prosperous cities, though, smoke from homes and industry could dull the sky, just as it had on Earth. Everything took on a hazy cast. Only somewhere as remote as these islands could Ethan still look up at a sky full of stars.

Four Island, also called Frozen Island, was unlike the others. The rest of the Sevii Islands had similar subtropical climates. Here, an undersea current chilled the northeastern part of the island.

Lorelei's house stood beside a small lake at the foot of a mountain to the east. Her parents were still alive, but they found the cold too much and lived elsewhere on the island.

Though she lived alone, Lorelei had made the house warm and inviting. It was neat and clean, too. She clearly took good care of it when her work allowed.

"Make yourselves at home," she said, opening the door and going in first.

Sabrina followed. Ethan was about to enter when he noticed a dark cave across the lake, visible in the moonlight.

"What's over there?"

Lorelei leaned back out and followed his pointing finger. "Oh, that. It used to be a narrow crack. I widened it into the entrance you see now. The passage runs into the island, and inside it's a world of ice and snow. It doesn't look like much from here, does it? Most of the cold you're feeling comes from that cave."

It had been so long since Ethan played the games that he could not remember whether a cave like that had appeared in them.

"Are there a lot of Pokémon inside?"

"Of course. I even go in there sometimes to train with my Pokémon."

A thought struck Ethan, and he stopped with one foot almost through the doorway. "When did you last go in?"

Lorelei considered it. "About two weeks ago, I think. Why? Is something wrong?"

Ethan answered with a question of his own. "We've spent all day searching the islands without finding a lead. Have you considered looking inside that cave?"

Inside the cave? Lorelei froze.

It was right beside her house, and she had never seen a stranger go in. She had overlooked it completely. Yet she knew the old saying: the most dangerous place could be the safest hiding place.

Could Team Rocket really be in there?
