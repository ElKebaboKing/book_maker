As soon as Donphan rolled into the sandstorm, the crowd lost sight of both Pokémon. Even the cameras showed nothing but dense yellow sand. The wind howled, and the storm showed no sign of fading.

Under that noise, people could hear Donphan rolling and feel the ground shake. They had to imagine what was happening inside.

Tyson knew how this combination usually worked. Donphan ought to be striking Tyranitar about now. A tremendous crash rang out, like two trucks colliding, followed by Tyranitar's roar. The Pokémon had met.

It worked! Tyson felt a flash of relief. Then the sound of rolling stopped.

Why had Donphan stopped? Why could he hear only Tyranitar?

Before he could work it out, Tyranitar roared again. Something heavy slammed against the ground, then took blow after blow. A faint cry of pain reached Tyson through the wind. It sounded like Donphan.

He stared into the sand, but could see nothing. From his raised command platform, he could look over the storm toward Ethan. Ethan stood calmly with one hand in his pocket. He showed none of the panic a Trainer might feel at losing sight of his Pokémon and the ability to command it. He seemed unconcerned about the battle hidden in the sand.

Either Ethan had faced such a situation before or he trusted Tyranitar to win without his help. In either case, Tyson's tried-and-tested combination had failed.

The sandstorm would not stop just because he wanted it to. It had to run its course. Until then, Tyson was the one left worrying about what he could not see. What if Donphan fell first? He would have trapped his own Pokémon.

“Donphan, get out of there!” he shouted.

He needed Donphan back in sight so he could assess the damage and give a useful order. Abandoning the sandstorm and the surprise attack was worth that. But no Donphan emerged. Tyranitar's roars only sounded more frightening.

Something had gone wrong inside—perhaps from the very start. Tyson could not even recall Donphan, since he could neither see nor locate it through the sand.

Fortunately, the storm did not last much longer. The sand began to settle, and visibility slowly returned. A tall figure stood at its center, chest rising and falling. A smaller shape lay motionless before it.

Had the battle already been decided? So quickly? Everyone watched without blinking until the last of the sand cleared.

The standing Pokémon was Tyranitar. Its eyes were red, as though it had only just emerged from a wild, confused state. Donphan lay unfolded on its back, soft belly exposed in a deep pit. Marks of tremendous force scarred both its body and the ground around it.

The announcer stared, then cried, “Tyranitar must have used Superpower to stop Donphan's Rollout, then hit it with Thrash! Those cries we heard were Donphan's!”

Anyone who understood battles could see how Donphan had lost. It was still hard to believe such a sturdy Pokémon had been beaten so thoroughly, so fast.

Experienced Trainers felt sorry for Tyson. Donphan had borne the full force of Thrash, a powerful Normal-type move that sent its user into a frenzy of destruction. Living things and objects alike could be smashed in its path. The sandstorm had kept Tyranitar's attention fixed on the one opponent directly before it. Donphan had taken every blow.

In effect, Tyson had delivered Donphan to Tyranitar himself. Without the sandstorm, Donphan could probably have dodged a wild attack like that. A Pokémon using Thrash was hardly going to pursue it with perfect judgment.

Tyson knew that too, and his face darkened. It was not that he had never considered the risk. He had not expected Tyranitar to choose Thrash without a Trainer's command. Most Pokémon, unable to see in a sandstorm, would at least guard themselves. If Tyranitar had misjudged the moment and missed Donphan, the resulting confusion could have left it helpless before a counterattack.

Tyranitar had taken that chance. Its attack had also been strong enough to knock out Donphan, a Pokémon with excellent defense and stamina. Tyson had lost without seeing the battle, while Ethan had won without giving a single order.

Only now did Tyson clearly feel how far apart they were. He had seen and heard warnings before, but being forced this far made the difference impossible to dismiss. He had never battled an Elite Four member, only watched recordings. Yet a thought rose unbidden: perhaps even the Elite Four were no more formidable than this man.

Tyson startled himself. Ethan was a tournament entrant, just as he was. How could he rival or surpass the Elite Four? Surely that could not be right.

He shook the thought away and looked at Donphan's exposed belly. Tyranitar's Superpower must have overturned it, leaving its soft underside open to Thrash. Bad luck, Tyson told himself.

What he failed to see was the risk built into his own tactic. Rollout inside Sandstorm could overwhelm a small Pokémon. Against a Rock-type powerhouse like Tyranitar, it was a poor gamble. Repeating it would likely have ended the same way.

Ethan was surprised too. Donphan had fallen so easily that it felt as if the fight had ended before it began. Then he realized that his own Pokémon might simply be that strong.

They had fought Legendary Pokémon and clashed repeatedly with Team Rocket, Team Aqua, and Team Magma. Each battle had helped them grow. Ordinary competitors no longer felt particularly threatening when Ethan returned to them.

That was one reason he often left his old partners in care when he entered a new region and caught new Pokémon there. If every Conference match was as easy as this one, the tournament would feel like a formality.

He could see why the League appointed some talented young Trainers as Gym Leaders or members of the Elite Four. They were too strong to find much challenge in a Conference, so the League gave them a role and standing instead.

Would someone from the League ask him to become an Elite Four member one day?

Ethan smiled at the thought and recalled Tyranitar. Tyson still stood across the field, lost in thought. Ethan took out a new Poké Ball and released his next Pokémon without waiting to see Tyson's choice. Venusaur appeared before him.

Tyson came back to himself and felt a sting of humiliation. He had lost, yet Ethan was the one to show his next Pokémon first. Was Ethan so sure he could win even after giving away that advantage?

Then Tyson gave a bitter smile. What did it matter? The previous exchanges had already shown him that Ethan was beyond him. Even if his last two Pokémon each won a round, he would still have to beat four more of Ethan's to take the match. Could he honestly imagine doing that?

His Ever Grande Conference journey would probably end here, in the first round of the final tournament. He had arrived full of hope, only to meet an opponent like this. He could not accept leaving without a single victory.

At least one. He had to beat at least one of Ethan's Pokémon so the result would not look quite so dreadful.

Tyson chose his most trusted and strongest partner: Meowth. It had performed well in the preliminaries, though those short matches had not left a strong impression on the crowd. Its hat, boots, and upright stance drew attention now.

Team Rocket's Meowth stopped selling rice crackers once more and fixed its eyes on the other Meowth. It no longer cared about making money. It wanted to see whether this fellow Pokémon, whose fate seemed so like its own, could help Tyson knock out even one of Ethan's partners.

Go on, meow.

Ethan studied the Meowth carefully. Ash had told him its story. It had lost a fight with a Persian for leadership of its group and been badly injured. Tyson found it by chance and nursed it back to health. The hat and boots had first been part of its treatment, then became its trademark as it stayed with Tyson.

Perhaps the injuries or the memory of that fight ran deep. Like Ash's Pikachu, this Meowth refused to evolve. It had remained a Meowth and become Tyson's strongest Pokémon.

Ethan had expected Tyson to save his ace for the end. Sending it out now showed how desperate the battle had become. Tyson had no choice but to stake everything on it early.
