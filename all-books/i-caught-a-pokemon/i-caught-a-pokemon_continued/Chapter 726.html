The three of them climbed in silence. After another ten minutes or more, their legs aching, they finally reached the top of Sky Pillar.

They emerged onto a high triangular platform. As they stepped onto it, four green flames suddenly sprang up in stone basins at its corners. The flames appeared without warning, eerie and mysterious, as though announcing something to the sky or lighting the way for someone.

This had to be Sky Pillar's most important place: the Dragonhark altar.

It didn't look like much of an altar to Ethan. Apart from its stone floor and a slightly raised set of steps, there was nothing here. He went to the edge and looked down. Thick clouds surrounded the tower, although there should not have been clouds this dense at its height. They looked almost manufactured, meant to hide the view. The Draconid people had been thorough in protecting this place.

They had reached their destination. It was time for the next step. Ethan and Zinnia turned toward one another.

After a long silence, Zinnia spoke first. “I brought you here as promised. Will you tell me your plan now?”

The journey and the long walk across the sand had given her time to calm down. She was beginning to suspect Ethan had deliberately made her angry before. But it was too late to dwell on that. He was already inside her people's sacred place, and their rules had been broken. She could hardly undo it now. Was she really going to make an enemy of him and drive him out?

“What plan?” Ethan shrugged. “I'll summon Rayquaza and try to catch it. I was hoping you'd handle the summoning.”

“Me?” She gave him a cool glance. “Have you forgotten what I told you? Summoning Rayquaza requires the Rainbow Meteorite. It was lost long ago.”

“Surely that's not the only way.” Ethan studied her, but didn't press the point yet. “There's something I've been meaning to ask. You keep mentioning this meteorite. What does it look like?”

Zinnia shook her head. “I don't know exactly. It disappeared in my parents' time. I've only heard that it has a mysterious power: it attracts Rayquaza and can make it Mega Evolve into a stronger form.”

It could make Rayquaza Mega Evolve too? That sounded remarkably like the round, Key Stone-like rock he had just found. Was the stone in his bag the Rainbow Meteorite?

Perhaps the Draconid people had searched for it outside the tower for decades while it had been here all along, hidden in plain sight. The idea was far-fetched, but possible. It would explain the stone's unusual shape, the Key Stone-like feature, and its power.

“So you've never seen the Rainbow Meteorite yourself?”

Zinnia didn't answer. That was answer enough. Losing her people's greatest treasure was hardly something she wanted to keep discussing with an outsider.

Ethan took the stone from his bag and held it out in front of her. She looked at it, surprised he would produce an ordinary-looking round rock now, but said nothing. Ethan watched her face closely. She seemed genuinely unmoved. She hadn't lied; she didn't recognize it.

Then he turned the stone to reveal the Key Stone-like mark. Zinnia's pupils contracted.

“Wait. Is that...?”

He caught the flash of surprise. Ethan tossed the stone lightly up and down. Her gaze followed every rise and fall.

“Where did you get one of those?” she asked.

“You recognize it?”

She hesitated, then nodded. “From its shape, that's a Meteorite Key Stone.”

A Meteorite Key Stone? So it wasn't the Rainbow Meteorite after all. Ethan was surprised to have guessed wrong, but it hardly mattered. He turned his attention to what she had identified.

Most Key Stones in this world were dug out of mountains. A very small number reached the planet aboard falling meteorites. Both kinds could enable a Pokémon to Mega Evolve, but Meteorite Key Stones supposedly held an unusual cosmic energy. So few had been found that scientists still did not understand what, if anything, made them different.

Rumor claimed that Pokémon Mega Evolved through a Meteorite Key Stone gained strength beyond what an ordinary Key Stone could provide, thanks to that cosmic energy. There was no evidence to support it. Still, Ethan thought a rumor like that might have some basis.

He smiled. “Then perhaps I should replace my old Key Stone with this one.”

His pleased expression made Zinnia ask again, “Where exactly did you find it?”

“Didn't you just see me?”

She remembered finding him on the stairs surrounded by Pokémon, all of them apparently searching for something. So that was it. But how had he known a Meteorite Key Stone was there? He had claimed to have visited Sky Pillar before. Had he seen it then?

She was curious, though the stone itself was of little importance to her. An ordinary Key Stone and a Meteorite Key Stone served much the same purpose in her plans.

Misty listened to them. She didn't follow every part of their conversation, but she knew Ethan had found something valuable, and she was sincerely happy for him.

Ethan, meanwhile, finally understood why this stone had drawn Rayquaza's attention in the anime. It must have been the cosmic energy inside it.

“Let's set the stone aside,” he said to Zinnia. “I won't hide why I asked you to bring me here. I do need your help.”

“I've told you already. The Rainbow Meteorite is gone. I can't help you.”

“Are you sure?” Ethan's eyes moved to the Key Stones bound around her right leg. “You've prepared for this. Haven't you gathered a large number of them?”

Zinnia's eyes widened. “How... how do you know that?”

It was her greatest secret. She was an exceptional talent among the Draconid people, stronger than others her age and relentlessly determined. After the failure involving Deoxys last time, she had searched desperately for a way to summon Rayquaza without the Rainbow Meteorite. At last she had thought of one: sacrifice a large number of Key Stones to draw Rayquaza's attention and bring it down.

The theory and her experiments suggested it could work. Only the real attempt remained. Yet she had developed the method privately and told no one. How could Ethan know? Could he foresee the future? Did he have an informant among the Draconid people? An informant might even explain his claim that he had been here before. But was that possible?

Ethan interrupted her spiraling thoughts. “I won't ask for your help for nothing. You've been traveling in such a hurry these past few days that you probably haven't heard the latest news.”

That much was true. Zinnia had spent most of her journey in the air, traveling day and night with only one thought: reaching Lilycove City and meeting him. She had paid little attention to anything else.

“What are you getting at?” she asked.

Ethan looked to Misty, who understood and explained. “Yesterday, outside LaRousse City, two Deoxys fought Rayquaza and defeated it. Someone filmed the battle, and the video has spread online. It's caused quite a stir.”

“Rayquaza? Deoxys?” Zinnia frowned at Ethan. “What happened? Did you cause it?”

She did not doubt Misty's account. A quick search would verify it, and she didn't think Ethan would invent a story like this.

“Of course,” he said. “I'm telling you because I want you to know I can encounter Rayquaza, and I have the strength to defeat and catch one.”

Zinnia didn't question that. She had seen enough when he captured Deoxys to understand what he could do. But why bring it up now? Was he simply boasting?

“If you help summon Rayquaza this time,” Ethan continued, “I'll do my best to help you catch one as well. You and your people could fulfill the goal you've pursued your whole lives.”

For an instant, Zinnia's heart leapt so fiercely she could barely contain herself. She took a deep breath and forced herself to think.

“If catching one is so easy for you,” she asked, “why do you need my help?”

Ethan looked up at the sky, trying to appear inscrutable. “That doesn't matter. Will you accept the deal?”

“A deal...” She considered it, then shook her head. “I can summon and catch Rayquaza myself. I don't need your help. But I'll give you a fair chance to compete.”

Ethan regarded her with surprise. She had turned down an easier opportunity and chosen to rely on herself. She must have considerable faith in her own strength. It suited him, though. It would save him the trouble of catching another Rayquaza for her.

“What do you mean by a fair chance?”

“I'll summon Rayquaza. After that, which of us it chooses, and which of us it fights, will depend on our abilities.”

Ethan nodded. There was some risk in that arrangement: he might lose his chance at the black Rayquaza, if it appeared. But with the Meteorite Key Stone in hand, he felt confident he could attract its attention.

And if he truly had no chance with Rayquaza, so be it. He had gone this long without a Shiny Pokémon. Missing one more would not ruin his life.

“Agreed. Then please summon it.”

“Not yet.” With the matter settled, Zinnia relaxed. She sat on the nearby stone steps. “We need to wait until nightfall.”

“Nightfall?” Ethan wondered whether there was some special reason.

“Nothing complicated. My chances of successfully summoning Rayquaza are better at night.”

That was all? He hadn't expected such an answer. Still, the more she relied on this kind of luck, the better he felt about their chances of drawing the black Rayquaza. Luck had its own rules, after all.

He looked at the sky. The sun was blazing overhead. It was only just past noon, at the hottest part of the day. They had a long wait ahead.
