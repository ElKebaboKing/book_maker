Early the next morning, after breakfast, Ethan and the others filled their water bottles, said goodbye to Towa and Diana, and headed for the Mysterious Forest.

Even its entrance was unusual. A giant tree that must have stood for countless years had fallen there. Someone had hollowed out its center, turning the trunk into a long passage.

It ran for at least twenty meters. Ethan could only imagine how magnificent the tree had been while it was still standing.

The fallen trunk separated Arborville from the Mysterious Forest. It was only a few dozen steps, yet when they emerged on the other side, even the air seemed different. Perhaps that was only their imagination.

Wild Pokémon were everywhere. Pidgey, Pidgeotto, and Butterfree flew overhead. Hoothoot, Spinarak, and Heracross perched among the trees. Rattata, Sentret, and Nidoran roamed the ground. They had barely entered the forest, and already the number of Pokémon around them was astonishing.

Perhaps they had gone so long without being disturbed by outsiders that they felt no fear of the intruding humans. They only tilted their heads and watched.

“There are so many Pokémon here,” Misty said.

She spotted several Weedle crawling on the ground, shuddered, and hurried to Ethan’s side.

“There are. They’re packed in here.” Knowing how afraid Misty was of Bug-type Pokémon, Ethan kept her close. Then he looked toward Erika and Ash. “There’s a remarkable variety too. If either of you wants to catch something, this is a good opportunity.”

Both shook their heads. They had already caught the Pokémon they wanted, and nothing they had seen here tempted them to make another attempt.

They walked for another half hour, heading deeper into the forest. Then an endless wash of blue-green light swept toward them from beyond the trees. In an instant it spread through the forest around them and colored the canopy overhead.

As the light flashed past, an ethereal voice sounded in their ears.

“What was that?” Ash looked around in confusion. After his first moment of alarm, he realized the light had neither hurt nor affected him and began to relax.

“That must be the voice of the forest Towa warned us about,” Ethan said. He looked around and pointed. “The light and the sound came from over there. Let’s have a look.”

He strode off in that direction, and Ash and the others hurried after him. Erika lingered at the rear, about to remind them that Towa had said they must not move if they heard the voice.

Then she thought better of it. They were Trainers, naturally curious about anything unknown. A warning would not satisfy them until they had seen for themselves what had happened.

She wanted to see it too.

With a helpless shake of her head, she hurried to catch up.

They crossed a stream, climbed a slope, and after another ten minutes reached a moss-covered altar.

Calling it an altar was generous. It was little more than a pile of stones with a small stone tablet set on top.

Just as they arrived, a brilliant light flashed. A boy holding a strange Pokémon appeared out of nowhere and dropped to the ground before the altar.

The Pokémon wriggled free of his arms the instant they appeared, flew into the air, and vanished into the nearby trees.

Ethan had caught only a glimpse, but it was enough. That had been Celebi, the legendary Mythical Pokémon.

So it really was that part of the movie!

And the boy who had just appeared must be Professor Oak, brought from the past into the present.

Ash and the others had already run to the boy and helped him up.

“Wake up! Are you all right?” Ash called.

The boy did not respond.

Erika examined him and frowned. “He’s breathing, but he may be hurt. Let’s take him back to the village first.”

With an unconscious boy to care for, they could hardly continue their journey. Ethan brought out Tyranitar and had it carry him.

They retraced their steps through the forest and returned to Arborville.

Towa and Diana were startled to see them again barely an hour after they had left.

“What happened?” Towa asked.

“We found someone unconscious in the forest!”

Diana hurried to fetch a first-aid kit. By the time she and Towa came down from the treehouse, the boy had begun to wake.

He had plainly been frightened just before he fainted. The moment he opened his eyes and saw Ash close to him, he shoved him to the ground.

“I won’t hand Celebi over to you!”

“What was that for?” Ash landed hard on his backside. Angry and bewildered, he sprang up, grabbed the boy by the collar, and began struggling with him.

“Hey, stop!” Brock and Misty rushed in and pulled them apart. “Both of you, calm down!”

The boy seemed to come to his senses. He looked around blankly, unable to understand where he was. “Wh-what happened?”

Towa and Diana arrived with the first-aid kit. As soon as Towa saw his face, she gave a small gasp, then hurried to him in astonishment.

“You’re the boy from back then... Oh, thank goodness. Thank goodness...”

Everyone was startled by her reaction. “Grandmother, do you know him?” Diana asked.

Towa turned to her. “Do you remember the boy I told you about? The one who disappeared in this forest forty years ago?”

“What? You mean this is him?”

Towa nodded and looked back at the boy. “Yes. I remember him clearly. It’s him.”

Hearing them, the boy seemed to realize something. He stared at the elderly woman and murmured, “You’re the girl who gave me bread...”

Their disjointed conversation left Erika, Ash, and the others thoroughly confused. Only Ethan looked as though things were going exactly as he had expected.

Towa led everyone up to her treehouse.

After they sat on the sofas in the living room, she took a bundle wrapped in thick cloth from a cabinet and handed it to the boy.

“Look at this. Is it something you lost?”

He unwrapped the bundle. Inside was an old, worn notebook.

“It is mine... But how...?”

He recognized the notebook he always carried. It had been beside him only a moment ago, so how could it have ended up here? Its pages were yellowed and darkened, as if it had been stored away for years.

He looked up at Towa. Before he could ask, she said, “I believe you’ve traveled through time.”

Her words caused an uproar. Erika, Brock, and Misty all stared in shock. Their world held many strange powers, but traveling through time was something they had never heard of.

The boy and Ash, meanwhile, only looked blank.

One of them could not accept what he had heard. The other simply did not understand it.

Ash tilted his head. “What does that mean?”

Diana set cups of tea before them and explained, “It means this boy crossed forty years of time to appear here. He looks exactly as he did when my grandmother saw him forty years ago.”

Ash finally understood. “No way!”

He studied the boy beside him, but nothing about him looked forty years old. Ash was about to ask whether they had made a mistake. Then he remembered the blue-green light that had flashed through the forest when they found him and swallowed the question.

The boy stared at his notebook a moment longer, then suddenly cried, “Celebi! Where’s Celebi?”

“Celebi?”

Ash and the others shook their heads. They had reached the altar a few steps behind Ethan and had missed the Pokémon flying away.

“There was a Pokémon with me, wasn’t there?” the boy asked.

Misty searched her memory, then shook her head. “Sorry. We didn’t see one. You were lying there alone when we arrived.”

Ethan lifted his tea and took a quiet sip, looking as though none of this concerned him. He felt like a player waiting for a familiar story to advance: listen to the conversation, and the next event would follow.

Only Towa leaned forward and asked gravely, “You saw Celebi?”

The boy hesitated, then nodded.

After hearing the name so many times, Ash could not hold back any longer. “Who is this Celebi you keep talking about?”

“Celebi is a Mythical Pokémon,” Towa said, her expression complicated. “We also call it the God of the Forest. It gives life to the forest and has the power to travel through time.”

Brock looked at the boy. “Then Celebi’s power is what brought him to our time?”

Towa nodded. “Yes. Only Celebi could do that.”

“So that’s why we saw that strange light and heard the voice in the forest,” Misty said. “It was Celebi.”

“According to legend, Celebi lives beside the Lake of Life deep in the Mysterious Forest,” Towa said with a frown. “But no one has ever actually seen it travel through time before. I don’t know why it brought you here.”

“Celebi was badly hurt. Someone was trying to capture it, and I...” The boy suddenly stood. “That’s right! I have to go. That person might still be after Celebi. I have to save it!”

“I’m coming with you!” Ash leaped to his feet, his quarrel with the boy apparently forgotten.

“You can count us in too,” Misty said, rising. “We’re strong Trainers. We couldn’t just stand by and let this happen!”

“Thank you...” The boy looked touched. Then he realized something and gave an apologetic smile. “Oh, I’m sorry. I haven’t even introduced myself. My name’s Sammy.”

“I’m Ash. I’m going to be a Pokémon Master, and this is my partner, Pikachu.”

“I’m Brock. I used to be a Gym Leader, and now I’m a pretty good Pokémon Breeder, if I say so myself!”

“I’m Misty. Remember, I’m a world-class beauty!”

When Sammy looked his way, Ethan finished his tea and stood. He introduced himself, then gestured to Erika beside him.

“I’m Ethan. As you can see, I’m a Trainer too. This is my companion, Erika.”

They knew there was no time to lose. Once the introductions were done, they said goodbye to Towa and Diana and left the treehouse together.

Sammy was not seriously hurt. Fear and the strain of crossing through time had made him faint, but after a short rest he was himself again.

Ash and the others might never learn who Sammy really was. Ethan knew perfectly well: he was Professor Oak as a boy.

Professor Oak’s full name was Samuel Oak. At the moment, he was only in his teens, still young-faced, traveling alone and sketching during his school vacation.

Ethan had no intention of telling Ash or the others. He would not even tell Erika.

Traveling through time defied common sense. If too many people became entangled in it, the consequences could be impossible to undo. The fewer people who knew Sammy’s identity, the safer it would be.

They left the village, passed through the hollow trunk once more, and entered the Mysterious Forest.

It was their second time walking this path today, but no one was impatient with the repetition. Ash and the others were excited at the prospect of seeing a legendary Mythical Pokémon. Even their steps seemed lighter.

They headed deeper into the forest, searching as they went. A long walk brought them no trace of Celebi.

Then Pikachu seemed to sense something. Its ears twitched, and it darted into the bushes in another direction.

Ethan and Ash knew how keen Pikachu’s senses could be, so they followed. Soon they reached a broad clearing. Two trunks as huge as the one outside the village lay crossed on the ground. Farther away, a crowd of wild Pokémon had gathered around a hollow in a tree. From where the group stood, they could not tell what the Pokémon were doing.
