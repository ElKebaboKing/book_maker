Supporters on both sides were still shouting encouragement for their respective trainers.

By coincidence, the biker gang and Gary's beautiful cheerleaders had been seated right next to each other, forming two cheering sections that could hardly have looked more different.

Unfortunately, faced with a group of young women as attractive as Gary's cheerleaders, quite a few of the bikers could only stare. They had already lost most of their earlier enthusiasm for the match.

Down on the field, Ethan was surprised to see Fearow too.

As the saying went, a Ho-Oh down on its luck was no better than a Fearow!

Joking resemblance to Ho-Oh aside, Fearow was a medium-sized bird Pokémon with a long neck and rough, disheveled feathers. The red crest on its head and its long, sharp pink beak were its most distinctive features.

Fearow were said to be quick-tempered. Their broad wings could carry a heavy load through a whole day's flight without tiring them too much.

Ethan hadn't thought that far ahead when he chose Jolteon. He had simply expected the ice to crack and break as the battle went on, exposing the pool beneath. Once there was water on the field, an Electric-type would have far more opportunities to use it.

Who could have guessed that Gary would send out a Flying-type, handing Jolteon a type advantage right from the start?

What was he thinking?

Hmph. He must still be too young.

As soon as the referee signaled the start of the battle, Ethan gave his command.

"Jolteon, use Thunder Shock!"

A thick bolt of electricity shot from Jolteon's body toward Fearow. Fearow gave its wings a slight flap, rose into the air, and avoided the attack.

Gary knew he was at a type disadvantage, but his pride would not let him switch Pokémon the moment the battle began. He would look like a coward and invite ridicule.

Jolteon kept sending out electricity while Fearow flew around, dodging each attack. Gary pushed the distracting thought aside and decided to fight for a chance. After all, there were plenty of examples of a Pokémon winning despite a bad type matchup.

"Fearow, use Aerial Ace!"

Fearow circled overhead, sweeping past Jolteon again and again. Its sharp eyes stayed fixed on the target as it searched for a chance to land the strike.

"Jolteon, get into that group of ice blocks!"

At Ethan's command, Jolteon bounded between the dozens of blocks. The cover would obstruct Fearow's view and limit the angles from which it could attack, giving Jolteon a better chance to dodge.

Even so, Fearow had a wide view from above. After circling a few more times, it locked onto Jolteon.

The next moment, Fearow dove. A faint light appeared around its wings as it hurtled toward Jolteon at tremendous speed. Just as it reached Jolteon, it accelerated again, struck, and abruptly changed direction, climbing back into the sky.

Aerial Ace!

Bang! Ice chips flew in every direction.

The force and cutting edge of the attack shattered the blocks beside Jolteon. Jolteon tried to evade, but Aerial Ace was too fast and sent it flying.

Impressive as the move looked, a Flying-type attack dealt only half its usual damage to Jolteon.

Jolteon landed steadily. Seeing Fearow about to rise out of reach, Ethan shouted, "Don't let it get away! Use Thunderbolt!"

Jolteon's body blazed with light. Dozens of great bolts of electricity spread unpredictably across the sky.

Fearow swerved left and right, avoiding several bolts before one caught it by surprise and struck it squarely. Electricity crackled through its body.

This time the type advantage worked in Jolteon's favor: the hit did double damage.

Fearow cried out, its feathers looking scorched. At the sight, Gary stopped worrying about his pride. He hurriedly took out a Poké Ball and recalled it.

Tch. So that wasn't going to work.

After thinking for a while, he sent out his second Pokémon. In a flash of blue light, Nidoking appeared on the field.

"Oh? A Nidoking!" the commentator exclaimed. "Ground-types are immune to Electric-type attacks and can deal double damage to Electric-types themselves. But is Nidoking really a good choice on an Ice Field?"

Gary listened without changing his expression.

Along with Arcanine and Blastoise, Nidoking was one of his strongest partners. It had battled beside him for a long time and won him many matches. He knew he could rely on it.

Sending it out, however, had also been a choice forced on him by the circumstances.

From watching Ethan's earlier battles and facing him now, Gary could see just how strong Ethan's Pokémon were. His own other Pokémon—whether Grass-types, Dragon-types, or even Ice-types that would benefit from this field—were considerably weaker than Nidoking. Sending one of them out might not be enough to beat Jolteon.

Of course, with Nidoking on the field, Ethan was bound to switch Pokémon. Gary wondered what he would choose next.

From the trainer's platform, Ethan glanced at the ice beneath Jolteon's feet. Jolteon's Thunderbolt had sent so much electricity and heat across the area that fine cracks had appeared in the thick ice. The damage had not yet spread to the rest of the field, but the surface was no longer as solid as it had been at the start.

Both trainers had sent out their first Pokémon at the same time. Thanks to the favorable matchup, Ethan now had the advantage of responding to Gary's switch with one of his own.

An early lead could carry through the rest of a battle. He intended to keep it, not stubbornly pit an Electric-type against Nidoking's Ground typing.

Ethan looked up at Nidoking. It was a large, purple, two-legged Pokémon with a thick tail, powerful forelimbs, and a sharp, venomous horn on its head.

He knew Gary well enough to recognize Nidoking as one of his strongest Pokémon. He could not afford to take it lightly.

Ethan recalled Jolteon and opened his second Poké Ball.

"Come out, Gyarados!"

With a mighty roar, Gyarados landed heavily on the ice. Its blood-red eyes fixed on its opponent, and sharp fangs showed inside its open jaws.

Gary clenched his teeth at the sight.

Tch. Water against Ground. Another type advantage?

How boring! Couldn't this guy stop relying on type matchups and fight me fair and square for once?
