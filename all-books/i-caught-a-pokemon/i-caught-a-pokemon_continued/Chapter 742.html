Amid the spray, Machamp and Hitmonchan met in a flurry of fists. One attacked, the other guarded or dodged, then answered with its own blow. In a dozen seconds they threw nearly a hundred punches.

But two fists were no match for four arms. Hitmonchan punched so fast its gloves blurred, yet Machamp soon gained the upper hand. There was a hard difference in strength. A Gym Leader's ace was not going to go easy on the Pokémon of a Trainer from a remote island.

It was a fight on entirely different levels.

Gardevoir and Walrein's contest proved just as one-sided after their first probing exchange. Not just anyone could stand against one of Ethan's strongest regular partners.

Gardevoir seldom fought these days and could almost be overlooked. But it had been with Ethan longer than any of his other Pokémon, growing steadily stronger all the while. His mutated giant red Gyarados might surpass it, as might Gengar, who had lived for thousands of years. Apart from those two, no one in his usual lineup could beat it.

Under ordinary circumstances, if Rocky had formally challenged Ethan, his Walrein would never even have faced Gardevoir.

After scattering Walrein's Water Gun, Gardevoir used Psychic again. An invisible force wrapped around the Pokémon and lifted its entire body into the air.

The interval between Gardevoir's two psychic attacks was so short, and both moves so fast, that Rocky had no time to react. Before he understood what had happened, Walrein was trapped.

How could Gardevoir attack again that quickly?

“Walrein, break free!”

Rocky frowned. But Gardevoir's control of psychic power had long since become masterful. Walrein could not simply struggle out. Gardevoir pressed its hands down. Psychic force squeezed Walrein, then hurled it to the ground. It landed heavily on Hitmonchan, which Machamp had just knocked backward.

Thud!

The two Pokémon lay in a heap. The crowd's cheers for Rocky died away. Everyone stared, too shocked to speak. No one had expected this. The girl who had trained beside them for days and the young man who had only just arrived were dominating the island's strongest Trainer.

Only Misty's excited voice broke the silence. “Go, Ethan! Go, Bea!”

Walrein and Hitmonchan had both taken a hard hit. Their limbs were tangled, and they struggled to get up. Machamp strode toward them.

“Walrein, pull yourself together! Get up!” Rocky shouted.

His Pokémon needed no warning. Both could see Machamp coming. Their frantic attempts to rise only got in each other's way. They were experienced enough to recover from the confusion, though, and managed to stand before Machamp reached them.

Hitmonchan had barely raised its fists when Machamp's punches fell like a storm.

Thud, thud, thud!

Caught off guard, Hitmonchan took a dozen more blows. Machamp seized an opening, lifted it high with Seismic Toss, and slammed it back to the ground.

Walrein, meanwhile, fired a chilling, pale-blue Aurora Beam at Gardevoir. That was not enough to catch it. Gardevoir Teleported aside, appeared on Walrein's other flank, and struck with another direct Psychic. Walrein fell.

It was an overwhelming victory. Ethan and Bea had held nothing back and given their opponents no chance to recover.

For Ethan, Rocky was no harder to beat than an ordinary Team Aqua or Team Magma member. He was not Ash, and Gardevoir was not Pikachu. Its strength did not reset or fluctuate wildly so that a weaker opponent could fight it on even terms. Once Ethan had sent out one of his aces, he would press that advantage and leave the other side no opening.

“What?” Rocky could hardly believe how easily Walrein had fallen. This had barely resembled a Double Battle. His side had lost almost as soon as the Pokémon engaged. They had done no meaningful damage, and after only two or three hits, both had gone down.

Walrein had balanced abilities and excellent stamina. Since when had it been so fragile? Or was the gulf between them so wide that it only seemed weak?

Before Rocky could absorb the sight, something flashed at the edge of his vision. He turned to see his partner's Hitmonchan fly backward and land flat on its back. They had been completely defeated in just a few exchanges.

The crowd erupted. None of them had expected their island's strongest Trainer to lose like this. Rocky stared for a long moment before he grasped how far above him Ethan stood.

So strong. Why had Ethan been reluctant to battle if he had a Pokémon like that? And why was Bea, who had seemed so ordinary during training, just as impressive?

Rocky could not make sense of it. He knew that even if they fought again, he would lose. The thought left him dispirited.

“I lost,” he admitted. He recalled Walrein. “You're strong. Stronger than anyone I've met. But that doesn't prove your method is better than ours. Even after losing to you, we'll keep training our muscles.”

Ethan merely shrugged. He had no urge to gloat or resume their argument. How these people trained was their own business. Rocky had challenged him, and he had answered with overwhelming strength.

Gardevoir had never been threatened. The fight had not helped it improve. It had scarcely counted as a warm-up. That was not Ethan boasting; these were simply the Pokémon he had with him. Had he chosen Eevee, Metang, or Milotic instead, he might still have won, but not so effortlessly.

Rocky had been unlucky to challenge him at this particular moment. Ethan saw no reason to say more. He waved to the group and left with Bea and Misty.

To him, it was a minor interruption. After tonight, Rocky would probably disappear into the depths of his memory. Perhaps Ethan would recall the battle only when he thought back on meeting Bea again.

At sea off Sootopolis Island, the International Police patrol boat continued southwest. It had sailed for hours since Ethan and Misty disembarked. Its destination was not Sootopolis City but Slateport City, on the southern coast of the Hoenn mainland, where the International Police had their regional headquarters.

Shelly had spent hours sitting idly in the cabin. Before night fell, she went out onto the deck for some air. As soon as she emerged, she spotted Courtney leaning against the railing and gazing toward the horizon. Shelly clicked her tongue, then walked over and looked out to sea beside her.

They were both aboard a police vessel. Their names had been cleared, but Shelly still felt more at ease near a former member of an organization like her own than among officers she had once had to avoid. Courtney glanced at her and said nothing.

They stood together in an awkward but strangely companionable silence. Shelly lost track of time. The cool sea wind began to bite through her clothes. She looked at Courtney's windproof cloak, gave a small huff, and pulled her own clothing tighter.

She was about to go back inside when a rainbow caught her eye. It was beautiful, its colors touched with gold in the evening light.

Yet Shelly frowned. A rainbow at dusk? There had been no rain, nor could she see any mist in the air. Why would one appear out of nowhere?

Then it changed. A gap opened in the curved rainbow like a doorway, revealing a deep black space behind it, dark as a star-filled sky. Something Shelly had never seen before emerged from the opening.

A Pokémon, she supposed.

It was tall and bizarre. Its body seemed made of black crystal, gleaming with an uncanny dark light, like living armor shaped around a monster. It had no wings, yet floated in the sky. Three claws tipped each huge hand, and a three-sided crystal projection rose from each hand's back, fading from white to black. Its legs were thin, especially below the knees. Jagged edges lined its sides, and an enormous four-sided pyramidal tail extended behind it. Spikes that shone black jutted from its helmetlike head.

One look was enough to chill Shelly to the bone.

Alarm rang through her. She had never seen this frightening creature, and it was still far away, yet she felt certain it had come for their ship.

In the next instant, it vanished. When it reappeared, it was directly above the patrol boat.

“Look out!”

Shelly searched for someone to warn, but apart from Courtney, no one was nearby. Most of the officers were in the dining room.

The strange Pokémon made its move. It barely seemed to stir, but an invisible force stopped the entire patrol boat dead. The engines kept driving against whatever held it. The hull shuddered as if it had struck a solid object, and a shrill, grinding sound of twisting metal rose from below.

The sudden jolt threw everyone aboard off their feet. Shelly, accustomed to years at sea, caught the railing as she fell and quickly stood again. She looked up at the Pokémon, her face grim.

A Psychic-type. It had to be. In Shelly's judgment, no other kind of Pokémon could hold a patrol boat this large in place. That made it all the more dangerous.

Where had it come from? Why attack this ship?

There was no time to puzzle it out. Alarms rang across the water. After their first moment of confusion, the trained International Police officers regrouped and rushed onto the deck fully armed.

Hurried footsteps sounded beside Shelly. Anabel staggered out, still dizzy from being knocked down. She gripped the railing and stared at the black Pokémon overhead.

“What... what is that?”
