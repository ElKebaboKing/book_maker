Sebastian announced the result everyone could already see. “Milotic is unable to battle. The challenger wins the Gym match!”

Two sets of applause sounded from the stands. Misty was clapping, but so was Lisia. Her eyes sparkled with excitement at Ethan's victory. Misty glared at her.

“Why are you clapping? Your teacher lost. Aren't you cheering for the wrong person?”

Lisia turned to her with a look that said, So what? “I'm happy for my friend. Ethan won. Why shouldn't I applaud?”

Your friend? Misty seethed. How long had Lisia known Ethan before claiming that? Give her a few more meetings and she might start calling him “Ethan” with the same familiarity Misty used. Did being an idol make her this shameless?

Misty could not control what Lisia said, so she strengthened her own resolve. Once the challenge was over, she and Ethan had to leave Sootopolis immediately. Lisia must not get another chance to approach him.

Both girls rose and headed down toward the field. The passage from the stands narrowed, and they reached it together. Neither gave way. After exchanging a look, they pushed forward at the same time.

Lisia had stamina from singing and dancing, but Misty was stronger. She squeezed through first, then looked back with a triumphant expression. She said nothing, yet her meaning was clear: Whether it's this passage or Ethan, you won't beat me.

Lisia glared back. You may have won this time. We'll see who wins in the end.

Ethan knew nothing of the contest in the stands. All his attention was on Combusken. The light of evolution faded, revealing a new form: Blaziken.

Its types remained Fire and Fighting, but its body and abilities had matured. White feathers crowned its head above a V-shaped crest. Its eyes were blue, its claws seemed wrapped in bandages, and powerful legs supported its taller frame.

Blaziken would face opponents bravely to the last. Ethan recalled that it burned off old feathers every few years to grow bright new ones. In battle, hot flames could burst from its wrists, burning more fiercely against stronger opponents. Its legs and waist were said to be so strong that it could scale a thirty-story building in only a few leaps.

The evolution strengthened Ethan's regular team. At his signal, Blaziken cleared the broad pool in a single jump and landed on the bank. Together they approached Juan.

Misty and Lisia had reached the field as well. Misty stood beside Ethan. Lisia stood by her defeated teacher but smiled at Ethan, leaving Juan to bear his loss without her comfort. Juan's expression grew more complicated.

However reluctantly, Juan had to admit Ethan had beaten him. He cleared his throat and adopted an easy tone. “Well, you performed nicely. About as I expected. Don't let it make you overconfident, though. You're young, and you have a long road ahead. There is plenty of room for you to grow. I hope you keep working hard.”

Juan delivered it like an elder encouraging a promising junior. Anyone who had missed the battle might think he had allowed Ethan to win.

Ethan listened with a smile and let the words pass. He had achieved what he came for. There was no reason to needle Juan again. The man was trying to save face, and Ethan was happy to give him that much. Juan had lost the match; a few grand words would do no harm.

Misty felt less charitable. They had only just met Juan, and their encounter had hardly been friendly. Why was he lecturing Ethan now? She was about to say so when Lisia cut in first.

“That's enough, Teacher.” Lisia gave Juan an exasperated look. “After battling him, do you still not know who he is?”

Juan paused. Was there more to this challenger than he realized? “Oh? Then tell me who he is.”

Lisia was about to answer, but Ethan raised a hand to stop her. He knew she meant to tell Juan about his victories in two regions. He did not mind people knowing. Yet having the same achievements announced wherever he went would eventually make it sound as though he were showing off, even when someone else spoke for him. Relying too heavily on his reputation could do more harm than good.

“Who am I?” Ethan smiled at himself. “Just an ordinary challenger who arrived in Hoenn not long ago and is preparing for the Ever Grande Conference. Nothing remarkable.”

Lisia stared, then understood why he had stopped her. She wanted more people to know and love her as an idol. She knew how many others sought fame too, along with the respect and money it could bring. Ethan seemed untouched by the temptation to announce his achievements. She admired him all the more.

He really is captivating. She looked at him happily, then turned to urge Juan to fetch the Gym Badge.

Juan still wanted an explanation, but with Lisia pressing him, he put off the question until he could ask her later. Why was his dear student taking the challenger's side already?

He took a badge from inside his coat and tossed it toward Ethan. “Take it. The Rain Badge proves that you defeated me.”

The badge spun through the air. Juan's throw was a little high. His irritation showed in the angle; if Ethan wanted to catch it himself, he would have to step back.

Before it reached him, a sturdy gray claw closed around it. Blaziken had caught the badge for its Trainer. It fixed Juan with a fierce stare, fire seeming to burn in its eyes. Without an order from Ethan, it did nothing more. After a few moments it turned and placed the badge in Ethan's hand.

Ethan examined it. Three teardrop shapes—one blue, two fading from blue to pink—formed a larger triangular drop. The design was simple. He put it away and shook his head at Juan's petty throw, then said goodbye to Lisia.

She seemed to want to say more. Ethan left with Misty and Blaziken before she found the words.

They had only just stepped outside the Gym when quick footsteps sounded behind them.

“P-please wait!” Lisia called, a little out of breath.

Misty had been silently hoping never to see her again. She took a deep breath and turned with Ethan.

“What is it?” he asked gently. He liked Lisia. She was pretty, cheerful, and full of youthful energy. Talking with her had been easy and pleasant—apart from her constant sparring with Misty.

Lisia caught her breath. “About my teacher...please don't take his behavior to heart. He's really a good person. He's afraid someone will deceive me, so he doesn't like me getting close to men. That's why he was so hostile when he saw you.”

“Don't worry,” Misty said before Ethan could answer. “Ethan won't be angry over something so small. He won't give Juan another thought.”

“I didn't ask you!” Lisia shot back. “You don't have to answer for him.”

Here they went again. Were these two born to quarrel? Ethan pulled Misty gently behind him and asked her to be quiet for a moment.

“Misty's right,” he told Lisia. “I don't mind. I've met all sorts of people on my travels, including plenty whose attitudes were worse than your teacher's. By tomorrow, I may have forgotten the whole thing.”

“I'm glad.” Lisia let out a long breath. She met his eyes, then looked down shyly. “Um...will we see each other again?”

Misty nearly protested, but she remembered Ethan had asked her to stay quiet. She fumed behind him instead.

“I think we will,” Ethan said. “There's a long road ahead. We might cross paths anywhere, someday. And as a Coordinator, you'll visit cities that host Contests. That gives us plenty of chances to meet.”

“You're right!” Lisia brightened. “After the Ever Grande Conference, when you leave Hoenn, where will you go?”

“If nothing changes, Sinnoh.”

“Sinnoh?” Her eyes lit up. “Really? That's wonderful! My work will be centered there next. It must be fate. We can meet in Sinnoh too!”

Lisia insisted on exchanging contact details with Ethan. Only then did he and Misty finally leave the street where the Gym stood.

Misty remained gloomy as they walked. She had failed to shake Lisia off. Now Lisia could contact Ethan, and they might meet again in the near future. Why was she so hard to escape?

Lost in thought, Misty followed Ethan for some time before she noticed where they were going. This was not the way to the port. They were heading back to the Pokémon Center.

She pouted, hurt and suspicious. “Why are we going back there? Do you miss that girl already? Are we staying a few more days?”

Ethan flicked her forehead. “What nonsense. Several of my Pokémon are injured. They need treatment first. There's also something important I haven't taken care of. I need somewhere we won't be disturbed.”

Strangely, the little flick swept away Misty's annoyance. She returned to her usual spirited self. Ethan was right: despite the time his Feebas training had taken over the past few days, another unfinished matter was waiting for him.
