In Petalburg Gym's reception room, Ethan and Misty sat a little stiffly before a low table laden with fruit and snacks. Norman and Caroline sat across from them.

Ethan took a sip of tea, paused, then continued. “...And that was when Misty punched Drew in the face. She sent him stumbling and gave May the satisfaction of seeing someone stand up for her!”

He was talking about the unpleasant encounter with Drew before they had joined May at the Slateport Contest.

May spoke to her parents every day, but Ethan doubted she had told them about any of this. She would not have wanted them to worry.

Sure enough, Norman and Caroline frowned as they listened, then turned to Misty.

“May only tells us the good news,” Caroline said. “She never mentioned that she'd been treated like that. If you hadn't told us, we still wouldn't know. Misty...thank you so much! May is lucky to have a friend like you.”

Misty shifted awkwardly and gave an embarrassed smile. “You're too kind. May and I haven't known each other long, but we got along right away. That boy was acting so superior. I only did what a friend should do!”

Her modest answer made Norman and Caroline like her all the more. She was as pretty as their daughter and about the same age, and they appreciated her frankness, even with that slightly fiery streak.

Caroline turned eagerly back to Ethan. “What happened after that?”

“After that...we met Mr. Big and Dr. Abby. Their help made a big difference to May. She improved enough to reach the second round in her very first Contest. I expect she's told you that part, but there are details you might not know. Misty can tell you about those.”

Ethan handed the story over to Misty. His mouth was dry after talking for so long, despite all the tea he had drunk. Besides, Misty knew May better than he did. Before Norman and Caroline asked him something he could not answer, he might as well let her take over and enjoy a rest.

Misty had no idea what he was thinking. She launched into a lively account of the day of the Contest, adding her own opinions as she went. For May's parents, it was an entirely new view of events, full of things their daughter had never mentioned.

They listened with interest. The conversation went on for most of the day.

The clock's hands made their rounds. Outside the window, white clouds drifted past, sometimes fast and sometimes slow. It seemed to be a fine day.

“Yawn...”

Ethan had lost count of how often he had yawned. His cup had been refilled several times, he had been to the bathroom three times, and his legs had gone numb. Still, Norman and Caroline showed no sign of bringing the conversation to a close.

He was bored out of his mind.

How could Caroline and Misty have so much to talk about? They had gone from May to Max, from Max to their own interests and hobbies, and somehow circled back to May. There seemed to be no end to it.

He had got up especially early and arrived that morning to make sure nothing delayed the battle. Somehow, the battle had been delayed anyway.

He glanced at Norman. The man listened to his wife and Misty with a faint smile, nodding now and then. When he noticed Ethan looking, he returned his gaze warmly.

Norman could listen to every little detail without a trace of impatience. He really was a good man, a good father, and a good husband.

Ethan thought of the several women with whom his own relationships had grown complicated. Compared with Norman, he felt himself edging closer to being a scumbag.

At last, a loud growl from his stomach brought the long conversation to an abrupt halt.

Everyone looked at him. Ethan rubbed his stomach and smiled ruefully. “I didn't eat much this morning. I'm hungry.”

“Oh!” Caroline glanced at the clock and smiled apologetically. “It's noon already? I'm sorry I've kept you so long! Please wait here while I make lunch. You don't visit us often, so we must treat you properly today!”

Before Ethan or Misty could decline, or even make a polite protest, Caroline was on her feet and gone.

Once she had left, Norman turned to them. “I'm sorry. You must have been ready to get up ages ago. Caroline's been lonely since May and Max left to travel. She hasn't quite adjusted. That's why I let her keep you talking. As a man, I can't talk with her about these things the same way, and you're May's friends. I couldn't bring myself to interrupt her.”

So that was it. Ethan and Misty understood at last.

That explained why Norman, who normally would never keep a challenger waiting this long, had allowed the visit to go on without getting to the battle.

...

Lunch was a lavish seafood meal that filled the whole table. Caroline clearly knew how to prepare it: there was plenty of variety, and everything tasted fresh and delicious.

Ethan and Misty ate happily. If only Caroline had not kept asking them questions throughout the meal, they might have enjoyed it even more.

After lunch, Ethan finally got his Gym battle with Norman.

They took their places in the Trainer boxes on opposite sides of Petalburg Gym's battlefield. A very young apprentice stepped out to serve as referee.

Before the apprentice could explain the rules, Norman stopped him. Folding his arms, he addressed Ethan. “I don't need to go over the rules for you, do I?”

Ethan shook his head. Before parting with May and Max, he had heard Max talk at length about the Gym. He knew the match would be three against three. If Ethan had not stopped him, the boy might even have listed the weaknesses of every one of his father's Pokémon. Max admired Ethan that much.

What a disloyal son.

“How would you like to battle?” Norman asked. “Do you want us both to use our strongest teams? To be honest, I'd like to face a Champion who's won in two regions.”

“Please don't.” Ethan shook his head. “I've come to challenge the Gym, but I mainly want to train my Pokémon. Treat me like an ordinary challenger.”

Norman was a Trainer close to Elite Four level. Ethan had no intention of testing himself against someone like that yet. After he had traveled around the world and challenged the Gyms in each region, he could return for a serious battle.

“I see.” Norman did not seem to mind. He nodded. “Then I understand.”

He took a Poké Ball from his jacket and threw it. A flash of blue light revealed the Pokémon Ethan had seen Norman training when they arrived.

It was a Slakoth.

It looked like the sloths Ethan knew from Earth, with a vacant face, gray-brown fur, and darker stripes across its back.

In the stands, Misty aimed her Pokédex at it.

“Beep. Slakoth, the Slacker Pokémon. Normal type. Slakoth spends most of every day asleep, lying down for more than twenty hours. Three leaves are enough to satisfy it for a day. Its heart beats only once per minute, and it is almost never seen moving.”
