The Ruins of Alph?

Ethan searched his memory. He knew a little about this place. Fossils of ancient Pokémon were embedded in its rock layers, and the ruins themselves held many unsolved mysteries.

They were home to a Pokémon little understood by humans—the Unown—and even concealed an entrance to the Sinjoh Ruins.

People in this world might know little about the Sinjoh Ruins, but Ethan had played the games. He knew they contained an altar called the Mystri Stage, built specifically to worship Arceus.

Arceus was the most powerful Pokémon, known as the creator of the world.

Anyone who reached the place and presented an offering to Arceus could receive the creator's blessing.

Who would have thought that getting lost would bring them here by sheer luck?

Ethan currently had neither the ability nor a way to enter the Sinjoh Ruins, but exploring this area still sounded worthwhile. If he searched through the rocks and soil, he might be lucky enough to find a few fossils or make some other unexpected discovery.

Despite those thoughts, he did not immediately begin digging at random. He and Sabrina first crossed the valley, following a well-packed dirt road to the building at its end.

Only when they drew near did they realize that the enormous domed structure was a multifunctional research center combining scientific study, ecology, and public exhibitions.

The exhibition hall was open to the public free of charge, so they pushed open the doors and went inside.

Cool air flowed through the building, making it much more comfortable than outdoors.

The entire first floor served as an exhibition hall. It was divided into many sections, and most of the exhibits concerned fossils.

A door at one side led to another area. Its sign identified the Ancient Pokémon Study Park, but the park appeared unfinished and was not yet open to the public.

Several other visitors wandered through the exhibits, keeping the hall from feeling too deserted.

Soft light fell over the displays, seeming to veil them in mystery.

Ethan smiled at Sabrina and offered her his hand.

She smiled back and gently linked her arm through his.

The young couple wandered through the exhibition hall. Every so often, they stopped before a display case, studied the object inside, and spoke quietly together.

Most of the exhibits were nothing unusual, merely fossils from Pokémon such as Kabutops, Omanyte, Anorith, and Lileep.

As they continued, Ethan suddenly stopped before one display.

Inside stood an exquisite sculpture. Many of its details differed, yet it bore a striking resemblance to the Mythical Pokémon Hoopa.

Just as Ethan leaned closer for a better look, a young scholar in a research coat approached him.

"Beautiful, isn't it?" The scholar also looked into the display case, speaking almost to himself. "It's difficult to imagine that people in such a primitive age could already create craftsmanship like this."

Ethan turned toward him. The man was tall and powerfully built, especially for a scholar or researcher. His hair rose in sharp spikes. Rather than seeming dull and withdrawn like some researchers, he gave off an open and cheerful impression.

Noticing Ethan's gaze, the scholar introduced himself.

"Hello. My name is Foster, and I'm the chief researcher at the Ruins of Alph park. Mr. Vale, did you come here specifically to visit us?"

Ethan looked at him in surprise. "You know me?"

"Of course. You're quite famous!" Foster nodded. "I've watched your matches, so I recognized you the moment you walked through the door. I've also read the paper you published with Professor Oak and Professor Ivy."

That paper again...

Ethan had merely benefited from the reputation of the two professors, yet it had made him recognizable to so many people.

Of course, that was no bad thing. It gave him common ground with researchers like Foster and sometimes made his travels more convenient, just as it had now.

Seeing Ethan's understanding, Foster added, "Oh, I forgot to mention that I attended Celadon University. I was one of Professor Oak's students."

Another Celadon University graduate...

Professor Oak truly had students everywhere. His own reputation was extraordinary, and his former pupils were scattered throughout the world, playing important roles wherever they went.

"It's a pleasure to meet you." Ethan shook Foster's hand and asked warmly, "What field do you study, Mr. Foster?"

"I specialize in the ancient history of Pokémon." Foster smiled broadly. "Some people believe Pokémon first appeared two million years ago, a theory based on Kabuto fossils found in the geological strata. I support the ocean-origin theory of Pokémon. I believe they appeared even earlier than two million years ago!"

An ocean-origin theory?

He had not expected a different world to produce such a similar hypothesis.

Ethan turned back and studied the sculpture again. "Mr. Foster, why do I feel that this... artifact resembles..."

Foster finished the thought for him. "You think it resembles Hoopa, don't you?"

"Yes."

Foster chuckled. "Evidence has long suggested that, at one point in history, some of Sinnoh's early inhabitants migrated here and became part of Johto. During their journey, they absorbed cultural traits from many other regions, including Kalos. That is why the sculpture we see today resembles Hoopa without matching it exactly."

"I see!"

Ethan had arrived in this world from another one and had never attended school here, so his knowledge of its history was limited. Now that he had finally met an authority on the subject, he asked humbly for guidance.

"Mr. Foster, what exactly are the Ruins of Alph?"

Perhaps because he had taken a liking to Ethan, Foster answered without reservation.

"Johto's earliest record of civilization dates back roughly fifteen hundred years, and its birthplace was this place beneath our feet, now known as the Ruins of Alph. No one knows who built it, but our research shows that its civilization is extremely ancient. It even developed the earliest known writing system!"

Foster's explanation gave Ethan a deeper understanding of this world.

The more he learned, the stronger his sense of belonging became, and the more he grew to love this place.

Then Foster added, "By the way, we provide tools free of charge. If you're interested, you can try your luck outside and see whether you can discover any rare fossils!"
