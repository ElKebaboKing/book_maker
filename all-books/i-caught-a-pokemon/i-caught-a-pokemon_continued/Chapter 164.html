Logan shook his head and drove away the absurd thought.

Damn it. I'm not cornered yet. Why am I already giving up?

He recalled Rhyhorn and took out his third Poké Ball.

"Go, Venusaur!"

Venusaur had been his first Pokémon. By sending his strongest partner out now, Logan hoped to stop Ethan's momentum and at least win one bout back.

Across the field, Gyarados's Flying typing gave it an advantage over Venusaur's Grass typing, but its Water typing was vulnerable to Grass-type attacks in turn.

Following his usual approach, Ethan recalled Gyarados and reached for another Pokémon to keep the pressure on. Before he could make his choice, he felt the shadow at his feet stir.

Haunter rose halfway out of it.

"That Venusaur looks like his strongest Pokémon. Why don't you let me handle this one?"

"You want a turn too?" Ethan raised an eyebrow. He quickly considered how the two Pokémon matched up, then nodded. "All right, go ahead. Be careful, though. An easy-looking battle can still turn on you."

"I know!" Haunter rolled its eyes and floated completely out of the shadow. It drifted toward the field without looking back. "Just stand there and watch me work."

At the commentary booth, the host smiled when he saw Logan's Venusaur.

"What a choice! Logan has sent in Venusaur now. It's one of his most impressive partners, and it may be exactly what he needs to turn this match around—or at least take one point!"

Like many experts, the host expected Ethan to win the championship. He did not expect another overwhelming sweep. Surely Logan would defeat at least two of Ethan's Pokémon.

Lance had a different view.

"Perhaps," he said with a knowing smile. "But from what I've seen, Haunter is one of Ethan's strongest Pokémon too. It won't go down easily."

"Really? Haunter is his strongest?" the host asked. "I assumed Gardevoir or Gyarados was his ace. In that case, an ace against an ace should be quite a show!"

Sometimes it was. Other times, an apparent contest between two aces became a one-sided defeat.

As the bout began, the crowd watched in astonishment. Logan's Venusaur seemed unable to touch Haunter.

Poison Powder and Sleep Powder were swept away by strange gusts of wind. Haunter slipped in and out of sight, avoiding the rest of Venusaur's moves.

Even a fully charged Solar Beam was canceled out by a Shadow Ball that looked almost casual.

What!?

Logan stared, stunned. What was this monster? How could it be so strong?

The spectators began to whisper as well. Weren't they watching a clash between two aces? Why did Venusaur look so helpless?

Ethan stood with his hands in his pockets, relaxed. He had not given a single command. Haunter had driven Venusaur to the brink on its own.

The host heard the murmurs and felt his face heat up. He had been so confident about the ace-versus-ace spectacle, and already the match had made a fool of him. At least nobody seemed to be paying attention to his commentary now.

Finally, Haunter appeared behind Venusaur and struck with a devastating Night Shade. Venusaur fell, bringing the score to three to zero.

In Indigo Stadium's control room, live broadcast director John watched the ratings climb and could hardly stop smiling.

Ethan's strength and popularity had proved how wise John's earlier decision had been. Directing more resources toward Ethan had brought in viewers and increased John's own influence within the Pokémon League.

Looking at the handsome, cool-looking trainer on the screen, John liked his choice more than ever.

The door opened behind him. He turned and saw President Goodshow walking in.

"Mr. President!"

Staff members hurried to their feet. Goodshow waved away the ceremony and walked over to John.

"How are the ratings?"

"Not bad." John kept his face calm, though pride leaked into his voice. "We've only broken the all-time record and gained a few more ratings points. Nothing special."

Goodshow stared at him.

It wasn't John out there battling. Why did he sound so proud?

Shaking his head, Goodshow turned to the main screen.

The trainers had switched Pokémon again, and the fourth bout was underway. Logan had sent out Scyther; Ethan had answered with Jolteon.

As a Bug- and Flying-type, Scyther was taking a terrible beating from Jolteon's Electric-type moves. It could barely stand.

Goodshow watched for a while, then sighed. "Ah, it's wonderful to be young. If I were thirty years younger, I'd enter the tournament and chase my dreams too."

"You're still young, Mr. President! I can see you going strong for another thirty years," John said. Then curiosity got the better of him. "Were you a trainer when you were younger? I bet you were impressive."

It was meant as ordinary flattery. To John's surprise, Goodshow laughed.

"You're right! I was quite a famous trainer in Kanto in my day. Actually, Ethan reminds me a little of myself back then. Ha ha ha!"

Reminds him of himself?

John's eyes widened. He looked at Ethan's tall, lean figure on the screen, then at the short old man beside him. He could not imagine two people with appearances more different.

He rubbed his eyes and checked Ethan's handsome face again. Then he looked once more at Goodshow's weathered features.

Come on. They looked nothing alike.

John swallowed the words that had almost escaped him. Mr. President, you'd need a much straighter face than that to make anyone believe you!
