Ethan had won a useful early advantage, but Anabel's Metagross was still a little stronger than his. That was only natural. It was one of her main partners and had spent a long time beside her, battling a stream of challengers at the Tower. Its strength had been tested and refined constantly.

Ethan's Metagross had fought its share of battles, but it was still developing. Compared with most Trainers' Pokémon it was excellent; compared with the partner of a truly accomplished Metagross user, it fell slightly short. More time and experience would close that gap. There was no way to rush it.

Ethan knew this, which was why he took the round so seriously. After the poor opening, Anabel grew more cautious. Her steady, carefully measured attacks made the fight harder. She was the Battle Tower's Frontier Brain for good reason.

The Metagross clashed again and again. Anabel's pushed Ethan's to the brink more than once, only for his direction to help it recover lost ground. In the end, they were evenly matched. Both fired Hyper Beam in a final exchange, and both fell.

The green-haired referee raised his hand. “Both Metagross are unable to battle! This round is a draw!”

Anabel and Ethan saw the result differently. She found it frustrating. Her Metagross was stronger, and her silent communication with it gave her another advantage. By every measure, she should have won. Ethan's command had dragged the round to a tie anyway.

For Ethan, it was a fine outcome. His relatively less powerful Metagross had taken one of Anabel's main Pokémon out with it. The experience would strengthen its confidence and skill. The effort had cost Ethan some mental energy, but a good meal and rest would restore that. If it helped him win, it was worth spending.

The tally now stood at two to one in his favor. Anabel felt a pressure she had not known in years. She had been behind before, but usually when she was holding back or had not chosen her strongest team. Today she was fighting in earnest, yet Ethan had driven her to this point. It reminded her of her first journey as a Trainer, when she had been too weak to answer a formidable opponent.

She had thought those days were behind her. Now that helpless feeling had returned. She shivered; excitement and tension prickled her arms. She wanted to defeat the man facing her, while knowing how wide the gap between them might be.

Anabel recalled her unconscious Metagross and took a deep breath. She tried several times to calm herself, but could not quiet the heat rising within her. The desire to overcome this opponent came from somewhere too deep for that.

Ethan recalled his Metagross as well. He did not share her turmoil, but he had learned how difficult she was and could sense her determination. He had put her under tremendous pressure.

He took out his third Poké Ball. Almost at the same moment, Anabel released her last Pokémon. Blue light flashed, and Scizor and Espeon appeared on the field.

May sat straighter in the stands. She knew Ethan's Scizor well. The Espeon across from it drew her attention.

Espeon resembled a small cat, less than a meter tall and light on its feet. Purple fur with a velvet sheen covered its body, its eyes were purple, and a red gem rested on its forehead. Its design suggested a legendary two-tailed cat as well as the features of a fennec fox.

People said Espeon had developed its power to foresee danger in order to protect itself. It was fiercely loyal to a Trainer it trusted, and that protective instinct sharpened its ability to predict what lay ahead. The short, fine fur all over its body sensed tiny movements in the air. From those changes it could anticipate the weather and an opponent's actions; the tips of its forked tail moved subtly when it was reading an opponent.

It was less suited to battling at night. In sunlight, the red gem on its forehead glowed as it stored psychic power, which Espeon could then release through the gem. Once that power was exhausted, the gem's color faded.

It was an elegant Pokémon, and understandably popular. May's interest went beyond that. She was already thinking about how her newly hatched Eevee might evolve one day.

What did Ethan call thinking ahead? Planning before the need arose? Preparing for rain before it fell? Whatever the phrase was, there was no harm in considering it early. Espeon was one possibility she liked, but the other evolutions appealed to her too. Seeing one battle would help her decide.

The final selections had been made blind. Ethan had not known Anabel would send Espeon. Seeing his Bug/Steel-type Scizor face her Psychic-type made him feel a little sorry for her. Already behind, she now had an unfavorable type matchup. Anabel's face showed a trace of bitterness when she saw it.

Determination soon replaced that look. Ethan nodded to himself. She was young, but she would not give up. She deserved her place at the Battle Tower.

For a moment he considered switching Scizor for Dragonite. As the challenger, he was allowed to do so. But Anabel had decided to fight through the disadvantage. Changing his Pokémon now, even with good intentions, would seem disrespectful. He kept Scizor in.

Anabel stood at the edge of defeat. Normally, losing a challenge would be no disaster. A Frontier Brain was there not merely to guard a facility, but to test Trainers and award a Symbol when one earned it, spreading the Battle Frontier's reputation. Today was different. She had brought out all her main Pokémon and treated Ethan as a formidable opponent. Losing on those terms could shake her confidence.

Then again, a battle this demanding might teach her something and help her grow. What she gained or lost would depend on how she understood it afterward.

Scizor and Espeon took their positions. After two rounds of powerful moves, the wooden floor had begun to break. Splinters and raised boards marked the arena, though Ethan had not even brought out one of his heavier Pokémon. The damage might affect this final round.

“The third round begins now!” the referee called with another sweep of his arm.

Anabel's three Pokémon all had Psychic typing, yet the first two had also been capable at close quarters. Ethan was ready for Espeon to fight that way too. It was one reason he had chosen Scizor blindly. Bug and Steel typing made it less vulnerable against Psychic opponents, and Scizor excelled up close.

Both Pokémon moved at the signal. Like Metagross in the previous round, Espeon opened with Psychic. Ethan had anticipated it. At almost the same instant, Scizor used Double Team. The move let it avoid the invisible attack, then sent several red figures rushing toward Espeon together.

Which one was real? Anabel's eye for detail would be tested.

She watched closely and picked correctly. At a silent command from her, Espeon fired Psychic at the figure on the far right.

Ethan saw that she had found the original. He ordered Quick Attack. Scizor accelerated and escaped the strike. The rush had brought it close to Espeon, however.

At the sight of Scizor's gleaming pincers, Anabel kept her composure. She did not have Espeon trade blows. It used Agility, ran clear, and opened a safe distance. Scizor's attack struck nothing.

Ethan understood the plan: use Espeon's speed to stay out of reach and wear Scizor down bit by bit. He could see what Anabel was doing, yet had no easy answer. The speed difference was too great. Espeon was the second-fastest of Eevee's evolutions, behind Jolteon but far ahead of most Pokémon. Scizor, sheathed in heavy-looking steel, could not simply chase it down.

From safety, Espeon used Psychic again. Ethan had Scizor dodge while he searched for a way through. Psychic attacks did only half damage to its Steel typing, but evasion could not continue forever. If Anabel and Espeon stayed patient, they could still exhaust Scizor.

He needed a more forceful tactic. Ethan made his choice.

Scizor used Double Team to avoid another Psychic. Including the original, six red figures charged Espeon at once.

Anabel was surprised he had repeated the same maneuver. Perhaps, she thought, her evasive strategy had left him with no other option. She did not let that assumption make her careless. Once more she studied the figures for the real Scizor.

Recognizing a Double Team once often made it easier the next time. A Trainer learned how the Pokémon moved, and success gave them confidence. Anabel watched for a few moments and concluded that the far-right figure was the original again.

She silently ordered Espeon to attack.

Just as it began, she caught a movement from the far-left figure. Her heart jolted. Was that the original? Had Ethan deliberately shown her a false pattern the first time?

With no time to think further, Anabel sent another urgent command, redirecting Espeon toward the figure on the far left. Its invisible Psychic power struck almost instantly.

The figure shattered. It had been a copy.

Anabel had guessed wrong. If that was only a shadow, where was the real Scizor now?
