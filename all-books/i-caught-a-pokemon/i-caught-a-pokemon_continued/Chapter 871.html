Ethan scanned the sea below for an island where they could shelter from the storm.

They had flown too far from the Whirl Islands. Blue water stretched in every direction, with no land, island, or coral reef in sight. There was nowhere to hide from the storm, not even a place to land.

“Ethan! Look over there!”

May's sharp eyes had picked out a small dark speck on the sea beneath the storm.

Ethan looked closely. It was a little boat adrift on the water. It was not large, but it would be more than enough to keep off the wind and rain—provided it could withstand the storm without sinking.

He patted Charizard's neck. Understanding, Charizard descended toward the vessel.

It was only a little over ten meters long, with a single wheelhouse that looked flimsy against the sea.

As they drew nearer, Ethan saw it was neither a fishing boat nor a passenger or sightseeing vessel. Apart from a winch, the deck was empty, and a long antenna rose above the wheelhouse. It looked like a privately owned research boat.

Waves and strong winds rocked it, but for now it seemed secure.

Whatever kind of boat it was, it was their only choice. The storm was almost upon them. Ethan thought he could already smell rain in the wind.

Charizard dropped to wheelhouse height and flew just above the sea. Ethan turned to look inside.

A light was on. A red-haired woman was busy at the controls.

Rather than jump aboard and startle her, Ethan waved from Charizard's back.

Perhaps Charizard's orange form stood out over the water, or perhaps his wave caught her eye. Either way, the woman soon noticed him and May. She looked startled, then opened the door and hurried out.

“Excuse me!” Ethan raised his voice over the wind. “Could we come aboard to shelter from the storm?”

The small vessel might not be entirely safe, but it would at least spare them a soaking and keep them out of the lightning.

The woman did not hesitate. She waved them over. “Hurry, get on!”

With her permission, Ethan jumped onto the deck and helped May down after him. Once they were steady, he recalled Charizard into its Poké Ball.

The woman ushered them into the wheelhouse. Only then did Ethan get a good look at her.

She seemed to be twenty-five or twenty-six. Her long, fiery red hair was tied in a casual ponytail, and she had fair skin and a pretty face. Perhaps for comfort during long stretches at sea, she wore only a white tank top above the waist. It gave her a brisk, capable air.

As soon as she shut the door, the wind's roar softened. They could still feel the boat rocking, but at least they were out of the weather.

The woman returned to the controls. Without looking back, she asked, “You two are Trainers, aren't you?”

“That's right. Thank you for letting us aboard. Otherwise we'd have had to face the storm out there.” Ethan nodded, then he and May introduced themselves.

“People ought to help one another when they're away from home. I'm sure anyone else would have done the same.” She smiled. “I'm Luka. You could call me a researcher of sorts.”

Like the color of her hair, Luka had a warm, lively manner.

“A researcher of sorts?” May asked. “Why only ‘of sorts’? What do you study, Miss Luka?”

Steering toward the edge of the storm, Luka laughed. “Just the Pokémon living around here. I don't have a doctorate or anything, so ‘of sorts’ seems right.”

There were many independent researchers like Luka. They had no official funding, titles, or positions. What they did have was a boundless love of Pokémon and the determination to devote themselves to studying them.

While they spoke, the storm reached them.

The world around the boat suddenly darkened. Rain poured down, enormous waves rolled past, and lightning flashed through the sky, briefly illuminating everything.

With the storm all around them, no one felt like talking. They fell silent and watched through the windows, their moods rising and falling with the waves.

Ethan was not worried for his own safety. He had received the sea's blessing, and he had so many Pokémon with him. What could a storm do to him? He simply did not want himself and May left drenched and miserable.

In fact, his presence aboard might protect Luka. If the boat capsized, he could help her. That was the worst case, though, and he hoped the vessel would come through intact.

Under Luka's hand, the little boat skirted the storm's center and headed for its edge, tossing like a leaf on the sea.

Her skill at the helm found them a comparatively safe route. It took considerable effort to navigate the wind and towering waves, but she brought them out without mishap.

Luka really was an old hand at the wheel—no, a seasoned captain. It helped that she had altered course before the storm reached them. Without that head start, the passage would never have gone so smoothly.

At last the clouds parted and the sky above them cleared again.

There was one problem.

Ethan checked the map on his phone and shook his head. They had avoided the storm, but their course had shifted. He could no longer tell where they had drifted from the waters near the Whirl Islands.

Reaching Ecruteak City would take a longer route now.

While he studied the map, May suddenly cried out, “Ethan! Look—an iceberg!”

“An iceberg?” Before Ethan could answer, Luka shook her head. “You must have seen something else. There have never been icebergs in these waters. It's too warm, and the climate isn't right for them. Wait—that really is an iceberg!”

She had turned her head in the middle of her objection and seen it herself. Ethan looked up too.

A huge, uneven mass of ice was drifting slowly toward them. There was no mistaking it.

“Incredible,” Luka said. “I've sailed these waters for years, and I've never seen an iceberg here.”

“Maybe the storm carried it in,” Ethan suggested. He had no better explanation.

Then they noticed shapes moving across the ice.

They were too far away to make out clearly, but their silhouettes did not look human. They seemed to be large Pokémon. All three grew curious.

“Should we go and see?” May asked.

Luka answered by turning the wheel. The boat headed straight for the iceberg.

A few minutes later, they could see its whole shape. It was not one solid mountain of ice. Several smaller bergs were joined to a flat expanse, where groups of snow-white Snorlax were frolicking and playing.

Apart from their color, they appeared no different from ordinary Snorlax in size or any other feature. Against the ice, though, they were a remarkable sight.

“White Snorlax?” May exclaimed. “Are they all Shiny Pokémon?”

Luka hesitated, then shook her head. “They certainly look different, but I thought Shiny Snorlax were blue. I've never heard of a white one, much less a whole group of them.”

May turned to Ethan. “Do you know what's going on?”

He thought for a moment. “I remember a legend from somewhere in Johto. They say that if you build a snowman on the coldest night of the year, it will come to life as a Snowman Snorlax.”

May drew a sharp breath. “Really?” She looked back at the Snorlax. Their white bodies did resemble snowmen. “So all of these used to be snowmen that came alive?”

“Silly.” Ethan tapped her on the head. “It's a folk tale. Surely you know that can't be true. These Snowman Snorlax are probably a group with an unusual coloration that people haven't fully studied yet.”

May held her head and stuck out her tongue. “I suppose so.”

By then the boat had reached the iceberg. Luka cut the engine, went out on deck, and looped a rope around a protruding block of ice to secure the vessel.

When she was finished, the three of them climbed onto the ice. The Snowman Snorlax did not seem surprised by their arrival. They only watched the newcomers curiously.

Luka took out her camera and snapped more than a dozen photographs. Then she turned to Ethan and May. “Sorry, you two. I'm going to stay here for quite a while and study these Snowman Snorlax. Feel free to go on without me.”

For an independent Pokémon researcher, finding a place like this was an extraordinary chance. She intended to spend several days observing the Pokémon's behavior and habits in this nearly untouched refuge.

She did not mean to reveal its location, but she wanted to satisfy her own curiosity.

Seeing Luka prepare to make camp, Ethan and May thanked her again and headed off on their own. They had met by chance; now it was time to part.

They left the shore and walked toward the iceberg's interior. Snowman Snorlax were everywhere. Ethan estimated there were more than a hundred.

That alone was astonishing. Snorlax had enormous appetites; a whole small island might be needed to feed just one. The amount of food this many would eat in a day was almost beyond reckoning.

May, who had a Munchlax of her own, found it especially hard to believe.

“Where are we going, Ethan?”

Ethan took a Poké Ball from his belt pouch and smiled. “Now that we've found Snowman Snorlax, I can't pass up the chance.”

“You're going to catch one?”

“Of course. I've always liked Snorlax, and these are especially rare. I've never even seen one before. How could I let the chance go?”

Ethan had not expected two surprises so soon after returning to Johto. First Entei a few days ago, and now Snowman Snorlax. Both were unexpected discoveries.

May felt a little envious. A Snowman Snorlax…

Her own Munchlax was adorable, but these Pokémon were so rare. If she caught one, it would hardly need to perform in a Pokémon Contest. A few small movements onstage might be enough to earn high marks.

But she already had a Munchlax.

They walked slowly among the Snowman Snorlax while Ethan searched for one to catch.

He was not the Trainer who had just set out on his journey anymore. As one of the world's strongest Trainers, he no longer caught every Pokémon that interested him. With so many to choose from in a single group, he needed to consider his choice carefully.

He kept walking and looking until they reached an ice forest near the center of the berg. There he finally found a suitable one.

Beneath an ice tree sat a Snowman Snorlax larger and fatter than the others, steadily tossing white fruit into its mouth. From its bearing, if it was not the group's leader, it was at least a dominant Pokémon.
