Ethan and May stayed at the Kimono Sisters' home over the following days.

May practiced constantly. Apart from meals, sleep, and a few everyday chores, she spent every spare moment working with her Pokémon on the lawn. Miki, Kuni, and Zuki offered advice from their experience as stage performers whenever they had time. May took it seriously, and her performances improved rapidly.

As for Ethan, he did spend time training the Pokémon in his reserve team and the Snowman Snorlax he had recently caught. He also considered how the three legendary beasts might combine their powers in an attack. But more often, even during the day, he disappeared into Miki's, Kuni's, or Zuki's room for hours at a time.

He was like a tireless bee, always returning to the flowers for honey.

Naoko, Sayo, and May knew perfectly well what he was doing and pretended not to notice. What they could not miss was how the three older sisters glowed. Their skin and color improved day by day, and they looked as radiant as flowers at their height. Even the other women found themselves admiring their beauty.

Most of all, Miki, Kuni, and Zuki wore smiles that came from genuine happiness. The whole house felt lighter for it.

As the Grand Festival drew near, those days had to end. Brandon still had not contacted Ethan. Whatever the reason, he had rested long enough and it was time to leave.

Parting was painful, especially after the closeness they had shared. The sisters knew Ethan had ambitions that would not let him stay in one place until he had reached his goals. Understanding did little to ease the sadness.

They tried to keep their composure and helped pack everything he would need. Even Zuki stopped sparring with him. She stayed quietly at his side whenever she could, making the most of the time left.

On the night before his departure, Ethan and the three older sisters gathered in one room. No one said it aloud, but none of them wanted to surrender their last night together to another, not even a sister. They shared his company and finally fell asleep together beneath the covers.

Once again, Ethan had Ho-Oh, his new best friend, to thank. Its blessing had left him with plenty of stamina. If anyone spoke ill of Ho-Oh now, he would be the first to object.

...

After breakfast the next morning, Ethan and May stood outside the front gate with their backpacks, saying goodbye to the five sisters.

Miki straightened Ethan's collar and quietly repeated reminders he had heard many times already. He listened without complaint, then drew her into a long embrace.

“I know. Take good care of yourselves too.”

She nodded against him. After a moment she stepped back to make room for her sisters.

Kuni met him with a gentle smile. “Be careful on the road. Call us often, and tell us about everything interesting you see.”

“I will. I'll send photos and videos too.”

Kuni showed her love more quietly than Miki or Zuki. Ethan treasured the warmth in her gaze whenever she looked at him. He held her, breathed in the scent of her hair, and kissed her cheek.

She blushed. They were standing outside, where anyone might see them. She did not object to people knowing about their relationship, but choosing when to share it was different from becoming the subject of gossip by accident.

Fortunately it was early, and few people passed through this wealthy neighborhood of walled homes. No one witnessed the kiss.

Then Ethan turned to Zuki. Before he could open his arms, she hugged him and kissed him boldly. He tried to speak, but she deepened the kiss. Kuni, Naoko, and Sayo flushed at the sight.

When they finally parted, Zuki gave a small huff. “You're my man now. You're traveling for me too, since I can't go myself. Catch more powerful Pokémon, and don't lose. Come back with honors I can be proud of.”

She had placed her own wish to travel in his hands. Ethan smiled and nodded. “I'm aiming for the very top. I'll give you every reason to be proud.”

He looked toward Naoko and Sayo. They were the youngest sisters, and now they were family too; he had become their brother-in-law. He meant to say something fitting, but Naoko's expectant look drew him forward. Almost without thinking, he gave her a light hug.

Naoko blushed harder. Miki, Kuni, and Zuki traded meaningful glances. Ethan released her at once and hugged Sayo just as lightly, with the easy affection of family. The older sisters relaxed.

Focused on Ethan, they missed how quickly Naoko's and Sayo's hearts were beating.

May bowed to all five. “Miss Miki, Miss Kuni, Miss Zuki, Miss Naoko, Miss Sayo—thank you for taking care of me!”

“Do your best at the Grand Festival,” Miki said. “We'll be watching you on television.”

“And make good use of all the work Naoko and Sayo put into your training,” Kuni added.

The two younger sisters offered May their own heartfelt wishes. Moved, she embraced each of them.

“It's getting late,” Ethan said when they separated. “We should go.”

The five sisters placed their hands before them and bowed in a traditional farewell. “May your journey be safe and your battles victorious.”

Ethan waved. “Go back inside. I'll return when I have the chance!”

Holding back his reluctance to leave, he released Charizard. He and May climbed on, and at his quiet instruction Charizard spread its wings. It rose into the sky and carried them out of sight.

The sisters watched until Charizard was no more than a black speck. Miki was the first to gather herself. “They're gone. Come on, let's get our minds back to what we have to do.”

The others finally looked away. “It feels strange already,” Kuni said with a sigh. “After living together all these days, the house will be so quiet without them.”

“It took only a few days for you to become this attached,” Miki said fondly. “Imagine if they had stayed longer. We've had a good rest, though. It's time to return to the stage.”

“That's right.” Zuki straightened. “Trainers have been arriving in Ecruteak to search for Ho-Oh. We can tell its legend to them through our performances.”

...

Charizard flew throughout the day and landed near the Grand Festival venue at the Indigo Plateau around dusk. The competition would begin the day after next. Ethan had timed their arrival so May could rest and prepare tomorrow, become accustomed to the atmosphere, and enter the Festival the following day.

Charizard had barely touched down when a familiar voice called from beside the open ground. “Over here!”

Ash, Brock, and Max stood up from a rock and waved to Ethan and May. Smiling, the two of them climbed down and went to meet their friends.

The reunion eased the sadness Ethan had carried from Ecruteak. His mood had been heavy for much of the flight, but seeing them helped.

He had arranged this meeting with Ash by phone the day before. During their own travels, Ash had earned six Frontier Symbols and challenged Brandon. His first attempt had failed: Sceptile had fought with all it had, but lost to Brandon's Regirock.

Ash had wanted a rematch. Brandon, however, had an agreement with Ethan and was busy searching for Regice, the last of the three Regis he needed. He had postponed Ash's challenge.

With time on his hands and the Grand Festival approaching, Ash knew May would be competing. He had come to the Indigo Plateau with Brock and Max to meet her and Ethan. Yesterday's call had settled when and where they would gather.

The three boys looked much as they had before, though without May among them they gave the impression of a rather rough-and-ready traveling team.

They did notice something different about Ethan. None could say exactly what it was. He simply seemed to look a little better than before—perhaps even handsomer.

“You two are slow,” Ash said, grinning. “We've been waiting ages!” His eyes moved to Ethan's Pokémon. “Charizard? How is it here? Can it leave the Charicific Valley now?”

Seeing Ethan's Charizard reminded Ash of his own. He knew the species well enough to see that Ethan's Pokémon was stronger than before, its gaze sharper and more aggressive. Even its presence was imposing. Ash did not like admitting it, but Ethan's Charizard seemed stronger than his.

Ethan laughed and told him how his Charizard had defeated the valley's strongest Charizard and reached the top there.

Ash could only envy him. His own Charizard still had a long way to go.

They headed into the town together. The Festival had not yet begun, but it was already crowded. Coordinators had arrived to compete, visitors to watch, vendors to serve them, and television reporters to cover the event.

Ethan and May had registered early and reserved a room at the Pokémon Center. Ash and the others had arrived in good time too, so no one had to worry about finding somewhere to stay.

As usual after meeting up, they went to a nearby restaurant for dinner. Over the meal, Ash, Brock, and Max asked how confident May felt about the Grand Festival.

She answered with a bright smile. “Just wait and see! I've been through special training. I feel ready.”

The three exchanged surprised looks. Max finally voiced what they were thinking.

“May, don't forget that everyone here has earned five Contest Ribbons, just like you. They're strong. You shouldn't underestimate them.”

“I'm not underestimating anyone,” May said, waving a hand. “I just don't intend to lose!”
