Murals of ancient Pokémon covered the passage walls. Ethan recognized some of them, but others were unfamiliar. He took a few quick photographs of the murals and statues as he and May walked on. They passed several obvious traps, carefully avoiding the mechanisms rather than touching them.

“What do you think this place was?” May asked, looking around. “It even has traps. Could it be a tomb?”

“Something like that. Underground ruins tend to be either tombs or hiding places. Until we find a distinctive feature or whatever secret was kept here, we can't be sure what this one was for.”

Perhaps they had chosen the right direction. After another hundred or so steps, a wide bend, an underground crossroads, and several dozen more meters, they reached a strange, broad door with a few recesses in its surface.

May frowned at it. “That door looks different from the rest of the ruins. Do you think it's the room with the secret you mentioned?”

“There's an easy way to find out. We go inside.”

Remembering the traps they had passed, Ethan kept his distance and signaled to Gengar. For once it did not grumble. Apparently it was curious about the other side too. It slipped into the wall and vanished.

Ethan and May waited quietly. Barely a dozen seconds later, Gengar emerged again.

“The door has a trick to it,” it said with a grin. “Good thing you didn't go straight through. As soon as the first person passes it, it slams shut and locks. Only a switch nearby can open it again.”

Without waiting for Ethan's reply, Gengar went to a stone pillar, pulled out a loose brick, and pressed the switch hidden behind it.

Stone scraped against stone. With a series of harsh clicks, the door opened and revealed a dark space beyond.

Having a Ghost-type Pokémon made this sort of exploration easier. Gengar could find mechanisms that would defeat most people, and even follow concealed passages through solid ground to places no human or ordinary Pokémon could reach.

Just as Ethan and May prepared to enter, hurried footsteps sounded behind them. Someone was running down the corridor, getting closer. They stopped and turned in surprise.

A few breaths later, a middle-aged man appeared, panting from his run. He seemed equally surprised to find them. “Who are you?”

Ethan looked him over before answering. The man was in his forties, wearing olive-green expedition clothes. His grayish hair was a little long and untidy, his complexion pale, his gaze sharp, and his mouth set in a stern line.

Ethan was fairly sure who he was, but asked anyway. “Who are you?”

The man studied them closely. “I'm Brandon. How did you get into these ruins?”

So this was the final Frontier Brain: the Pyramid King of the Battle Pyramid. Of all people, he was the one Ethan would expect to find here.

“Brandon?” May stared, then turned to Ethan. “Then he's the—”

Brandon's eyes narrowed before she could finish. “You know me?”

“Of course,” Ethan said. “We came to challenge you. We were nearly at the Battle Pyramid when the ground collapsed beneath us and dropped us into the ruins.”

“I see.” Brandon folded his arms. “Then you've defeated the other six Frontier Brains and earned their Symbols.”

His gaze passed over their shoulders to the open door. “Visitors aren't allowed in this area. Even if you entered by accident, you should leave as soon as possible.”

Ethan did not move. “We've lost our way. Mr. Brandon, what is this place?”

Brandon hesitated, deciding whether to tell them. They were already standing in the ruins, though, so there was little point in concealing it.

“These are the ruins of Pokélantis. It was a mysterious ancient kingdom. I learned it existed only when I found references to it in old documents.”

“Pokélantis?” Ethan and May exchanged a look. Neither had heard of such a country.

“Few people have. According to legend, the empire of Pokélantis tried to control Ho-Oh and conquer the world. It angered Ho-Oh, which destroyed the entire kingdom and erased it from history in a single night.”

Brandon walked to their side and looked into the dark room without entering. “The story says the king escaped. Seeking revenge, he used a strange power to seal Ho-Oh in a stone orb and hid it somewhere. It may still be hidden to this day.”

The King of Pokélantis. Ho-Oh. A stone orb. Those words suggested something extraordinary, especially with Ho-Oh involved.

Thinking of Ho-Oh reminded Ethan of Johto, where it had stopped him from catching the three legendary beasts. He had encountered it himself, which was enough to know this part of the legend could not be true. If Ho-Oh had been sealed in ancient times, who had he seen? What would become of Johto's stories about the three beasts? The tale had plainly been exaggerated.

He rubbed his chin. “So the room ahead is where the orb is supposed to be?”

“Possibly. I've searched for information about this kingdom for years. I discovered these ruins only a few days ago and have been examining them bit by bit. Your arrival disrupted that plan. Whether the orb is actually in this room remains to be seen.”

Ethan remembered that Brandon was not only a Frontier Brain but an adventurer and archaeologist.

Brandon went through the doorway first. Ethan and May followed with Gardevoir and Gengar.

The instant they entered, braziers along both walls lit on their own, one after another, illuminating the room to its far end. There, a gigantic statue several meters high sat on a stone throne, holding a ring-topped staff.

The firelight revealed an elderly man in a cloak and rounded hat, with wrinkles covering his face. Perhaps it was only the shifting light, but the statue looked sinister. A cold draft stirred from an unseen crack or corner.

All three stared.

“Is that the King of Pokélantis?” May asked cautiously. She kept her voice low, as though speaking too loudly might wake something.

Neither man answered. They had noticed a second, smaller stone throne beneath the statue. No one sat there. At first it seemed unremarkable, except for the stone orb resting on its seat. It resembled a Poké Ball.

May followed their gazes and saw it too. The orb was slightly larger than a modern Poké Ball and earth-yellow all over. A band of carved markings ran around its middle, with a square black-purple gem set into it like a switch.

“There really is a stone orb! Could Ho-Oh be inside?”

May was astonished. Then she remembered Misty telling her that she and Ethan had met Ho-Oh. The orb could not hold it. Why, then, had the orb been placed in such a prominent spot?

All three approached, but no one reached out to touch it. Brandon silently approved of their caution. Neither visitor had tried to seize a rare artifact on sight.

“From my research and eyewitness accounts, Ho-Oh is still active somewhere in the world,” he said. “So the orb doesn't contain Ho-Oh. Something else may be inside.”

Ethan knew as much, but he was still curious. “Should we open it and see?”

He was beginning to recall the story tied to these ruins. The orb held the king's spirit, if he remembered correctly. In the animated story, it had possessed Ash and controlled him for quite some time.

Brandon shook his head. “Curiosity is useful, but we don't know what's inside. We don't even know whether it could still be alive after thousands of years. Until we understand it, we shouldn't touch it carelessly.”

That was the difference between an archaeologist and a tomb robber. Another person might already have picked up the orb and opened it. Brandon could resist the temptation.

Ethan wanted to meet a soul from thousands of years ago and hear how it spoke and thought, but he did not want some strange being possessing him. Since neither man moved closer, May certainly would not.

The orb seemed to grow impatient. It began to rock gently on the throne, though no one had touched it.

Perhaps the presence of living people had awakened the king's spirit. Unable to lure anyone near, it shook the orb to draw their attention. Ethan and Brandon were too wary to take the bait. If anything, the movement made them more cautious.

Gengar floated to Ethan's side, frowning. “Be careful. I sense something familiar in there, and I don't like it. It's almost like…a ghost.”

Ethan nodded. Brandon glanced over in surprise, then looked away. Even he was not used to hearing a Gengar speak.

The orb rocked harder. With no one coming close, however, its invitation was useless.

Perhaps it could wait no longer after thousands of years, or perhaps it feared losing this rare chance at a host. Black light flashed from the stone orb, and it opened on its own.

A hazy, dark figure floated out and rose into the air. It was too dense and shadowed for Ethan to see its features. He could only make out the shape of an old man, rather like the statue behind it. This was probably the king's spirit.

The figure circled the room as if choosing a target. When it reached the space above Ethan, it suddenly dived toward him. Between middle-aged Brandon and young May, the spirit apparently preferred the body of a young man.

Ethan frowned. He had kept his distance, and it still chose him. What rotten luck.

He did not have to act. Gengar, a Ghost-type Pokémon and an expert at dealing with spirits, moved in front of its Trainer.

“Heh heh heh. I won't let an old thing like you take over my Trainer!”

Gengar thrust both hands forward. Dark energy struck the king's spirit, which shrieked as if badly hurt and flew backward.
