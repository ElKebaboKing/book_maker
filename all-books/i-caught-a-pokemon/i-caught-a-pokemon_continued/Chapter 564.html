The battle seemed to be turning Ash's way again.

But just when everyone expected Snorlax to keep dominating, Gary sent out Scizor. With its extraordinary speed, it knocked out Snorlax and then Muk, swinging the advantage back to Gary.

More importantly, Ash had only one Pokémon left that he had not sent out, while Gary still had three available. From any angle, Gary seemed more likely to win.

The broadcast cut to Misty and Brock in the section for friends and family. Both looked tense and worried for Ash.

The spectators, in contrast, were delighted by a match whose outcome they could not predict. The announcer's promise before the battle had proved true: this was a fierce contest worth watching.

On the field, Ash took out his last Poké Ball with a solemn expression. He threw it high, and Charizard appeared before the crowd.

"Huh?" Erika stared at it in surprise. "Wasn't Charizard at Charicific Valley? How did it get here?"

"That's not so surprising," Ethan said with a smile. "Ash must have felt uncertain about facing Gary and called it back for this match."

Since leaving his own Charizard at Charicific Valley, Ethan had often exchanged messages with Liza, who ran the place. She kept him informed about its training.

Both his Charizard and Ash's were hardworking Pokémon. In only a little over two weeks at the valley, they had climbed considerably in its rankings.

Ethan's Charizard had reached the upper ranks and was challenging for a place in the top twenty. Ash's was in the middle ranks and could now fight the valley's ordinary residents on even terms.

Back on the field, Gary seemed to have expected Ash's Charizard. He did not switch Pokémon and left Scizor in the battle.

Perhaps he wanted Scizor to test how strong Charizard had become. But Scizor was four times as vulnerable to Fire-type attacks. The round had barely begun when it fell to Charizard's flames.

Gary's expression did not change. He sent out another Pokémon.

With a booming thud that shook the ground, a round, heavy Golem landed on the field.

As a Rock-type, Golem could withstand Charizard's fire. But Charizard could fly while Golem remained on the ground, leaving each with its own possible path to victory.

Then came an amusing moment. Knowing fire would do little to Golem, Ash ordered Seismic Toss. He had underestimated its weight. Charizard managed to lift Golem only a short distance before losing its grip and dropping it back to the ground.

Charizard was still the stronger of the two. As Golem used Rollout, Charizard struck it with Dragon Rage and knocked it out.

With two consecutive wins, Charizard had brought the match level again.

Each Trainer now had only one Pokémon remaining: Ash's Charizard, already on the field, and Gary's ace, Blastoise. Their round-of-sixteen match had come down to its final clash.

The matchup favored Gary. Blastoise's Water type countered Charizard, and it had rested while Charizard fought two rounds.

As the final battle began, everyone watched closely.

Just as expected, Blastoise took the early advantage. Even when Charizard flew into the air, Blastoise fired Hydro Pump from both cannons on its back. The streams formed a kind of antiaircraft barrage that kept Charizard from approaching.

But Ash rarely fought by the expected rules.

At that disadvantage, he suddenly ordered Charizard to breathe fire over the rocks around the field.

The spectators looked at one another in confusion. Gary and Blastoise were just as puzzled by what Ash was trying to do.

As Charizard kept breathing fire, the field grew hotter. Smaller rocks broke apart in the heat, and the whole rocky surface became so hot that it visibly reddened.

Gary did not know Ash's plan, but he knew he could not let it continue.

At his command, Blastoise sprayed water across the field to cool it. The cold streams struck the hot rocks and instantly raised great clouds of steam that covered nearly the whole arena.

"I see..." Ethan understood what Ash intended.

The steam hid the battlefield from view. Fortunately, it dispersed almost as quickly as it had formed.

When the spectators could see again, Charizard had somehow closed the distance. It and Blastoise had locked their four limbs together and were struggling for control.

The announcer took a moment to recover. "Charizard couldn't get near Blastoise before, but the steam let it approach safely! Ash's strategy is astonishing!"

More importantly, with Blastoise's arms pinned, it could not turn its body and aim its cannons at Charizard.

That was bad news for Gary.

Still locked together, Charizard carried Blastoise into the air and used Seismic Toss again. It spun rapidly several times, then dove and drove Blastoise hard into the ground.

Boom!

Dust billowed upward. The spectators strained to see. Both Pokémon rose from the ground, but Blastoise was covered in injuries. It swayed a few times and then toppled backward.

The referee raised his flag. After trailing earlier in the match, Ash had fought back to win.

The stands burst into cheers. Delia jumped up and down with joy beside a visibly awkward Professor Oak, who seemed unsure how to react.

"He really won!"

Erika covered her mouth in surprise. Ethan had predicted Ash's victory before the match, but as the battle grew fierce, she had thought Gary was ahead. She had not expected Ash to turn it around.

"Gary made a mistake," Ethan said with a shake of his head. He sounded a little sorry for him. "He panicked during that last Seismic Toss and didn't give the right command. I'd have told Blastoise to withdraw into its shell. The impact would still hurt, but it wouldn't have gone down so easily."

While the people around him celebrated, Ash stood motionless on the field, staring in a daze. He seemed unable to believe that he had won.

Gary took the loss more easily. He looked at Ash, then left the battlefield without a word.

In the lobby, Ethan drained the last of his cocktail and stopped watching the screen. He would wait there for Ash to return.

Four matches were scheduled for the day. Three had finished, sending Jon Dickson, Ash, and Ethan into the quarterfinals. The two Trainers in the next match were both in the other half of the bracket. Ethan did not know them well, and neither was an immediate rival, so he had little interest in watching.

Jimmy and Marina's match, along with the remaining battles, would take place tomorrow. That gave Ethan time to study Ash's team carefully.
