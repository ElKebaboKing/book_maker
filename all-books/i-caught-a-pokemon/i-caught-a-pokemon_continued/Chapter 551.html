It was nine at night by the time Ethan and Erika returned to their suite in the competitors' village.

They bathed together and lay down. Ethan did not press for sex tonight. Wanting to save his strength, he held Erika close and went over the day's battles in his mind.

Overall, he was pleased with his performance. His Pokémon had fought well and met the expectations he had set for them before the matches.

Erika rested her cheek on his chest and listened to his strong heartbeat. Seeing him lost in thought, she asked softly, "What are you thinking about?"

"The matches the day after tomorrow."

Ethan breathed in the scent of her hair and smiled. "After tomorrow's opening ceremony, the real battles begin. Then we'll be facing strong Trainers who fought their way out of their groups. It won't be as easy as it was today."

"Are you worried about what's ahead?"

"No." He stroked Erika's smooth back, his expression serious. "My goal is the championship. However strong my opponents are, and whatever round I meet them in, I'll give everything I have to beat them. The only question is whether we meet sooner or later."

It was a bold claim, but Ethan had good reason to trust the strength of his Pokémon.

...

While Ethan fell asleep with Erika in his arms, full of hopes for the future, the lights still burned in the nearby Pokémon League building. An intense debate was under way.

League officials disagreed over how to run this year's tournament.

Perhaps they remembered the benefits of Ethan's popularity at the Indigo League. John, the broadcast director, and many others wanted high ratings. They hoped to repeat what they had done there: promote Ethan as a favorite for the title and shape his tournament path so that he would meet opponents of similar strength, making every battle exciting.

President Goodshow and those who sided with him wanted a fairer competition. The League should not interfere with the schedule or manipulate matchups. They also thought it was bad for the League if one person monopolized the championship. Every competitor deserved equal attention.

The two sides argued until their faces were red. Neither would yield.

When the meeting broke for a rest with no decision reached, John went to the smoking area with several of his relatives. In private, he made no secret of his frustration with Goodshow.

"The old man ought to step down," he told them. "Ratings are what matter most! Ethan looks good and he's an outstanding Trainer. If I don't put the spotlight on him, should I put it on ordinary entrants who don't look remotely like champions?"

Somehow, his words eventually reached Goodshow. The president only shook his head helplessly.

The two men simply saw things differently. No amount of persuasion would settle that.

Fortunately, when the meeting resumed, the moderates helped them reach a compromise. Goodshow allowed John to give Ethan a larger share of the publicity resources. But the League would not interfere with the tournament schedule or match selection. The competition had to remain fair.

Ethan would never know about that discussion, though he soon noticed changes around him.

The next morning, he had barely finished breakfast when several League staff members arrived and invited him to shoot promotional videos.

There was nothing improper about the invitation. A few popular local Johto entrants had been asked to come to the shoot as well.

Compared with them, however, Ethan received more elaborate makeup, more time before the camera, and several changes of setting.

Even in the group photo, he stood right at the center.

Ethan did not like drawing this much attention, but the League had arranged it, so he followed the staff's directions.

By the time he escaped the shoot at noon, a large crowd of reporters was waiting for him.

Like sharks scenting blood, they had already marked the popular entrants as their targets. More reporters had their eyes on Ethan than on anyone else.

He had little choice but to answer a few questions in careful, official language.

"Ethan, what's your goal for this tournament?"

Looking at the microphone thrust almost into his face, Ethan replied earnestly, "To work hard and reach the next round, of course. If I make the final, all the better."

Someone else asked, "Are there any opponents here who have left a strong impression on you?"

"I think everyone who has reached the preliminaries is strong and deserves respect."

"Is there anything you'd like to say to them?"

"I only hope we all perform at our best and give the audience the most exciting battles we can."

His answers were modest and careful, giving the reporters nothing they could twist into a story.

When they looked ready to ask more, Ethan called over some League staff. With their help, he slipped away quickly.

He had not gone far when his phone rang.

It was Alexa, the beautiful reporter from Lumiose City who had interviewed him during the Indigo League.

Ethan found an empty corner and answered.

A woman's voice came through at once. "Hello, Mr. Ethan. Do you remember me?"

"Of course." Ethan recalled meeting her in Kanto. "How could I forget the famous reporter from Lumiose Press? What brings you to call now, Alexa?"

Even as he asked, he had already guessed what she wanted.

Alexa laughed. "The same thing as last time. I'd like another exclusive interview with you. Please don't turn me down!"

So it was another interview.

Ethan thought for a moment and agreed.

Alexa had interviewed him at the Indigo League, and the two had formed a friendship of sorts. Refusing her now would feel unkind.

"All right. There's still some time before the opening ceremony, and I'm free at the moment."

"That's wonderful!" Alexa sounded delighted. "We're at a café nearby. Could you come over now, if it's convenient?"
