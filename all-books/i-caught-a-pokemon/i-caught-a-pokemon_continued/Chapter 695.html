Lilycove City was said to be where the land ended and the sea began. On Hoenn's eastern coast, it was the largest and busiest city on that side of the mainland.

Its port was crowded, and it was known for both a department store and an art museum. Lilycove was only a little smaller than Mauville City, yet it drew even larger crowds. Every year, when summer was at its hottest, tourists flocked to its broad white beaches to enjoy the city's energy.

After a day's short voyage, Ethan and Misty arrived early the next morning. Before they could leave the harbor, they passed an area filled with moored yachts and heard someone calling from a distance.

“Hey, young man over there!”

Only when the call came a second time did Ethan realize it was meant for him. Under a sunshade some way off, an old man was waving from a deck chair.

He had a silver beard and wore a broad-brimmed white sailor's hat, a long black coat over his bare chest, jeans, and heavy boots. He looked like a retired ship captain, perhaps in his seventies or eighties. His frame was still sturdy and his gaze sharp.

“I think he's calling you,” Misty said. “Do you know him?”

“No. Let's see what he wants.”

They followed the narrow steps toward the sunshade. Before Ethan could speak, the man stood and looked him over. “You're that Ethan fellow, aren't you?”

Standing, he was about as tall as Ethan. He must have been powerfully built in his youth; age had only made him somewhat leaner. An unmistakable presence remained.

“I am.” Ethan was about to ask how the man knew his name. Then he took a closer look. “Are you...Drake of the Elite Four?”

The old man stared, surprised, then burst into laughter. “Well, that's unusual. You recognized me!”

Misty covered her mouth. She had sensed there was something special about him, but she had never guessed he belonged to Hoenn's Elite Four. What a coincidence to meet him here.

Ethan understood why Drake had known his name. Anyone familiar with his tournament results might recognize him. A man who still called him “young fellow” despite those victories must not be impressed by the title of Champion. Along with the sailor's clothes and his age, that had made Drake a reasonable guess.

Hoenn's Elite Four were capable, but less familiar to the public than their counterparts in other regions. Even many locals knew their names without having seen them. They mostly kept to themselves.

Drake, for instance, had become one of the Elite Four when he was young. His tenure stretched back to the generation of Agatha in Kanto, Bertha in Sinnoh, and Drasna in Kalos. Few people anywhere had held such a position longer. Yet he was also a captain and an explorer who spent much of each year at sea. How could ordinary people get to know him?

The other three had their own reasons for staying out of the public eye. Sidney's name might sound feminine, but he was a man: a former troublemaker who specialized in Dark types. A single tuft of red hair rose from his otherwise shaved head, and his fierce expression hardly made him look approachable.

Phoebe and Glacia were women with very different images. Ethan thought they also had fewer chances to make public appearances. Phoebe was young and looked like an island dancer in a grass skirt. Glacia carried herself like a distant, aristocratic lady. Each had admirers, but neither drew the broad public enthusiasm that a visible figurehead might.

Hoenn lacked someone like Lance: a prominent figure with an impressive manner, a widely admired Pokémon specialty, and an appearance that inspired trust. Ethan kept the thought to himself. Fame aside, an Elite Four member deserved respect, and he was pleased to meet one by chance.

“Recognizing you isn't strange,” Ethan said. “Anyone who knows much about Hoenn's Pokémon has heard of you. You're practically a living piece of its history, and highly respected. How did you recognize me?”

Drake laughed. “My crew, that's why! Long voyages can be dull. With little else to do, the lads watch tournament matches from other regions. I learned about you from them.”

“I see.”

“You're in Hoenn to compete at the Ever Grande Conference?”

“Yes. I have six badges. Two more and I can enter.”

“Just as I thought.” Drake nodded with satisfaction. “The boys have been guessing which region you'd challenge after Johto. I was the only one who said you'd come to Hoenn. I also told them you'd win the championship.”

That was a high opinion indeed. “You're giving me too much credit. Hoenn's Trainers are stronger overall than those in Kanto or Johto. I can't promise I'll win.”

“Too much modesty saps a young man's spirit,” Drake said with a snort. “They may be strong, but do you think you've stood still? Anyway, your greatest obstacle at this tournament won't be the other Trainers. It'll be the Pokémon League itself.”

“Why do you say that?”

“You've probably guessed. The League won't sit by while one Trainer wins championship after championship in different regions. When the tournament starts, expect obstacles at every step. They'll put the toughest opponents in your path.”

Ethan smiled. “Wouldn't that be fine? To win, I have to defeat them all eventually. What difference does it make whether I face them early or late?”

Drake watched him for several seconds, then laughed again. “I take back what I said. You have plenty of spirit. You just keep it quiet. I knew I was right about you.”

His expression grew serious. “I haven't met someone this interesting in a long time. Why don't you battle me while we're both here? If you become Champion, you'll have the opportunity to challenge the Elite Four anyway. As you said, early or late—what difference does it make?”

“Not now.” Ethan shook his head. “This was my plan in Kanto and Johto too. I won't challenge any of the Elite Four until I've competed in every regional tournament I intend to enter. Only then will I turn my attention to all of you.”

Drake looked a little surprised but understood. “Fair enough. I won't press you. I only wonder whether I'll live long enough to see that day.”

“You will. You're still strong. I expect you'll reach a hundred.”

Misty had listened quietly. Now she asked, “Mr. Drake, if you're a captain, where is your ship?”

“There.” Drake pointed across the harbor.

They followed his finger to a huge wooden sailing ship with five masts.

“A sailing ship?” Misty had imagined that an Elite Four member would own a luxury yacht.

“What would youngsters like you know?” Drake scoffed. “A sailing ship is the romance of the sea! No modern machinery, only the wind and currents carrying you across the water, over great waves and through whirlpools. That's an adventure worth chasing!”

He looked ready to set out that instant. Misty did not share his enthusiasm. To her, a boat was a place to relax on deck in the breeze. She loved Water-type Pokémon, but she would rather stay away from storms and huge waves.

Ethan could sympathize a little. Before coming to this world, he had enjoyed pirate films and their ships. He also understood the older captain's nostalgia. Modern vessels were useful, but this one could still make Drake feel like the young adventurer who had first pursued the sea.

After they had admired the ship, Drake turned back to Ethan. “If we aren't battling, come aboard and look around. Quite a few of my crew are your fans. They'll be pleased to meet you.”

Ethan accepted the invitation. He and Misty followed Drake to the ship. Up close, it was larger and more imposing than it had looked from the dock: a black hull below white sails, with ropes everywhere like a pirate vessel.

A rope ladder dropped from above. Ethan looked up. A man in his late twenties, wearing a white shirt and a light tan from the sun, waved to Drake.

“Captain! Preparations are complete. We can depart!”

“Not yet.” Drake climbed the ladder with practiced ease. “We have guests.”

“Guests?” The sailor stared. Apart from a few old friends, he could not remember Drake bringing visitors aboard. He studied Ethan with open curiosity.

On the dock, Ethan tugged the ladder to make sure it was secure, then told Misty to climb first. He would follow below her in case she slipped.

She did not need the help. After traveling through two regions with Ash and enduring all kinds of rough weather and trouble, she found a rope ladder easy enough. What made her self-conscious was having Ethan climb behind her while she wore shorts. She wondered whether he would look up—and found she did not entirely mind the idea.

Ethan urged her onward before she could dwell on it. She climbed with a blush, and he followed. He could hardly avoid looking upward altogether, but he kept his attention on the rope in front of him. He regarded Misty as a younger sister and had no interest in treating her otherwise. Looking straight ahead also avoided an awkward misunderstanding.

When Ethan reached the level of the deck, Misty offered him a hand. She wore a small, expectant smile, as if hoping to see something in his eyes. He took her hand without changing expression and stepped aboard.

At Drake's call, the sailor who had lowered the ladder and several of the others gathered around them.
