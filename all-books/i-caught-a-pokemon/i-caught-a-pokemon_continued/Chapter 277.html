It was over. Drake had lost.

The referee raised the green flag for Ethan. On the scoreboard above them, every one of Drake's Pokémon icons went dark.

The announcer sighed, then spoke with genuine warmth. "Congratulations to Ethan Vale! He has defeated Drake and won the Winner's Cup. All six of his Pokémon gave outstanding performances today. He has earned the title of Honorable Trainer in every sense. Let's give him the applause he deserves!"

After seeing the six rounds, the announcer could no longer doubt Ethan's ability. The visiting champion had not won by luck. He had been plainly stronger, and even his easy smile made him hard to dislike. The crowd rose to applaud both Trainers for the match. Many who had watched today would become Ethan's fans.

On the field, Drake recalled Dragonite before the spreading fire reached it. Ethan recalled Charizard too. Gyarados had grown through its fight at the Lake of Birth and Evolution; this battle had brought Charizard growth of its own.

Staff members hurried onto the field and released seven or eight Squirtle. Their Water Guns soon put out the blaze. When the flames and smoke cleared, the grassy field was a muddy wreck. Its former shape was scarcely recognizable. Beneath the blackened remains, a few embers still glowed faintly red.

Drake approached Ethan and offered his hand, as he had before the match. "That was exhilarating. Congratulations on becoming an Orange League Honorable Trainer. Your place in the Palace of Victory is waiting."

Ethan shook his hand under the spectators' gaze. "Thank you. And... I'm sorry my Pokémon were too rough in a few rounds."

"Don't worry. It was a competition. We both fought to win." Drake hesitated, then added, "May I ask you something?"

"Of course. What is it?"

"All your Pokémon are so strong. How do you train them?"

Ethan scratched his head. "Mostly in the ordinary way. I don't have a special method." Seeing Drake's disappointment, he continued, "But I do have a suggestion. If you want your Pokémon to grow stronger, don't stay in one place. The world is wide. Travel, see more of it, and battle strong Trainers from elsewhere. That experience can help them improve quickly."

"Travel more..." Drake shook his head. He had once done exactly that. When he felt his strength had stopped growing, he returned to the Orange Islands and became Supreme Gym Leader. Ordinary travel no longer seemed enough to change him.

Ethan read his expression. "Speaking of strong Trainers, have you heard of Lorelei?"

"The Elite Four member from Kanto and Johto? Of course. Around these islands, everyone knows her name, from small children to the elderly."

"She's coming to the Orange Islands soon. If you want a real challenge, try battling her."

"Challenge Lorelei?"

"Yes. She's powerful. You'll learn plenty from that fight. I know her a little, so mention my name when you meet. I'm sure she'll give you especially thorough attention." Ethan grinned.

Drake thought it over, then nodded. "I will challenge her."

"Before you leave, though, don't go right away," Ethan added. "I think a boy may come to challenge you in a few days. I'd like you to beat him first."

"A boy?" Drake could not see why a child merited special attention.

"About ten years old. Don't underestimate him because of that. He's strong."

"Is he your friend? How will I recognize him?"

"He wears a cap, and a Pikachu follows him everywhere. He can look a little foolish, but he's more capable than you'd expect."

Drake took the warning seriously. "Then I'll do everything I can to defeat him. And when we next meet, I'll be stronger too. I'll be the one challenging you."

Ethan had heard promises like that many times. "I'll look forward to it."

They exchanged contact details in a friendly spirit. Carl approached with the trophy. Drake took it from him, held it carefully in both hands, and presented it to Ethan.

"Congratulations again. You earned this."

Ethan accepted the trophy with a smile and raised it high. Streamers burst around him, and camera flashes caught the moment.

The Orange League did not give him another champion's title, but the trophy mattered to Ethan all the same. Whatever the level of competition, it was the second trophy of his career.

Carl applauded gently. "Congratulations on becoming our newest Honorable Trainer."

For Carl, the day brought both good news and bad. His publicity efforts had given this Winner's Cup its highest television audience ever. Viewers across many regions had learned more about the Orange League.

But the Orange League had lost. Its strongest Trainer had been beaten six to zero by the champion of a major League. To outsiders, it confirmed the Orange League's reputation as a small competition beneath the others. Carl feared that image would be difficult to shake for a long time.
