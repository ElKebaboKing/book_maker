Meteor Falls?

The name sounded familiar to Ethan. He thought the place appeared in the games, though he could not remember the details. But a place called a waterfall must have plenty of water, and water would attract more wild Pokémon.

Could they find a rare species here?

He held his curiosity in check, and the two continued along the main path.

As he had guessed, the deeper they went, the more Pokémon they encountered. There were more kinds of them too.

Some, like Diglett, Dugtrio, Geodude, Graveler, Onix, and Golbat, were common in Kanto and Johto. Others, such as Lunatone and Solrock, were Pokémon neither of them had seen before.

Ethan even caught a glimpse of an Aron darting into a small cave.

Misty watched it disappear. “If Brock came here, he'd think he'd found paradise!”

Ethan laughed and was about to answer when his flashlight illuminated a small, unusual Pokémon floating a short distance away.

“Is that...?”

Startled, he quickly aimed his Pokédex at it. Though the cave was dim, the device soon returned an entry.

“Beldum, the Iron Ball Pokémon. A Steel- and Psychic-type Pokémon. Its cells are said to be magnets, with magnetic force circulating through its body in place of blood. By repelling the planet's magnetic field, it can float in the air. Beldum gathered in groups move together with the coordination of a single Pokémon and communicate through changes in their magnetic fields. In battle, Beldum sends out magnetism from its rear to pull an opponent close, then pierces it with its sharp claw. When angered, it may rampage and release a powerful magnetic field that damages machinery. If you encounter a Beldum in poor condition, give it a magnet.”

“Beldum?”

Misty studied it with interest after hearing the entry. She walked closer until she could see it clearly.

Despite its name, it looked more like a stone security camera than a dumbbell to her. A crest rose above its almost round head, and its long body ended in a large claw. The head sat at the front of the body like a ball in a socket, with one large red eye at its center.

Misty often met Pokémon that resembled animals or plants, but she had never heard of one shaped like a dumbbell.

The world held all sorts of things. Then she remembered Magnemite, a common sight in Kanto. If there could be a Pokémon that looked like screws, a dumbbell-like Pokémon was not so strange after all.

She was about to keep walking when she turned and saw Ethan striding toward Beldum, already reaching for a Poké Ball at his waist.

“Ethan, do you want to catch it?” she asked in surprise.

“Of course!” He took a ball in hand and sent Torchic toward Beldum. Then he turned back to Misty. “It may be small and unremarkable now, but its final evolution, Metagross, is incredibly strong.”

Metagross? Misty gave a half-understanding nod. She had never seen one, but if Ethan said it was strong, she trusted his judgment.

Torchic ran up to Beldum on its two little legs.

It cried at Beldum excitedly. Beldum merely glanced at it, turned away, and floated into a side passage.

Ethan followed without hesitation, with Torchic at his side. After a moment, Misty hurried after them. “Ethan, wait! Have you forgotten what the old man said? We have to stay on the main path or we'll get lost!”

Ethan kept walking. He looked back and laughed. “We're Trainers. Why let rules like that stop us? Don't worry; we'll find a way through. A few confusing paths are no great obstacle. Come on, before it gets away!”

Perhaps his smile was infectious. Misty nodded firmly, put the warning out of her mind, and followed with quick steps.

Why worry? As long as she was with Ethan, what would it matter if they spent weeks trying to get out of this cave?

Beldum floated ahead, Torchic close behind. Ethan and Misty followed a little farther back.

They did not lose ground, and Beldum was not particularly fast. But it could fly over obstacles while they had to watch the uneven path beneath their feet, so they could not run at full speed.

They chased it for some time, turning left and right, crossing an underground river, and heading deeper into the cave. By the time Beldum stopped, they were so far from the main path that they could no longer find their way back.

Ethan had no attention to spare for that now. Seeing Beldum hovering in place, he realized they had reached its habitat.

Many unusual minerals lay nearby. They appeared to be magnetic, and that magnetism was Beldum's food.

Ethan glanced at Torchic. Chirping, it charged Beldum again and used Ember, sending hot sparks from its beak.

Beldum hung in the air, watching the people and Pokémon with its single eye. Its expressionless face gave Ethan no clue what it felt.

Perhaps it was confused. People might occasionally pass through the cave, but Beldum had probably only ever seen them from afar. This was likely the first time anyone had chased it and stood in its path.

Maybe no one had ever disturbed it, or maybe it had no predators here. It seemed to have lost all caution. Beldum did not even try to dodge before Ember struck it.

Ethan frowned. After finally finding a Beldum, had he found a foolish one?

Only when the flames burned it did Beldum seem to understand that it was under attack. Belatedly, it twisted to extinguish the fire and lunged at Torchic.

Torchic did not need a command to avoid that attack. It simply hopped aside.

Then Torchic raised one claw and scratched Beldum, jolting its whole body.

Beldum turned and used Tackle again. Torchic dodged as before and landed another hit.

After this happened several times, Misty looked at Ethan. She hesitated before asking, “Ethan... why does Beldum keep using the same attack? Doesn't it know any other moves?”

“No, it doesn't. Wild Beldum that haven't been trained only know Tackle, despite being Steel and Psychic types. It looks weak now, but like Magikarp, it'll gain tremendous strength after it evolves.”

The comparison to Magikarp gave Misty a clearer picture. Beldum's Tackle was rather like Magikarp's Splash.

She had still not overcome her fear of Gyarados, but she knew that a Pokémon weak at first could change dramatically through evolution.

Once Torchic understood Beldum's simple pattern, it handled the battle with ease.

Before long, another Ember knocked Beldum to the ground.

Ethan threw the Poké Ball he had been holding at it.

He and Misty watched as a red beam drew Beldum inside. The ball rolled several times on the ground, then came to rest with a click.

After so long on the road, Ethan no longer showed much excitement when he caught an ordinary Pokémon, unlike when he captured a Legendary Pokémon. Still, he was pleased, and a faint smile appeared on his face.

Misty clapped happily. “Congratulations, Ethan! Which number Pokémon is this for you?”

“My twenty-first,” he answered without hesitation.

Including Beldum, he now carried eleven Pokémon, leaving only one place below his limit of twelve.

He had not expected to catch them so quickly. He had been in Hoenn only a few days and had already added three new Pokémon.

Ethan picked up the ball and was about to leave, but he looked around the area once more.

Minerals of many kinds lay scattered about. He recognized some and could not name others.

In one corner, something glinted in the beam of his flashlight.

Curious, he went over and picked up a small, round stone as clear as crystal.

“What's this?” Misty came closer. A mark like a crescent moon showed on its surface. “It doesn't look natural. More like a carved gemstone.”

Ethan studied it for a while before saying uncertainly, “I think it's a Key Stone.”

“A Key Stone?” Misty had clearly never heard the name. “What's that?”

“Remember the Mega Stones I found earlier?” Ethan asked.

“Of course.”

“This is what works with them. It's a natural stone, though no one knows exactly how it forms. What people do know is that it holds a mysterious power. It resonates with a Mega Stone carried by a Pokémon and lets that Pokémon Mega Evolve.”

Mega Evolution! Once Misty heard what the stone was for, she forgot her questions about how it had formed.

She crouched and searched the ground carefully. Before long, she had found another seven or eight Key Stones.

Delighted, she divided the stones into two shares. She kept one and gave the other to Ethan. Whether they used them later or gave them away, the find was an unexpected prize.

Misty eagerly searched the area again but found nothing else. Satisfied with what they already had, she gathered her things and turned back with Ethan toward the way they had come.

They had walked barely a dozen steps when the first fork brought them to a halt.

“Which way did we come from?” Misty looked left and right. They had run so quickly after Beldum that she had paid no attention to the details.

Ethan shook his head too. He had been focused on Beldum and had not watched the route.

Misty sighed. “We should have marked the way.”

“It's all right,” Ethan said with a smile. “There's a little trick for situations like this. If we keep going in the same direction, we'll generally reach an exit, as long as this isn't a particularly complicated maze.”

It was a technique he knew from exploring outdoors. And if it failed, their Pokémon could help. They would not be trapped here forever; the question was only how long it would take to get out.

After discussing it, they chose the passage farthest to the right. They walked for a long time before noticing that the ground beneath their feet was growing damp. Somewhere ahead, they heard flowing water.

They exchanged a look. This was not the route they had taken in. But after coming so far, neither wanted to turn back, so they followed the sound.

The path sloped gently upward. The rush of water grew louder. They turned a corner, ducked through an opening only just high enough to pass, and suddenly found themselves in bright light.

Before them, more than a dozen waterfalls of different sizes formed a vast cascade inside the cavern. Water thundered down. A huge opening in the cave roof let sunlight pour in, driving away the gloom.

At the foot of the falls lay an enormous round pit. Its shape did not look like the work of running water. It looked more like the mark of a meteorite impact.

Misty looked up at the falls. Over the roar, she had to raise her voice as she spoke near Ethan's ear. “This must be Meteor Falls, right?”

Ethan did not know the waterfall's name for certain.

Still, this must be the place. With a crater like that, the connection to meteors was hard to miss. And when he looked closely, droplets sprayed from the highest falls like countless shooting stars. The sight was magnificent.
