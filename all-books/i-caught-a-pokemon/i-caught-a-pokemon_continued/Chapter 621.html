Wally had lost. By rights, that meant his hope of proving himself to his uncle had failed. To everyone's surprise, though, the man began to applaud.

“That was an excellent battle,” his uncle said with a smile. “I saw how hard you've worked. You lost because your opponent was too strong.”

Wally's eyes lit up. He watched his uncle hopefully, waiting to hear what would come next.

His uncle met his gaze and nodded with satisfaction. “I agree. You can challenge the Gym and pursue your dream.”

“Yes!”

Wally jumped for joy. The shy boy would only show so much excitement in front of a crowd when something mattered this deeply to him.

Combusken had already returned to Ethan's side. “Well done.” Ethan stroked its head in encouragement. Then he looked up at Wally's beaming face and shrugged at Misty.

“Well, we should be going.”

They took Combusken through the crowd and soon disappeared. By the time Wally recovered enough to thank Ethan, the older Trainer was gone. Wally didn't dwell on it; he was too happy about his uncle's decision.

Ethan and Misty were about to continue their walk through Mauville Park when a voice called from behind them.

“Please wait!”

It was an energetic woman's voice. At first Ethan didn't realize she was addressing him. Then he heard footsteps hurrying after them and turned. A man and a woman were approaching together.

The woman looked about twenty-five or twenty-six. She was pretty, with short light-brown hair, subtle makeup, and pearl earrings. She wore a blue strapless top, fitted white trousers, and white heels, and carried a microphone. She looked every bit the big-city reporter.

The man seemed about the same age. In a cap, gray T-shirt, and jeans, he carried a video camera on his shoulder. Their equipment made it obvious that they worked for a television station.

The woman's eyes brightened when Ethan faced her. “It really is you! You're the champion of both the Kanto and Johto Leagues!”

“Shh...” Ethan quickly gestured for her to lower her voice. People recognized him from time to time now, but he had only just arrived in Hoenn and didn't want to draw attention yet.

“Oh, sorry.” She smiled apologetically. “We're reporters from TV Mauville. We were conducting interviews nearby, happened to see your battle, and took the liberty of following you.”

“I see.” Ethan nodded. “What are you covering? Has something interesting happened here? Any big news?”

“No,” the man said without thinking. “It's just a feature about parents and children—”

The woman interrupted him with a loud cough and an awkward smile. “We're really just looking around. We don't have a particular story in mind.”

Before Ethan could ask anything more, she hurried on. “I've talked so much without introducing us. How rude of me! I'm Gabby, and this is my partner, Ty. Would you be willing to give us an exclusive interview?”

Gabby and Ty? Ethan thought for a moment but couldn't place them. He could tell Gabby was avoiding the subject of their current assignment, though, and let it go.

“Sorry. I don't want to do an interview.”

“Really?” Gabby looked startled. “Didn't you give exclusive interviews during the Silver Conference and the Indigo League?”

She meant the two interviews Alexa had conducted with him. Ethan wasn't surprised she knew about them; a reporter would have better access to that information than most people.

He shook his head. “Those were late in the tournaments, after I'd achieved something worth talking about. I've only just arrived in Hoenn. I have one badge and haven't even qualified for the Ever Grande Conference. What grounds do I have for an interview now?”

“You're too modest,” Gabby said with a smile. “With your ability, collecting eight badges and entering the Ever Grande Conference should be easy. You might even win the whole thing!”

Ethan only smiled and shook his head, leaving that prediction unanswered. An interview this early would expose him to other competitors before he was ready. It could also leave viewers thinking he had started boasting before he had even collected the badges.

Gabby saw that he had reservations, but she kept asking. Ethan held firm. Eventually, worn down by her persistence, he offered a compromise.

“An interview now is out of the question. When the Ever Grande Conference comes around, if the opportunity is there, I'll consider you first.”

“Well...” Gabby hesitated. She could tell that was as far as he would go, so she smiled and accepted. “It's a deal. Let's exchange contact details.”

Her determination made her ambition plain. With a resigned sigh, Ethan exchanged phone numbers with her, made an excuse, and left with Misty.

Watching him go, Gabby raised her brows triumphantly and clenched a fist in excitement.

“Why are you so happy?” Ty asked.

“You idiot!” She gave him an exasperated look. “This is a rare opportunity. Do you want to spend the rest of your career standing in parks interviewing parents about time with their children?”

Ty still looked puzzled. “What's that got to do with him?”

“Everything! He won both the Silver Conference and the Indigo League. He's obviously come to Hoenn aiming for another championship. He's mysterious, hardly ever appears in the media, and audiences love him. If we can build a connection with him and land even one interview, our standing at the station will soar. We might finally get back into the studio!”

“Oh, I see!” Ty's eyes lit up. “We can't let a chance like that slip away.”

“Now you understand.” Gabby lifted her chin, then looked in the direction Ethan had gone. Determination filled her eyes. “Whatever happens, I'm holding on to this opportunity. I'm tired of standing outside in the wind and sun!”
