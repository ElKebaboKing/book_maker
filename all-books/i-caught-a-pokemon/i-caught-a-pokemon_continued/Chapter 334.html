Boom!

Something enormous seemed to crash onto the ice. The entire frozen field shook, and the sound of cracks spreading across its surface made Pryce's heart tighten.

At last, the white mist slowly dispersed. Pryce instinctively stepped forward to see what had happened. Abomasnow lay motionless on the ground, while Gardevoir stood over it, breathing lightly.

What happened? It lost?

Pryce frowned.

Abomasnow should have held the advantage inside the mist. It should have tracked Gardevoir by scent. How had it ended up being the one knocked down?

Unlike the baffled Pryce, Ethan had followed everything happening inside the mist in real time through telepathy.

With Ethan guiding her, Trainer and Pokémon had worked together. Gardevoir attacked several times in succession, striking without pause with Confusion until Abomasnow finally fell.

Without Pryce's help, Abomasnow could only fight by instinct. Naturally, it was no match for Gardevoir.

Telepathy had made all the difference. In a place with almost no visibility, it had turned the battle into two against one.

Ethan waited a long while. When Abomasnow still did not move, he said, "It looks like I won."

Pryce studied him deeply, then gave a quiet smile.

"Young man, your future is truly limitless. Not only have you won a championship, but your Pokémon are remarkably powerful. Never mind when I was your age—even as I am now, I'm nowhere near your match!"

"You're too kind." Since Pryce had praised him, Ethan naturally returned the courtesy. "You're the one who's still vigorous in old age. If I reach your age, I may have lost the ability to command Pokémon in battle altogether!"

Pryce's smile widened. He knew Ethan was merely being polite and did not take the words too seriously.

Even so, he had formed an excellent impression of Ethan.

As they spoke, Pryce recalled Abomasnow and walked slowly across the field. He stopped before Ethan, took a badge from a small box, and handed it over.

"This is the Mahogany Gym's Glacier Badge. It proves you defeated me. Take it."

Ethan thanked him and accepted the badge.

Whether because of the cold air in the Gym or the material itself, the badge felt slightly chilled in his hand, almost like solid ice.

He opened his palm for a closer look. It resembled a six-pointed snowflake, and its blue-green surface glittered beneath the lights.

After Ethan placed it in his badge case, Pryce spoke again in the tone of someone offering hard-earned wisdom.

"I'm glad to see a Trainer like you emerge, and I expect great things from the road ahead of you. But I must give you one warning. Never grow too close to Pokémon. They are animals, after all. One day, they will leave you."

Ethan knew Pryce had trapped himself in that belief, so he merely smiled and did not respond.

Pryce saw only a Pokémon's apparent betrayal. He completely ignored the far greater number of Pokémon who struggled and fought for humans every day, whether selflessly or bravely.

Ethan wondered how Pryce would feel if he someday learned the truth, released the knot in his heart, and looked back on the warning he had given today.

Would he be ashamed of such misguided advice?

That was a matter for the future. It would not affect Ethan, and now that he had the badge, it was time to leave.

...

After departing Mahogany Town, Ethan and Sabrina decided to return to Violet City first. It was the closest destination in a straight line. They would rest there properly before setting out to challenge more Gyms.

They still had to cross the forest they had traveled through on the way here, and retracing the same route on foot would be tedious. Ethan called out Charizard, and he and Sabrina rode it through the sky.

Whoosh...

A fresh breeze brushed Ethan's cheeks.

The endless forest rushed past below.

With nothing to do along the way, Ethan grew a little bored and reviewed his recent gains.

Only a few days had passed. First, he had helped the International Police destroy a Team Rocket base and research facility, dealing the organization a serious blow. Then he had caught Larvitar, Dratini had evolved into Dragonair, and the radio waves had greatly increased Gyarados's power.

Everything seemed to be moving in a good direction.

Most important of all, he had obtained both the Glacier Badge and the Rising Badge, bringing him another step closer to competing in the Silver Conference.

Charizard flew on with wings spread wide.

They remained in the air from morning until afternoon. A journey that would have taken more than a day on foot passed in only a few hours.

Feeling that they should be near Violet City, Ethan looked down in every direction. There was no sign of the city.

Hm? Are we not there yet?

Puzzled, he took out his phone and checked the map. Only then did he discover the problem. He had never corrected Charizard's course and had given it only a general direction, allowing them to drift away from their route.

Fortunately, they had not strayed far. If they turned northeast and flew for another half hour, they would reach Violet City.

Just as Ethan prepared to tell Charizard to change course, Sabrina, seated in his arms, suddenly pointed below.

"Look over there."

Ethan followed her finger. Not far ahead, the dense forest suddenly opened into a vast stretch of empty ground. A valley appeared amid a sprawling field of rocks.

Even from this distance, Ethan could see many ancient buildings in the valley. Groups of people in hard hats moved busily among them.

"Should we take a look?"

Sabrina had no objection.

Ethan patted Charizard lightly on the neck. Understanding at once, Charizard flew toward the valley.

When they reached the area overhead, they saw that the people below formed an archaeological team of scholars, researchers, and excavation workers.

A huge circular facility stood at the end of the valley. People hurried in and out, making the place look exceptionally busy.

Are there ruins here?

Curious, Ethan had Charizard land nearby.

Charizard beat its wings and swept up layers of dust, filling the surrounding area with a cloud of grit.

The workers seemed accustomed to unexpected visitors. They merely glanced at Ethan and Sabrina before returning to their tasks. No one approached them or tried to drive them away.

Ethan jumped down from Charizard and, sensing something, looked to one side. A nearby sign bore several large words:

Ruins of Alph
