Ethan understood the situation now. Luna was a spoiled child who cared only about amusing herself and easing her loneliness, without considering anyone else.

She treated attacks on Trainers, and battles forced on people who did not want them, as an exciting game. The butler had not told her parents; he had hidden the truth. Unable to stop her himself, he had let it continue. He had not helped her attack anyone, but his silence made him complicit. If Luna went on like this, she would eventually cause a disaster.

Before he arrived, Ethan might have had no place involving himself in her affairs. Scizor had attacked him too, however. Now the matter concerned him directly.

Scizor gathered another Hyper Beam and fired toward him. This time Gengar did not intervene. Gardevoir used its psychic power to halt the attack.

Luna saw a chance. She was furious with Ethan for meddling, but wary of the Gengar that had already defeated Red Lightning. Seeing another Pokémon take its place gave her some relief.

She was young, but no fool. Red Lightning had beaten many Trainers in this large city, showing her all sorts of Pokémon. Her wealthy upbringing also gave her access to plenty of information. She knew what Gardevoir was. Scizor's Steel typing gave it an advantage against Gardevoir on both offense and defense. She did not know why Ethan had set aside Gengar, but she would seize the opportunity.

“Red Lightning, use Agility!”

Scizor rushed toward Gardevoir. Luna imagined it dodging Gardevoir's Confusion, closing the distance, and keeping the Psychic-type pinned down. Then victory would belong to Red Lightning.

She trusted her Pokémon. After all its victories in the city, she knew how strong it was. The Trainers she had met before had failed against it, and she expected this irritating man to fail too.

Reality disagreed. Scizor had covered barely half the distance when Gardevoir raised its right hand. It predicted exactly where Scizor would step. Psychic force hit like an invisible wall and hurled it backward.

“Red Lightning!” Luna cried, stunned.

Scizor got up at once and charged again. This time it knew a direct approach would not work. It used Double Team. Five copies appeared alongside the real Scizor, and six identical red figures lunged at Gardevoir.

Luna smiled. She had seen this many times. A weak opponent fell to Hyper Beam. A stronger one might resist that, but Agility and Double Team let Red Lightning close in and defeat it. So many Trainers had lost to this combination. Surely it would work again.

Gardevoir lifted its other hand. Both hands moved, and a second surge of psychic force struck. The real Scizor flew backward, while all five copies vanished at once.

“What?” Luna's face changed again. Since meeting Ethan, she had gone from delight to alarm to anger and back several times. Now even Red Lightning's familiar combination had been broken.

Scizor stood again, but did not rush forward. It fired Swift from a safer distance. Gardevoir formed an invisible barrier before itself, and every star struck that barrier instead. Scizor's attack achieved nothing.

Luna's heart sank. Her Pokémon looked like a small child being toyed with by an adult. Could the gap between Red Lightning and this Gardevoir really be so wide?

She was intelligent despite her age. A child who could direct one Scizor to victory against so many Trainers had talent and had grown through repeated battles. Both her command and Red Lightning's strength exceeded what most children her age could manage, even surpassing plenty of ordinary Trainers. She knew it, which was why she had acted so fearlessly.

Now the skill she prized was failing before Ethan, and the confidence she had built over so long was breaking with it.

As she searched for something to do, the butler stepped in Ethan's way again.

“Stop!” His voice was stern. “Sir, leave this property at once. You're trespassing. If you refuse, I'll inform the master and mistress and hold you responsible. Perhaps you don't know that they are famous inventors and influential people in this city.”

The old butler was loyal to his employers, but he was hardly being reasonable. Did he think a threat would frighten Ethan? Luna's parents were inventors; so what? Their devotion to work had plainly left them little time to supervise their daughter.

Ethan shook his head. He did not care to argue. He took his Special Investigator of the International Police credentials from his wallet and held them up.

“Read this. I didn't come here at random. I followed the person responsible for the attacks.”

He had not wanted to invoke that position, but this was the second time a situation like this had forced his hand. An official title was undeniably useful where a private citizen might be turned away.

Despite his poor eyesight, the butler could read the credentials. He stiffened. An International Police Special Investigator? And Ethan was calling Miss Luna responsible for a crime?

This had gone beyond what he could manage. He took out his phone to call her parents, but he was too late.

Without waiting for another order, Gardevoir subdued Scizor, held Luna with its psychic power, and used Teleport to take them both away. Before the butler could cry out, Ethan released Dragonite and left with May. A moment later, the rooftop estate was empty except for the butler, frantic with worry.

…

Gardevoir appeared outside the Commerce City police station with Luna and Scizor. Seconds later, Ethan and May arrived on Dragonite.

He had noticed the station on the way to Luna's villa and asked Gardevoir to remember its location for this purpose. The officers came out in surprise at the group's arrival. Ethan told them what had happened.

They were astonished. Red Lightning, the figure that had thrown all of Commerce City into turmoil, was a Scizor. The person directing it was a girl of only eight or nine.

They took Luna and Scizor inside, contacted their superiors, and notified her parents. Once the handover was complete, Ethan left the matter with them.

He had no wish to be hard-hearted toward a little girl. But what she had done was too serious for a simple apology to erase. Even if he forgave her attack on him, would all the Trainers she had hurt forgive her?

The graffiti was trivial. The injuries were not. How many Pokémon had their worried Trainers rushed to the Pokémon Center after Scizor defeated them? That was why Ethan had taken matters this far. Luna needed to learn that her actions had consequences.

What the police did next, how her parents apologized to the Trainers, and whether Luna came to understand her wrongdoing were no longer his decisions. He did not even want to follow the case. He had done his part.

Outside the station, May seemed about to speak, then held back. Ethan glanced at her and sighed softly.

“You think I handled it badly?”

She shook her head, hesitated, then nodded. “I think there must be a better way. Maybe…we didn't need to take her to the police. If someone explained it to her first, she might learn to do the right thing.”

She was still a little naive. Ethan smiled without arguing. “Maybe. But I think people trained to handle this should take it from here. We're travelers. We can judge what happened without deciding what sort of person she is. A child doesn't get a pass for wrongdoing because she's young or rich.”

May still looked uncertain, so he continued. “Think about what would have happened if she hadn't met us today. How many more people would she and Scizor have attacked? And we're traveling. We don't have the time or means to guide her through everything she needs to learn.”

May nodded. He had persuaded her to some extent, though she still had reservations.

Ethan looked up at the blue sky. “We aren't trying to punish her. We want the attacks to end and the people she hurt to receive an answer.”

…

They left Commerce City and continued toward the Battle Tower. After camping for the night, they arrived around noon the next day in a small town called Gardenia Town. At last, May had found the Pokémon Contest she had been hoping for.

Something else surprised her even more. Outside the town's Pokémon Center, she and Ethan ran into Ash and his friends again.

It was quite a coincidence. May had left Ash's group, so they had no particular reason to stop and watch Contests anymore. Yet here they were. To Ash and his companions, however, their meeting did not seem so unexpected.

“See?” Ash said proudly to Brock and Max at the Pokémon Center doors. “I told you I had a feeling we'd meet Ethan and May here. I was right!”

He hurried over to Ethan. “How many Symbols have you got?”

“Five. One more at the Battle Tower, then I can challenge the last facility. What about you?”

“Heh, heh.” Ash opened his Symbol case to show Ethan. “I've got four! Metallica Island is next!”

Their routes had crossed. After Ash challenged the facility on Metallica Island, he would still need to make a long detour to the Battle Tower for his final Symbol.

Even so, Ash was moving fast. He still got lost and caught up in incidents along the road, but without May traveling with him, he no longer stopped for her Contests. That alone had made his journey smoother and quicker.
