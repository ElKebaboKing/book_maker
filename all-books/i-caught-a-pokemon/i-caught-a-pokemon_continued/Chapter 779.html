It was the same stadium, but the atmosphere for the round of sixteen felt livelier than it had in the round of thirty-two. The number of seats and spectators had not changed. The crowd was simply more excited, cheering louder for battles that promised to be fiercer.

The whole Ever Grande Conference was caught up in the celebration. Promotional portraits of the competitors had appeared at bus stops and department stores in Hoenn's major cities. Even employees at some large companies were quietly neglecting their work to watch the annual event.

It drew more attention than the Grand Festival, which had ended only recently. Now it seemed to have the entire region watching.

At 8:30 that morning, with everyone looking on, Ash and Morrison opened the day's schedule.

Whatever the two had discussed the night before, they stood solemnly in their Trainer's areas. Morrison in particular looked nothing like his usual carefree self. He was tense and conflicted. One glance showed he was still fighting with himself.

The rules were the same as in the previous round. The announcer introduced both Trainers, then the wheel chose the field for the first half. It stopped on the Ice Field. A rectangular field covered in thick ice rose into place at the center of the stadium. Everything was ready. The battle began.

Ethan and the others sat among the spectators. Wanting to watch in comfort this time, and hoping to keep May from eating all his snacks again, Ethan had brought a large supply. He also waved over the stiff-backed Team Rocket trio and bought drinks from them at a price so low it was practically a giveaway. Fully prepared, he settled in to watch.

Ash's first Pokémon was Corphish. After hesitating for a long while, Morrison sent out Girafarig.

Others might think Morrison had struggled to choose a Pokémon. Ethan and his friends knew he was struggling with how to face Ash at all. It was hard to see such a cheerful boy brought to this state. It showed how much he valued their friendship.

Ash had already faced Gary and Richie in fateful matches, and he had fought Ethan as a friend. He was used to meeting people he cared about in competition. Of the two, he seemed calmer.

As for the Pokémon, Girafarig was a Normal and Psychic type, while Corphish was a pure Water type. Neither had a type advantage or a clear edge from the field. On paper, this should have been an even match.

But Morrison seemed to have lost all composure. He commanded Girafarig like a complete beginner, choosing moves without a plan and making basic mistakes. Its performance was a mess. The crowd was baffled. How could a Trainer this amateurish have reached the final sixteen?

“This won't work,” Max said, shaking his head. He did not know Morrison very well, but he could see the problem. “If he keeps fighting like that, he's bound to lose.”

Ethan took a sip of his drink. “Does Morrison's condition look familiar to you?” he asked Misty and Brock.

They turned to him. “You mean…?”

“It's like Ash when he faced Richie at the Indigo Conference.”

The name took them back to Kanto: another boy in a cap, with a Pikachu on his shoulder and a steadier temperament than Ash's. Morrison was in much the same state Ash had been then. He could not show his usual skill, and he barely seemed willing to fight.

It was almost fitting for someone so often called a heavier version of Ash to stumble over the same problem.

“Only Morrison can bring himself back,” Ethan said. “Though I doubt Ash will stand by and do nothing.”

They turned back to the field. Corphish was weaving around the ice pillars and rocks, running circles around Girafarig. Morrison could not settle himself enough to give useful orders, and Girafarig grew even less steady. After evading several attempted counterattacks, Corphish knocked it out with Crabhammer.

Ash had won the first round. He did not look pleased. He watched Morrison with a troubled expression.

Morrison did not recall Girafarig or send out a second Pokémon. The referee frowned and raised a flag to warn him. It was a bad sign. If the referee raised the flag again and Morrison still failed to act, he would be judged to have forfeited and disqualified.

Boos rose from the stands. This was the annual Ever Grande Conference, already down to the final sixteen. Was this really the match they had come to see? Dissatisfaction spread quickly, and Max and the others began to worry about Morrison.

“What is he doing?” Max scratched his head as he heard the crowd turn. “Why is this so hard for him? Ash has already decided to fight with everything he's got.”

Ethan shook his head. The choice was simple enough to understand: victory or friendship. Only boys as earnest as Ash and Morrison could get so caught in it that they could no longer move. It showed how much Morrison cared about his friend, but as a Trainer, he was falling short.

In Morrison's place, Ethan would apologize and then battle at full strength. To him, that was the greatest respect one could show a friend.

Morrison remained motionless. The referee raised the colored flag again. “Morrison has lost the will to battle, so—”

Ash interrupted before the referee could finish. Ethan's group was too far away to hear what he said. They saw him call out to Morrison with fierce emotion. Morrison gritted his teeth and finally took out his second Poké Ball.

It held Growlithe. By type, that was hardly a wise choice. Morrison had already lost a round, and Ash's Water-type Corphish was still on the field. Sending out a Fire type seemed to deepen his disadvantage.

Perhaps Growlithe could make something of the Ice Field. Then again, if it melted the ice and exposed water, that might help Corphish even more. Morrison's choice was hard to fathom. Had his emotions clouded his judgment so badly that he had sent out the wrong Pokémon?

With the crowd still wondering, the second round began. Ash kept Corphish in and ordered an immediate, forceful attack, as though he hoped to wake Morrison up.

The earlier exchange had helped. Morrison's condition improved somewhat. He still could not bring himself to attack Ash's Pokémon directly, but at least his commands now made sense.

It was nowhere near enough. Corphish had the type advantage, and Morrison remained hesitant. Ash took a second easy win. The spectators grew angrier. What kind of battle was this? Their boos drowned out almost everything else.

Even Ethan grew impatient. He understood Morrison's feelings, but people had come to watch a match, not to watch one competitor flinch from fighting. Morrison was wasting time and the enthusiasm of everyone across Hoenn who had made room in their day to watch.

Some had traveled a long way to sit in these stands. Others had stolen a few minutes to follow the broadcast. One poor round could be forgiven. Failing to recover for a second was another matter. Youth, innocence, inexperience, and devotion to friendship were not enough to excuse it.

Across the field, Ash called something to Morrison again. Morrison's expression shifted visibly: first flushed with embarrassment, then angry, and finally pained.

Holding his fallen Growlithe, he murmured something to it. Then he seemed to find new energy and shouted aloud. At this distance, perhaps only Ash and the referee could hear his words. But in an instant, Morrison's old spirit seemed to return.

“Finally, he's back to normal.” Ethan gave a huge yawn and snorted.

Ash and Morrison might have moved each other with their friendship, but it had not moved everyone else. Now that the match was about to become a real battle again, Ethan felt a little interested.

Morrison looked lively once more. He recalled Growlithe and, without waiting for the referee, promptly took out his third Poké Ball. Blue light flashed. Swampert appeared before Corphish.

Ash did not recall Corphish, though it had already fought two rounds and spent considerable energy. For one thing, Corphish had knocked out two Pokémon without taking damage and still had momentum. For another, Ash did not want to take advantage of Morrison's earlier hesitation.

At the signal, both Pokémon charged, trading attacks at long, middle, and close range. Hydro Pump met Bubble Beam. Mud Shot met Aqua Jet. Focus Punch met Crabhammer.

Their direct exchange was as clear and exciting as any the crowd could have wished for. In only a few minutes, they attacked and counterattacked without either gaining the upper hand. Their blows shattered ice rocks into fragments and cracked the frozen surface. Nearly half the field was reshaped by the fight.

At last, Focus Punch and Crabhammer met again. The collision erupted, and both moves struck their targets. Corphish and Swampert fell together.

The referee looked them over and raised the flags. “Corphish and Swampert are both unable to battle! This round is a draw!”

Audiences could be fickle. They had booed Morrison for his hesitation; one fine round was enough to change their minds. They cheered both Pokémon with all their strength, as though they had forgotten their irritation.

Morrison had lost three Pokémon, so the match entered its intermission. Max and the others decided to visit Ash in the rest area and encourage him. Ethan stayed in his seat. He could not be bothered to move or to watch the two boys promise each other they would fight seriously.

The break passed quickly. When the Trainers returned, the second-half field had been chosen: the Grass Field.

Ash sent out his first partner, Pikachu. By coincidence, Morrison sent out Steelix.

That was both good news and bad news. At its best, Pikachu could show astonishing power against Pokémon with a type advantage over it. It was as if Ash's talent for winning bad matchups suddenly switched on, and Pikachu might knock one out in moments.

The bad news was that it did not happen often. In this round, Pikachu ran and attacked, trying to find an opening, but it could not draw on all that hidden potential. When it traded Iron Tails with Steelix, Steelix struck it down. Pikachu was unable to battle.

Since the main tournament began, Pikachu had lost twice in a row to Pokémon with favorable types against it: Katie's Dugtrio, then Morrison's Steelix. It was surprising, yet it made a certain kind of sense.

Pikachu's reputation for being able to match almost anyone, and lose to almost anyone, had not come from nowhere.
