"Grandpa, that monkey professor you told me about is here!"

As the little girl led them through the courtyard, she called loudly toward the main house, paying no attention to Professor Elm's bright red face behind her.

"You little fool!"

An old man hurried out of the house at her shout. "Don't say things like that in front of people!"

"Huh?" The girl blinked up at him in innocent confusion. "But... aren't you the one who called him that?"

"Ahem..."

The old man choked and rushed forward to cover her mouth. He gave Professor Elm an embarrassed smile. "Sorry, Elm. This is my granddaughter, Maizie. She's too young to know any better and says all sorts of nonsense. Please don't mind her."

Professor Elm scratched his cheek and tried to sound unconcerned. "It's... it's all right. It's been a while, Mr. Kurt..."

Perhaps hoping to move past the awkward moment, the old man turned to Ethan and Erika.

"And who are these two?"

Ethan was studying him too.

The old man wore a traditional kimono. His hair was gray-white, his thick black eyebrows tilted slightly upward, and his eyes were bright. His loose skin gave him a stern look.

Any child who happened to pass him would probably be so frightened by that face that they would hardly dare breathe!

"I asked them to help us study the GS Ball," Professor Elm said. "This is Ethan, the champion of this year's Indigo League, and the young lady beside him is the Celadon Gym Leader from Kanto."

"Oh?" The old man looked them over. "Impressive people. I'm surprised you managed to invite them."

Professor Elm hurriedly shook his head. "You've misunderstood. I didn't arrange for them to come. We met by chance."

He then introduced Kurt to Ethan and Erika.

"This is Mr. Kurt. He's retired now, but he's still a renowned master of handmade Poké Balls!"

He makes Poké Balls?

No wonder Professor Oak had sent the GS Ball to him.

"So you're Ethan!" Recognition dawned on Kurt's face. "My son told me about you. Since you're from Kanto, you must know Professor Oak."

Ethan nodded. "Professor Oak is a respected elder I trust. I've been fortunate enough to know him, and he's helped me several times."

Kurt laughed. "I've known him since we were young. Does he still recite those embarrassing senryu poems? And Agatha—we go back a long way too!"

Those shared connections made the group feel much less like strangers.

Ethan still found Kurt's name peculiar. It sounded like a nickname, yet it was his real name!

"You mentioned your son. Who is he?"

"He works at Silph Co.," Kurt said. "A fool who only knows how to make Poké Balls with modern technology. When Team Rocket attacked their headquarters, he said you saved them."

That brought them even closer.

Kurt invited them into the main house. Maizie served them hot tea, then sat quietly to one side.

She kept glancing at Erika. The beautiful woman kneeling beside the older boy looked lovely no matter how Maizie looked at her!

Every movement Erika made was graceful and pleasing to watch. She was just like the well-bred ladies Grandpa was always talking about.

It was a pity that Maizie could never seem to live up to what he hoped for, no matter what she did.

Kurt brought over a red cushion. Resting on it was an unusual Poké Ball, half gold and half white.

"This is the GS Ball," he said with a sigh.

At his words, Kurt and Erika leaned forward slightly to examine it more closely.

It was Ethan's first time seeing the GS Ball too, but he already knew its secret, so it did not stir much curiosity in him.

He glanced at it twice, saw that it matched what he remembered, and looked away. Then he took a small sip of tea.

Ah! Hot!

He set down the cup and turned toward the courtyard. A huge Apricorn tree grew there, its branches full of round fruit.

Those Apricorns were used to make Poké Balls. With heating, hammering, cooling, and other steps, followed by special finishing techniques, a craftsman could turn one into a handmade Poké Ball.

Even now, many older people still used—and trusted only—these Apricorn Balls, unlike most young people.

From that perspective, Kurt's son had strayed far from the family craft by joining Silph Co. instead of learning to make them!

Professor Elm and Kurt began discussing the ball, drawing Ethan's attention back to the room.

Elm turned the GS Ball over and over in his hands, frowning. He could make nothing of it.

"I heard this ball can't be transferred by machine?"

"That's right," Kurt said. "That puzzles me too. Usually only old-fashioned, handmade Poké Balls can't pass through modern transfer machines."

Professor Elm raised his brows. "Yet its materials don't appear to be handmade. I'm sorry, Mr. Kurt, but I can't tell anything more just by looking. We'll have to examine it with instruments."

Kurt was a little disappointed, though he still held out hope.

"Sigh... We can't open it, and it doesn't react to Pokémon at all. If we knew who made it, we might solve everything. But neither Oak nor Ivy, who discovered it, knows where it came from. They could only name it after the letters 'GS' on its surface."

Professor Elm looked surprised. "Even you can't tell who made it?"

Kurt shook his head. "I'm hardly the only craftsman in the world. Plenty of little-known people are remarkably skilled and use all sorts of strange materials. Without a maker's mark, there's no way to identify them."

When both men fell silent, Ethan gave a small cough. "Actually, I heard a rumor about a special Poké Ball while I was traveling."

Every eye in the room turned to him.

Ethan spoke slowly. "Someone once told me of a unique ball made with a capture net woven from a Rainbow Wing and a Silver Wing. It was crafted specifically to catch an exceptionally rare Legendary Pokémon!"
