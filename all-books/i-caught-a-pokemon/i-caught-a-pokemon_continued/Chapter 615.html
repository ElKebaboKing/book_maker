Flannery looked about Ethan's age. She was pretty and had a good figure, with a black cropped T-shirt above her jeans. Her bright red hair had been arranged into several broad sections, like the petals of a flower in full bloom.

As a Trainer who specialized in Fire-type Pokémon, she seemed every bit as fiery as her chosen type. Her enthusiasm was infectious, though the way she treated strangers like old friends could be a little overwhelming.

What Ethan couldn't understand was why hearing that someone had come to challenge the Gym had provoked such a reaction from her.

“Isn't Mr. Moore the Lavaridge Gym Leader?” he asked hesitantly.

Moore was the previous Lavaridge Gym Leader and a former member of Hoenn's Elite Four. He was an elderly man with glasses and a kindly appearance.

Before coming to Hoenn, Ethan had read a Trainer's travel guide that clearly named Moore as the Lavaridge Gym Leader. He hadn't expected the position to change hands in the few days since then.

“My grandpa?” Flannery shook her head. “He's retired and passed the Gym on to me. Come on, let's battle already! I feel like I'm on fire!”

Her grandfather?

So she was the new Gym Leader.

Flannery was plainly impatient and excitable, but she was cheerful too. From this brief exchange alone, Ethan could tell she would be easy to get along with. Perhaps that was a common trait among Trainers devoted to Fire-type Pokémon.

The change of Gym Leader didn't really matter to him. His only regret was missing the chance to face a former Elite Four member.

Before he could say anything else, Flannery took out a Poké Ball and threw it. A large turtle with orange-brown skin appeared before them.

“Enough talk! Let's have a great Gym battle!”

She was about to order the turtle into action when Misty hurriedly stopped her.

“Wait!” Misty stared at her. “Miss Flannery, shouldn't an official Gym battle take place on the Gym's battlefield?”

“Oh, I forgot.” Flannery paused, then smiled awkwardly. “I got carried away. Sorry! I've only been Gym Leader for a few days, and I'm still getting used to things.”

Her explanation did little to reassure Misty.

Still getting used to things? Surely everyone knew where a Gym battle belonged. Her grandfather had run this very Gym, yet she'd forgotten something so basic!

Having grown up in a Gym family herself, Misty knew more about the job than most people. Flannery's lack of professionalism left her unimpressed.

Ethan said nothing. It wasn't that he was more forgiving because Flannery was beautiful. Everyone started out inexperienced, and unfamiliar work could make anyone flustered. He could also see how much she loved being a Gym Leader. Her excitable personality simply made her seem rather scattered.

Flannery led them into the Lavaridge Gym. Beyond its spacious entrance hall lay the battlefield. When Ethan and Misty saw it, both frowned again.

Perhaps because the Gym stood at the foot of Mt. Chimney, or because Lavaridge itself was so rocky, its Fire-type battlefield was covered with stones. At the moment, however, it was a wreck. The ground was pitted, the rocks had been smashed into countless pieces, and signs of battle were everywhere.

Noticing their expressions, Flannery gave another embarrassed laugh.

“Ahaha... I had a challenger yesterday, and then I forgot to clean up the field. So this is how it ended up...”

She had forgotten that too? Misty was speechless.

Repairing a battlefield normally wasn't a Gym Leader's personal responsibility. The Pokémon League provided substantial funding each year, enough for a Gym to hire people to handle such work.

Brock was an exception. With so many younger siblings to support, he had needed to spend the Gym's money on his family and couldn't afford other employees. But judging by its size, the Lavaridge Gym was hardly short of funds. It shouldn't have been run in this state.

Flannery wasn't merely careless; she seemed to have no management skills at all.

She was about to ask Ethan and Misty to rest while she fixed the battlefield, but Ethan stopped her.

“It's fine.” He shook his head. “The field's damaged, but I think a battlefield that's already seen a fight will be more complicated—and more interesting—than another ordinary rocky field.”

Flannery regarded him with new respect. Most Trainers would have waited for repairs, whether they wanted a fairer match or lacked confidence in their own strength. His indifference suggested he trusted his abilities.

Only then did she take a proper look at him. He was about her age and remarkably handsome, especially his eyes. Her impression of him improved at once. She let the repairs wait, and they took their places on opposite sides of the field.

“First, the rules.” Flannery finally sounded like a professional. She straightened up as she spoke. “When my grandpa was here, he'd decide how many Pokémon to use and which ones to send out after judging the challenger. That sounds too complicated, so I've decided to make this like other Gym battles: three Pokémon each!”

Then she remembered one more detail. “Oh, and only you can switch Pokémon during the battle. Is that okay?”

Ethan nodded. Those rules were much the same as at other Gyms.

What her grandfather had done was another matter. Adjusting a challenger's match to their ability on the spot required both considerable strength and a wealth of experience. Flannery didn't yet have either, which explained why she hadn't adopted his approach.

Ethan introduced himself, and Flannery quietly repeated his name before directing her large turtle onto the field. Ethan and Misty both studied it.

Though the turtle stood only fifty centimeters tall, it was remarkably heavy. Dark gray rings marked its limbs and long neck, each foot bore three claws, and a short tail protruded behind it. Its shell was mostly dark gray, with five fiery red hollows around it that seemed to vent excess heat from its body.

Misty had never seen one before, so she took out her Pokédex and aimed it at the Pokémon.

“Beep. Torkoal, the Coal Pokémon. Fire type. Torkoal are often found in abandoned coal mines. They continually dig up coal and burn it in the holes in their shells. When attacked, they release black smoke and escape. Smoke from their nostrils makes a sound like a train whistle.”

So it was called Torkoal. Misty nodded, put away her Pokédex, and turned her attention back to the match.

From the Trainer's area, Ethan considered which Pokémon to send out. By its presence and condition, Torkoal was clearly no weakling. It might even be one of Flannery's strongest partners. Eevee, Combusken, and Beldum weren't ready to face it.

Instead of making his choice immediately, he asked, “Miss Flannery, is that your strongest Pokémon?”

“My strongest?” Flannery thought for a moment and smiled. “One of them! I have a Magmortar too. Those two are the partners I trust most!”

Just as he'd suspected. As a new Gym Leader, Flannery still had much to learn. She hadn't prepared any ordinary Pokémon for beginner challengers; she had sent out an ace instead.

He glanced at the scarred field and asked cautiously, “What sort of Trainer challenged you yesterday?”

“A little girl who hadn't been traveling long,” Flannery said with a smile. “She was adorable. She told me Lavaridge was the first Gym she'd ever challenged!”

“Did she win?”

“Of course not!” Flannery looked puzzled by the question. “She used all three of her Pokémon and only just managed to beat my Magmortar. Then I sent out Torkoal and told her it was about as strong as Magmortar. I don't know why, but she started crying. She ran away without even battling it!”

Ethan had no words.

How cruel! Looking again at the marks all over the field, he could picture a novice Trainer who had worked hard to catch three Pokémon and arrived full of hope for her first Gym challenge, only to run straight into the Gym Leader's best team and be overwhelmed.

After giving everything she had to defeat a single Pokémon, she hadn't even had a chance to catch her breath before facing another of equal strength. No wonder she'd been devastated. That battle must have left a lasting shadow over her journey.

What a frightening woman.

Still, knowing that Torkoal was one of Flannery's strongest Pokémon made Ethan's choice simpler. He took a Poké Ball from his waist pouch and threw it across the field. A flash of blue light released Tyranitar.

An ordinary opponent called for one approach; an ace called for another. Apart from Gardevoir and Gengar, all the non-Legendary Pokémon currently traveling with Ethan still needed training. Whatever his opponent sent out, he had someone to meet it.

As soon as Tyranitar appeared, it threw back its head and roared, just as it had before.

“ROAR!”

The sound echoed through the enclosed Gym, loud enough to shake their ears.

Flannery might have been careless about running a Gym, but she had an unusual eye for Pokémon. One look at Tyranitar told her it was no ordinary opponent.

“Oh?” She frowned and examined it, her voice turning unusually grave. “That Tyranitar looks strong. I've only felt something like this from my grandpa's Pokémon!”

Was Tyranitar strong? Ethan merely shook his head without answering.

He already had two exceptionally powerful and ferocious Pokémon in his red Gyarados and Crystal Steelix. Beside them, Tyranitar was only average—middle of the pack for its species at best. It had yet to distinguish itself, much less satisfy Ethan.

That was why he had brought Dragonite, Tyranitar, and Scizor on this journey: he wanted to concentrate on training them.

Tyranitar fixed its gaze on Torkoal. The two Pokémon stared each other down, their fighting spirit rising until both were ready to strike.

Flannery, fiery as ever, settled into battle after her moment of admiration. Her eyes grew serious, a complete change from the excitable young woman of a moment ago. Now she looked like a proper Gym Leader.

With no other staff present, she served as the referee herself.

“Begin!”

At Ethan's command, Tyranitar attacked from across the field. It roared, and a succession of sharp stones surged up from the ground toward Torkoal.

Stone Edge, a Rock-type move.

Torkoal was far too slow to evade it. Instead, it resorted to a turtle Pokémon's most familiar defense: it drew its head and limbs into its shell, letting the hard casing absorb the Stone Edge.
