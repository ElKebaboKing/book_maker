An attendant led May to a lounge on her own.

May wanted to ask where Ethan was going and why they had been separated. Before she could, several more attendants arrived with plates of plump fruit and tempting desserts. Her attention went straight to the food.

“Miss May, please rest here for a while. We're preparing a guest room for you. Once it's ready, you can take a bath and settle in for the night.”

May nodded, then realized what the attendant had said. “Oh! Yes…all right.”

After the attendants left, she tried some fruit and then helped herself to a dessert. She ate contentedly for a while.

May loved snacks, but even the best food lost its hold on her after she had tasted it. Where had Ethan gone? Why hadn't he come back? Could something have happened to him?

Alone in an unfamiliar building among strangers, it was easy for a young girl to imagine trouble.

When they had landed on the terrace, Ethan had introduced May to the beautiful older woman. Then he and Maya had gone off together, leaving an attendant to bring May here. A long time had passed since then.

May looked outside. It was already past ten. Curious and worried, she set down what she was eating and slipped out of the lounge before the attendants returned.

A long corridor stretched outside. Whether because it was late or because this part of the temple was usually quiet, she saw no one. She could not even hear footsteps. Only the faint sound of waves reached her through the windows.

The silence in such a large building unsettled her.

Maya hasn't locked Ethan up, has she?

The priestess was beautiful and dignified. She had looked close to Ethan, much as Erika did, and May doubted she meant him harm. But Ethan had several women in his life. If Maya found out, she might be furious.

Could she already know? The thought made May increasingly uneasy.

She understood that Ethan's relationships could be seen as playing with several women's feelings. If they became public, he might at least be scolded and, at worst, lose his reputation. Maya was a Sea Priestess with standing and influence. An argument with her could be especially serious.

I should at least explain things for him.

May looked both ways, worked out the route back, and headed deeper into the Temple of the Sea.

The building's layout was complicated, perhaps to give it a solemn atmosphere. She nearly lost her way several times. The night was dark, and much of the corridor was lit by candles instead of electric lights. It made her uncomfortable—and a little afraid.

She did not release a Pokémon. That might draw the attendants' attention. Gathering her courage, she went on alone.

For a temple this large, the lack of guards surprised her. It did allow her to reach Maya's room without being stopped.

May stood before the closed door. Beyond it was Maya's bedchamber and the terrace where they had landed. Ethan had gone inside and had not reappeared.

What were they doing? Was Maya reprimanding him? Were they getting along?

There were no windows on either side. May tried pushing the door. It would not open; it seemed to be bolted from within. She managed to shift it only enough to make a tiny gap.

Through that gap, she heard a woman making broken, unfamiliar sounds.

May recognized Maya's voice, but it sounded strange—at moments pained, at others impossible to place. It rose, sharpened, fell, and softened.

May had never heard sounds like that before. Even so, her face turned bright red.

Wh-what is that?

She had an inkling. The thought made her heart race and her mouth go dry. Almost without meaning to, she leaned toward the gap and opened her eyes wide.

Just as she put her eye to it, footsteps sounded from farther down the corridor.

May started and quickly stepped back. A few seconds later, the attendant from the lounge came into view.

The woman looked surprised to find May there. Her gaze flicked to the narrow gap in Maya's door.

“Miss May,” she said smoothly, “what are you doing here? I was coming to find you.”

May's ears burned. She wanted to sink through the floor. “I… I was just feeling cooped up, so I went for a walk.”

The attendant smiled. She clearly saw through the excuse, but did not say so.

Voices in the corridor seemed to reach the people inside the room. The sounds beyond the door stopped abruptly.

The attendant noticed too. She gestured down the corridor. “Your room is ready, Miss May. Please come with me.”

May could scarcely remember following her. Only when she stood in a bright, warm, comfortable guest room and heard the attendant say good night and close the door did she come back to herself. Her heart was still pounding.

…

May did not see Ethan again until the next morning.

She sat at a long table in a spacious dining hall, eating breakfast. When she happened to look up, Ethan was coming through a side door, looking thoroughly refreshed.

She opened her mouth to greet him, then thought better of it. She lowered her head and took a spoonful of porridge.

Ethan did not notice her embarrassment. But as he came closer, he saw the dark circles beneath her eyes. “What's wrong? Didn't you sleep well?”

May felt a rush of shame. Keeping her face as calm as she could, she gave a small nod. “I guess I'm not used to the bed. I didn't fall asleep until dawn.”

That was not the real reason. After what had happened the night before, she had bathed and lain awake for hours with one thought circling in her head.

She had glanced through the door only briefly, yet she had seen enough to know she should not have looked. In the moonlight, two figures had been on the bed. She could not see exactly what they were doing, but Ethan had been bare-skinned and above Maya.

The glimpse had shaken her. She had turned over in bed for ages and even had a strange dream when she finally slept.

She could tell Ethan none of that. It was something she meant to keep buried.

To stop him noticing her discomfort, she asked, “Where's Maya? I haven't seen her.”

Her cheeks warmed again. Did the question reveal that she knew Ethan and Maya had spent the night together? Fortunately, Ethan did not read that much into it.

“She left just after dawn. There was an important ceremony she had to conduct.” He sat down. “Let's eat quickly. Afterward I'll show you some of the well-known places around here.”

May's attention shifted. Once they finished breakfast, they left the temple and visited Inland City down the mountain. Then they went to the Whirl Stadium, where the Whirl Cup had been held.

“See this place?” Ethan said, pointing toward the battlefield. “This is where I knocked out Ash and Misty and earned the title of Sea Hero.”

Show-off.

May was glad he had brought her to see the island and its sights. Still, something puzzled her. They had been in such a hurry that they had flown here on Charizard. Why did he suddenly have time for a leisurely tour?

The answer arrived soon enough.

From the stadium they traveled to Bluefinland, a town on the other side of the island. Residents were streaming from every direction toward its square. May overheard their conversations: Sea Priestess Maya would hold a ceremony there today to bestow the God of the Sea's blessing.

Oh, so that's why we're sightseeing. Ethan had brought her along because he wanted to attend Maya's ceremony. May shook her head helplessly.

In the midst of the ceremony, Maya stood with her scepter in hand. She looked sacred and untouchable, carrying all the dignity of her office. The people around her praised her and bowed in reverence.

Yet May could not keep the image from the previous night out of her mind: Maya on the bed beneath Ethan.

Wonderful. Now I can't forget it. She sighed in frustration.

After the ceremony, Ethan and May met up with Maya and returned to the Temple of the Sea.

What happened next was even more trying for May. Ethan and Maya shut themselves in a room again and did not appear for the rest of the day. May ate dinner by herself.

At least she had her own Pokémon and Ethan's for company, so she was not lonely.

She next saw him at breakfast the following morning. This time, to her relief, Ethan was finally ready to leave with her. Otherwise, every night in bed might have brought back that glimpse through Maya's door.

Maya came to see them off in person. Ethan and May climbed onto Charizard and nodded goodbye. With a few beats of its wings, Charizard rose from the Temple of the Sea and flew into the sky.

It was fast. When May looked back, the temple was already far away. Even so, she had the distinct feeling that Maya was still standing there, watching them go.

…

Charizard carried them north from the Whirl Islands. Their next destination was Ecruteak City, where they would visit the Kimono Sisters.

Ethan's Eevee had spent an entire regional journey waiting to evolve. It was ready, but Ethan had not settled on a form for it. Perhaps talking with the five sisters in Ecruteak would help him finally make a choice.

Charizard's shadow skimmed over the sea. The sunshine seemed perfect for travel. Ethan and May both thought so at first.

Half an hour later, the weather changed. Wind rose, then strengthened. The clouds grew dark. An unbroken bank of storm clouds appeared on the horizon, lightning flashing within it, while the sea below turned rough.

“A storm!” Ethan called back to May.

She leaned around his arm to look ahead. Her brow tightened. “Can we fly through it?”

Ethan shook his head. “Charizard could make it alone. We couldn't. Even if lightning missed us, the cold rain and wind would be too much.”

The wind grew stronger. May had to shout for him to hear her. “Then what do we do?”
