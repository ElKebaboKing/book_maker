"It is strange," Ethan agreed. He did not understand why the Orange League had made a rule that disadvantaged its own Supreme Gym Leader. After watching several more match recordings and getting a better sense of Drake's ability, he said, "Even under that restriction, he hasn't lost. He's held on to the Winner's Cup all this time. We can't underestimate him."

Sabrina was about to answer when she frowned and turned toward the sea. "Something out there is coming this way fast."

Ethan looked too. The water seemed calm, with only a few speedboats moving about. He saw nothing unusual, but he trusted Sabrina's powers. Her psychic senses often warned them of things before anyone else noticed. If she felt something approaching, he believed her.

Seconds later, a dark shape appeared in the distance, flying so low that it seemed to skim the water. As it neared Pummelo Island, Ethan made out a muscular man riding a Dragonite.

Whoosh!

Dragonite raced toward the beach. At its rider's direction, it changed course, climbed sharply into the sky, and then dropped from the clouds toward Pummelo Stadium on the hill.

People along the shore cheered and waved. They had clearly recognized the Trainer. Ethan and Sabrina exchanged a glance. They had not seen his face, but who else would be arriving on a Dragonite? It had to be Drake.

Only a moment ago they had been studying ways to battle him. Now they had seen him in person. The crowd's continuing excitement showed Ethan just how popular Drake was in the Orange Islands.

After a while, Ethan got up and went with Sabrina to a nearby stand selling cold drinks and fruit. A man in his fifties sat there in a rocking chair, fanning himself. He stood to greet them.

"What can I get you?"

Ethan looked over the drinks and ice cream, then stopped at a pile of plump oranges. "Do oranges ripen at this time of year? I thought they were a late-autumn fruit."

"You're visiting from another region, aren't you?" the vendor said with a chuckle. "These are warm-season mandarins, a local specialty. They ripen now, and they're wonderfully juicy."

He broke one open and offered it to Ethan. "Go on, try it. I promise you'll like it."

Ethan accepted the fruit, finished peeling it, and put half in Sabrina's hand. They each tasted a segment. Juice burst into their mouths as they bit down. It was sweet with just a little tartness.

Ethan ate the rest of his half and gave the vendor a thumbs-up. "These are delicious. I think they're the best mandarins I've ever tasted."

The vendor beamed. He was a lifelong resident of the Orange Islands, and seeing visitors enjoy something from his home delighted him.

A decorated car rolled slowly down the street beside the beach. Orange League emblems covered it, and a speaker on its roof repeated an announcement.

"Attention, residents of the Orange Islands! Tomorrow at ten in the morning, Pummelo Stadium will host a Winner's Cup match, the first in some time. Bring your families. Admission is free!"

"A match tomorrow?" the vendor said. "At last! I've waited so long for one."

He began packing up his stand. Ethan was surprised. "Why close so early?"

"I've got to reserve a good seat. If I wait, they'll all be taken."

"But the announcement said everyone can get in for free."

"They can," the man said with a hearty laugh. "But if you want a good view, you have to book ahead. What fun is it sitting at the back and watching the whole battle on a screen?"

He looked Ethan over. "If you're free tomorrow, you and your girlfriend should go too. Matches like this don't come along often. You'll enjoy it."

Ethan seized the opening. "I've heard Drake is strong. Is he really that good?"

"Of course!" The vendor's voice rose with enthusiasm. "He's the best Trainer in the Orange Islands. Every one of his Pokémon is powerful, especially his ace, Dragonite."

"Then plenty of people must want to beat him. Why has it been so long since the last match?"

"Because he's too strong! Hardly anyone dares challenge him. Plenty can't even beat his first Pokémon, Ditto. You'll see. Whoever's challenging him tomorrow will get crushed, just like the others."

Ethan fell silent. The man had no idea he was speaking to the challenger. Ethan could only laugh to himself at being dismissed so easily.

"I'm not so sure," he said. "How about a wager?"

"A wager?"

"I say the challenger wins this time."

"Interesting! What are we betting?"

Ethan's gaze moved across the stand and settled on the mandarins. "Those. If I win, you give me a few dozen pounds of fresh mandarins. If I lose, I'll buy the same amount at twice your usual price. Deal?"

"Deal!" The vendor was easygoing, amused by the unusual offer, and utterly confident in Drake. "You're underestimating him, young man. Just watch. Drake will win again."
