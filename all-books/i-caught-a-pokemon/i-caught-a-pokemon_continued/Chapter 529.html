As they talked, the group followed the girl. This time Latias seemed to be guiding them. Whenever she came to a fork, she waited until they caught up before running deeper into the neighborhood.

The chase stretched Ethan, Ash, and the others into a long line.

Ethan and Erika were at the back. When no one else was close enough to hear, Erika said softly, "That girl isn't the same person we saw just now, is she?"

"Huh?" Ethan stared at her. "How did you know?"

"At first it was just a woman's intuition. Then I noticed the small differences in their expressions. But the clearest clues were her hat and what she was carrying."

"Her hat and what she was carrying?"

Ethan remembered that the girl at the cathedral had worn a white hat. The one running ahead of them wore no hat and carried no drawing board.

He had overlooked both details. Erika had not.

She had been sharp enough to notice the difference all along.

"You're right. They aren't the same person." Seeing how far ahead Ash and the others had run, Ethan leaned close enough to whisper so they could not hear. "Actually, I think the girl in front of us is Latias in disguise."

He did not tell Erika everything. If he seemed to know too much about what would happen, she might start wondering how he could possibly have known it.

Latias?

After her first surprise, Erika understood. "No wonder. I thought it was strange that you'd keep following Ash around just to chase some girl. I knew there must be a reason, but I didn't expect her to be Latias."

Considerate as ever, Erika did not ask how Ethan knew. He had revealed unexpected information like this many times before. In her view, a good partner did not need to question him about everything; she could simply listen.

"Otherwise, I wouldn't have played this chasing game for so long," Ethan said with a wry smile. Then his expression grew serious. "Be careful, though. Remember those two women? Latias is their target too. They might be watching us from somewhere right now."

In fact, he was right.

The thieves had only just been driven off and had no intention of giving up. Unseen by Ethan's group, a small spider-shaped robot clung to a nearby rooftop. Its camera sent everything it saw back to the women.

Latias led them through turn after turn in the complicated residential district, finally reaching a vine-covered passageway. She ran into it and, to everyone's astonishment, passed straight through what looked like a solid wall. Then she was gone.

As she crossed it, faint colored light shimmered over the wall, like a barrier.

Ash stopped in front of it, unsure whether he dared try the same thing. He looked toward Ethan when Ethan reached his side.

"Let's go." With everyone watching him, Ethan stepped toward the wall first. "The answers to what we've been wondering are on the other side of this barrier."

He touched it. It resisted his hand like a thin film. Without hesitating, he took another step and passed through.

When Ethan vanished, Ash and the others exchanged uneasy looks. Then they gathered their courage and followed.

The darkness lasted only an instant. On the other side, Ethan found himself in a vast circular garden. Old-fashioned walls enclosed it, and a huge glass dome arched overhead. Sunlight poured through the panes onto lush trees and plants.

Footsteps sounded behind him as Ash and the others emerged. They, too, marveled at the hidden world beyond the wall.

The garden covered an enormous area and was remarkably quiet. Apart from their group, there was no sign of human activity.

Small Pokémon played in the grass on either side. Flocks of Butterfree fluttered overhead. Everything seemed peaceful and unhurried.

They descended the stone steps and headed farther in. Latias, still in the girl's form, stood near the center. She had stopped running and smiled at them before walking toward a broad pond.

As they approached, they saw her sitting on a swing in the distance. Each time she kicked her legs forward, it swept through a wide arc.

They had just drawn near and were about to speak to her when a westerly wind rose beside them. Before they could react, water burst from the pond and a transparent Pokémon flew toward them at tremendous speed. Its body could still be traced by the way it distorted the space around it.

It rushed past them, spraying water into the air. Then it wheeled around and charged at them again.

This time Pikachu reacted. Apart from Misty's Togepi and the Gengar hidden in Ethan's shadow, it was the only Pokémon currently at anyone's side. It leaped in front of the group to protect them.

Crouching low, Pikachu fixed its eyes on the transparent Pokémon and unleashed a stream of electricity.

The attacker drove through the electric attack and sent Pikachu flying. It rolled a long way across the ground, completely overpowered. The transparent Pokémon turned its attention back to Ethan and the others, ready to attack again—until Latias, still in the girl's form, suddenly stepped between them.

At the sight of her, the Pokémon stopped at once. Its invisibility faded, revealing its true form.

It was about two meters long, white in front and blue behind, with a sleek body, wings like those of a jet, and feet resembling fins. It looked part bird, part fish, and part dragon.

There was no mistaking it. This was Latios, the Pokémon Ethan remembered seeing at Latias's side in the movie.

The others had never seen this Legendary Pokémon in person. Even so, they immediately connected it with the two statues at the harbor and recognized it.
