Ethan stared at Drew in astonishment. For a moment, he wondered if the boy actually enjoyed being hurt.

Had Misty's slap awakened some hidden side of him? Or was this going to turn into one of those ridiculous romances where a spoiled rich boy fell for the fierce girl who put him in his place?

Ethan raised an eyebrow to test his guess. “You mean Misty?”

Drew seemed to notice the look in his eyes. An awkward expression crossed his face. “Of course I mean her. She slapped me last time. You think I'll let that go? I'm going to pay her back.”

It was a plausible reason, yet it sounded to Ethan like an excuse. Once suspicion had taken root, it grew quickly. The more Drew explained, the more unusual his behavior seemed.

“Give it up,” Ethan said, watching him closely. “Even if you see her again, I doubt you'll do anything to her. And some things... simply aren't going to happen.”

The words carried two meanings. Drew's face changed. He was no fool; Ethan seemed to have guessed what was on his mind.

But how could he? Only in the past few days had Drew noticed that, night after night, he thought about the slap. Misty's face and every movement came back to him vividly. He was not even sure what those thoughts meant. How had Ethan seen through him?

Unwilling to talk any longer, Drew turned and entered the elevator without another word. Ethan watched until its doors closed.

He often said he thought of Misty as a little sister, but he knew he could be selfish. As long as they remained this close and their journey together continued, he would give no one else an opening, even if Misty herself had no interest in Drew.

Ethan shook off the thought and went to the floor-to-ceiling window. This was LaRousse's highest point. Most of the city spread out beneath him.

The city of technology had been laid out carefully. Residential, commercial, and office buildings were easy to tell apart. He scanned it several times but still could not pick out the research laboratory.

Where was the Deoxys core being kept?

He looked toward the far side of the island. A thick forest divided the city in two. He could make out the outline of the buildings beyond it, but not enough detail to identify them. Was the lab on the other side?

As he considered it, a voice message arrived from Misty. Ash's match was about to start.

Already? Ash must have found a teammate quickly. Since Ethan had made no progress with his search, he left the window and took the elevator down to the arena floor.

The battle arena occupied the broad middle section of the tower. Its circular field resembled a red-and-blue Poké Ball, with rings of spectator seats surrounding it.

The announcer was warming up the crowd when Ethan entered. His excited voice drew cheers from the stands.

Several matches took place here every day. As soon as enough competitors were ready, another battle could begin. Admission was free. A spectator could sit from morning until night and watch for as long as Trainers kept coming.

Ethan found the seat number Misty had sent. Brock's early arrival had paid off: he had secured places with an excellent view. They were neither too high nor too low, too close nor too far. Ethan could see the whole field without relying on the large screens overhead.

Misty, May, and Max were back, and the twins who had accompanied Rafe and Sid were sitting beside them.

When Misty noticed Ethan looking at the girls, she smiled. “You don't know their names yet, do you? This is Audrey and this is Kathryn. Audrey, in orange, is the older sister. Kathryn, in pink, is the younger. They look alike, don't they?”

“Nice to meet you. I'm Ethan.” He nodded to both girls and sat beside Misty. Then he told the group about seeing Drew.

“Drew lives in LaRousse?” May said in surprise. The others were taken aback too, but they soon turned their attention to the arena again.

It had all the flair of a high-tech city. Even the announcer, who also served as referee, flew around the arena on a small aircraft. Whatever the quality of the matches, the spectacle already outshone many other venues—even the Indigo and Silver Conferences.

Was that the appeal of a city built around technology?

The Battle Tower was also independent of the Pokémon League. Like the Whirl Cup, it was not run by the League or subject to its direction. LaRousse's wealth had equipped the arena with facilities many League competitions would envy.

Click. The lights over the stands went out, telling the audience the match was about to begin.

“Our first match today is a team battle!” the announcer called from above the field. “Let's welcome both sides!”

Under the spotlights, the floor opened and two platforms rose. On the red side stood Rafe and Sid. Opposite them were Ash and a handsome little boy who looked only seven or eight.

“That's Ash's teammate?” Misty blurted out. “He's just a kid! He looks about Max's age.”

“What's wrong with that?” Max adjusted his glasses and glanced at her. “Kids can be strong. Ash chose him, so there must be something special about him. Just watch!”

“Oh? I'll be watching, then,” Misty said with a smile.

If anyone could get the better of Max in an argument, it was Misty. He seemed a little afraid of her fierceness and did not talk back to her as freely as he did to Ash.

Ethan said nothing. He stroked his chin and studied the boy. Leave it to Ash to find the person at the heart of the story so quickly. His luck was remarkable.

The competitors' details appeared on a screen, and the announcer introduced them. “On the red side are Rafe and Sid from a southern city! They've won several matches together and are a formidable pair. On the blue side are two first-time competitors today: Ash and our local LaRousse boy, Tory!”

As usual, the announcer explained the rules. “Each competitor sends out one Pokémon. Partners fight together until both Pokémon on one side are defeated. Now, please send out your Pokémon!”

Matches here moved quickly. There were many entrants, and one pair could take the field as soon as another had finished. No screening trials or preliminaries stood in their way. If two people teamed up and were willing to battle, they would have an audience.

Skill levels varied, but the brisk pace and lack of delays made the matches exciting to watch.

Rafe and Sid released their Pokémon together: the Blaziken Ethan had seen earlier and the Blastoise Sid had mentioned.

Ash sent out Pikachu. Tory, however, made no move to release a Pokémon.

After more than ten seconds, the crowd began whispering. Even the announcer sounded puzzled. “It seems Tory isn't sending out a Pokémon. Has something happened?”

Max stared. “What's going on? Why won't he send one out?”

Brock frowned. “Maybe he doesn't have a Pokémon.”

“He doesn't?” Max echoed.

It sounded strange in a world where people and Pokémon lived so closely together. Even so, many people never owned a Pokémon, for all kinds of reasons. Could Tory be one of them? If so, why had he entered a battle?

Misty glanced at Max and teased him without mercy. “So that's what makes Ash's teammate special. You were right—he's making quite an impression.”

Max's face turned red. He stared at the boy, unable to understand why Ash had brought him onto the field.

Ash was talking to Tory, but they were too far away for Ethan's group to hear over the crowd.

When no second Pokémon appeared, the referee was nearly ready to declare a forfeit. At the last moment, Ash took out another of his own Poké Balls and handed it to Tory.

Tory hesitated, then opened it. Ash's Torkoal emerged.

It seemed to stretch the rule that each competitor use one Pokémon when Ash had supplied both. Still, they had avoided immediate disqualification, and the announcer, eager to get the match going, declared the start.

The spectators focused on the field. Rafe and Sid struck first, directing Blaziken and Blastoise into an attack.

Their teamwork was excellent. Blaziken leaped in and broke up Pikachu and Torkoal's formation. As Pikachu dodged, Blastoise caught it with Hydro Pump.

Blastoise followed with Bubble against Torkoal, then used Rapid Spin to knock it into the air. The first exchange had already cost Ash dearly.

No wonder Rafe and Sid had won so many matches. They were good.

Ash was no beginner now. He still had his mishaps, but after traveling through two regions he had become a regular contender for at least the top sixteen at major tournaments. If he was struggling this early, his opponents were capable Trainers.

Of course, he and Tory had no teamwork at all.

Ash tried to turn the match around, but Pikachu could not fight alone. Tory stood like a bystander, giving Torkoal no commands.

Even Max could see the problem. “Why is he just standing there? Does he really know nothing about battling?”

Misty stopped teasing him. She looked worriedly at Ethan. “Is Ash going to lose?”

“Probably,” Ethan said with a helpless shake of his head. “Unless he changes what's happening, it's only a matter of time.”

Ash directed Pikachu in a counterattack, but Torkoal, without guidance, sent out a burst of flame that hit Pikachu's tail instead of their opponents.

The crowd booed. They wanted a fast match, but not one where a team could not work together at all.

Ash finally took charge of both Pokémon himself. One Trainer could hardly match the attention of two, though, especially against Pokémon as strong as Blaziken and Blastoise.

He held on a little longer. Then Blaziken used Overheat. Its fierce flames struck Pikachu and Torkoal together, knocking both out.

May covered her mouth. “They really lost...”

Before the match, she and Misty had joked that Ash might be eliminated immediately. May had not expected a defeat this lopsided. He had barely managed a proper counterattack.

“Pikachu and Torkoal are unable to battle! Rafe and Sid on the red side win!” the announcer declared.

The one-sided match had not satisfied the crowd, but they still gave the winners a hearty round of cheers and applause.

Below, Ash gathered Pikachu and Torkoal into his arms. He looked dejected as the platform carried him and Tory back underground.

Ethan sighed and stood. “The match is over. Let's go see them.”

Ash had come here full of anticipation, only to lose badly within minutes. Instead of showing what he could do on this stage, he had fought an embarrassing match. Most people would be shaken. Fortunately, Ash was Ash. He would probably recover from the disappointment without anyone comforting him.
