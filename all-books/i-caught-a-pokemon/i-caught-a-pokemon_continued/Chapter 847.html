May had not expected things to turn out this way. Brock was not finished surprising her. Fired up by his decision to compete, he challenged her directly.

“You'd better be ready, May! The brilliant Miss Yuma has personally vouched for me. I'll be the biggest obstacle between you and the Gardenia Ribbon!”

May had thought the Contest would have no strong rivals. Now a friend was entering to compete against her. Brock was a formidable opponent. If he passed the appeal round, his years as a Gym Leader would make him difficult for most Coordinators to beat.

But May was not afraid. Yuma could teach Brock; May had Ethan beside her. In appeals or battles, she intended to win.

She smiled. “I'm looking forward to it. Just be careful you don't go out in the first round.”

“Same to you.” Brock folded his arms and smiled confidently through his narrow eyes. “You and Eevee are the ones who need to be careful.”

Once he had committed to entering, Brock seemed transformed. He went off with Yuma for private training. Ash and Max followed, curious to see what sort of special methods she had. Ash forgot all about his proposed battle with Ethan.

That left Ethan and May together again. Brock's challenge had stirred May's determination, and she returned to practice with renewed energy. Ethan could only shake his head and recline on the bench, waiting to see what would happen.

…

“It's performance time!”

At Lillian's familiar opening cry, the Gardenia Contest began amid the audience's cheers. Ethan sat in the stands with Ash and Max. With two friends competing, they had stayed out of the contestants' waiting room rather than favor either one. They would simply watch and cheer for both May and Brock.

Lillian explained the rules, introduced the same three familiar judges, and welcomed the first Coordinator to the stage.

To Ethan's surprise, it was Mollie, the girl who had asked May for directions on the lawn. She carried a long wooden stick, whose purpose was not yet clear. Despite being a newcomer onstage for the first time before so many people, she showed no nerves. She walked out gracefully and bowed.

Then she released the Raticate they had seen with her. Mollie threw the stick high. Raticate sprang after it and caught it between its two large front teeth. In moments it had gnawed the wood to pieces. The simple performance showed off Raticate's particular charm, and the crowd cheered.

“She doesn't perform like a beginner,” Ethan said after Mollie finished. “Plenty of people who've entered several Contests couldn't stay that calm and composed. Give her a few years, and I think she'll be one of the top Coordinators.”

Ash and Max were startled to hear him praise someone so strongly, but they could see why. Mollie received a good score. She bowed once more before leaving the stage, as poised and polite as ever.

Several other contestants followed with competent, unremarkable appeals. Then it was May's turn.

As planned, her partner was Eevee. She used a ball as a prop, creating an interplay between herself, the ball, and her Pokémon. There were no dazzling moves. Instead, the routine brought out Eevee's grace and skill. May had clearly planned it with care. She and Eevee made no major mistakes, earned another high score, and safely advanced to the battle round.

Brock performed last. After seeing everyone else's appeals, he could not help feeling nervous. His partner was Bonsly, the Pokémon Yuma had admired.

Bonsly began with Fake Tears. Streams poured from its eyes like little fountains, making the audience laugh. Then the mood changed. Brock and Bonsly tossed and caught baseballs together, displaying the Pokémon's dexterity and cuteness.

Like May, Brock had chosen not to rely on flashy moves. His performance drew its appeal from his Pokémon's own character. The audience applauded warmly.

When all the scores appeared, Ethan looked up and saw that May, Brock, and Mollie had all advanced to the Contest Battle stage. Working with a new Pokémon, May placed third in the appeals. That was lower than her recent best, but still a solid result. Brock placed sixth and Mollie eighth—strong debuts for two newcomers.

The draw for the next round brought less welcome news for Brock and Mollie: they would face each other first. Only one of the new Coordinators could advance.

During the interval, Ash, Max, and Yuma went backstage to encourage May and Brock. Ethan stayed in his seat. As before, watching this Contest felt rather like watching a film whose ending he already knew. He would leave the preparations to the others.

The break passed quickly. Ash and the others returned to the stands, and the Contest Battles began. Brock and Mollie were first.

Brock sent out Marshtomp. When Mollie released her Pokémon, a startled murmur spread through the audience. She had an Absol.

The Dark-type Pokémon was a four-legged creature with something of a fox or a graceful large cat about it. White fur covered its body, red eyes looked out beneath a curved horn, and long fur fell across its face in a shape like a hooked jewel.

Its appearance was striking, but that was not the chief reason people reacted. Some might mistake it for a Mythical Pokémon on first sight, although Absol was simply uncommon rather than exceptionally rare. What made people wary was an old rumor.

Absol could sense changes in the sky and earth that warned of disasters. It came down from the mountains when danger approached. Over time, people came to call it the Disaster Pokémon. In more superstitious times, they had blamed it for the catastrophes it tried to warn them about and driven it away.

In truth, its ominous appearance concealed a gentle nature. It sometimes even protected fields without the farmers knowing. It had been misunderstood.

Still, for a beginning Coordinator to own an Absol was remarkable. Unfortunately for Mollie, her first Contest Battle was against Brock, a former Pewter Gym Leader.

He might lose his composure around a beautiful older woman, but the young girl opposite him saw an entirely different side. Brock would give her no mercy in battle.

Mollie had Absol attack immediately. Perhaps she had only recently caught it, for it had few strong moves at its disposal. It relied on Scratch, Razor Wind, and Quick Attack. Marshtomp had not reached its final evolution either and knew no overwhelming technique, but Brock's experience and judgment created a gap between the two teams.

Mud Shot was enough to shut down Absol's speed. Brock finished with Water Gun, and Absol fell. The Pokémon had exchanged fewer than three rounds of attacks before the result was decided.

Mollie left the stage with Absol, visibly disappointed. Facing an opponent that strong in her first Contest Battle could easily shake her confidence. Ethan sympathized, but Brock could not be expected to hold back. Giving his full effort was a responsibility to himself and his Pokémon. This was a competition, not a Gym challenge. Taking it easy on Mollie here would have been a mistake.

The audience applauded both of them. Other pairs battled, but Ethan's earlier judgment held: apart from May and Brock, nobody in this Gardenia Contest looked especially difficult. May's Munchlax performed well, and she and Brock met in the final.

Brock had kept his word. He was May's greatest obstacle to the Ribbon.

The final paired Munchlax against Marshtomp. Max looked from one end of the stage to the other, then spread his hands. “One side is my sister, and the other is Brock. I don't know who to cheer for.”

Ash had an easy answer. “Cheer for both of them!”

With the audience waiting and their friends watching, the final began. May attacked aggressively from the outset, sending Munchlax at Marshtomp again and again.

Brock knew how difficult she could be. He ordered Protect to block as many attacks as possible while he searched for an opening. He had been a Gym Leader, and he had traveled with May for a long time. He spotted a weakness quickly.

Between Munchlax's attacks, Marshtomp used Mud Shot. The spray coated Munchlax and turned a broad patch of ground beneath it slick. Munchlax lost its footing and fell hard.

Marshtomp slid over the mud to close in. Its unusual hands and feet made the wet ground an aid rather than an obstacle; it moved faster across it. Munchlax struggled upright, slipped again, and fell. Marshtomp drove its head into Munchlax.

But as Marshtomp tried to pull away, Munchlax grabbed hold of it.

Brock had not expected that. He had avoided Water Gun because it would wash away the mud. His plan had been to beat Munchlax while it could not stand steadily. Somehow it had seized a chance anyway. May and her Pokémon truly had grown.

There was no time to admire them. Brock tried to get Marshtomp free, but Munchlax clung on as though to a lifeline. Marshtomp pulled and Munchlax held tight until both of them slid off the muddy patch onto dry ground.

There Munchlax released it on its own. Brock felt no relief. His attack had failed, and at such close range Munchlax now had the advantage.

Ash turned toward Ethan. “Who do you think will win?”

“May,” Ethan said without hesitation.

“Why my sister?” Max asked.

Ethan laughed. “Isn't it obvious? It has nothing to do with their strength or how they're doing. May is my traveling companion. Of course I want her to win.”
