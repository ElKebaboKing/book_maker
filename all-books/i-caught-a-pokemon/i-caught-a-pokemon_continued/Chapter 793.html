Ethan, Misty, and May got out of the car and entered the Battle Arena with Scott.

Just beyond the gate, men and women in blue training clothes stood in two neat lines on an open-air practice ground, throwing punches again and again. A blonde woman led them from a raised platform. She wore a tight blue outfit beneath a black martial arts uniform.

She had to be Greta, the Arena's Frontier Brain. Ethan and the others did not want to interrupt the class, so they went around the side and waited quietly some distance behind her.

From closer up, Ethan studied her. She looked only seventeen or eighteen, with pleasant features and a long, toned build that spoke of years of exercise. Her blonde hair had straight bangs, with each side curling up like a fist. It was an unusual choice of hairstyle, though the longer he looked, the more it seemed to suit her.

Scott leaned close and said softly, “There she is—Miss Greta. Don't let her youth fool you. She's a rare talent in karate! I went to a great deal of trouble to persuade her to come here.”

He sounded proud to have recruited her as a Frontier Brain. Finding someone so capable had not been easy, nor had convincing her to accept the post. Building an Arena of this size had also demanded a great deal of money and effort from him.

Ethan did not yet know how strong Greta was, either at karate or at Pokémon battles. But there were at least thirty students training under her. So many people would hardly have sought her instruction if she had nothing to teach.

As Ethan's group looked around and waited for the morning practice to end, someone else entered through the gate. Ethan did not notice until Misty gave a startled exclamation.

“It's Bea!”

Bea? The first memory that came to Ethan was the night he had reached out in his sleep and touched the wrong person. He turned quickly. Bea had arrived at the practice ground with a backpack over one shoulder and a grave look on her face.

It really was her. What was she doing in Kanto, and why had she come here at the same time? Wherever Fighting-type Trainers gathered, it seemed Bea was bound to appear.

Perhaps her presence was simply too strong, or perhaps Greta recognized a fellow martial artist. Unlike Ethan's group, Bea caught Greta's attention immediately. Greta stopped her movements and watched the newcomer with a serious expression.

Her students noticed the change in her. They stopped practicing and followed her gaze. Some looked puzzled, others surprised or curious, and a few openly admired Bea. Most could not understand why their teacher seemed so wary of her.

Only the two women knew what they had sensed: each recognized the bearing of a skilled fighter in the other.

Bea ignored the many eyes on her and walked steadily up to Greta. She gave a formal karate bow. “Are you the Frontier Brain here? I've come to challenge you.”

Straight to the point, as ever. Greta took in Bea's clothes, her practiced bow, and the strength she carried herself with. She returned the bow.

“That's right. I'm Greta, the Frontier Brain of this Arena!” Folding her arms, she looked Bea up and down and answered in an energetic voice, “I accept your challenge!”

“Wait a moment!” Ethan called.

His voice drew everyone's attention. Only then did Greta, Bea, and the students realize another group was standing nearby.

“Who are you?” Greta asked in surprise. Then she spotted Scott beside them. “Mr. Scott? When did you get here?”

Bea was just as stunned to see Ethan. She had never imagined she would run into him here. The memory of that night rose in her mind at once, and her heart gave an involuntary jolt.

Scott laughed and led the group over. “I came for a reason, of course. Let me introduce you. This gentleman is no ordinary challenger. He's Ethan Vale, champion of the most recent Indigo Conference and Silver Conference, and now of this year's Ever Grande Conference too! He's here to challenge your Battle Arena.”

Scott traveled the world regularly, so it was no surprise that he knew about Ethan's three championships. Greta, however, was taken aback. She knew Ethan had won the Indigo Conference in Kanto, even if she knew less about what had happened in Johto and Hoenn. She had never expected a champion in three regions to come and challenge her.

Was he treating her as a diversion? On second thought, he was probably here to train his Pokémon.

Ethan nodded to Greta, then looked at Bea. “It's been a while, Bea.”

Nearly a month had passed since they parted. He had not expected to see her again so soon, let alone here. Misty stepped forward too. “It's good to see you again, Bea!”

Bea nodded to Misty, then met Ethan's eyes. Her feelings were more complicated than she could hide. After weeks of training, she had thought she had put that incident behind her and could face him calmly. Seeing him again stirred her emotions like a stone dropped into still water.

Scott and Greta watched the exchange in surprise. “You know each other?”

Of all the coincidences, both challengers had arrived together and turned out to be acquainted. At least that would spare everyone a dispute over who went first.

Greta waved to her students. “Raise the wheel!”

A section of the ground beside them split apart, and a huge wheel rose from beneath it. It had three colored sections, each marked with a different number of dots from one to three.

Bea and Ethan stared at it. Greta explained with a smile, “The rules here are simple. A one means a one-on-one elimination match, a two means two-on-two, and a three means three-on-three. Which of you wants to challenge me first?”

Ethan and Bea looked at each other. Bea hesitated, ready to let him take the first match, but Ethan gestured for her to go ahead.

“Ladies first.”

He had spoken before she could. Bea was not one to make a fuss over it. She nodded and stepped up to the wheel.

A long lever protruded from its edge. Bea pulled it hard, sending the wheel spinning. It slowed and finally stopped with its pointer on a yellow section marked by three black dots. She would fight Greta in three rounds.

“Good,” Greta said. “A three-on-three elimination match. The first to defeat all of the other Trainer's Pokémon wins!”

With the number of rounds decided, everyone moved to prepare for battle. Ethan was not up yet. He followed the students into the Battle Arena's main hall with Misty and May.

The students knelt around the edges. Ethan and the girls copied them, kneeling in the area reserved for guests. In most respects, this place resembled an ordinary Gym. Apart from the wheel, Ethan had seen little to explain what made a Battle Frontier facility different or why it was called part of a frontier. Perhaps he would understand later.

Greta and Bea took positions on opposite sides of the hall. An older student stepped forward to referee.

As the one accepting the challenge, Greta released her Pokémon first. She tossed three Poké Balls at once. Blue light flashed, and three Pokémon appeared outside the battle area: Hariyama, Medicham, and Hitmonchan.

All three were Fighting types, fitting Greta perfectly. It looked even more like a Gym now.

Bea released her Pokémon as well. Three more flashes revealed Grapploct, Hawlucha, and Pangoro, facing Greta's team across the hall. They too were Fighting types. This would be a clash between two teams built around the same specialty.

Bea's partners opened the eyes of the Kanto spectators. Pokémon from distant regions were still a novelty here. These three looked especially fierce, each with an intimidating expression that made the students worry for Greta.

Greta herself did not recognize them well. As a Frontier Brain, she had studied Pokémon from other regions, but reading about a species was different from facing it in battle. Few Pokémon from so far away reached Kanto. The unfamiliarity made her less certain of what to expect.

It was a limit of living in one region. Then again, Kanto Pokémon might seem just as unusual to Trainers elsewhere.

Although both teams had been revealed, this was no simultaneous three-on-three battle. Each Pokémon would enter in turn, and each bout would have its own winner. The reveal seemed designed to put both Trainers' options in plain sight. Ethan was not sure what purpose that served, but it was at least one difference from an ordinary Gym challenge.

Without further talk, the two women chose their first Pokémon. Greta sent Hariyama into the battle area. Bea chose Pangoro. Both were Fighting types that preferred strength over finesse. Their opening clash promised to be fierce.

Ethan had seen Bea's Pangoro fight before and knew how formidable it was. Scott had also told him that Hariyama was Greta's ace and the partner she used most often. This might be the decisive bout of the entire match.

At the referee's signal, the battle began. The two Pokémon charged with heavy strides and met in the center.

It was a contest of raw power. They traded crushing blows, neither willing to give ground. Even a heavy hit could not make either take a step back. There were no intricate exchanges of technique, but this had its own appeal. The force of it set the audience's blood racing.

The students clenched their fists. With every shift in the struggle, they cheered or shouted encouragement. Every collision held their attention.

No matter how fierce, the contest could not last forever. Greta was an excellent Trainer—Scott would never have recruited her otherwise—but she had met a formidable opponent.

Bea was an official Pokémon League Gym Leader. She traveled widely to train and challenge others, and her strength could not be dismissed. She was no match for an Elite Four member, perhaps, but she was more than capable of facing a Frontier Brain.

In the end, Pangoro's greater resolve and strength won out. It brought Hariyama down first and took the opening round.
