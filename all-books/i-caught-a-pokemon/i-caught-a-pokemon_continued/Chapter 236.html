Ethan bought two tickets. He and Sabrina passed through security and boarded the passenger ship.

They had arrived just in time. Murcott Island was neither a commercial center nor heavily populated, so few large ships stopped there each day. Ten or fifteen minutes later and they would have missed this one. The next ship on a similar route toward Kumquat Island might not pass through until the following evening.

Several bird Pokémon flew overhead toward the Bug Pokémon forest. Ethan could not tell whether they were going there to feed or to hunt the Pokémon among the trees.

He and Sabrina climbed the gangway to the deck. The vessel was not especially large, and it appeared to make short trips between the Orange Islands. Its fittings, though, were unexpectedly luxurious.

Crystal chandeliers hung overhead. Ornaments of all shapes lined the passageways, and a thick red carpet ran from the middle of the deck into the cabin. The place gleamed.

Ethan took his eyes off a finely made handle shaped like a Horsea. "The Orange Islands really do know how to cater to tourists. I didn't expect a ship we boarded on a whim to be this extravagant." He smiled at Sabrina. "Looks like choosing this over Charizard was a good idea."

Sabrina rolled her eyes. She had been the one to suggest a different way of traveling. He did not have to sound so delighted with himself.

They had booked a cabin with one large bed. After finding it and putting away their luggage, they went to the restaurant.

After several intimate encounters, neither needed to spell out what their relationship had become. They could share a room and sleep in the same bed without treating it as a question.

As Ethan looked around the ship, he had to admit that sailing was slower than flying on Charizard but far more relaxing. They could sit on comfortable sofas, eat, watch television, and enjoy the journey. There was no sea wind striking their faces and no fear of being caught in a downpour.

Still, he should enjoy this sort of comfort only now and then. Too much of it might wear down his resolve, and he would lose the chance to train along the way.

The lavish ship reminded him of the S.S. Anne. That was hardly a reassuring comparison. Better to put it out of his mind and hope this voyage ended safely.

When Ethan and Sabrina entered the restaurant, the handsome pair drew plenty of attention. To the other passengers, they looked well matched in both appearance and bearing, and more than a few stole glances at them.

They chose a table by the window. Ethan pulled out Sabrina's chair, then called a server and ordered a generous, fairly expensive lunch.

The main course was prime beef flown in from Paldea. Seared to perfection and served with local side dishes, it tasted every bit as good as its price suggested. On a distant stage, a pianist played soft music while they ate at an unhurried pace and enjoyed the elegant room.

When they had finished, Ethan leaned back and looked through the window. On the aft deck, a group of Trainers had organized an impromptu Pokémon battle tournament in a simple battle area. Their table gave them a clear view.

Watching the Trainers work up a sweat brought back memories.

"Time goes quickly," Ethan said. "When I first came from Kalos to Kanto, I was traveling alone with only Ralts. I used to battle on ships like this to get stronger. Looking back, those were good days. I still remember them clearly."

Ethan seldom spoke about his past, so Sabrina listened carefully. She imagined him crossing the sea alone to challenge himself in an unfamiliar place. It could not have been easy.

A cheer rose from the deck. They turned to see that a strong young Trainer had just defeated another opponent. He had won five battles in a row. That alone marked him as better than most of the people taking part.

Ethan studied him. He looked eighteen or nineteen and wore practical fighting clothes. His untidy hair fell past his ears, with a green ribbon tied around his head. He looked less like a Trainer than a fighter from a contest with no rules.

Sabrina glanced from the young man back to Ethan. She could see the interest in his eyes.

"Does he remind you of yourself?" she asked softly.

"A little," Ethan said. Then he grinned. "But he's nowhere near as handsome as I am."

"How vain." Sabrina rolled her eyes again, though she laughed. "Are you going to try your hand?"

"I think I will. A little exercise after lunch. Want to come?"

She answered by getting up to go with him.

They left the restaurant for the aft deck. By the time they arrived, the young Trainer had defeated another opponent.

"That's six wins in a row!"

"He's on another level. No one here can beat him!"

The spectators talked excitedly while the Trainer scanned the crowd with wide eyes, looking for his next challenger.

"Another one down! Well? Anybody else think they can take me on?"

No one answered. People looked at one another, but none stepped forward.

"How disappointing," he muttered.

He was beginning to think nobody would accept when a calm man's voice came from behind the crowd.

"If there's no one else, I'll give it a try."
