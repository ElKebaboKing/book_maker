Ash hurried to the altar and took out the last orb, which flashed with electricity inside. He fitted it into the remaining recess of the stone tablet.

The three orbs sat in a row. Everyone watched. Several seconds passed without anything unusual happening.

"Huh? Why isn't it working?" Ash looked at Melody. "Are they just decorations? Do they really have any power to stop the fighting?"

Melody remained calm. "Wait. You'll see."

As the others wondered what she meant, brilliant light burst from the three orbs. It grew brighter, then merged into a soft green glow. A curtain of light spread over the entire altar.

A strong wind blew outward from the tablet, clearing away the nearby snow. Flowers lay everywhere, filling the air with fragrance. The place seemed to have returned to how it had been before the disaster.

Glowing green liquid flowed from beneath the tablet. It filled a circular channel at its base and crept up the stone pillars beside the altar. Only now could everyone see the strange symbols carved densely into the paving stones below.

The color made them step back. Could something that green be poisonous?

Melody walked to the center of the altar and took her unusual flute from inside her clothes. She lifted it to her lips, closed her eyes, and moved her fingers over it. The first note sounded.

Wooo...

A graceful melody carried across the altar. When its sound reached the pillars, they began to pulse with light in time to the music. They seemed to amplify the tune and send it far across the islands.

Ethan felt its calming effect at once. The tension he had carried through the battle eased from his body.

The fighting Pokémon heard it too. Their interest in battling faded, and they came down to listen. Even the God of Thunder, unconscious on the water, opened its eyes and began to stir.

Wooo, wooo...

As Melody played, green liquid sprayed up like a fountain and flowed from the shrine into the sea. It spread quickly in every direction. Ice and snow melted wherever it reached, as visibly as grease clearing when detergent touched dishwater.

The music soothed the anger of the three island birds. They circled a few times, then turned together and flew back toward their respective islands. By now they were exhausted. Even without the flute, they could hardly have kept fighting much longer.

Lugia raised a huge waterspout from the sea. It swept away the last dark clouds, revealing the sun after what felt like a long absence.

For all the danger and drama, the crisis had ended rather abruptly. Ethan took out his phone and photographed the rare sight, as he often did.

Lugia descended above the altar and spoke into their minds.

To the two commanders, and to the one who brought peace: thank you for all you have done. Thank you for protecting this world.

The two commanders were Ethan and Ash. They had shared the work: Ash sought the treasures while Ethan held off the birds. Melody was the one who brought peace. Her festival tune, Lugia's Song, had finally ended the battle.

Melody lowered her flute. Relieved that the fighting had stopped, she clasped her hands before her and looked up at Lugia.

"We should be thanking you, God of the Sea. Thank you for protecting Shamouti Island for so long and keeping the three birds in balance."

The television crew had been stunned by the legendary Pokémon. Only now did they pay attention to the people at the altar. One of them recognized the handsome young man standing there: Ethan Vale, the new Indigo League Champion from Kanto.

What was he doing here? Was he involved in this crisis?

The crew swung their cameras toward him. The Champion, Lugia, the three sacred birds, an extraordinary battle, and extreme weather across the regions—put together, it would be a sensational story.

Before they could record Ethan, Professor Oak stepped in front of the cameras.

"I'm sorry, everyone," he said. "Please don't broadcast what happened here."

"What? Why not?" one of them asked.

Oak gave a small laugh. "The weather affected other regions, but several legendary Pokémon are involved. Publicizing this could cause panic. I think it would be best to let the matter pass without a report."

"I agree," Professor Ivy said. "I live in the Orange Islands, and I don't want outsiders disturbing these Pokémon. Let this end here."

The crew exchanged looks. As journalists, leaving a story this big unreported pained them. But Professor Oak carried enormous influence in Pokémon research, and Professor Ivy was an authority in this region. They could not lightly refuse either of them.

After a hard moment, they agreed. They could only imagine how high the ratings would have been if the footage aired.

Lugia had finished thanking the group. It rose into the sky once more.

Until we meet again.

It plunged into the sea and vanished, leaving the legendary Pokémon of the Orange Islands to live on in people's stories.

Ethan watched the calm water. He would have liked to catch Lugia, but this was plainly not the time. They had just fought side by side, and he could not bring himself to act against it now.

Never mind. I'll probably meet another Lugia someday.

"Ethan," Misty said, remembering that he had never met Professor Ivy. "Let me introduce you. This is Professor Ivy. Like Professor Oak, she's a well-known Pokémon researcher."
