The wind blew almost all night.

Ethan slept badly. Again and again, the howling outside his window startled him awake.

Misty had fared no better. When they met in the dining room the next morning, each could see dark circles beneath the other's eyes.

“Ahh...” Misty yawned widely and rubbed her eyes. “The wind was so loud last night. I kept worrying it would break my window!”

“Same here.” Ethan shook his head. “Even headphones didn't help.”

There was good news, at least. Despite the night of high winds, the typhoon hadn't reached Dewford Island as expected. Endless dark clouds still hung in the distance, but its center appeared to have shifted from the island's eastern side toward the west. It remained a threat, though for the moment they seemed safe.

“Let's eat quickly,” Ethan said. “While we can, we should get to the Dewford Gym. If the storm changes course again, we won't be able to go out at all.”

Misty knew they had little time and didn't argue. They ate in a hurry, fed their Pokémon, and set out lightly, leaving their backpacks behind at the Pokémon Center.

The town looked nothing like it had the day before. It was only a little after seven, but the streets were empty. Not a single pedestrian was in sight.

A broadcast from somewhere nearby kept warning residents to shelter at home and stay off the streets in case the typhoon made landfall. Every door was shut. Through some windows, they could even see tape placed across the glass as a precaution.

Ethan and Misty exchanged a look. The urgency was clear. They picked up their pace and hurried toward the Gym.

Whoosh!

Wind struck them. Perhaps it was their imagination, but it seemed stronger than when they had left the Pokémon Center.

They looked up and saw they hadn't imagined it. The distant cloud bank had turned again and was moving toward Dewford Town.

Just what they had feared! They had only just left town. The remaining distance to the Gym was about the same as the distance back to the Pokémon Center.

Ethan couldn't bring himself to turn around now. They had already come this far; going back would make the trip a waste. Besides, what was a typhoon and heavy rain to a Trainer? If he couldn't endure a little hardship, he might as well give up and become a Coordinator! Every ordeal was another chance to grow stronger.

Resolved to challenge the Gym, he pressed on into the wind. He didn't even consider asking Dragonite to fly them there.

That made the journey harder for Misty. Her slim build left her at the mercy of the gusts, and several times she nearly fell. Ethan caught her each time. Before long, they were walking arm in arm, supporting one another.

Whoosh, whoosh, whoosh...

They left town and started up the slope toward the cliff where the Gym stood. The higher they climbed, the harder the wind seemed to blow.

“Ethan...” Misty opened her mouth and caught a lungful of air. She covered it with her hand before trying again. “You have Suicune, don't you? Couldn't it stop this typhoon?”

“Probably.” Ethan lowered his head against the wind. “But don't count on it. The storm formed naturally. We shouldn't interfere with it too much, or we'd upset the ecological balance.”

“How would stopping it upset the ecological balance?” Misty asked.

“A typhoon's formation and path are complicated. There's more to them than we can see. It often brings heavy rain. That rain may cause some damage on the coast, but it also supplies water farther inland and nourishes the land. If I stopped this storm now, it could set off a chain of effects in those inland ecosystems.”

He paused, then added, “Besides, the damage here should be small. Coastal towns like Dewford have drainage systems. Rainwater that pours into town will soon flow back out to sea.”

At ten years old, Misty had never thought about storms in those terms. Once she understood his reasoning, she stopped suggesting it. Ethan really did know a lot. Someone more simple-minded might have used a powerful Pokémon just to show off, without considering any of that.

Walking against the wind wasn't easy, but the Gym wasn't far. After several dozen minutes, they reached it.

Just beyond the unusual sign they had seen the day before, they spotted someone standing at the Gym doors. It was Bea!

Like them, she must have hoped to challenge Brawly before the typhoon arrived. They had all had the same idea. But why was she standing outside?

Bea heard their footsteps and turned. Ethan approached her, looked at the doors, and understood. They were shut tight, with a large padlock hanging from them. The Gym hadn't opened. Was no one inside?

He checked his phone. It was already 8:47. By normal business hours, the Gym should have been open.

“Oh, come on...” Misty sighed. “I wanted to get inside and out of this wind. If it keeps blowing like this, my skin will crack!”

Ethan signaled for her to be patient, then asked Bea, “How long have you been here?”

Bea looked startled that he had spoken to her. They had met before, though. After a moment's hesitation, she answered, “Half an hour.”

“Half an hour?” Misty stared. “You've been standing here at the door the whole time?”

Bea nodded faintly.

“My goodness, you're really stub— I mean, you're very patient.” Misty caught herself just in time. Calling Bea stubborn would have been rude.

Bea didn't seem to mind. She said nothing more and appeared willing to keep waiting.

Ethan shook his head, then walked around the Gym a few times. He remembered its general layout from yesterday and wanted to see if there was a back entrance or contact information posted somewhere. He found neither.

When he returned to the front, Misty hesitated. “Ethan... do you think the people who work here might not come in today?”

Both Ethan and Bea looked at her.

She cleared her throat. “When we left the Pokémon Center, the town was broadcasting a warning for everyone to stay home. Maybe Brawly listened and decided not to come to the Gym.”

Ethan frowned. The possibility hadn't occurred to him. Even a careful person could miss something, and he had never claimed to be exceptionally wise.

Before setting out, he had thought only about himself. As a traveling Trainer, he wasn't going to let a typhoon stop him from reaching the Gym, however hard it rained.

But running the Gym was only one part of Brawly's life. He wasn't a traveler trying to toughen himself through every hardship. Staying home during extreme weather would be perfectly reasonable.

Brawly loved surfing and the sea, but he had no reason to brave a typhoon merely to get to work soaking wet.

“Maybe I'm worrying over nothing,” Misty added, seeing Ethan's thoughtful expression. “Don't mind me.”

He knew she meant to comfort him. Still, she was probably right. All he could do was hope Brawly was committed enough to open the Gym in this weather.

On second thought, Brawly had arranged for an acting Leader so he could go surfing. Expecting him to turn up during a typhoon seemed unlikely.

“You have a point.” Ethan nodded to Misty. “Let's wait until nine. If the doors are still locked, they're probably closed for the day. We'll go back to the Pokémon Center.”

Bea listened silently. She offered no opinion, but she stayed where she was. Apparently she too had decided to wait until nine.

Ethan and Misty found a level stone step sheltered from the wind and sat down. Bea remained standing. Gusts sent her silver-gray hair flying, and the black, rabbit-eared headband above it swayed back and forth.

“Would you like to sit with us?” Misty asked her.

Bea glanced over and gently shook her head. Standing in the wind was training for her endurance. In any case, it was only a dozen or so minutes until nine. She had already stood there for half an hour without moving.

In that short time, the typhoon drew closer. The entire palm grove outside the Gym began to sway violently.

Ethan checked the time. A few minutes still remained, but he decided not to wait.

“Let's go back.”

“Huh?” Now that he wanted to leave, Misty hesitated. “Are you sure? There's still a little time. Shouldn't we wait?”

“If Brawly were coming, he'd already be here. A few more minutes won't change it. He isn't opening today. We'll have to challenge him after the typhoon passes.”

“All right...” Misty stood and started downhill beside him.

As they passed Bea, Ethan called, “Don't keep waiting. Come down with us. The storm's almost here, and if you stay much longer, you could be trapped on the cliff.”

Bea looked toward the horizon. The typhoon had reached the waters just off Dewford Island. Landfall now seemed inevitable; the air already smelled heavy with moisture.

Waiting would change nothing. She nodded and followed Ethan and Misty down the hill.

The three moved briskly along the path, Ethan and Misty ahead of Bea. They deliberately slowed a little so she wouldn't fall behind, but after a few hundred meters, they realized they had misjudged her.

Her toned muscles showed how often she trained. She was fitter than either of them and had a faster stride. She caught up in moments.

Once they reached the foot of the hill, the town would be another ten or fifteen minutes away. They had underestimated the typhoon's speed.

Rain began without warning. A few small drops pattered against them, then within seconds the shower turned into a downpour.

They had traveled light. Neither Ethan nor Misty had brought a bag, and Bea carried only a small backpack. None of them had a raincoat or even an umbrella.

Their clothes soaked through instantly. Water poured over their heads and streamed down their faces as though someone had turned a faucet on them.

It was too late to race all the way into town. Going back to the Gym and breaking a window for shelter would take too long as well. The rain was so hard that speaking was difficult, visibility was poor, and they could scarcely keep their eyes open.

Fortunately, Ethan remembered a cave nearby. He had noticed it on the way up, and it was close enough to offer temporary shelter.

He waved to Misty and Bea, left the path, and ran toward the rocks. Misty knew the cave too and understood at once; she hurried after him. Bea didn't know why they had changed course, but guessed they had found somewhere to escape the rain. She followed without hesitation.
