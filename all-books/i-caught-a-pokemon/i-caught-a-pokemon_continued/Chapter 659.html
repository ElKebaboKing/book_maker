Hitmontop fell.

Combusken had defeated its opponent, but it clearly could not continue either. Ethan had won the first round, though both sides had paid for it. For Brawly in particular, that was a result he could live with.

And yet...

Brawly's brow twitched. He had a growing feeling that he might fail to live up to Shauna's expectations today.

Then Ethan sighed and said apologetically, “Sorry. I would have liked to battle you with Fighting-type Pokémon, but Combusken can't go on, and it's the only one I have. Please let me use a Pokémon of another type for the next round.”

Another type? Brawly felt a flicker of hope.

He had to admit that Combusken was somewhat stronger than his Hitmontop. Losing was disappointing, but there was nothing he could do about it now. If the next Pokémon wasn't one with a type advantage against Fighting-types, though, Brawly still thought he had a good chance of winning.

He nodded. “Of course. This is a Fighting-type Gym, but challengers aren't required to use Fighting-type Pokémon. Choose whichever one you like.”

Ethan smiled. After challenging so many Gyms, he knew the rules perfectly well. He had brought it up in advance so no one could complain that he had fought unfairly if Brawly lost.

Brawly recalled Hitmontop and took out another Poké Ball. This would be his fourth Pokémon to battle today. If he kept losing, he might brood over it for a long time.

But a Gym Leader had to be ready for that. He would either defeat a challenger or be defeated. He could blame no one else; such matches were part of his life.

He threw the ball, revealing his fourth choice: a Machamp.

Ethan immediately remembered the unusual sign outside the Gym. The Machamp carved into it must have been this very one. He hadn't expected to meet the model in person.

Off the court, the girls from the Gym began to talk at once.

“Oh! I didn't think Brawly would send Machamp out!”

“Neither did I. I haven't seen it battle in ages, not since Hariyama became his ace!”

“With Machamp out there, he'll definitely win this one!”

Ethan understood. Before Hariyama had taken its place, Machamp must have been Brawly's strongest Pokémon. Hariyama's rise had gradually pushed it into a supporting role. To beat Ethan, Brawly had brought out even the Pokémon he usually kept in reserve.

Did he think that would be enough?

Ethan recalled Combusken and ran his fingers over the Poké Balls in his waist pouch. He considered how to face a muscular Pokémon with four arms.

If he chose a Pokémon with a type advantage, the girls would probably dismiss his victory as a matter of favorable typing. He wanted to win so decisively that they would have nothing to say.

He ruled out several Pokémon and took out Tyranitar's ball. Fighting-type attacks were four times as effective against Tyranitar. If he beat Brawly with that disadvantage, surely no one could object.

“Go, Tyranitar!”

The light from the thrown ball faded, and Tyranitar's enormous body appeared on the court. It gave Machamp a thunderous roar.

Brawly narrowed his eyes. Tyranitar had an imposing presence, fierce and domineering. It looked like a formidable Pokémon. But why had Ethan sent it out? Surely he knew Machamp had a type advantage. Was he underestimating Brawly, or did he truly have no better choice?

Whatever the reason, Brawly's chances had improved considerably.

The Gym girls brightened too. Machamp's Fighting-type attacks would be four times as effective. How could it lose?

In the stands, Bea frowned at the sight of Tyranitar. She couldn't make sense of Ethan's choice either. But his calm expression suggested he had selected it deliberately. Perhaps he wanted to train it under difficult conditions.

Only Misty, who knew what Tyranitar could do, had a good idea of Ethan's intention. He wanted this match to silence everyone who might question his victory. She sat up straight, eager to watch.

As for the possibility that Ethan might lose? What a joke.

After a brief pause, the referee gave the signal, and Ethan's second battle began.

“Tyranitar, Stone Edge!” Ethan ordered at once.

Sharp rocks thrust up from the floor toward Machamp. They would be difficult for it to dodge, but Machamp had another answer. Just as a pointed stone rose beneath it, Machamp extended all four arms and stopped the rock with sheer strength.

That's impressive. Ethan nodded to himself. Brawly had trained this Machamp well if it could catch an attack like that.

But how many times could it do so?

“Tyranitar, again!”

As long as Machamp couldn't close the distance, Ethan thought, all that strength would be of little use. Tyranitar gathered energy and unleashed a second Stone Edge.

Machamp did not wait for the next rock to reach it. With all four arms, it lifted the stone it had just stopped and hurled it at Tyranitar without even taking time to aim.

The rock traced an arc through the air. Ethan understood why Machamp had seemed so unhurried about approaching. It had a way to attack from a distance after all.

Its throw was remarkably accurate too. Though it had barely looked, the stone flew straight at Tyranitar. Machamp must have practiced this countless times. Still, a rock like that would do little to Tyranitar.

As it arrived, Tyranitar lowered its head and struck it with the armor-hard spikes at the back of its skull. The stone shattered. Fragments streamed down through the gaps in Tyranitar's armor, leaving its head unmarked.

Meanwhile, the second Stone Edge reached Machamp. Its arms were still extended from the throw, and its balance had not yet recovered. It could not brace against the new rock as it had before. It leaped to the side and barely avoided it.

That first exchange gave both Trainers a clearer sense of their opponent's strength. Simple attacks would not decide this battle.

At Brawly's command, Machamp charged toward Tyranitar. Ethan had no intention of letting it approach unopposed. At a sweep of his hand, Tyranitar summoned a Sandstorm and hid itself in swirling yellow sand.

The sand spread across the court, despite the Gym being indoors on an island.

Brawly did not hesitate. He ordered Machamp straight into the storm. What could a mere Rock-type move do to it? Poor visibility made surprise attacks a danger, but close combat was Machamp's specialty. He had no reason to fear a fight at that range.

Machamp strode into the sand and vanished. Soon roars from Machamp and Tyranitar sounded one after the other. They had found each other and begun to fight.

Yet something about the sounds unsettled Brawly and the Gym girls. Tyranitar's roars remained as fierce as ever, while Machamp's cries grew lower and increasingly pained.

Brawly's heart lurched. Was Machamp struggling? That made no sense. It held the type advantage. If either Pokémon was in trouble, it should be Tyranitar.

The storm hid the battle from everyone outside. Another troubling thought struck Brawly: perhaps the gap between the two Pokémon was so great that Tyranitar's disadvantage no longer mattered.

But that did not fit what he had seen. Ethan's Combusken had defeated Hitmontop, one of Brawly's stronger Pokémon, but had been injured badly enough that it could not fight again. His Pokémon did not seem overwhelmingly powerful. Tyranitar looked strong, certainly, but how much stronger could it be?

Brawly was so desperate to know what was happening that he wanted to rush into the Sandstorm himself. Of course, he couldn't. The wind and dense sand swallowed the light and sounds of the moves within it. The spectators could only guess at the fight from the rising and falling roars.

Time passed under that tense uncertainty. The storm gradually thinned, and everyone peered into it.

Through the hazy sand, they made out two shapes still fighting. As they had expected, it was a close-quarters battle. A wall of rocks surrounded Machamp, restricting its movement. Broken stones lay scattered nearby, crushed during the struggle.

Both Pokémon bore wounds, but Machamp was covered in them. Tyranitar had suffered far less. The fight within the storm had plainly been fierce, and Machamp had taken the worst of it.

Brawly and the Gym girls frowned. Machamp was losing? How was that possible?

Bea was just as astonished. Anyone who knew her well knew that her strongest Pokémon, the one that best represented her, was a Machamp, not Pangoro. She understood the species well enough to grasp how startling this result was.

Ethan watched their reactions with his hands in his pockets. From a distance, Machamp looked built for close combat: muscles everywhere, four powerful arms, and every apparent advantage in a grapple. But it had a weakness. Compared with its upper body, its legs were less formidable.

Tyranitar had exploited that weakness in the Sandstorm. It used Rock Slide to raise obstacles around Machamp and restrict its movements. With visibility so poor, Machamp had found itself trapped among the rocks.

The greater reason for the result, however, was Tyranitar's own strength. Machamp excelled at close combat, but Ethan had never meant to match it technique for technique. Machamp had its advantages, and Tyranitar had its own.

Tyranitar could not fight with four humanlike arms, but it had sharp claws and fangs. It could bite and tear like a wild beast. The Sandstorm gave it cover to unleash its savage strength.

Low Sweep, Karate Chop, Vital Throw—whatever Machamp tried, it still had to withstand Tyranitar's collisions and Thrash.

Ethan had never intended to defeat Machamp with Dark-type or Rock-type attacks. His real answer was the powerful Normal-type moves Tyranitar's tremendous strength made possible.
