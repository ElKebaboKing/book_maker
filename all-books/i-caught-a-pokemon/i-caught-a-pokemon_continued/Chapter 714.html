“I see...”

Phoebe did not blame Ethan. One look at him told her he had done everything he could. If there was anyone to blame, it was herself. She had acted rashly, fallen for Maxie's trick, and set all the trouble that followed in motion.

She stood and bowed slightly to him. “Thank you for everything you've done. Since things have turned out this way, there's no reason for me to stay. I'll be going.”

Her bow did not come from a member of the Elite Four. It came from a granddaughter and a friend, genuinely grateful for his help.

Ethan thought more highly of her for it. His opinion of her improved again. It was no wonder she had become one of the Elite Four at her age. Apart from her occasional impulsiveness, he could find little fault with her character.

“You're leaving?” Misty had just returned with a tray and heard Phoebe's words. “Now? It's late. Won't you stay the night?”

Phoebe smiled as if the darkness outside did not concern her. “It's all right. I'm used to traveling at night, and Ghost-type Pokémon like the nighttime best.”

“All right.” Misty could tell she had made up her mind and did not press her. “Where will you go?”

She had known the girl her own age only a short time. But they had fought together, extinguished the fire on Mt. Pyre, and traveled to Lilycove side by side. They were hardly close friends, yet they could speak to each other as friends.

“The orbs were stolen,” Phoebe said without hesitation, “but I still have to take my grandparents back to the mountain and help rebuild their home. They've lived there all their lives. Whether or not they need to guard the orbs, it's their home.”

Misty set Ethan's tray before him and hugged Phoebe.

“I hope we meet again someday.”

“So do I.”

Ash and the others could only watch awkwardly. They barely knew Phoebe and had hardly spoken to her. After the two girls embraced, they joined Ethan and Misty in seeing her out.

When they returned to the dining area, the absence of a stranger made the atmosphere noticeably more relaxed.

Ash could not wait to tell Ethan what had happened on their travels. Ethan ate, nodding from time to time.

They had indeed come to Lilycove for May's Pokémon Contest, as Ash had said. It had ended that afternoon. May had won the Lilycove Contest and earned her third Ribbon.

“Your third Ribbon already?” Ethan stared at May in surprise. She was improving faster than he had expected.

May smiled shyly. “Yes. After losing my first Contest in Slateport, I entered the Fallarbor, Verdanturf, Rubello, and Lilycove Contests. I was eliminated at Rubello, but I won the other three and got their Ribbons.”

“Congratulations. You're getting closer to becoming a top Pokémon Coordinator. Two more Ribbons and you can enter the Hoenn Grand Festival! It's a shame I arrived too late. I would have liked to see your performance at the Lilycove Contest.”

May blushed and waved a hand. “I'm working toward it! But don't worry about missing this one. There will be other chances.”

She was a little embarrassed to hear a boy say that in front of everyone. And knowing what Ethan had accomplished, she felt recognized by his praise.

“Me too! Ask me!” Ash broke in, impatient now that May had finished her news. “I've got six Badges already!”

“Six?” Ethan raised his eyebrows. It was a little surprising, but it made sense. “That's as many as I have. Where are you headed next?”

Ash's journey did not involve taking on whole organizations as often as Ethan's, but it had plenty of surprises of its own. Somehow, Ash could find the right path even after arriving in the wrong place. He was earning Badges quickly.

“I'm going to Mossdeep City to challenge the Mossdeep Gym,” Ash said. “What about you, Ethan?”

Ethan shook his head. “There's something important I need to do first. Besides, my next challenge is the Fortree Gym. Once I've finished there, I can head to Mossdeep.”

“We still can't travel together?” Ash looked disappointed. “That's too bad. I thought that if we went to Mossdeep together, we could stop by LaRousse City on the way.”

LaRousse City?

The name was unfamiliar to Ethan. “What sort of place is that? Is there something special about it?”

Ash blinked. “You haven't heard of it? It's...”

He had barely begun to explain when Max interrupted him.

“LaRousse City is famous!” Max said, puffing out his chest. “They call it the city of technology. It has moving walkways, block-shaped robots, a huge park, and busy shopping districts. Best of all, there's a Battle Tower where Trainers can battle!”

Ash stood with his mouth open, awkwardly deprived of his explanation.

Max glanced at him and delivered one more blow. “And Ash only heard about it this afternoon. The moment he found out about the Battle Tower, he wouldn't stop saying he wanted to see it.”

With Max around, Ash could not keep even that small secret, much less show off his knowledge.

“Come on,” Ash complained. “Couldn't you let me impress Ethan for once?”

Max pushed up his glasses, unmoved. “I was just stating the facts.”

Their familiar bickering made everyone smile.

Amid the laughter, Ethan swallowed his food and thought about the city. Its name meant nothing to him, but the block-shaped robots and moving walkways suddenly rang a bell.

Wasn't that the city from the Deoxys movie? And speaking of Deoxys...

He had recently promised his own Deoxys that it could meet others of its kind during their travels. This might be his chance.

“Is the city far from here?” he asked.

Ash and Max looked startled by the sudden question. Brock took out a map, checked the location, and shook his head. “Not very far. It's close to Lilycove, at the fork in the river on the peninsula to the southeast. Do you want to go?”

Misty looked at Ethan with bright eyes. Max's description had interested her, and if they went with Ash's group, she could spend more time with May. May seemed to have the same thought; she watched Ethan hopefully.

He considered it for a while before answering. “I can't decide yet. Let me make a call first.”

A call? Ash and the others looked at one another. Did Ethan need someone else's permission to choose where he went? Whose?

Ethan rose, moved to a quiet corner, and called Zinnia. He did not need anyone's permission, of course. His plan had been to find her and ask her to take him to Sky Pillar.

But Ash and the others had given him another idea. He still intended to visit Sky Pillar. Could he make a trip to LaRousse City first?

Deoxys had traveled through space alone for so long that it longed for others of its kind. Perhaps his Deoxys could persuade the two Deoxys in LaRousse to help in the coming battle. Even if they were hard to approach or unwilling to help, letting his companion meet them would fulfill one of its wishes.

From that point of view, even a fruitless trip would be worthwhile.

To save time, however, Zinnia would have to come to him. He had no idea whether a phone call could persuade her to cross half of Hoenn. It was worth a try.

The phone rang for a while before she answered. Her voice was low. She did not sound sleepy, only dejected. Rayquaza's escape had clearly shaken her, and she still had not recovered.

Ethan came straight to the point and told her what he wanted. As he might have expected, she immediately refused when she heard he wanted to visit Sky Pillar.

“I can't help you with that,” Zinnia said firmly. “The Draconid people have guarded Sky Pillar for generations. Only we and a few others who have been granted permission may enter. An outsider like you cannot go in.”

Ethan told her about Groudon and Kyogre. She still refused. “I'm sorry. I really can't take you there.”

How stubborn could she be? Even an appeal to Hoenn's safety would not move her. Ethan thought for a moment and decided to provoke her.

“Even if you won't tell me, I know where Sky Pillar is.”

“You know where it is?” Zinnia laughed at the other end of the call.

She did not believe him. Few people had even heard the name Sky Pillar. Apart from the Draconid people and a handful of others, most knew nothing about it.

People with a particular interest in Groudon and Kyogre, such as Team Magma and Team Aqua, might find the name in old documents and learn that the tower was more than a legend. That was about as far as their knowledge went.

Ethan knowing the name did not surprise her. Claiming to know the exact location was nonsense. He must be trying to trick her into revealing it. Yes, that had to be it.

Then his next words made her eyes widen.

“Sky Pillar is hidden on an uninhabited island near Sootopolis City.”

Zinnia's heart pounded. She forced herself to stay calm. “You've guessed wrong. It isn't there.”

Ethan chuckled. “It isn't? You don't have to hide it. I've actually been there before.”

That part was true. Ethan had visited Sky Pillar—only in a game.

It was in the waters near the island that held Sootopolis City, just as he had said. In the game, he could follow a fixed route to the tower. This was the real world. Finding one empty island among the hundreds or thousands nearby would be far harder.

“How did you...?” Zinnia's voice rose before she caught herself. “Hmph. Even if you've been there, it won't help. A barrier protects Sky Pillar. You couldn't possibly...”

“You mean the barrier the people of Sootopolis established and maintain?” Ethan interrupted.

Damn it! He even knew about that? How was that possible? Had someone from Sootopolis told him?

The thought came to Zinnia at once. “Who told you?” she demanded.

Her sudden change in tone told Ethan his provocation had worked. He laughed softly. “I already told you. I've been there. I found all this out myself.”
