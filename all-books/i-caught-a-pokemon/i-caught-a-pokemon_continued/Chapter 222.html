After lunch, Ash and his friends left Shamouti Island in Maren's repaired speedboat, reluctant to go.

Ash's mother, Delia, stayed behind, to no one's surprise. With her son always traveling, she lived alone and must have felt lonely at times. She planned to return to Pallet Town with Professor Oak when he finished his research here.

Ethan did not want to view their friendship through a narrow lens, but it was hard not to wonder whether there was something more between them.

He had no time to dwell on that gossip. He had concerns of his own.

Once he and Sabrina had packed, they left their luggage in their rooms rather than depart at once. They headed for the altar on the other side of Shamouti Island. A rare Pokémon still waited there for Sabrina to catch.

They walked through dense forest. Ethan looked at the trees around them. The freezing caused by the God of Thunder had damaged them to varying degrees. Their leaves and branches looked wilted, but the trees had survived and were still full of life.

It was quiet now. With no one else around, Ethan and Sabrina finally had a chance to be alone.

He had wanted to say something on the way. Yet Sabrina treated him exactly as she always had, and he found that puzzling. After what had happened between them, shouldn't she want to talk about it? She seemed almost unmoved. She was the one who had the most at stake.

Ethan could not make sense of it. In the end, he kept quiet. Sabrina was hard to read. She must have her own thoughts about last night; he would wait for her to raise the subject.

They crossed the forest and climbed over a broken cliff before reaching the altar. The talking Slowking was there, standing with its hands behind its back and gazing out to sea like an old man at peace with the world.

Hearing them approach, it turned. "Ah. It's you."

It seemed a little surprised to see them again. "The end of the world has been averted. The God of the Sea has gone back beneath the waves, and this year's festival is over. You have no reason to come to the altar anymore."

"You're wrong about that," Ethan said. "We have another reason."

He gave Slowking a pitying look, shook his head, and stepped back to let Sabrina forward. This was her capture to make. He did not need to take part; the altar was her stage.

Slowking had no idea what awaited it. It watched Sabrina approach, looking confused. Only when she took out a Poké Ball did it sigh.

"Oh, dear. I don't want to fight you, and I don't want to leave this island. Could you please go away and pretend you never saw me?"

If Ethan had been the one trying to catch it, he would have respected that wish and given up. Unfortunately for Slowking, it was facing Sabrina: the coldhearted girl who was all too willing to use her psychic power against someone weaker.

She regarded it coolly. Its wishes did not change her mind. She wanted this talking Pokémon, and she sent out her own.

A flash of blue light revealed Kadabra on the altar.

Slowking tried to reason with Sabrina again. She paid no attention. Through telepathy, she ordered Kadabra to attack.

Kadabra raised its spoon and sent a burst of Confusion straight at Slowking.

Slowking might look vacant, but unlike its unevolved form, it was remarkably intelligent. It had been watching Kadabra closely. It thrust out a hand and answered with psychic power of its own.

Bang!

The two attacks met in midair and canceled each other out. Neither Pokémon gained ground.

Ethan looked around, found a flat rock, and sat down to watch. He even had time to let several of his Pokémon out to watch with him.

Kadabra was a pure Psychic type. Slowking was Psychic type too, but it was also a Water type, which gave it another option in battle. Kadabra had Sabrina on its side, though. Her commands made better use of its moves, and she could watch for openings it might miss.

The two Pokémon traded attacks for a while. Ethan turned to Gardevoir with a smile. "You're not the only one getting stronger. Sabrina's Kadabra has grown a lot too. Do you remember fighting it when we challenged Saffron Gym? It feels like yesterday."

Gardevoir looked at Kadabra and nodded slightly. As another Psychic-type Pokémon, it could feel the two strong powers clashing.

A Water Gun shot past Ethan, drawing his attention back to the fight. He had no idea how old this Slowking was, but its moves were powerful. It did not seem used to battling, though. It was more of a thinker than a fighter and apparently got little exercise; after such a short fight, it was already panting.

Its intelligence was another distraction. Slowking kept glancing at Ethan's Pokémon, especially Haunter, which wore a mischievous grin. It worried they might rush into the fight at any moment.

Sabrina heard Ethan's praise of Kadabra and glanced at him without showing what she felt. In truth, she minded that Kadabra had not defeated Slowking quickly in front of him. Everyone wanted to look their best before someone they liked, even a girl with psychic powers.

She told Kadabra to stop, then walked toward Slowking herself.

Slowking watched in confusion. Did she mean to end the fight?

Instead, Sabrina raised a hand toward it. A frightening surge of psychic power enveloped Slowking and knocked it down before it could react.

For all its intelligence, Slowking had never faced anything like this. It had not imagined that a human could wield psychic power, much less that this woman would be stronger than Kadabra.

Ethan was stunned too. Sabrina had entered the battle herself. Had watching Kadabra made her eager to fight? Or did she think a Pokémon she caught with her own hands would obey her more readily?

He scratched his head, unable to understand it.

Slowking struggled to its feet. Before it could fight back, a second wave of psychic power struck it, then a third. It went down again.

Pain ran through its body, and it mourned its freedom. It had spent much of its strength fighting Kadabra. It could not keep battling this terrifying woman.

When Sabrina saw that Slowking had stopped resisting, she promptly took out a Poké Ball and threw it at the Pokémon.
