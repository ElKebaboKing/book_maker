By eight the next morning, Pummelo Island had lost its usual leisurely pace. People appeared from every direction and streamed toward Pummelo Stadium.

There were few local vehicles on the small island, so the roads did not seize up completely. The sidewalks were another matter. Crowds filled both sides of the streets as far as anyone could see.

Pummelo Island did not have nearly enough residents to account for them all. Many had heard about the Winner's Cup and taken boats from neighboring islands overnight. Those who lived nearby had arrived in two or three hours and found rooms where they could sleep. Others had spent the night rocked by waves and chilled by sea wind. They came ashore without even time for a hot breakfast and hurried toward the stadium, anxious to secure a seat.

The difficult journey had done nothing to dampen their spirits. Families and friends chatted excitedly about the coming match. A few visitors from other regions thought Ethan had a good chance, but most Orange Islanders supported Drake. They wanted their own Supreme Gym Leader to win, strengthen his reputation, and retain the Winner's Cup.

At 8:40, Ethan and Sabrina reached the stadium's side entrance. A hat, sunglasses, and a mask hid most of Ethan's face. Carl waited outside with a younger man in a suit. Several reporters from around the Orange Islands stood nearby with microphones and cameras. Security guards kept an eager crowd behind a cordon.

From across the street, Ethan took it all in. "Quite a reception," he said. Then he joked to Sabrina, "It looks like they're waiting for a movie star. Too bad I'm arriving on foot instead of in a car. I hardly fit the occasion."

"Getting nervous?" Sabrina asked with a smile.

"Of course not." Ethan started across the road. "They promoted the match so heavily yesterday, and now here I am, looking completely ordinary. I can imagine their supporters thinking, 'Is that all? He doesn't look like anyone special.'"

Sabrina gave him an odd look. "Why are you brooding over what they think? It isn't like you. Besides, getting an opponent to underestimate you is usually just what you want."

"True. Let them think I'm harmless until I show my strength." Ethan sighed. "But I thought about it last night. Drake represents the Orange League, and I carry the title of Indigo League champion. I may have come here on my own, but if I win or lose, it will affect the Indigo League's reputation too."

"Don't put so much pressure on yourself." Sabrina patted his back, offering comfort she rarely showed so openly. "Forget the spectators. Give it everything you have, and you'll have nothing to regret."

Ethan looked at her in surprise. He had not expected Sabrina to be the one reassuring him. She had changed so much since they met.

When they reached Carl, he barely glanced at the disguised Ethan. Only when he noticed Sabrina did he look again. "Mr. Vale? Is that you? Why are you dressed like that?"

Ethan glanced toward the reporters. "I didn't want to be recognized on the way here and get held up before the match. It's only a little disguise."

At his name, the reporters surged forward.

"Mr. Vale! I'm from Orange Islands TV. Could I ask you a few questions?"

"Mr. Vale! Metropolitan TV here. What made you cross the sea to challenge the Orange League?"

"Did you come because you recognize the Orange League's strength and chose it as your next goal?"

The questions tumbled over one another. Microphones nearly touched Ethan's face. He did not answer. He simply stepped beside Carl.

Carl felt a headache coming on. He had arranged for the reporters to be there, hoping their questions would generate excitement and publicity. He had assumed Ethan would welcome the attention. It was a chance to become more famous. He had misjudged him.

With Ethan refusing the interview, Carl caught the eye of the young man beside him and signaled for him to hold the reporters back. Carl then led Ethan and Sabrina through a staff entrance into the stadium.

They passed along a long corridor and reached an inner hall, where several other Orange League officials waited. Carl introduced them one by one. Ethan shook hands with the well-fed executives and exchanged a few composed words with each before Carl escorted him to the competitors' lounge.

"You really are impressive, Mr. Vale," Carl said as he opened the door. "I don't often see someone your age speak so easily with senior officials, without a hint of nerves."

"It's nothing," Ethan said, waving off the compliment. "I've been through a few similar occasions. I've had some practice, that's all."
