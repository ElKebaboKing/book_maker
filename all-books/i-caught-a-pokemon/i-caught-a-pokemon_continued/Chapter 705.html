The underwater passage to the open sea was longer than Ethan expected. Light strips ran along its walls, so they could at least make out the way ahead.

Milotic swam with determination. The water it displaced washed over Ethan and Courtney.

After only a dozen seconds, a deep crash sounded behind them, audible even underwater. The underground harbor must have collapsed completely. They had escaped with barely any time to spare.

A shock wave traveled through the water. Ethan felt the sea shudder and push against them. It did little to slow Milotic, but Courtney could not swim and had kept her eyes shut. The jolt startled her into losing her breath. Bubbles streamed from her mouth.

Ethan could have held on for several more minutes. The blessing he had received as the Sea Hero had changed his body in a remarkable way. Whenever it helped him underwater, he silently thanked Maya, the Sea Priestess with whom he had shared a night.

Courtney was another matter. He watched her anxiously, hoping she could endure a little longer.

Milotic finally emerged from the long tunnel and headed upward. Sunlight shone through the water above them.

Courtney could not hold out. She coughed, and seawater rushed into her mouth.

A dozen or so seconds later, Milotic broke the surface with a leap. They were still at sea. Ethan leaned against Milotic, wrapped both arms around Courtney from behind, and pressed hard against her abdomen. She brought up a mouthful of seawater.

Her eyes stayed closed and she did not wake, but her heart was still beating faintly. Ethan did not think she was in immediate danger of dying.

Deoxys rose from the water beside them. It had protected itself with psychic power and moved that way through the sea, though more slowly than Milotic.

“Take us up there,” Ethan told it.

Without moving, Deoxys wrapped psychic power around Ethan, the unconscious Courtney, and Milotic. It lifted them all to the top of the cliff.

By chance, it was the same cliff Ethan had examined before entering the base. Their footprints were still there. He had searched its face for an opening, but never looked beneath the water and had missed the tunnel.

He laid Courtney flat, removed her cloak, and opened the front of her uniform. Gengar floated out of his shadow and made an appreciative clicking sound.

“I know it's been a while since you've been with a human female, and you're young and full of energy,” Gengar said, “but is this really the time? Should we give you some privacy?”

“What are you talking about? I'm trying to save her.”

“Really? Then why are you taking off her clothes? It looks just like what you do with your women at night.”

“Go away.” Ethan refused to explain further. He moved Courtney's clothes aside and pressed both hands against her swollen abdomen. After a couple of thrusts, she coughed again and brought up more seawater. Her abdomen relaxed.

She opened her eyes weakly. She could not even lift a finger. After looking at Ethan, she stared at the sky for a long while without speaking.

A light breeze crossed her exposed stomach. She noticed her clothes, pulled them back into place, and sat up sharply. Ethan spoke before she could.

“You died.”

She blinked. “What do you mean?”

Ethan leaned back against a rock. “Without me, you would have died in the base or drowned in the sea. So this life of yours is mine now.”

Courtney frowned. “You think that will make me tell you where the boss went?”

“How do you plan to repay me for saving you, then? I don't think you're the sort of person who'd answer a good deed with betrayal.”

She had no reply. Courtney was not cruel by nature. In Team Magma, she had followed orders, but had done little that she considered unforgivable. She could not easily turn on someone who had saved her. And after narrowly surviving, she understood that she had not truly wanted to die so miserably. She had come out of the dark place her thoughts had trapped her in.

Then another image flashed before her eyes.

On an island covered in volcanic rock, Groudon rose roaring from the stone, its body seemingly bathed in magma. A blast of energy from its mouth destroyed several nearby islands. The sea glowed red. Fire spread while Pokémon fled in every direction. Maxie and his people came ashore from the submarine and approached Groudon.

Before they could act, Groudon opened its mouth. A surge of energy struck them.

Courtney jerked back to the present. Ethan was in front of her again.

What was that? She had seen a similar image in the underground harbor, and now it had returned. Was it a premonition? A warning?

“Why am I seeing this?” she murmured.

Ethan noticed her expression. “What is it?”

“I... I...” Courtney hesitated. Should she tell him? She did not know whether the vision was real or just something her exhausted mind had invented. But what if it was about to happen? Maxie and the others would be in danger.

Ethan interrupted her thoughts. “Remember what I said. Your life is mine now.”

No one had ever said anything like that to Courtney. She was a Team Magma commander, but in Maxie's eyes she had been a piece he could discard. She looked at Ethan with complicated feelings. If their places had been reversed, Maxie would never have saved her.

“I think I saw something,” she said at last.

“Saw what?” Ethan looked around. Nothing unusual was nearby.

“An image appeared in my mind. Soon, the boss and his people will be attacked by Groudon. None of them will survive.”

Ethan raised his eyebrows. “A premonition or a hallucination? Have you ever had an ability like this before?”

She shook her head. She could not explain it. It felt as though something was trying to warn her.

“Then part of you already suspects Maxie's plan won't go smoothly,” Ethan said. “If you're worried, tell me where he's headed. We can go there and find out what's really happening.”

Courtney fell silent. She wanted to know whether Maxie and the others were safe. She had done all she could to delay Ethan—even tried to die with him. But giving him Maxie's destination would mean betraying Team Magma.

Ethan's phone rang. Shelly was calling.

He had replaced his phone long ago with one suited to his travels. It could withstand water and connect briefly by satellite, allowing calls even from remote places. He answered in front of Courtney. Shelly's departure from Team Aqua had not become public, but it could not stay secret forever. Ethan saw no need to hide their contact.

“Have you found out where Maxie is going?” Shelly asked at once.

Courtney recognized her rival's voice. Why was Shelly calling Ethan?

“Not yet,” Ethan said, glancing at Courtney. “I caught Courtney, but she won't say a word.”

Shelly fell quiet for a moment and sighed. “Let me talk to her.”

Ethan turned on the speaker and held out the phone. This might be what changed Courtney's mind.

“Courtney, listen,” Shelly said calmly. She told her what had happened at the Weather Institute and later in the undersea cavern. “Groudon and Kyogre are alike in this. Once either undergoes Primal Reversion, it will bring destruction to the world.”

Courtney stared, trying to absorb the news. Team Aqua's second-in-command had left the organization and was now with Ethan? Archie had failed to control Kyogre because of him? Most unsettling was what Shelly said about Kyogre. Would controlling Groudon lead to ruin rather than the better world Team Magma promised?

Ethan watched her until he thought the moment was right. “Will you work with me now?”

She studied him. “I'll take you to the boss. But I need to see for myself whether what you've said is true.”

Ethan shrugged. Her doubts did not matter for the moment. She would show him where to find Maxie and the orb.

Courtney drew a breath and gave him a set of coordinates. Shelly heard them and ended the call.

Ethan sent out Dragonite. “Come on. We should leave.” He climbed onto its back and offered Courtney his hand.

She had never imagined traveling with him like this. Still uncertain, she gritted her teeth and took his hand. He pulled her up behind him. Dragonite leaped and beat its wings, carrying them into the sky.

Ethan messaged Misty. She and Phoebe had put out the fires on Mt. Pyre and were on their way toward him. Without a flying Pokémon to ride, they had to walk through the remote area. He had no time to wait. He asked Misty to head for Lilycove City instead; they could reunite there after he dealt with Maxie.

Misty replied almost immediately. She understood she could do little more here. She agreed, urged Ethan to be careful, and promised to wait in Lilycove for his victorious return.

With Courtney guiding them, Dragonite flew southeast. Maxie had a head start, but a submarine was not especially fast. Dragonite drove forward like an arrow, covering hundreds of meters in moments.
