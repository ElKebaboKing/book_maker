Archie could be reckless, but even he knew sending ordinary members close to Groudon and Kyogre would be sending them to their deaths. He ordered them to stay back and delay Ethan. Archie and Matt continued the chase with their own Pokémon.

Ethan knew approaching the two ancient Pokémon was dangerous, too. He recalled the members of his team he did not need for this push. Gengar, Gardevoir, Dragonite, Raikou, and Suicune remained with him as he drove through the opposition.

Shelly and Courtney made no changes. Each had brought out only a few Pokémon. Courtney fought alongside three Mightyena rather than her usual Camerupt. A Fire-type Pokémon would have little chance against Team Aqua's many Water types.

The Mightyena formed a disciplined little pack. They were strong individually, but stronger together: when one attacked, the others guarded it. Mightyena naturally favored group action, followed a leader, and worked together to pursue prey. That made them attractive to commanders in both organizations.

Shelly's problem was different. On land, she could not use her strongest sea-dwelling Pokémon. She relied on Walrein and Crawdaunt, both capable partners. Team Aqua's members faced the same limitation. They too had left their favored fully aquatic Pokémon in the water and fought with those that could move ashore. Both sides lost some of their strength.

Archie had still been overconfident to think those members would stop Ethan. They had failed to break his defense with Archie and Matt commanding them. Without either leader, they stood even less chance. Ethan's team cut through their lines like a blade, with Shelly and Courtney following.

Raikou was especially difficult for Team Aqua. Its Electric attacks had a natural advantage against their Water types. Most Pokémon it struck went down on the first hit; few could endure a second. Explosions flashed among the members, their flames almost immediately doused by the rain.

“He's too strong. We can't fight him!” one member groaned.

Gardevoir defeated his Pokémon with Psychic. It flew several meters and landed on top of him. Pinned beneath it, the member could only watch Ethan's group pass.

“And Lady Shelly is with him,” said another injured member, clutching an arm. “How are we supposed to attack her?”

“Idiot. Why are you still calling her ‘Lady Shelly’?”

“Because I respected her. She's why I joined Team Aqua.”

Archie had expelled Shelly, and the whole organization had heard. Some who had admired her could not accept it. Was she being forced? Did she have a reason they did not know? They wished it were a false report. Seeing her fight beside Ethan ended that hope, yet many still could not bring themselves to attack her with all their strength.

...

At the island's center, Groudon and Kyogre unleashed their power. The ground trembled beneath them and threatened to break apart.

Lightning cracked overhead. Rain soaked Maxie's clothes. He glanced back. Archie was still closing in, clinging to the pursuit like a dog that would not let go. Maxie had run toward the ancient Pokémon expecting Archie to turn away, but had underestimated his courage and his obsession with the Blue Orb.

Maxie could understand it. If an easy chance to recover the Red Orb lay before him, he might pursue it just as fiercely.

He looked toward Kyogre and considered trying to capture it with the Blue Orb. He took several steps, but each attempt to move closer was stopped by flying volcanic rock or a wave of force. His human body could not withstand either. He had reached the limit of how far he could go.

Damn it. I have the orb, but I can't get near enough to use it.

Neither Groudon nor Kyogre had managed to defeat the other. They stopped almost together and gathered power. A dreadful pressure rose from both of them. Everyone nearby sensed that something was wrong.

Before anyone could act, enormous red and blue bursts of energy rose more than a hundred meters from the center of the island.

They collided. The explosion swept everything around them. The blast threw people and Pokémon away. The island beneath them took the worst of it. Volcanic rock melted or shattered under the force. Then another wave of energy expanded across the land, threatening to engulf it all.

Ethan pushed himself up and saw it coming. “Go!”

He recalled the Pokémon around him and sent out Dragonite again in the same breath. He climbed on and pulled Shelly and Courtney aboard, one on either side, after they recalled their own partners.

Dragonite shot toward the edge of the island. It was fast, but the blast wave caught it before it cleared the shore. Dragonite rolled, throwing all three people into the sea.

Splash!

The water was icy, but the heat vanished at once. Shelly surfaced first, swimming powerfully. Ethan followed, then remembered Courtney could not swim. He dived back and brought her up. She had held her breath and emerged gasping, but had not swallowed water this time.

Dragonite's smooth head appeared behind them. They looked back at the island. Most of it had disappeared. Its outer rim remained, but the raised volcanic rocks at the center were gone. A huge crater had taken their place, and seawater rushed into it through the broken ground.

Groudon and Kyogre stood inside, both wounded and breathing hard as they watched each other.

“What was that?” Shelly murmured.

Team Aqua and Team Magma had chased the two Pokémon since their organizations began, but much of what they knew came from old writings and stone tablets. They did not know every detail of their powers. The attacks they had just witnessed were unfamiliar.

“Precipice Blades and Origin Pulse,” Ethan said. “Their signature moves.”

Shelly and Courtney stared at the crater. “Such power...”

The moves were powerful, but Ethan was not stunned by the blast alone. Together, they had done damage comparable to Tri-Bird Assault from his three legendary birds. That comparison covered only a single use. His birds were exhausted after their combined attack and fought at a fraction of their strength afterward. Groudon and Kyogre had used their signature moves and were still ready to fight. Their individual power was far greater.

And this was before Primal Reversion. The same attacks would grow more terrifying in their primal forms.

After a while, the effects of the explosion faded. Some Team Aqua members farther away had reached the water in time. Many others floated motionless on the surface. Ethan could not tell whether they were alive.

He searched among them for Maxie and found nothing. Had Maxie been vaporized or sunk? Ethan was concerned about the Blue Orb still in Maxie's possession. Losing Maxie might rid Hoenn of a dangerous man, but losing the orb would cause other problems. Archie, Matt, and Tabitha were missing too. Ethan doubted all of them had died so easily. He expected at least some to reappear before long.

Courtney held onto Ethan's waist to stay afloat. Her hand brushed the pouch tied there. It was empty.

“Where's the other orb?” she asked.

Ethan looked down. The bottom of the pouch had been torn open. The Red Orb was gone.

He stared, then sighed. The blast must have ripped the fabric and thrown the orb free. How could he search for it now? The sea was beneath them, rain fell from dark clouds, and visibility was poor.

Shelly noticed the empty pouch and said nothing. It was terrible luck, but she could not blame Ethan. Had she carried it through that blast, she might have lost it too. They had finally brought the two orbs together, only to lose both within minutes.

At least neither Groudon nor Kyogre had fallen under anyone's control. They had fought and destroyed an island, but that was better than the disaster the Teams had intended.

For Ethan, the matter did not feel finished. “I have a feeling this isn't over. The orbs are lost, but when we next see Archie and Maxie, I bet they'll have them.”

Shelly frowned. “Why do you say that?”

“A hunch. Call it a rule about villains.” Ethan sighed. “Next time, we won't be facing ordinary Groudon and Kyogre. They'll have undergone Primal Reversion. And I think we'll face Rayquaza as well.”
