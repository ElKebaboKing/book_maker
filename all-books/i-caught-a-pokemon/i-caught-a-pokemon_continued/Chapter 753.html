At five o'clock that afternoon, the intense first day of preliminaries came to an end. After totaling every contestant's score, the organizers announced the top 64.

The Coordinators waited tensely. Those who had performed well expected to advance, while those whose appeals had gone badly still looked up at the big screen, hoping for a lucky place in the next round. Many had a rough idea of where their scores put them, but with so many entrants, no one could be certain until the official results appeared.

The Festival came only once a year. Honor, reputation, and prize money were at stake. Anyone eliminated today would have to wait until next year to try again.

“Now, let's reveal the results!” Vivian cried.

The big screen changed, displaying 64 names and portraits in order. Robert had the highest score. Surprisingly, Drew was not second. That place belonged to a woman with long, dark blue hair. Ethan and his friends had not expected her, but there had been three points between Robert and Drew, and the field was full of talent. Someone surpassing Drew was hardly impossible.

Their eyes swept down the list. May's portrait appeared at number 48.

She was delighted and deeply relieved. She had expected to advance, but only seeing her name there finally let her relax.

“A feast! A feast!” Ash shouted. “May made it to the main competition! We have to celebrate properly!”

As the others cheered, Ethan checked the rest of the list. He did not recognize most of the entrants, but Harley's portrait appeared near the bottom. He had advanced too. Whatever Ethan thought of the man's character, Harley was capable. If he put less effort into scheming against people, he might do even better. But that habit seemed rooted so deeply in him that Ethan doubted he would get much farther without changing it.

That evening, the group enjoyed the feast Ash had demanded. Caroline paid for it, both to celebrate her daughter's place in the main competition and to thank Ash, Brock, and the others for looking after May and Max on their journey.

Ash did not mind who paid as long as the food was good. He and Pikachu devoured it as though they had not eaten for days. Everyone but Caroline was used to the sight. If anything, they appreciated how openly Ash enjoyed himself.

The first day of the Grand Festival ended in that easy, friendly atmosphere.

The schedule allowed one day of rest after the preliminaries. The main competition would begin on the third day, giving May more time to prepare.

But the next morning, just as they finished breakfast in the dining hall, Harley appeared again.

“Good morning, May!”

He wore such a bright smile that, however little they liked him, May and the others could hardly ignore his greeting.

“Good morning, Harley,” May answered.

Harley approached with an overfamiliar air and an affected smile. “You're going to practice, aren't you? So am I! Would you mind practicing together?”

May repeated the refusal Ethan had helped her prepare the day before. “I'm sorry. I've already made plans with my friends. We're going somewhere farther away today, so I can't practice with you.”

Harley paused. This was the first time May had refused him. After their earlier encounters, he knew she was kindhearted and easily embarrassed. That was why his little schemes usually worked on her. But this answer did not sound like her at all.

He was surprised, but kept smiling. “Don't say that. We're both Coordinators, and we've both reached the main competition. Shouldn't we help each other? Besides, aren't we friends?”

Harley was like a burr that would not come loose. Ethan did not know how he had first met May or what had happened between them, but she could not seem to shake him. Harley was right about one thing: unlike Misty, May struggled to refuse people. If she were alone, she would not know what to do.

Fortunately, even without Caroline beside her, she had someone else to rely on. May said nothing to Harley and turned to Ethan with a helpless look.

Ethan sighed inwardly. He had hoped May could handle this herself, but Harley was more than she could manage for now. Ethan stepped in front of her and faced him.

It was the first time they had properly confronted one another. Harley studied Ethan's face, then met his eyes and felt an abrupt, oppressive pressure.

“Didn't you hear her say she has plans?” Ethan asked coldly. “Leave while you still can. You seem to care about your reputation, so don't make me throw you out. I won't be this polite the next time I ask.”

His words were much harsher than May's. Harley's expression changed. Behind Ethan, Misty loosened her clenched fists. It was probably fortunate Ethan had stepped in first. Given her temper, she might already have unleashed the full force of her anger on someone this persistent.

Harley looked Ethan up and down, frowning. “Who are you?”

“You don't know who he is?” Ash cleared his throat, ready to list Ethan's titles, but Ethan raised a hand to stop him.

“Who I am is none of your concern,” Ethan said. “All you need to know is that you're not welcome here.”

It was blunt, and being dismissed like rubbish in front of everyone was more than Harley could bear.

“What did you say?” His voice rose. “Who do you think you are, talking to me like that?”

“I'll say it once.” Ethan remained cool. “If you keep bothering May, don't expect me to be courteous.”

Harley wanted to lash out and teach this stranger a lesson. But he had spent so long laying the groundwork to make May lose the competition. He could not throw that work away now. Absolutely not.

He drew a breath, forced down his anger, and turned to May with an ingratiating tone. “May, you don't feel that way, do you? We're friends, aren't we?”

“Well...”

May had not expected Ethan to be so direct. Now Harley appealed to her, and she could not find an answer. He was clearly trying to make her speak for him.

Harley assumed the man who had appeared at May's side was a recent acquaintance. Otherwise, why had he never seen him at any of her previous Contests? Harley had known May through several of them. Surely she would choose his side.

May looked torn. It was not that she could not choose between Ethan and Harley. She wanted to refuse Harley, but could not say it to his face. Ethan stepped in again.

“This is ridiculous,” he told Harley. “You're an adult. Can't you stop picking on a young girl and act like a man? Drop the affected smile, and stop putting on that feminine way of speaking.”

That finally struck Harley's temper. Misty, Ash, and Brock exchanged glances too. They had rarely seen Ethan so confrontational. His words were harsh, but after Harley's behavior, hearing them was satisfying.

Harley could not stand such mockery. May had once merely failed to warm to his Cacturne and called it frightening, and he had held that against her ever since. Ethan had now insulted him to his face.

He could deceive and sabotage May, but looking at Ethan, he wanted to drive the man's head into the floor and make him pay for his rudeness.

“I see.” Harley took a deep breath. The artificial smile vanished. Outwardly he looked composed, but rage boiled inside him. “You're determined to oppose me. Fine. If you're looking for trouble, I'll give it to you.”

That was what Ethan had been waiting for. Words alone would not make Harley go away. From the moment he stepped forward, Ethan had expected they would have to battle.

“At least you've chosen a man's way to settle this.” Ethan pointed a thumb behind him and smiled. “Come to the battle field out back. I'd like to see what someone like you can do.”

Harley followed him out of the dining hall without a word, his face dark. Ash, Max, and Brock could not stay seated either and hurried after them.

May opened her mouth as she watched them go, but Misty stopped her.

“Leave it to Ethan.” Misty gave her a brilliant smile. “Two men fighting over a girl—it's just like a romance drama! And with someone like Ethan involved, it's an experience not everyone gets.”

“What are you talking about?” May's face turned bright red. Still, Misty's words made her think that letting Ethan handle Harley might be better than enduring his constant attention herself.

Ethan led Harley from the dormitory hall toward a nearby outdoor battle field. It was a little after seven in the morning. With no matches today and the hour still early, few spectators or passersby were about. Even the competing Coordinators had not come out yet. The whole area looked empty and quiet.

After following Ethan for several dozen meters, Harley's stare grew more venomous. A vicious idea took hold. Seeing no one nearby, he released Cacturne and kept walking behind Ethan. Ethan gave no sign that he had noticed. Harley flashed his Pokémon a look, ordering it to attack from behind.

Its target was not another Pokémon. It was Ethan himself.

Trainers and Coordinators shared an unwritten rule: in a Pokémon battle, official or private, they did not attack the opposing Trainer unless defending themselves. No law enforced it, but breaking that boundary would earn widespread condemnation. Most people respected it. Harley clearly did not.

Cacturne swung its spiked arm at the back of Ethan's head.

Just then Ash and the others emerged from the dormitory hall and saw what was happening. Ethan had no Pokémon beside him, and Cacturne's strike was almost upon him. Their faces changed.

“Ethan! Look out!” Ash shouted.

He was too far away. Even if Pikachu raced to Ethan now, it would not arrive in time.
