Kanto had Pokémon Contests too. They differed a little from the Contests in other regions, but the idea was much the same. Ethan and Misty had never had reason to look into them before. Now they were traveling with May, a Coordinator, and that had changed.

Ethan took the flyer from Misty. The Contest would be held in a forest park within Saffron City, not far from the Gym. It was a sizable event, but it would not begin for several days. There was time to challenge another one or two Frontier facilities before returning.

He needed to ask May what she wanted. “Will you come with us to the next facility, or would you rather stay here for now?”

“I'll travel with you,” May answered without hesitation.

“You don't need time to prepare?” Ethan gestured toward Sabrina. “If you want to stay, you can stay with her. You won't need to worry about food or a room, and there's space here for training.”

“Really, it's all right.” May shook her head. “I'd rather keep traveling with you. Misty will help me practice along the way.”

Ethan let the matter rest. He had made the offer for May's sake, but pressing it might make her think he wanted to send her away. Traveling would be good for her too. She would see more than she could by training in one place, even if she had less time to prepare specifically for the Contest.

That evening, the four of them ate dinner at a Western-style restaurant. Ethan explained to Sabrina why he had returned to Kanto.

She was disappointed to hear he would leave the next day. She knew he had goals he wanted to pursue and did not try to stop him. Besides, he would return in a few days for May's Contest. Knowing they would see each other again soon eased the parting.

They went back to the Saffron Gym that night. With another separation ahead, Ethan and Sabrina spent more time together. After the afternoon they had already shared, it tested his stamina. Even an iron-bodied man might have struggled with so many rounds in so few hours. Fortunately, the oysters he had ordered at dinner seemed to help when he needed them.

Still, Misty looked concerned when she saw the shadows under his eyes the next morning. Ethan waved her worry away with a smile. A little exhaustion was nothing beside being apart from Sabrina for so long.

After breakfast, they said goodbye outside the Gym. Sabrina stood on the steps, a little tired herself but glowing. Ethan smiled up at her.

“You don't need to walk us any farther. You're a Gym Leader—what would people think if they saw? Don't worry. I'll be back in a few days.”

Sabrina nodded. Ethan waved once more and left with Misty and May. She watched him go.

Not long after leaving the Gym, the three passed a middle-aged man walking toward it. They had gone a few steps beyond him when Misty hesitated.

“Why did that man look familiar?”

“Did he?” Ethan raised an eyebrow. He thought back but did not remember meeting the man.

“I've got it! He's Sabrina's father. I met him when I came with Ash to challenge the Saffron Gym.”

Sabrina's father? Ethan looked back. The man was indeed heading toward the Gym. Surprised, Ethan shook his head. “Never mind. Let's keep moving.”

…

Celadon City was the nearest city to Saffron. Ethan knew very well who was there, but he did not dare go to see Erika yet. He had only just reunited with Sabrina. He needed a little time before he could face Erika with a clear mind.

There was another reason too: his stamina—and his aching lower back—would not allow it.

Instead, he chose the second Frontier facility near the area south of Lavender Town as their destination.

Misty could have left the group by now and gone home to Cerulean City. Perhaps she was reluctant to part, or perhaps she did not miss her three sisters quite enough yet. She had shown no inclination to go. Ethan did not suggest it either. He had traveled with her for so long that he was used to having her beside him. He would miss her if she left.

They took the main road east out of Saffron City. As they walked, Misty told May about the strange and funny things she and Ash had encountered in this area. Her stories stirred Ethan's memories of his own early travels.

He felt a strong nostalgia. Kanto really had been his starting region, the place where he took his first steps as a Trainer and met so many people and Pokémon.

After a little more than half a day, they reached Lavender Town. The town was famous for ghostly incidents and as a resting place for departed Pokémon. Like Mt. Pyre in Hoenn, its Pokémon Tower was where people held funerals and memorials.

None of the three wanted to linger. They stopped for lunch, then planned to continue while there was still daylight. Ethan's phone rang.

To his surprise, the caller was Miki, eldest of Ecruteak City's five Kimono Sisters. Why would she be calling him?

“Hello, Miss Miki. Is something wrong?”

“Well…” Her voice came through the phone, with faint voices of her younger sisters behind her. “Are you planning to visit Johto any time soon, Mr. Ethan?”

“Johto?” Ethan raised an eyebrow. “Has something happened?”

“Nothing like that.” Miki sounded a little embarrassed. “Your Eevee has been away from us for a long time, and we miss it. We hoped it might come back to visit.”

So that was it. Eevee had spent a long time with the sisters, longer than it had with Ethan. To them, it was family. Since they had entrusted it to him, he ought to bring it back when they asked.

“The timing works out well,” Ethan said after a moment. “I've just returned to Kanto. Once I finish what I'm doing here, I'll make time to come over.”

“Really? That's wonderful! We'll look forward to seeing you.”

They exchanged a few more words and hung up. Ethan had barely lowered his phone when it rang again. At first he thought Miki had forgotten something, but the screen showed a different caller: Liza of Charicific Valley.

What was happening today? Why was everyone calling him at once? There was only one likely reason Liza would contact him, so he answered immediately.

“Mr. Ethan,” she began, “are you free any time soon?”

“What's happened? Is something wrong with my Charizard?”

She heard the concern in his voice and laughed. “It's good news. Your Charizard has trained hard and is preparing to challenge the strongest Charizard in the valley. If it wins, it'll be the strongest here. You're its Trainer; I thought you'd want to see that.”

What? His Charizard was ready to challenge the valley's strongest? Had it grown that much? Ethan was thrilled. Now he had every reason to go to Johto.

“Will it happen in the next few days? Because…”

Liza cut him off. “You're busy right now, aren't you? Don't worry. I called to let you know in advance. Charizard doesn't have to challenge it this week. It can wait until you have time to be there.”

It was unusually thoughtful of Liza to arrange things that way. It gave Ethan enough time to plan his travels. He thanked her and ended the call.

He felt newly energized. Once Charizard finished this challenge, it could return to his team. Eevee's visit to the sisters might also provide the occasion for it to evolve. Either event could make his team much stronger.

…

The three left Lavender Town and traveled north. Their next destination was called the Battle Dome. The name sounded grand, but Ethan assumed that, in the end, it would be much like a Gym and offer little unusual.

Misty was disappointed. Between “Battle Arena” and “Battle Dome,” she had expected the buildings to match their names or at least serve some special purpose. Apparently she had expected too much.

By evening, after a full day's travel, they reached their destination. They were too tired to inspect the Dome. They found rooms at a Pokémon Center and soaked in hot baths, washing away the day's fatigue.

The next morning they approached the facility refreshed—and discovered that it was anything but ordinary. The Battle Dome was far grander than the Battle Arena. Hundreds of people streamed through its entrance, making the area bustle. Ethan spotted reporters in the crowd and helicopters circling overhead.

“This is quite a production,” Misty said in surprise. “Why are so many people here? Is there an event?”

“I don't know. Let's ask someone.”

They moved through the crowd and soon learned the reason. A Frontier match was being held today featuring Tucker, the Battle Dome's superstar. Tucker was also its Frontier Brain.

“So it's just a battle,” Misty said. “I thought there was some other event to draw a crowd this large.”

“At least we can see how popular Tucker is.” Ethan shook his head. “Come on. Let's go in.”

The Dome's interior surprised them again. Nearly every seat was filled. Men and women of every age had come, though groups of young women were the most noticeable. Ethan and the girls were increasingly curious about the man they were waiting to see.

They found their seats. Soon the lights dimmed and the large screen played a promotional video for the challenger. As it ended, a young female Trainer stepped onto the stage.

A second screen lit up on the opposite side. An announcer's voice rang through the Dome.

“A Pokémon battle blooms like a fleeting flower! People of the Battle Dome, revel in dazzling moves and the art of ingenious tactics! The Dome's superstar—Tucker—has arrived!”

Spotlights flashed and petals fell. A man in a black cloak descended into a Trainer's area. Suspended as though on wires, he swept in a circuit above the arena before landing.

The crowd reached a new pitch. Shrill screams from his female fans filled Ethan's ears.

“He's incredibly popular!” Misty covered her ears. May nearly buried her head against Misty to escape the noise.

Ethan could not hear Misty over the cheering. He kept his eyes on the cloaked man, curious to see what kind of person this Frontier Brain was.

Tucker landed and swept his cloak aside, revealing himself. In a strong voice, he called, “Beautiful people, welcome to the Battle Dome!”
