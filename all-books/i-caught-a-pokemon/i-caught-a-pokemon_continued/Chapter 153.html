Gary recalled Nidoking and gripped its Poké Ball tightly.

Counting the injured Fearow, he had now lost two rounds in a row. His last choice had to be a Pokémon capable of turning the match around. Out of all the Pokémon he owned, only a few had that kind of power.

Should he send out Blastoise? Or would Arcanine be the better choice?

Blastoise was his very first Pokémon and the partner he trusted most. Arcanine was just as strong and had helped him defeat countless opponents.

But Ethan's Jolteon had the advantage against Blastoise, while Gyarados had the advantage against Arcanine.

Before the match, Gary had considered all kinds of possibilities. He had expected a hard fight. He had never imagined being cornered like this.

He looked across the field at the handsome man with the calm expression. A breeze happened to lift a strand of Ethan's hair, making him look almost unfathomable.

Was Ethan really that strong? Was Gary truly this weak beside him?

He had been driven to the edge of a cliff. One more loss and he would fall. Thinking back on all the boasts he had made, Gary could only see how childish they sounded now.

While he stood there wrestling with those thoughts, a chorus of high-pitched voices reached him from the stands.

He turned. His cheerleaders were shouting with all their strength. They were too far away for him to make out their words, but their excited faces told him what mattered: they wanted him to keep going.

Everyone...

Gary felt something stir in his chest. Then he saw the elderly man in a white lab coat sitting beside them.

Grandpa came too?

Professor Oak's eyes met his, and a sudden warmth filled Gary.

"Hmph. You finally decided to come out of your laboratory and watch one of my matches?" he muttered.

He might have sounded dismissive, but the smile spreading across his face said otherwise. Seeing his grandfather there gave him courage.

Finally, Gary's gaze found Ash.

You've already made it to the next round. There's no way I'm falling here!

Gary faced Ethan again and pushed away his doubts. With so many people supporting him, how could he give up now?

He put away Nidoking's Poké Ball and decisively chose his third Pokémon.

"Go! My final choice!"

Blue light flashed, and an Electabuzz appeared on the field.

"Oh!" the commentator cried. "After giving it plenty of thought, Gary has chosen Electabuzz! As an Electric-type, it has an advantage against Gyarados. And against Jolteon, it won't be at a type disadvantage either. An excellent choice!"

An Electric-type?

Ethan recalled Gyarados and ran his fingers slowly across the Poké Balls at his waist. After a moment's thought, he sent Jolteon back out.

In that case, let's see how a battle between two Electric-types goes.

"Jolteon, I'm counting on you!"

Jolteon sprang from the blue light with a short cry and eagerly rushed up to face Electabuzz.

The referee announced the start. Neither Pokémon bothered to test the other. Their fight immediately became fierce, harsh crackles filling the stadium as brilliant electricity flashed across the field.

Spectators seated closest to the action had to narrow their eyes against the glare. Ice blocks exploded one after another. Cracks spread through the frozen surface.

Then both Pokémon unleashed Thunderbolt at the same time. The tremendous burst of energy collapsed a broad section of the Ice Field. Water shot high into the air before falling like heavy rain over the ruined battlefield.

The once-thick ice had broken into countless pieces, drifting across the pool with the waves.

By the time the audience caught up with the furious exchange, Jolteon and Electabuzz were standing on separate floes, facing each other and panting.

The fight had looked evenly matched, but Gary sensed something was wrong.

Electric-type attacks should have dealt only half their usual damage against another Electric-type. Yet Electabuzz was covered in scorch marks, while Ethan's Jolteon looked almost untouched despite all the hits it had taken.

Gary stared for a moment before the answer struck him.

Tch. Jolteon's Ability is Volt Absorb...

No wonder Electric-type moves weren't hurting it!

Gary immediately ordered Electabuzz to switch to moves of other types.

Quick Attack. Swift. Low Kick. Light Screen...

He tried almost every move Electabuzz had. Without its Electric-type attacks, though, it was like a tiger without teeth. Its claws were still sharp, but it had lost its most dangerous weapon. Gradually, it fell behind.

Jolteon, meanwhile, could attack freely. With quick feet and nimble movements, it darted between the floating sheets of ice, searching for any opening.

At last, a large block drifted past and briefly obscured Electabuzz's view. Jolteon seized the chance. One Pin Missile ended the battle.

"Wow!"

The audience burst into applause and cheers for the dazzling fight. Gary saw Electabuzz fall and instinctively stepped back, his thoughts turning hazy.

Conviction alone could not erase a gap in strength. No matter how badly someone wanted to win, that desire could not change the result on the field.

The confidence Gary had only just regained broke apart again, like water rushing through a collapsed dam.

Two of his Pokémon had now been defeated. He stood in a daze until the referee prompted him to send out Fearow once more.

By then, everyone seemed to know how the match would end.

Some of Gary's cheerleaders had tears in their eyes, unable to accept what they were seeing. Even a few of Ethan's supporters offered Gary a sympathetic round of applause.

Though Ethan knew Gary had little chance left, he remained cautious and sent out his third Pokémon.

Charizard soared into the air and fixed its eyes on the prey before it.

The two Flying-type Pokémon pursued each other across the sky. But the gap in strength was too great. Charizard's Flamethrower caught the already injured Fearow and sent it falling into the water.

The referee raised a colored flag.

"Fearow is unable to battle! All three of Gary's Pokémon have been defeated! The winner of the final Ice Field match is Ethan Vale!"

The crowd cheered without restraint. Gary knew those cheers belonged to the victor. They had nothing to do with him.

He gave Ethan one last, complicated look, silently recalled Fearow, and left the stadium in a daze.
