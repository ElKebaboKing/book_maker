Robert's appearance put Drew under tremendous pressure too. He realized that Robert might be his greatest rival in this Contest.

No longer in the mood to mock May, Drew turned away with a grim face. Without another word, he left the waiting room and disappeared down the corridor.

Ethan and the others didn't notice him slip out. Their attention was still fixed on the television.

Several contestants performed after Robert, but perhaps his near-perfect showing had shaken them. A few made mistakes they normally wouldn't have made. Such basic slips were the last thing a Coordinator wanted, yet they kept happening. Robert had clearly put them under pressure.

May had no time to worry about the other contestants. The appeal round was nearing its end, and her own turn was coming.

“Good luck!” The announcement called her to the stage, and Ethan and the others offered their encouragement. “Believe in yourself. You can do it!”

Vivian lifted her microphone to introduce the final contestant. “Only one Coordinator remains in our first round! Please welcome contestant number fifty, May of Petalburg City!”

May took a deep breath. Under her friends' encouraging gazes, she straightened her small frame, nodded to them, and marched out of the waiting room with the determination of someone who had decided there was no turning back.

None of them had expected her to draw the last number. She would have to close the round.

After so many performances, the audience and judges might already be tired of watching. Unless she offered something special, the previous contestants could throw her off, and even a good appeal might fail to make an impression on weary viewers.

But she had drawn number fifty. Whatever she thought of the position, she had to go out and perform.

Misty clasped her hands, her eyes glued to the screen. She looked almost more nervous than May. “Come on, May. You can do it!”

May came through the contestants' entrance and walked to Vivian's side.

Vivian smiled at the crowd. “This is May's first Pokémon Contest, and she looks a little nervous. But once the performance begins, I'm sure she'll leave that feeling behind. Now let's welcome her Pokémon and see what they've prepared for us!”

Mr. Big and Dr. Abby had both offered May advice, and her friends had cheered her on. Once the appeal began, she quickly found her rhythm.

She threw a Poké Ball and released Beautifly. At the start of the performance, Beautifly merely fluttered its wings, scattering the fine scales unique to butterfly Pokémon. Beneath the spotlights, they drifted down like a tiny galaxy.

May tossed a flying disc. Beautifly used String Shot to flick it back into her hand like a whip. They repeated the exchange several times, showing the audience how well they worked together.

On May's final throw, Beautifly used Gust to send the disc back along a wide arc. May leaped high, spun neatly, and caught it. The audience applauded.

Misty and the others breathed easier. May had performed smoothly so far. Unlike Drew, who had let his Pokémon display its beauty alone, she interacted with Beautifly throughout the act. Their almost acrobatic routine gave the audience something different to enjoy.

It swept away the spectators' and judges' fatigue. They watched closely again.

May didn't disappoint them. For the finale, Beautifly used Silver Wind and began to spin. A small silver vortex formed around it, spreading glittering powder outward. The wind carried that powder across the stage until everything seemed bathed in silver light.

Under that glow, May struck a stylish pose and bowed to the audience. Beautifly landed behind her, and together they completed their curtain call.

Applause thundered through the arena.

Back in the waiting room, Ethan and the others were relieved that May had finished so well. Before they could celebrate, the camera turned toward the judging table, and their eyes followed it.

The audience could applaud all it liked, but these three people would decide whether May advanced.

Nurse Joy spoke first, smiling. “That was a very good performance for a first Contest!”

She tapped her screen. Her score appeared: 8.7!

Mr. Contesta, the director of the Pokémon Contest Committee, spoke next. “I agree. Such a smooth and accomplished performance is a pleasant surprise. She hardly looked like a beginner. She has a promising future, and I'll be watching her progress!”

His score appeared: 8.9!

That left Mr. Sukizo, the president of the Pokémon Fan Club. Vivian held the microphone out to him. As before, he offered just one short sentence.

“I liked it.”

His score was 9.1!

It was the highest of the three and the only score above nine. Crossing that mark felt like clearing a difficult threshold; a score over nine carried a different weight. May's appeal had truly won the club president's approval.

The computer added the judges' scores and displayed her total.

26.7!

May jumped for joy. Was it a high score? Not especially. It was well behind Drew's and Robert's scores in the twenty-nines, and it was comparable to the totals of several unremarkable contestants.

But this was May's first Pokémon Contest. As Mr. Contesta had said, she showed real promise. With more competitions and experience, she could become an excellent Coordinator.

Ethan felt proud of her. He clearly remembered May's first Slateport Contest in the anime: she had made a mistake during her appeal and received a slightly lower score.

His presence had affected her performance, even though he hadn't personally done much to train her. In the anime, she hadn't practiced with Mr. Big or met Dr. Abby and received her advice. A good beginning would help both her confidence and her growth.

“All fifty contestants have now completed their first-round appeals,” Vivian announced. “Please wait a moment while we determine which Coordinators will advance to the second round!”

May left the stage and returned to the waiting room. The moment she came through the door, Ethan and the others greeted her with enthusiastic applause.

“Congratulations! You did really well!”

“Yeah, Sis! That was even better than you usually do!” Max added.

May was happy, but her face still showed her worry. “I wonder if my score will be enough to get me into the second round...”

“Don't worry.” Ethan smiled. “I worked it out. Your score is a little above the middle of the field. Unless something unexpected happens, you should advance.”

She couldn't relax until she saw the official list. After a short, anxious wait, Vivian announced the results.

“Thank you for waiting! Eight Coordinators have been selected for the second round. Here they are!”

Eight portraits appeared on the big screen. Ethan and the others spotted May almost immediately, in the last position.

“I made it!”

Now that she knew she had advanced, May jumped up. She and Misty held hands, bouncing and laughing with excitement.

Ethan looked over the other finalists. There were both men and women, most of them fairly young.

As expected, Drew and Robert occupied the top two places. The remaining finalists had also delivered noteworthy appeals. The list was much as Ethan had predicted. The judges had seen their merit, and the audience could see it too.

Brock chose that moment to temper May's excitement. “It's great that you made the second round, but the next part is a battle. May isn't used to battling yet. That could be a problem for her.”

May fell quiet. He was right. Almost all her practice had gone into preparing an appeal strong enough to clear the first round. She had little experience with Contest battles.

“It's okay,” she said, seemingly untroubled. “I'm already happy to have reached the second round. I never even dreamed of winning the whole Contest.”

She knew her own abilities. Rather than aiming for the highest prize at once, she was ready to improve one step at a time.

“Now we'll randomly assign the matchups for the second round!” Vivian announced.

On the big screen, the eight portraits shuffled and flashed through different positions. A few seconds later, they settled into a tournament bracket.

The Contest battle round was split into two halves. Coordinators on opposite sides would not meet before the final.

May searched for her opponent. She found Drew's portrait almost at once, facing her own.

“What?!” she cried. “I have to battle him?”

Misty and the others exchanged startled looks. What were the chances?

“That's bad luck,” Ash sighed. “Drew won't be easy to beat, May. You...”

A sharp look from Misty cut him off. May was already frowning. Misty didn't know what to say, so she stood silently beside her.

Ethan shifted in his seat and smiled. “If you don't want to lose, should I lend you one of my Pokémon? They're pretty strong. I guarantee one of them could teach that boy a lesson for you!”

Ash's eyes lit up. “In that case, May, you can borrow my Pikachu too!”

“Ethan!” Misty said helplessly. “Stop teasing her. You know cheating isn't allowed. And Ash, don't make it worse!”

Ethan laughed. He hadn't meant the offer seriously. He only wanted to lighten May's mood.

After competing in so many tournaments, he knew the consequences of cheating. If anyone discovered it, May would lose her result and might even be banned from future Contests for life. Even if she had agreed to borrow one of his Pokémon, he wouldn't have done it.

More importantly, a Coordinator couldn't switch Pokémon between the appeal and battle rounds. They had to use the same partner throughout. Almost everyone knew that rule, so they understood Ethan was joking. Only Ash had taken him seriously.

Sure enough, May looked a little happier. “I'll be all right with my own Pokémon. If I can't beat him this time, there'll be a next time and a time after that. I know I'll win someday!”

The tense mood lifted.

The disappointed contestants left the venue while those who had advanced waited eagerly. After a brief interval, the Contest battles began.

One match in the upper half of the bracket went first. The second brought May and Drew onto the battlefield.

A Contest battle was quite different from an ordinary Pokémon battle. There was a five-minute time limit. Each Coordinator had to use dazzling moves to reduce the opponent's score as much as possible. The one who did the better job before time ran out would win.

May and Drew stood facing each other. Drew looked confident, even arrogant. May didn't expect to win, but she refused to show him any weakness.

After all, she believed Beautifly's Flying type gave it a twofold advantage over Roselia's Grass type. She had a chance.

With both Coordinators and their Pokémon ready, Vivian signaled the start of the battle. The clock began to count down, and Drew gave the first command without hesitation.

He knew the type matchup was against him, but he didn't seem worried. He ordered Roselia to use Magical Leaf, opening the attack from a distance.
