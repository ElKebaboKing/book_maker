Ethan had no patience for a longer argument. He walked into the shop's back courtyard, opened a Poké Ball, and released the Crystal Onix.

It was still covered in wounds from their battle and had not fully regained its strength. A few hours of rest inside the ball had helped, though, and even in its tired state its crystal body gleamed.

"It... it really is the Crystal Onix?"

Mateo and Marissa stared, scarcely able to believe what they were seeing. Just as they moved closer for a better look, Ethan recalled it.

Mateo rushed over and clasped Ethan's hand. "Where did you find it?"

Ethan pulled his hand free. "Where I found it isn't the point. You've seen that I have this unique Pokémon. Do you believe me now?"

"I do! Would you sell it to me? I could trade you another Pokémon for it."

"I'm sorry. I don't need the money, and I don't trade away my Pokémon."

"But I need it!"

"And I'm a trainer who needs it too. Forget about buying it." Ethan's answer was firm. "I'll ask you again: can you make this Never-Melt Ice into something a Pokémon can wear?"

Seeing that Ethan would not change his mind, Mateo hesitated, then nodded. "With the right tools, yes. I can make what you want."

His attitude had changed so quickly that Ethan was surprised.

"Good. Then let's keep to my offer. Once you finish the ornament, I'll let you study the Crystal Onix. Perhaps it will give you the inspiration you're looking for."

Sabrina was lost. Why had Ethan sought out a glassmaker for the ice? And what did inspiration have to do with it?

Mateo saw her confusion. "My grandfather's shop did well when he ran it. He was a master glassmaker and created Pokémon sculptures every day. Since he died, I've tried as hard as I can, but I can't make work that satisfies me the way his did."

Now Sabrina understood why the display window held so few pieces. She glanced at several glass Pokémon on a worktable. They looked carefully made, with no obvious flaws. Mateo must be measuring himself against his grandfather and rejecting work that most people would happily buy.

That ambition, and the craft he had inherited, explained why Ethan had come to him.

"My pieces lack something," Mateo went on. "They look stiff, as though they have no life. I heard that when my grandfather was young and still learning, he saw the Crystal Onix. After that, he made one lifelike piece after another. I thought seeing it myself might give me the inspiration to improve."

Only then did Mateo stop and look at Ethan curiously. "How did you know I was looking for it?"

"You said the island's glassmakers have all been searching for it. I assumed you were too. Marissa's message mentioned it, after all. The rest wasn't hard to guess."

"I see. So I have Marissa to thank." Mateo patted his sister's head. "The others call the Crystal Onix an embodiment and symbol of the god of glass. They want to find it because they think it will help them sell their work."

Ethan could see that Mateo wanted his own work to improve too. His aim was better craftsmanship, while the others wanted better sales, but all of them hoped the Onix would make their glass more appealing. It did not matter to Ethan.

He handed Mateo a set of photographs showing parts of Articuno and a sheet of measurements.

Though none showed the entire bird, Mateo had spent years making Pokémon figures. He recognized it at once. "Is this Articuno?"

There was no point concealing it once Mateo had guessed. "Yes."

"You really are something," Mateo said. "You've caught the Crystal Onix and you own Articuno. What kind of ornament do you want for it?"

"You can choose the shape. I don't have a design in mind. It only needs to stay out of Articuno's way when it battles."

"Where can I find you when it's done?"

Ethan pointed toward the street. "We're staying at a guesthouse on the edge of town. Come there whenever you've finished."

He and Sabrina left Mateo's shop and returned to the old woman's guesthouse. They had been away only that morning, and their rooms had not been rented to anyone else.

Over the next few days, Ethan trained his Pokémon in the courtyard by day. As they worked together, the Crystal Onix settled into his team.

In the evenings, he and Sabrina walked through town and tried the local food. The days were easy and pleasant. Spending so much time together brought them closer, until they looked almost like a couple.

Ethan expected the routine to last a few more days. But Mateo was eager to see the Crystal Onix again. He worked long hours, barely stopping to eat or sleep, and in only three days had shaped and polished the Never-Melt Ice into the ornament Ethan wanted.
