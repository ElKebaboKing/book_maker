Pangoro drove a Sky Uppercut into Hariyama's chin. The powerful blow lifted its massive body clear of the floor and sent it flying.

“Hariyama!” Brawly cried in alarm. There was nothing he could do but watch. Hariyama's eyes rolled upward, and it fell backward with a heavy thud.

The Gym fell silent. The women watching stared wide-eyed at the motionless Hariyama. They could hardly believe Brawly's ace had lost.

The two Pokémon had fought for some time. At points the battle had seemed evenly matched, with both landing hard blows and taking injuries. Yet Pangoro had won.

How? Brawly stepped back, stunned. He had entered the fight brimming with confidence. Now even his strongest Pokémon had fallen to Bea's Pangoro. He couldn't understand how her Pokémon was powerful enough to defeat both of his.

Ethan, watching from the side, thought the answer was plain. Both were Gym Leaders, but Brawly spent his days surfing and let his Pokémon spend much of their time playing in the water with him. Bea trained alongside her own Pokémon with relentless discipline. Why would Brawly expect to win?

Hariyama and Pangoro had looked evenly matched during the exchange, but Pangoro was stronger and tougher. Its determination was exceptional too. More than once, it had gritted its teeth through a hit and counterattacked. Losing to such an opponent was only natural.

Pangoro returned to Bea's side and raised its head proudly. It swept a defiant look over the girls who had been so certain Brawly could not lose. They instinctively looked away.

Its Trainer said little, but Pangoro could stand up for her. Beating an opponent was the clearest way to do it.

At last the referee recovered and raised her flag. “H-Hariyama is unable to battle! The challenger wins this Gym match!”

Brawly quickly composed himself and applauded. “What a fine battle.” He felt the defeat keenly, but put on a smile as he approached Bea. “Your Pangoro is remarkable. I knew it was strong. In fact, you're the strongest female Trainer I've faced since I first received a Pokémon!”

He took a badge from his pocket and offered it to her. “Please accept this as proof you defeated me.”

Bea didn't even look at the badge. She bowed to Brawly, turned, and headed for the exit. When she passed Ethan and Misty, she hesitated, then sat beside Misty instead of leaving the Gym.

Brawly stared after her. Why hadn't she taken the badge? Winning a Gym match and accepting its badge was perfectly normal. Had he done something wrong?

Ethan stood and walked over. “Sorry. She doesn't talk much, so let me explain. She didn't come for a badge or to enter the Ever Grande Conference. She challenged you to train her Pokémon.”

“Oh, I see...” Brawly finally understood.

He was about to say more, but Ethan continued toward the challenger's side of the court. Brawly's interest stirred. “Are you here to challenge me too?”

“That's right.” Ethan glanced at Hariyama, still lying on the floor, and feigned sudden realization. “Oh! Were those your only two Pokémon? Without them, can you still defend the Gym?”

“Ha, of course I can! What a question...” Brawly's eyebrow twitched. He was a Gym Leader. Did this man really think he owned only two Pokémon? Ethan's tone was irritating too.

He looked Ethan over. The younger man seemed a few years his junior and perhaps a little more handso—no, about equally handsome.

Then he glanced at the spectators. Apart from Bea, who had caught his eye, Ethan had arrived with another pretty girl. She was still young, but Brawly could tell she would grow up to be beautiful. Ethan seemed fortunate in his companions, which only annoyed Brawly more.

“You want a Gym battle?” he asked again.

“Of course.” Ethan smiled innocently, as though he hadn't noticed the hostility in Brawly's voice. “I'm nowhere near as strong as she is, though. I don't have any especially powerful Pokémon. Please go easy on me.”

Not very strong? Brawly's eyes lit up. Perfect. Bea's victory had embarrassed him in front of her and all the women at the Gym. Here was his chance to recover some pride.

He softened his expression, afraid of scaring the challenger off. “No problem. I have a few Pokémon that aren't especially strong either. Let's have the match.”

Misty laughed from the spectators' seats. Ethan's act was so obvious! Yet Brawly had swallowed it and taken him for an easy opponent.

She could see that Brawly wanted to make up for his loss by defeating Ethan. He had no idea whom he was facing. Ethan was younger and had achieved far more. Sabrina, Erika, and Misty herself—the world's prettiest girl, in her opinion—were all more beautiful than Brawly's admirers. Most of all, Ethan's Pokémon were nothing like the weak team he claimed to have.

She had no intention of warning Brawly. “Go, Ethan!” she called, then sat back with her legs together to enjoy the show.

Bea turned a curious look on Misty. She had left after her battle with Shauna and hadn't seen Ethan fight. Brawly had seemed only somewhat stronger than Shauna today; perhaps Ethan could manage him. But why had Ethan said he wasn't very strong?

Misty caught Bea's gaze and shook her head, suppressing a laugh. If she explained, she would sound as though she were boasting about Ethan. Bea could discover his abilities for herself. Misty was more interested in whether Bea's calm expression would survive learning that Ethan was Champion of two regional Leagues.

Ethan and Brawly faced one another on the court. Unlike before, a few of the Gym's women cheered for the handsome challenger. Brawly grew more irritated. These women usually gathered around him. Why were some of them changing sides?

The rules had already been announced, so the referee didn't repeat them. Each side would still use two Pokémon.

After a moment's thought, Brawly chose his first.

“Come out, Hitmontop!”

Blue light flashed, and a Pokémon with the look of a streetwise fighter appeared. With Makuhita and Hariyama defeated, Brawly still had more than a dozen other Pokémon. Only a few were strong enough for this sort of match, and Hitmontop was one of them.

Ethan had fought Hitmontop before. He knew how skilled it was with its legs. Among the Pokémon he wanted to use here, he had just one Fighting type that also specialized in kicking.

“I'm counting on you, Combusken!”

Combusken emerged. Brawly relaxed at the sight. Hoenn newcomers could choose Torchic as a starter, and he had faced Torchic and Combusken often while running the Gym. This one hadn't even evolved into Blaziken.

Ethan had told the truth, Brawly thought. His Pokémon really was on the weak side. At least it wasn't some unfamiliar Pokémon from another region, or a fully evolved powerhouse like Bea's. Everything seemed under control.

“Brawly! You can do it!”

Shauna appeared in the second-floor gallery at last. She hadn't watched Brawly's match with Bea after running away, but she had heard he lost. Of all the girls at the Gym, her support for him was the most unwavering. Now she came out to cheer for the man she liked.

Her voice encouraged Brawly. Not everyone had lost faith in him. He decided to set aside her unauthorized attempt to act as Gym Leader, smiled broadly, and waved back.

Thank you for loving me so steadfastly, Shauna. Watch me beat this man and give you a reason to be proud!

His confidence returned. This time he didn't ask the male challenger for his name. He went straight to the battle.

“Hitmontop, close in and use Triple Kick!”

Ethan would not underestimate someone who specialized in Fighting types, especially while using a Fighting type himself. “Combusken, use Detect!”

His safest approach was to prepare before Hitmontop reached him, watch the attack, and counter from defense.

Combusken's eyes sharpened. For an instant, Hitmontop's movements seemed slower to it. Hitmontop spun forward upside down like a top and thrust a spiked foot high toward Combusken's face.

At Ethan's command, Combusken sprang back out of the way. Then it caught the opening after Hitmontop's kick and countered with Double Kick.

The battle was on. Unlike Bea's contest of raw strength, this was a test of technique.

After several exchanges, Brawly saw that Combusken wasn't overwhelmingly powerful. It was difficult, but not impossible, to handle. His Hitmontop seemed evenly matched with it, unlike Makuhita against Pangoro.

That was still more troublesome than he had expected. Why were all his challengers today so hard to beat?

The Pokémon kept exchanging kicks. Both favored attacks with their legs, so the action stayed low: a kick, a clash, a retreat, then another kick and another clash.

In only a few minutes, they had traded more than a dozen attacks. Most were blocked or countered, but each Pokémon found openings and landed several blows. Their stamina ebbed. Soon both were panting and looked ready to fall.

Hang on... Brawly watched Hitmontop wobble. It could no longer spin as smoothly as when it entered the field. His heart was in his throat.

Combusken, by contrast, stood its ground through sheer Fire-type fighting spirit. Its sharp eyes remained fixed on Hitmontop.

That determination startled Brawly. Only then did he remember the perseverance and grit he had once held so dear.

Have I spent too long taking it easy on this island, far from the mainland? Have I strayed from the path of fighting I used to follow?
