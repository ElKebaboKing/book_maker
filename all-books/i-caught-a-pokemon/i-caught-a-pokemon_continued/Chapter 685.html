Ethan turned. Misty stood on a low wall behind him with an ice pop in her mouth. When he looked her way, she jumped down, came over, and handed him a second one.

“Why didn't you explore?” Ethan asked. “I gave you the day off. You didn't have to stay with me.”

“There wasn't much to see.” Misty took the ice pop from her mouth. “The buildings have different shapes, but after a while they all start to look the same. And the festival's still several days away. Nothing much is happening yet.”

She gave him a teasing look. “I'm glad I came back, though. Otherwise I'd have missed your fierce struggle against these children.”

“What struggle? I swept the field!”

“Of course you did,” she said, grinning wider. “Against two little boys. Very impressive.”

Ethan ignored her and turned to the boys. “Well? Want another battle? I'm ready whenever you are.”

They glanced at each other and shook their heads. “No. We can't beat you. But beating us doesn't mean we've given up!”

“You don't want to battle, but you won't admit defeat. What do you want?”

The boy in the baseball cap said, “Beating us is nothing. If you can beat Kinta, then we'll admit you're better!”

“Kinta?”

“He's the strongest of us,” the rosy-cheeked boy explained. “Neither of us can beat him. You won't either!”

Ethan gathered that Kinta was the leader among the local children. “All right. Go and get him. I'll wait here.”

The boys dashed away. Only then did Ethan accept the ice pop from Misty and sit beside the pool. He called Milotic over and reviewed the battles with it while they ate.

Going over a match was an important part of a Pokémon's growth. It helped Milotic recognize what it needed to improve and remember what to watch for next time.

They finished their review, but the boys still had not returned. Ethan began to think they had stood him up. Then Misty pointed across the park.

The two boys were coming back, breathless, pushing a third boy between them. This must be Kinta.

Ethan studied him. Kinta was a year or two older than the other boys, perhaps around Misty and Ash's age. He looked thoroughly unwilling to be dragged here. Ethan heard him complaining long before they arrived.

“Slow down! I'm busy. I don't have time for one of your little play battles.”

“It's different this time!” the boy in the cap pleaded. “We met someone really strong. We can't beat him. You have to help us!”

Despite Kinta's resistance, they pushed him in front of Ethan. He wore an aloof, self-important expression.

“I hear you're strong,” Ethan said before Kinta could speak.

Kinta raised an eyebrow and studied him cautiously. He had expected another child, not an adult. Why would an adult battle his two younger friends?

“You're the one who bullied them?” he asked.

“Bullying?” Ethan laughed. “We had a friendly battle. When they lost, they said only you could beat me. That's why they fetched you.”

“And you call that friendly? You're a grown man battling little kids. Aren't you embarrassed?” Kinta sighed with exaggerated maturity, broke free of his friends, and took the other Trainer's position.

“I thought they'd found another child, someone who wasn't worth my time. But you're an adult, and you beat my friends. I can't ignore that.”

Ethan stared. Kinta had a remarkably big mouth for a boy. This was the second time someone had criticized him for battling children. What was wrong with battling them? Kinta was a child too.

Back on Earth, Ethan had heard sayings about never dismissing a young person for what they had yet to become. The words themselves might not exist here, but the meaning did. Who could know whether one of these children might turn out to be a talent like Gary—or someone like Ash?

Ethan could battle Legendary Pokémon one day and children at a park the next. Taking every opponent seriously was part of how he had come this far. In their youth, these boys had the good fortune—or misfortune—to meet him.

Kinta glanced at Milotic, then threw his Poké Ball. A Tentacruel appeared on a step in the water.

The sight stirred Ethan's memory of the giant Tentacruel he had faced in Kanto. Back then, handling it had been difficult. He had traveled and grown so much since that he could deal with it easily now. He even had more Legendary Pokémon than many new Trainers had ordinary partners. Seeing Tentacruel again made him feel how much time had passed.

He set the thought aside and directed Milotic into position.

“Go, Kinta!” the rosy-cheeked boy shouted. His friend in the cap stepped forward to serve as referee.

“Begin!”

The battle started at once. Ethan quickly saw that Kinta was more experienced than the younger boys. His commands were sharper, and Tentacruel was much stronger than their Pokémon. Kinta had reason to be confident.

Even so, he was still a child. He did not yet match Trainers who traveled and battled regularly. Ethan judged him only a little stronger than Wally had been when they met.

Early on, Tentacruel's many tentacles and poison put Milotic under pressure. Once Ethan understood Kinta's attack pattern, he brought the match under control. Milotic used Refresh to clear the poison, leaving little to threaten it. Ethan took the opening and finished the battle in a decisive series of attacks.

Kinta went still. The younger boys stared, unable to believe the result.

“K-Kinta...you lost?” the rosy-cheeked boy asked.

Kinta flushed. “Idiot! I didn't lose. I just let my guard down!”

He turned to Ethan. “That one doesn't count. Battle me again!”

The reluctant, careless air he had worn before was gone. Now he wanted badly to defeat Ethan in front of his friends.

“Another battle? Sure,” Ethan said. He was here to train Milotic. A stronger opposing Pokémon would make the practice more useful.

Kinta breathed in, recalled the unconscious Tentacruel, and took out a second Poké Ball with a serious expression.

He had claimed carelessness, but he knew he had fought with everything he had. Milotic itself had not seemed overwhelmingly strong. Ethan's commands were the problem. One moment the battle had been even; the next, Kinta had been unable to respond.

He had felt that suffocating helplessness only once before, against a man he admired. Could this stranger really be that strong? No—someone who went around challenging children could hardly compare with his hero.

Ethan knew none of Kinta's thoughts. He watched the boy's expression shift before Kinta finally threw the ball.

Marshtomp appeared.

Marshtomp was the evolved form of Mudkip, one of Hoenn's three starter Pokémon. It usually lived on thick, muddy ground and buried itself in mud to sleep. Over time it had developed strong hips and legs, letting it stand and walk steadily even on soft terrain.

A thin membrane covered its body, helping it survive on land and move quickly through mud. In the right conditions, it could make full use of its abilities.

For Kinta to bring Marshtomp out now, it was probably stronger than Tentacruel. Its eagerness to fight and the determined look on Kinta's face suggested something more: Marshtomp was likely his first Pokémon and his ace.

Have I become some great rival he has to overcome? Ethan smiled and sent Milotic out again.

“Come on, Kinta!” The two younger boys cupped their hands around their mouths and shouted so loudly that people nearby glanced over. When they saw only an adult battling a group of children, they lost interest.

The second match began. Kinta stayed focused and cautious, but the pattern repeated. After their opening exchanges, Ethan read his movements and commands. Suddenly Kinta's attacks no longer posed a threat, and Milotic defeated Marshtomp with apparent ease.

Once again Kinta felt unable to breathe under Ethan's control of the battle. “How...?” He stepped back, pale-faced, and stood stunned.

His two friends sat down hard, as though the sky had fallen. Misty leaned lazily against a bench and yawned. After the battles she had seen, this little match gave her no urge to applaud.

Ethan called Milotic back to him and stroked the scales on its head in encouragement. Then he looked at Kinta.

“Well? Another round? I'm ready whenever you are.”

It was the same tone he had used with the younger boys. Ethan meant nothing by it, but it made Kinta's temper rise. Of all people, he did not want to lose to a man who sought out children to battle.

Yet both his Pokémon had been beaten. He could no longer deny the difference between them. He took a deep breath and forced himself to calm down.

“Don't get smug. Beating me doesn't mean much. If you can beat the person I bring back, then I'll admit defeat.”

Haven't I heard that before? Ethan glanced at the two younger boys, then nodded. “Fine. Go find whoever you like. I'll wait here.”

“You said it!” Kinta shot him one last resentful look and led his friends out of the park.

Ethan laughed as he watched them go. He had only wanted two easy opponents to let Milotic practice. Instead, one challenger had led to another.

That suited him. As the opponents changed, the battles grew harder, saving him the trouble of searching for Trainers at the right level. He wanted to see who they would bring back next.
