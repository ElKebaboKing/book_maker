When Ethan and Ash reached the flame relay route, they were surprised to find Misty, May, and the others already there. They had only just finished their ramen when Ethan messaged them. How had they arrived first?

Misty grinned at their faces. “Didn't expect us? You thought you could leave us behind and watch the relay yourselves? No chance! We weren't going to miss this.”

At the ramen shop, the group had happened to meet one of the torch runners, who traveled with a hat-wearing Meowth. After Ethan's message came, they decided to follow the runner to the relay. They had been closer to the route than Ethan and Ash, so they arrived ahead of them.

Now that they were all there, they watched the ceremony together. It had been underway for some time, and several runners had already carried the flame. The best viewing spots were taken, leaving the group to watch from a distance beside the road.

According to the story, the flame had been taken from Moltres. To most people, that made it sacred and mysterious. To Ethan, Moltres was a Pokémon he could see every day.

Misty smiled throughout the relay. Ethan found that curious. She had seemed troubled for days, but her usual cheer had returned after the Grand Festival party. He did not know what had happened, only that he was glad to see her happy again. Perhaps May had cheered her up. He ought to thank May when he had the chance.

Back in Ever Grande City, Ash and his friends went to check into the dormitory. Ethan parted from them and visited a delivery company. Kennedy's package had arrived: the Meteorite Key Stone. Ethan had eagerly awaited it, wanting to see how it differed from an ordinary Key Stone.

Its appearance disappointed him. Finished and polished, it looked almost exactly like any other Key Stone. No one could have guessed its origin by sight alone. He would have to test it through a Pokémon's Mega Evolution and judge whether it made any difference.

Ethan put it away and went to the police station. The reward promised by the International Police had finally arrived too. A bespectacled senior official, formal and scholarly in manner, presented it instead of Anabel or Looker. After photographs and speeches, Ethan received the money.

Five million, with no tax deducted. It was far more than he had earned for winning either the Indigo League or the Silver Conference, and it gave his bank account a welcome boost.

The award also included a certificate naming him a Special Investigator of the International Police. He now had a measure of official standing. When confronting dangerous organizations in other regions, he could do so with recognized authority.

The Ever Grande Conference had not even begun, and he had already gained a great deal.

When Ethan returned to the competitors' dormitory, Ash had just finished contacting Professor Oak and his mother, Delia. It was only natural to tell the two people most important to him that he had arrived. Ethan hesitated, then sent word to Sabrina, Erika, and the Cerise family too.

They were pleased, but work and distance would keep them away. They would watch on television. Ethan did not mind. If Sabrina and Erika both arrived in person, that might cause more trouble than their absence. The Cerise family would have been no problem, of course.

Afterward, the group went to the Pokémon transfer machine. Ash had decided to challenge the Conference with his current team. Ethan had other plans. His Hoenn Pokémon had grown strong, but they might not be enough to win the title alone. The partners who had traveled with him through two earlier regions deserved a chance to compete too.

He sent several Legendary Pokémon away and brought back Tyranitar, Scizor, Venusaur, Jolteon, and Blastoise. Alongside Dragonite, Eevee, Milotic, Metagross, and Blaziken, they formed his Conference roster.

Crystal Steelix and his enormous red Gyarados remained at Cerise Park. Both were so powerful that Ethan excluded them without hesitation.

Now he had to devote the remaining days to intensive training and getting his team into its best condition. However close he and Ash were, they were competitors. They would train separately until they met in the tournament, keeping their tactics and true strength private.

...

Time raced by. Before they knew it, the preliminary round had arrived.

Unlike the Grand Festival, the Ever Grande Conference had three stages: a preliminary round, preliminary tournaments, and the main competition. More than six hundred Trainers had entered. Reducing that field to the 256 who would reach the main competition promised fierce competition.

The first stage used one-on-one battles, one Pokémon per Trainer. That format made upsets possible. When opponents were closely matched, an unfortunate type matchup could mean immediate elimination.

After ten days of training apart, Ethan and Ash met again. They had stayed in the same dormitory building, yet rarely crossed paths. Ash had seen Misty now and then, but this was his first sight of Ethan in all that time. Ethan had left early and returned late each day, spending nearly every waking hour outside meals at the training grounds. No one knew what he had done there.

Ash was curious about the results, but Ethan did not volunteer any details, and Ash did not ask.

“Be careful,” Ethan said with a smile. “Don't get eliminated before you even reach the main competition.”

“That's not happening!” Ash clenched his fist. “Just watch. I'll make it through!”

“You never know,” Misty teased. “What if your first preliminary opponent is Ethan? You'd be on your way home straight away.”

“Don't jinx me!” Ash sputtered. “Misty, keep your mouth shut!”

The preliminary matches took place not in the large arena on the lake, but in fifteen matching venues around the outer grounds. Each so-called small venue held three thousand spectators. With all fifteen in use at once, the scale was impressive.

Enthusiastic crowds nearly filled them to bursting. The Ever Grande Conference needed no advertising; its name alone drew attention across Hoenn. Even the preliminaries attracted viewers in droves.

Ethan was assigned to Venue A4. Misty accompanied him there while Ash went to his own venue. Most entrants had reached this point by earning eight Badges, though some had qualified through other routes. Their skills varied widely, making the preliminaries unpredictable.

Ethan was in A4's third match. While waiting, he watched two battles that were not especially strong.

The first pitted two young boys against each other. They looked about Ash's age, but seemed fresh from a Trainer school. Their theoretical knowledge was good. Their Pokémon, however, fought by the book, trading rigid attacks and defenses as though reciting a lesson.

The spectators showed little interest. Misty yawned. Perhaps only the boys themselves believed they were engaged in a thrilling, evenly matched contest. After nearly seven minutes, the red-haired boy eked out a win. The audience gave him polite, thin applause. Oblivious, he beamed and waved back.

The second match was more engaging. Two middle-aged men dressed like office workers threw themselves into directing their Pokémon, sweating and gesturing so vigorously they seemed to work harder than their partners. Their Pokémon met at close range, exchanging solid blows. Whatever the Trainers lacked in polish, the fight was livelier than the first.

The older man lost. He was not eliminated yet, however. With so many entrants to reduce to 256, some would need an extra match while others received byes. His strong showing earned him another chance. Only a second defeat would end his run.

Then it was Ethan's turn. He demonstrated what an overwhelming difference in level looked like.

He used Eevee, and that was enough. From the referee's start signal to the flag marking the finish, the match lasted 42 seconds. Ethan's opponent never landed a single hit before Eevee's relentless attacks brought its Pokémon down.

The audience stared for a beat, then erupted. Some spectators had only just opened their bags of chips. They looked up after putting the first bite in their mouths and found the battle already over. This was nothing like the two matches before it.

For many in Hoenn, it was their first look at the handsome man in the Trainer's box, one hand in his pocket and a calm smile on his face.

His opponent looked from its fallen Pokémon to the referee's flag, mouth open. More than a year of work, and it was over almost before it began. The battle would leave a mark on that Trainer.

Ethan left the field to the crowd's cheers and lightly slapped Misty's raised palm.

“That was fast,” she said with a laugh. “I expected an easy win, but I didn't think the other Trainer would last less than a minute.”

“Eevee did well.” Ethan smiled down at it. “It gave the other Pokémon no opening. I should be through to the next round. I wonder how Ash is doing.”

“Want to see? His match was scheduled later. It probably hasn't started.”

“Of course. Let's go.”

On their way to Ash's venue, they spotted Team Rocket's trio again. Since Ethan had begun traveling with Ash, he saw Jessie, James, and Meowth far more often.

This time they were not causing trouble. Dressed as vendors with small boxes hanging from their necks, they sold rice crackers, milk candies, juice, and boxed meals. They knew how to earn money from a crowd. When they were not trying to steal Pikachu, they showed business sense and worked hard, even if Jessie complained. It was a pity they kept choosing the wrong path.

Ethan and Misty reached Ash's venue as he emerged from the competitors' passage. His match was about to start. May and the others were in the stands, with two unfamiliar people beside them.
