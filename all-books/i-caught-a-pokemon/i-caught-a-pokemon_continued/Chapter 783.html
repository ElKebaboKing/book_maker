The stones were part of Rock Tomb, a move commonly used by Rock- and Ground-type Pokémon. Dozens of huge rocks fell around Blaziken and drove into the ground.

Shizuko had underestimated its speed. Blaziken easily dodged the first stones. As more fell, it had less room to move, but then it accelerated sharply. A few leaps carried it out of Rock Tomb's reach. It sprinted forward, jumped high, and slammed into Claydol with Quick Attack.

With the running start behind it, Blaziken reached its top speed. It was almost a streak of lightning. Claydol had no time to react before the hit landed.

Oh no. The thought had barely formed in Shizuko's mind when the attack succeeded. Blaziken was too fast for her to respond, let alone give a command. She had guarded against so much, yet it had found an opening.

Blaziken knocked Claydol to the ground again. This time it was close enough to prevent it from rising. Fire wound around Blaziken's claws, and it struck with Fire Punch.

Claydol shuddered in pain. Ethan ran through the moves it might know and narrowed his eyes. It looked as if Claydol meant to take Blaziken down with it. “Don't give it the chance!” he shouted.

Flames covered Blaziken's right leg. Before Claydol could carry out whatever it intended, Blaze Kick knocked it out.

The referee raised a flag. “Claydol is unable to battle! Blaziken wins!”

Ethan had won three rounds, bringing the match to intermission. He walked to the rest area to the sound of cheering. In the stands, Ash and the others applauded and marveled at him.

“I can't believe Ethan really took down three with one Pokémon,” Ash said. He admired Ethan sincerely, and the sight made him want to battle him again.

“He's frightening,” Morrison agreed. “We're all among the final sixteen, and he beat three Pokémon with just one. If he keeps this up, he could win the whole tournament.”

“Actually, you're both counting wrong,” Brock said. Ash turned to him in confusion.

“Strictly speaking, Blaziken would have taken down four,” Brock explained. “If Shizuko hadn't recalled Houndoom at the last moment, it was only a few exchanges away from losing too.”

Ash and Morrison realized he was right, which made the feat even more startling. Who could take down four opponents at this Conference with a Pokémon that was not even their ace? Only a Trainer far stronger than the rest of them.

In the rest area, Ethan leaned back in a chair and closed his eyes to conserve his energy. He had not been chasing a three- or four-Pokémon sweep. He had long since stopped needing a showy result to prove his strength or draw attention.

He had kept Blaziken out because most of his other Pokémon had already taken a turn in the Conference. His older partners had enjoyed the tournament atmosphere again, and none of the remaining opponents appeared as difficult as Tyson. He could now give his newer Pokémon experience.

More than anything, Blaziken's condition and determination had persuaded him to leave it in. But four rounds had drained much of its stamina and focus. The intermission had also broken its momentum. Ethan did not plan to send it out in the three rounds remaining after the break.

The rest period ended quickly. Before the match resumed, the field wheel spun as usual for the second half. Its pointer stopped on a frost symbol. Ethan and Shizuko would battle on the Ice Field.

Ice Field. Ethan rubbed his chin. Articuno was his only Ice-type Pokémon, so he could not draw on a type-based field advantage with the others. Blaziken might have melted the ice and created an unexpected opportunity, but after four rounds it was in no condition to return.

Who should he send out? Still considering it, he went back to the Trainer's area when called.

Both Trainers took their positions, and the referee waved the flag. Like Ethan, Shizuko did not immediately choose a Poké Ball. Her solemn face suggested that she too was struggling over her lineup.

Their concerns were different. Ethan had several newer Pokémon he could use and was weighing which suited the field best. Shizuko needed one that could withstand his attacks and perhaps win back a point.

Ethan chose first. At the referee's urging, Shizuko took out a Poké Ball too. Two flashes of blue light revealed their choices.

Shizuko had not brought back Houndoom. She sent out Piloswine, apparently leaving Houndoom in its ball to rest longer.

Ethan was not surprised. Piloswine might look heavy and clumsy, but it fought with the same headlong aggression as Primeape. In appearance it resembled a mix of a yak and a wild boar. Its Ice- and Ground-type body was covered in long brown hair, even over its face. Only its snout and two long tusks showed through.

That thick, long coat protected it from the cold. Its legs were short, but its broad, rough hooves kept it from slipping in snow. With hair obscuring its eyes, it often charged about blindly. Its sensitive nose still told it what was nearby. It was highly alert to sound, and its keen sense of smell could find mushrooms buried beneath frozen ground.

That was why Ethan had compared it to Primeape. Both could attack with such relentless force that, once they caught an opponent, they would keep battering it. Ethan also knew that Piloswine had earned much of Shizuko's reputation at this Conference. It might not officially be her ace, but its strength was not far off.

Ethan's choice was Milotic. Fire for the first half, water for the second. That seemed fair enough. More to the point, Milotic had a favorable matchup against Piloswine on both offense and defense. Before the round began, the balance already leaned his way.

Shizuko's expression darkened. After so much thought, she had chosen a Pokémon that happened to be weak against his. Her bad position had become worse. She could only hope Milotic would be easier to handle than Blaziken.

To the crowd's cheers, the battle began. Three losses had not changed Shizuko's style. She ordered Piloswine to attack first.

Piloswine drove its four heavy hooves over the ice and charged with Take Down. Its approach shook the whole field. It could use ranged moves, but trading those attacks with Milotic would favor the Water type. Shizuko preferred to close in and win at short range. If Piloswine could reach Milotic, its weaker close-combat ability ought to leave it vulnerable despite its type advantage.

Ethan understood the plan. It was the best way to beat his Milotic. But reaching it would not be so simple.

Milotic fired a powerful Hydro Pump straight at Piloswine. Shizuko frowned and ordered it to change course. Against Water Gun she might have let Piloswine push through. Hydro Pump was far stronger. Even with its considerable stamina, Piloswine could not endure many hits from a move strong against it.

The detour left it farther from Milotic. Ethan smiled faintly. The blast had only grazed Piloswine's shaggy coat, but it had achieved what he wanted. He waved a hand, and Milotic fired another Hydro Pump.

Shizuko had to redirect Piloswine again. It covered more ground, but it was fast enough to reach Milotic's vicinity in spite of the delays.

Then Milotic slipped behind a large block of ice and vanished. Shizuko had Piloswine charge straight through rather than waste time going around. The solid block exploded into countless shards under its impact. Ice chips filled the air, but Milotic was nowhere to be seen.

What? Where had it gone?

Shizuko's first thought was that it had dug beneath the ice. Milotic was not Steelix, though. It could not use Dig. She scanned the field and found it gliding across the ice at an astonishing speed.

How was that possible? Only two or three seconds had passed since it hid behind the block, yet it had traveled more than twenty meters. Was it as fast as Blaziken?

Looking more closely, Shizuko saw a shallow sheet of water beneath Milotic's body. There was not much of it, but on the smooth ice it let Milotic move almost as though it were swimming. When had it put the water there?

The answer came to her at once: those two Hydro Pumps. So that was it. Ethan had planned this from the beginning.

Across the field, Ethan saw understanding dawn on her face and smiled.

The Ice Field was different from the Rock and Grass Fields. Water spilled across it would not soak into the ground, nor would it freeze immediately. For a while it stayed on the surface, making a broad, slippery film.

Most Pokémon would struggle to move on wetter ice. A Water type could use it to move smoothly, like a fish in water. If Ethan had no Ice-type Pokémon to exploit the field, he would turn it into a Water Field instead.

Hydro Pump's volume had made the plan possible. Water Gun would not have spread enough water. Piloswine might also have forced its way through the weaker attack, ruining the setup. Each part of the plan supported the next. That was why Ethan had chosen Hydro Pump.
