Just as Ethan had thought, the girl was Casey. What surprised him was how little ground she had covered since they parted the previous afternoon.

It was probably her first time traveling alone. She must have wanted to catch every Pokémon she encountered and spent most of her time trying.

"I'm Ethan Vale, and I'm a Trainer too," he said. "This is Sabrina, my traveling companion."

Sabrina folded her arms and lifted her head a little haughtily. Her long, straight black hair stirred in the breeze, but she made no move to greet Casey.

"Ethan Vale?" Casey frowned. The name sounded familiar. Studying his face, she felt sure she had seen him somewhere before. Then she cried out, "I remember! You're the Indigo League champion! I watched a replay of your final match!"

Her voice was so loud that Ethan had to cover one ear.

"There's no need to be that surprised." He shook his head. "Every League crowns a champion each year. Add all the regions together, and there must be at least a dozen new champions annually. This year I happened to be one of them."

Inside, he was rather pleased with himself. Now that's how a champion stays humble. Even when someone recognizes you, you keep your modesty!

"You're nothing like the others!" Casey's eyes shone. "You won the whole tournament without losing a single match! My dad, my mom, my grandpa, my grandma, and our other relatives all watched you battle. They like you too!"

That reminded her of something. "This is such a great chance. Please battle me!"

"Battle you?" Ethan looked her over. She knew he had won a championship and still wanted to challenge him. Casey was so new to traveling that she had little sense of other Trainers' strength and no idea what it meant to be afraid.

Then again, if Ash encountered a champion he had seen on television while out in the wild, he would probably be unable to resist challenging them too.

But Casey had only just set out, and Ethan's Pokémon...

He considered his options. Charizard, Gengar, Gardevoir, and Gyarados would be far too much for her. Even against Jolteon, Crystal Onix, Scyther, or Venusaur, she had no real chance.

Looking into her hopeful eyes, he could not bring himself to refuse.

"All right. Let's have a battle."

"Yes!"

Delighted, Casey ran back to make plenty of room between them. She bowed politely. "This is my first battle against another Trainer. I hope you'll teach me a lot!"

"It's just a friendly match, not a tournament. You don't have to be so formal." Ethan smiled helplessly, still wondering which Pokémon would give her a reasonably even contest without leaving her discouraged.

At last, he took out Dratini's Poké Ball.

Casey spoke to the Chikorita by her feet. "Go, Chikorita! Our opponent is strong, so let's open with a fastball!"

Chikorita nodded and trotted into the middle of the clearing.

"Dratini, I'm counting on you!"

Ethan opened the Poké Ball. Blue light flashed, and a small, snake-like Pokémon appeared opposite Chikorita.

"Wow, a Dratini! I've never seen one before. It's so cute!"

Casey stared at it in disbelief. New Trainer or not, she knew how rare and precious a Dratini was. She had never imagined Ethan would have one. She pulled out her Pokédex and pointed it at Dratini.

"Beep! Dratini, the Dragon Pokémon. A Dragon type. This serpentine Pokémon is covered in pale blue scales. It has purple eyes, white fins on either side of its head, and a large, round white nose. Dratini usually live near swift-flowing water, such as waterfalls, and grow by repeatedly shedding their skin."

When Casey put her Pokédex away, Ethan smiled and explained the rules. "One Pokémon each. No special restrictions. The first Trainer to make the other Pokémon unable to battle wins."

"Got it!" Casey nodded eagerly.

Her innocent expression made Ethan sigh inwardly. She was almost certain to lose. Dragon-type attacks would do ordinary damage to a Grass type, while Chikorita's Grass-type attacks would deal only half damage to Dratini. The matchup was against her from the start.

Ordinarily, Ethan would not exploit a type advantage against a novice. But Dratini was the only relatively weak Pokémon on his team. Sending it out was the best option he had, and Casey did not yet have enough experience to recognize the disadvantage.

Sabrina stepped to the side to act as referee. Once both Pokémon were ready, she said flatly, "Begin."

Casey snapped into action, swinging her baseball bat. "Chikorita, use Razor Leaf!"

Even at half strength, Ethan had no intention of taking the attack head-on. "Dratini, fly up!"

Without any visible effort, Dratini rose into the air and avoided the sharp leaves. The pearl-like growth on its head let it fly as freely as a bird despite having no wings.

"Use Twister!"

Dratini opened its mouth. A great whirlwind sprang up in the clearing, growing larger as it swept toward Chikorita.

"Quick, dodge!" Casey shouted.

Chikorita was not especially fast, and the Twister moved too quickly. In a blink, the little Pokémon was caught inside. It spun around and around like a coin trapped in a washing machine. When the wind died, Chikorita fell hard to the ground with a thud, stars circling over its head.
