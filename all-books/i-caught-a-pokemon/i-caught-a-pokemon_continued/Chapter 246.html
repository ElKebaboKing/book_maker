From a distance, Rind Island looked covered in forest. Cliffs of varying heights ringed its shores, forming a natural barrier between its interior and the open sea. A low mountain rose in the middle.

Tracey had been right: it appeared to be uninhabited. At least from outside, Ethan could see no sign of human activity.

The Magikarp did not swim past the island. They poured into a river mouth and headed inland. Ethan looked around and spotted the submarine they had seen earlier, moored in a sheltered bay. Its hatch was closed and it sat motionless. The old man must have gone into the island after the Magikarp.

"What now?" Ash asked.

"What else?" As Lapras approached the shore, Ethan was the first to jump off. "We've come this far. Let's go in and see."

Once everyone had landed, Ash recalled Lapras. Sabrina brought Charizard down and joined Ethan. The group set out on foot along the riverbank.

The river was packed with Magikarp as far as they could see. The fish fought their way upstream, their red bodies filling the water in a spectacular procession.

"Wow. You don't see a sight like this every day," Misty said over the constant splashing.

Ethan nodded. It reminded him of stories from his old home about fish returning from the sea each year to lay their eggs farther inland. Perhaps the Pokémon here had a similar habit. He took out his phone and recorded the scene.

While they watched the river, the old man's voice came from behind them. "What are you young people standing around for?"

They turned. The submarine's pilot was approaching with more than a dozen boxes and bags piled on his back. They had been following his trail, yet somehow he had come up behind them. His luggage stood higher than he did. How could such a small old man carry it all and still move so easily?

Seeing them stare, he began distributing the boxes among them without asking. In a moment, even Sabrina and Pikachu had a small box apiece.

Ethan and the others were caught off guard. The stranger had evidently decided they were free porters.

"Hey, sir, what are you doing?" Ash asked, holding a large box in his arms.

The old man did not answer. Now carrying only a small backpack, he strolled along the river toward the forest. After a few steps, he noticed they had not followed and beckoned them on.

"Come along. Don't just stand there!"

Come along to where? They exchanged baffled looks, then followed him.

"Wait a minute! Will you tell us what you're doing?" Ash called.

The old man did not even slow down. Misty looked down at her box and sighed helplessly. "What is going on?"

"Let's see where he's taking us," Ethan said. "We'll probably understand when we get there."

He glanced at Sabrina. She still held her box and looked calm. In a low voice, he asked, "You're not angry, are you?"

She shook her head. In the past, she would have used her psychic powers to send a stranger this rude flying through the air. Now such a small imposition hardly mattered to her.

They continued beside the river, deeper into the forest. The ground rose from a gentle slope to a steep climb. They were ascending the mountain they had seen from the sea.

The change in height made the current stronger, yet the Magikarp kept swimming against it toward higher ground. Ethan was beginning to understand. If they were not going to spawn, something else must be drawing them.

After several more minutes, Misty's curiosity overcame her. She hurried up beside the old man. "Who are you, sir?"

He looked at her friendly face and smiled. "I'm Quincy T. Quackenpoker, a Pokémon Watcher."

A Pokémon Watcher? The others all looked at Tracey. He shook his head. Despite sharing the profession, he had never heard Quackenpoker's name.

Misty lifted the box in her hands. "What's in all these?"

"Isn't it obvious? Equipment for observing Magikarp!"

"Equipment to observe Magikarp?"

"Of course." Perhaps Misty reminded him of his granddaughter, for he explained a little more. "I prepared every piece especially for watching these Magikarp."

Tracey's interest flared. He hurried to the river's edge and pulled out his sketchbook. "So I was right! These Magikarp must have some special ability or behavior. I'll observe them too."

He had stepped too close. A Magikarp leaped from the water, startling him and splashing him in the face. His enthusiasm cooled at once.

"Foolish boy," the old man snorted. "Those are ordinary Magikarp. There's nothing worth observing about them."

"Huh?" Tracey asked, ignoring the water on his clothes. "Didn't you just say all this equipment was for Magikarp? If they're ordinary, why bring it?"
