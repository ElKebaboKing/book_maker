“I'm coming with you!”

Phoebe's face tightened when Ethan said he would follow Team Magma alone. She immediately offered to accompany him. Ethan had other plans.

“Stay and help Misty put out the fires. She has Water-type Pokémon, but too few to handle a blaze this size by herself.”

Phoebe looked around. The fire was at its fiercest. Her grandparents' house and the area around the stone shrine were both engulfed. If they let it spread, flames might consume the whole summit. Mt. Pyre would become a mountain of fire in more than name.

The cliffs around the summit kept it somewhat isolated, so the blaze could not easily travel downhill. A strong wind might still carry embers over the edge and start a much larger fire below. They could not wait.

Phoebe sighed. “All right. But be careful. Team Magma isn't easy to deal with. Keep them in sight. Once we put the fires out, we'll come and join you. Then we'll face them together.”

She had seen enough of Ethan's Pokémon to know how capable they were. Team Magma had gained no advantage against him, and several times he had broken a stalemate. His strength might be no less than hers. That made it easier for her to accept his decision to go alone.

Misty looked at him. Ethan met her gaze. “I'm leaving this to you.”

“I understand!” She drew herself up. “Don't worry. You can go in peace!”

Ethan stared at her. Why did that sound like something to say to a dying man?

He beckoned Dragonite over and climbed onto its back. Then he recalled his other Pokémon. Under Misty's and Phoebe's eyes, Dragonite took off and plunged into the fog beyond the summit.

Misty drew a deep breath and faced the flames. “All right. Let's get started.”

...

Visibility was almost nothing for several dozen seconds. Then Dragonite broke through the thick fog into clear air, and Ethan's view opened.

Blue sky stretched above him, the sea lay far ahead, and green forest covered the ground below. No flames burned here. No stubborn fog obscured the scenery.

Looking down, he spotted Team Magma hurrying down Mt. Pyre's middle slopes.

He patted Dragonite's neck. “Fly higher. Follow them from a distance, and don't let them see us.”

Dragonite climbed into the clouds and hid high above them.

Team Magma noticed nothing. Since leaving the summit, Maxie had been too delighted by the operation's success to think calmly. Tabitha had rejoined the group carrying the Red Orb. They really had it. Once they found Groudon, their great dream could be realized. The thought made Maxie's blood race.

As for where Groudon was, Team Magma had recently uncovered important clues, much as Team Aqua had with Kyogre. They had a rough idea of its hiding place. Without a way to control it, they had not dared approach. The orb changed that. It was time to act.

At the foot of the mountain, the whole group boarded jeeps hidden among the trees. The convoy drove northeast, presumably toward a base.

Maxie had arranged precautions against anyone following. Two vehicles brought up the rear, and the group used other measures to check for a tail. From above, Ethan saw it all. He kept his eyes fixed on Maxie's jeep and ignored the distractions.

The vehicles raced along the ground while Dragonite flew through the clouds.

More than two hours later, the convoy entered another rugged mountain area close to the coast. The vehicles slowed. Ethan directed Dragonite down and landed on a small hill some distance away.

He recalled Dragonite, sent out Gardevoir and Scizor, and called Gengar from his shadow. He watched the convoy carefully. None of Team Magma's members seemed to have spotted him.

The jeeps disappeared into a dark tunnel.

Ethan did not rush after them. “That must lead to a Team Magma base,” he told Gengar. “They'll have the tunnel heavily guarded. Going straight in would alert them. Slip inside and see whether there's a hidden entrance nearby.”

“Leave it to me.”

Gengar was used to the troublesome, covert jobs Ethan gave it. It sank into the ground and headed toward the tunnel.

Ethan sent their location to Anabel and Phoebe, then examined the surrounding terrain. On the map, he was not far from Lilycove City. After all that travel, he had circled back toward it.

The tunnel ran into a low mountain. Team Magma must have hollowed out much of its interior. Dense trees surrounded it, but the forest's peaceful appearance was misleading. Cameras and patrols were likely hidden among the foliage. A few more steps could bring him into view.

This won't be easy.

He studied the mountain from one end to the other. On the far side, a cliff dropped to the sea. Forest concealed the approach, the mountain itself made a solid fortress, and water protected the rear. It was an excellent place to defend. Could this be Team Magma's main base?

Ethan began moving carefully toward the coastal cliffs. A base like this might have a secret exit below them, and such an exit would probably be less heavily guarded than the front tunnel.

He left a marker for Gengar, then traveled through the trees with Gardevoir and Scizor. To avoid alarming Team Magma, he took a wide, indirect route.

The forest was thick. Scizor went first, cutting vines Ethan could not pass with its pincers. That was why he had brought it. Whenever they encountered a gully or a rise too difficult to climb, Gardevoir carried Ethan across with Teleport. The detour took little time.

At the cliff's edge, Ethan looked down. At least thirty meters separated him from the water. Waves struck the rock face and withdrew, exposing hard reefs of many sizes.

Something rustled nearby. Gardevoir and Scizor turned toward it, ready for a fight. Gengar emerged from the forest, following the marker Ethan had left.

It shook its head before Ethan could ask. “It is their base. But I found only one entrance, and it's guarded closely. I can get in, but I can't take you through without being noticed.”

Ethan took the news calmly. Still scanning the cliff for anything that resembled an opening, he sent Gengar down to search.

Gengar floated along the rock face and soon returned. “I went around it. There's no entrance above the waterline that I could find.”

That surprised Ethan, though it also made sense. Team Magma had built a secure base and left no easy way in.

Only one option seemed to remain. “Then we'll have to attack the front. A base like this probably has a self-destruct system. Go back, find anything they could use to destroy it, and disable it before we start. I don't want a surprise halfway through.”

“What a life,” Gengar sighed. “You're the boss, so you give an order and I run around until my legs give out. Being a Pokémon is hard.”

It complained, but had already turned toward the base.

“That one...” Ethan shook his head with a smile. “Come on,” he told Gardevoir and Scizor. “We're going back. This time we hit the front with everything we have.”

...

Inside Team Magma's surveillance room, two members watched the monitors covering the tunnel approach.

This was the organization's main base, and its guards were well trained. They did not slack off. Word had just arrived that the operation was a success, but they had also been ordered to watch for anyone following the convoy. Every unusual movement mattered.

They had seen nothing for a long time. Neither let his attention wander.

Then one of them leaned toward a screen. “Hey. Is that a person?”

His partner looked. A figure was moving slowly toward the tunnel entrance. It was too far away to make out. As it came closer, they saw it was no human being but a creature neither recognized.

“What is that? A Pokémon?”

“Must be. I've never seen one like it.”

The strange Pokémon was vaguely human-shaped. Its body was orange, with long, tube-like legs and no visible feet. A blue-green mask seemed to cover its face, leaving only its eyes clear. Its arms looked like tentacles twisted together.

“That's bizarre. Is it wild? Did somebody send it?”

They swung the camera to check behind it. All they saw was dense forest.

“Let's have the entrance guards take a look.” The member picked up a radio and reported the approaching Pokémon.

“A strange Pokémon near the entrance? Understood. We'll check it out.”

Several guards in the tunnel exchanged weary looks after their colleague put down the radio.

“How many times is that now?”

“The surveillance room makes a fuss over every Pokémon. They sit in the air-conditioning while we have to keep walking back and forth.”

“Bet it's just another one that wandered in by accident.”

They complained, but sent several members toward the exit. This happened every few days. Usually, they simply drove away a wild Pokémon that had strayed too close. The surveillance room's ‘strange’ one was probably just a rare species.

The guards walked out at an easy pace and looked into the distance. The monitors had been right: a Pokémon none of them had ever seen was floating slowly toward the entrance.
