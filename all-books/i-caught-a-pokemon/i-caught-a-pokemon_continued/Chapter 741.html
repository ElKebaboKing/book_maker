“If you're friends, why not join us for some muscle training?” The man grinned at Ethan and Misty again. “Ah, I forgot to introduce myself. I'm Rocky. I started this training ground and run it, and I'm the strongest Trainer on the island. If you want to be as strong as I am and reach the heights of Pokémon battling, you have to train your muscles without giving up!”

Rocky was clearly confident in his abilities. Perhaps that was how he had gathered such a crowd.

But the strongest Trainer on this island? Its only settlement was a small town, with few residents and probably fewer Trainers. Visitors might rarely stay here at all. Ethan could not imagine proudly introducing himself with such a limited title.

He would not have mentioned it even if it were true of him. That was not because winning two regional tournaments had made him look down on ordinary Trainers. There were simply too many little islands and towns in the world. If each had someone claiming to be its strongest Trainer, the claim meant very little.

And Rocky was inviting others to become as strong as he was. Even Ethan would not say something like that.

“Sorry,” Ethan said, shaking his head. “Traveling over mountains and across seas tires me out enough. I don't want to spend more energy on exercises like these. Besides, I think sound tactics and developing your Pokémon are the only way to reach the top of Pokémon battling.”

It was the opposite of Rocky's view. Ethan normally had the manners not to say such a thing to a man's face. But Rocky had apparently drawn Bea into basic exercises without understanding how strong she already was. He was wasting her time while insisting that his own narrow approach was the answer. His hearty smile did not make Ethan like him any better.

Rocky frowned. “Oh?” He looked Ethan up and down. “You've got it completely wrong. An excellent physique is crucial to Pokémon battling. It can even be the key to victory.”

“There's no point arguing,” Ethan said with a shrug. “I can't change your mind, and you can't change mine.”

Rocky had evidently not expected to disagree so sharply with a visitor. “You come to Muscle Island and say that? You don't know our traditions. Fine. If you think your method is better, let's settle it the way Trainers do. We'll battle and see which approach works.”

“A battle? Well...” Ethan hesitated.

Rocky took his hesitation for fear. “What, don't you dare? Was all that talk? Maybe you're simply afraid of hard work.”

“Ordinarily, I'd battle you ten times if you liked,” Ethan said with a sigh. “My Pokémon are in an unusual situation right now, though. This isn't a good time.”

It was no excuse. He had just fought Primal Groudon and Primal Kyogre and had not adjusted his lineup. The seven Legendary Pokémon with him—Deoxys, the black Rayquaza, Zapdos, Articuno, Moltres, Suicune, and Raikou—were hardly fair choices against Rocky.

His other Pokémon were Gardevoir, Gengar, Dragonite, and two reserves he had kept with him: his giant red Gyarados and Crystal Steelix. Those twelve were all he currently carried. Which one could he use without making the battle feel like bullying?

If he had to name the weakest, it would be Dragonite. Even then, he feared Rocky might come away from a battle with it permanently shaken.

The people exercising nearby heard the exchange and gathered around. Rocky explained what the visitor had said. At once the crowd united in outrage at Ethan's strange ideas.

“Hey, kid! You're good-looking, but what a weakling!”

“Only someone strong in body can reach the top of Pokémon battling!”

“That's right! Hard muscle training makes you the strongest!”

“Female Machamp rules!”

Ethan sighed at the jeers. He had had enough of these people, with muscles where their brains should be. They were rude and would not listen. Now he had little choice but to battle. He had intended to give Rocky some dignity, as a visitor merely passing through. That courtesy was losing its appeal.

Then let thunder break through the darkness.

He reached for Gardevoir's Poké Ball, ready to teach them a lesson. Before he could draw it, Bea stepped in front of him.

They had become friends after sheltering together from the rain in a cave on Dewford Island. Bea might know little of social niceties, but she would not stand aside while a friend faced a hostile crowd. Besides, she knew Ethan had objected to Rocky's training because of her. She could not leave him to handle the trouble alone.

She looked Ethan in the eye. “I'll take this battle for you.”

She had seen him hesitate and assumed something was wrong with his Pokémon. She knew from watching his Gym battle on Dewford Island that he was strong and not afraid to fight. Why else would he hold back? Perhaps his Pokémon had been badly injured and he had not had time to treat them. It was the only explanation she could think of.

Ethan and Rocky both turned to her. Ethan was surprised that she had stepped forward. Rocky was surprised for another reason: this pretty, short-haired girl was a Fighting-type specialist like him, yet she was taking the side of someone he considered an outsider to their training.

He studied her for a moment, then glanced pointedly at Ethan. “Anyone can battle me. But I wouldn't hide behind a woman.”

The taunt did not trouble Ethan. Misty was furious on his behalf. She knew why he had hesitated: his Pokémon were too strong. Any one of his Legendary Pokémon would cause a sensation merely by appearing, and Rocky had mistaken his restraint for cowardice.

She glared at Rocky. “Ethan, do it. Teach him a lesson!”

Ethan reached for a Poké Ball. At this point, he could only show them the difference between their abilities. His movement convinced Bea even more firmly that something was wrong with his Pokémon.

“No. Let me handle it,” she insisted.

“It's all right. I can—”

“Leave it to me.”

Watching them argue over who would face him, Rocky felt they were looking down on him. He held up a hand. “Enough. If both of you want to fight, we'll make it a Double Battle!”

Ethan and Bea exchanged a glance and nodded, as though they had reached an agreement without words.

“All right,” Ethan said. “Two against two.”

The onlookers quickly cleared a space, abandoning their workouts to watch. Ethan and Bea took one side. Rocky chose a woman in her twenties with a red band tied around her head as his partner, and they went to the other.

“Go on!”

“Rocky, beat that rude man!”

“Show him what we can do!”

“Let him feel the power of muscles!”

The crowd shouted for Rocky. Misty answered from among them. She was only one person, but she cupped her hands around her mouth and called with all her strength.

“Go, Ethan!”

Once everyone was in place, Rocky set the rules. “We're only here to decide who wins. No need for anything complicated. Each Trainer sends out one Pokémon. Defeat both opposing Pokémon, and you win.”

Ethan and Bea nodded. A man acting as referee blew his whistle, and all four Trainers threw their Poké Balls. Blue light flashed, revealing four Pokémon in the clearing.

Rocky's choice surprised Ethan. Despite all his talk of muscles, he had sent out a Walrein rather than a Fighting-type. His partner fit Ethan's expectations better: she had a Hitmonchan.

For all the time they spent training their bodies, this group did not restrict themselves to Fighting-type Pokémon.

Bea sent out her signature Machamp. She was taking the match seriously. Ethan chose Gardevoir. In an ordinary battle, both Pokémon would be among their Trainers' strongest partners.

With all four ready, the referee signaled the start.

Before the battle, Ethan had wondered whether this group's physical training gave them some unusual way to fight. He soon found he had imagined too much. Rocky and his partner stood in place like any other Trainers. They neither moved with their Pokémon nor used their own bodies for anything. They merely looked more energetic.

Had all that tireless exercise accomplished nothing more than that? Ethan wondered, but the battle was underway.

At the four Trainers' commands, their Pokémon engaged. Bea and the other woman naturally matched their Fighting-types against each other. Both ordered them forward to settle the fight at close range. Ethan and Rocky took a more direct approach: their Pokémon attacked from where they stood.

Machamp ran toward the center of the field, swinging all four arms at Hitmonchan. Hitmonchan answered with both gloved fists. Just before their punches met, something hissed past them.

Gardevoir's invisible psychic force collided with Walrein's thick stream of water beside the fighters. The water burst into spray and drenched them both.
