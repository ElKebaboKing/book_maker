The Blizzard grew stronger, with no sign of easing. The temperature inside the battlefield fell and kept falling. Snow soon lay across the floor in a layer that was neither thin nor especially deep. Even Noland on the opposite platform was lost from sight.

Under Gengar's protection, May breathed into her hands. She pulled the towel tighter around herself and moved a few steps closer to Ethan, but she could still feel the cold.

She glanced at his clothes and wondered whether she should offer him the towel. Before she could speak, both Articuno dived out of the storm and returned to the battlefield below.

The Blizzard continued. With the birds closer, however, the shock waves of their attacks and the gusts from their wings scattered some of the snow around them. It was not their intention, but the movement let the spectators follow them again.

Ethan could see Noland across the field too. He had to admire the man. Despite the cold and with no Pokémon shielding him, Noland stood straight as ever, seemingly unconcerned by the storm.

People born in this world really were hardier than someone from Earth, Ethan thought. Then he remembered how much longer he could now stay underwater than an ordinary person. Perhaps this world was gradually changing him too.

He returned his attention to the battle.

Noland's Articuno had picked up more wounds inside the Blizzard. Ethan's bird had not escaped unharmed either. Fresh scratches marked its body. The two had clearly fought hard while hidden.

Most of the battle had taken place out of sight, but everyone could tell that the deciding moment was approaching.

With their Pokémon visible again, Ethan and Noland both ordered an all-out attack. Standing beside Ethan, May could hear his commands and see his decisions more clearly. She concentrated, trying to imagine how he saw the fight.

Noland noticed her beside him. He glanced over and said nothing, just as Ethan had expected. Perhaps he had no attention to spare. His Articuno was panting; Ethan's still had more energy. Noland knew he was at a disadvantage and had to make a final push.

“Articuno, use Ice Beam!” he called.

Using an Ice-type move against another Articuno was hardly ideal. But Steel Wing and the few other moves of different types his Articuno knew had not achieved much. Ice attacks were what it did best. Ethan's bird had no obvious way around their shared typing either.

That was why Noland had not panicked, despite being behind. Still, with his Articuno tiring, the next exchange might decide the match.

Ethan gave a faint smile and called for Ice Beam too.

Two identical blue-white streams of energy shot from the birds' beaks, racing toward each other.

Boom!

The beams collided and burst into brilliant light. The explosion drove the Blizzard outward, leaving a round pocket of clear air. Its shock wave crossed the Trainer platforms, struck Ash and the others in the stands, and kept spreading before finally weakening.

May cried out. The wind whipped her hair and almost took her bandanna. She ducked behind Ethan to keep her footing. The force of the Legendary Pokémon's attack was frightening.

The two Ice Beams were still pressing against each other. Then Ethan's Articuno gained ground. Its beam broke through Noland's and struck his bird.

Ash stared. “Why did Brother Ethan's Articuno win when they used the same move?”

Max and Scott had no answer. Brock did. “Because of its collar.”

They looked at the ornament around Ethan's Articuno's neck.

“What does it do?” Ash asked.

“If I'm right, it's made of Never-Melt Ice,” Brock said. “Wearing it greatly strengthens Ice-type moves. That's why Ethan's Articuno won the exchange.”

Ash frowned. “Isn't that cheating? If it's wearing something like that, how is this fair?”

“I wouldn't say that,” Brock replied. “Farfetch'd has its leek, Marowak its bone, Machoke its gold belt, and Grovyle the twig it carries in its mouth. Those are all items too. The Pokémon League allows them, as long as they aren't excessive.”

Ash opened his mouth. A twig or belt seemed more like decoration than something that made a move stronger. Then he considered Farfetch'd without its leek, or Marowak without its bone. Both would lose a great deal of fighting ability. Perhaps Never-Melt Ice was not so different. Even so, he felt Ethan had gained an unfair edge.

Scott saw his expression. “Look at it another way. Ethan obtained that Never-Melt Ice through his own ability. Why should using something he earned be unfair?”

Ash still looked ready to argue, but Scott went on. “In the end, a battle is decided by its result. A Trainer might win a Conference match through extraordinary luck, or because an opponent withdraws. Either way, a win is a win. People remember the winner.”

That was more complicated than Ash was ready to accept. Scott did not expect him to agree immediately. He only wanted Ash to understand that Pokémon battles were not always as simple as they seemed.

On the field, Noland's Articuno cried out and fell toward the ground. Ice-type attacks dealt only half damage to an Ice-type Pokémon, but Articuno's power was formidable, and the Never-Melt Ice had strengthened the beam. Even reduced, the impact hurt.

Before Noland's bird could rise, Ethan's swooped down. It landed on top of its opponent and raked it with both claws.

Perhaps fighting fierce Legendary Pokémon had awakened something wild in Ethan's Articuno. It could use powerful moves, yet it also relished a raw physical struggle.

Noland's Articuno had never faced an attack like this. Weaker Pokémon usually yielded to its command of ice. Only an opponent of equal standing could force it into such a position.

It tried to claw back, but pinned underneath the other bird, it could do little. Feathers scattered across the ground. Its long tail, once so graceful, stood out in disarray. The elegant Legendary Pokémon looked more like a bedraggled hen.

Almost by instinct, it used Sheer Cold in a desperate counterattack. Ethan's Articuno spread its wings and answered with an even stronger Sheer Cold.

The two bursts of freezing power erupted together. Ice seized everything around them and raced outward, forming sharp spines like the petals of a giant frozen flower.

“Watch out!” Gengar cried. It raised a barrier around Ethan and May. Across the field, Noland released another Pokémon to shield himself.

The ice kept reaching toward the spectator area. Pikachu blasted the advancing spines apart with electricity before they struck Ash and the others.

When the freezing power finally stopped spreading, the battlefield had become a world of glistening crystals and falling snow. At its center, where the two Articuno lay, a solid mass of ice hid everything from view.

Crack. Crack.

The great ice flower shattered. An Articuno wearing a necklace burst free and climbed into the air. It spread its wings and called triumphantly to the sky.

The other Articuno lay motionless on the ground.

Was it over? Sergio hurried in from wherever he had taken shelter. One look at Noland's bird was enough.

“Articuno is unable to battle! Mr. Vale wins this challenge!”

Noland stood still for a moment, then shook his head. He had lost completely. Ethan's Articuno held the advantage in both strength and experience. There was nothing to dispute.

With the fight over, Noland suddenly felt the cold. He began shivering. He told Sergio to get emergency care for Articuno, then clambered over the icy ridges toward Ethan.

May promptly handed him her towel. He wrapped it around himself several times, stamped his feet, and let out a long breath.

“I can feel my toes again.” He looked up at the Articuno still circling overhead, then at Ethan. “Your ordinary Pokémon are strong, but even your Legendary Pokémon can fight like that. I don't know how you train them.”

Ethan gave the same answer he always did: plenty of experience in battle.

His Articuno landed beside him. At about 1.7 meters tall, it stood only a head shorter than Ethan. It began arranging its feathers as though it had not just fought a fierce match.

Ethan stroked its still-cold body. Then he, May, and Noland headed back toward the stands.

On the way, Ethan asked May quietly, “Well? Did you learn anything?”

May did not know what to say. She had barely been able to see the battle. Ethan's commands had been brief, yet his Articuno understood each one. The match was on a different level from the battles she usually knew.

Ethan noticed her discomfort and patted her head. The moment his hand touched her hair, he realized what he had done.

He had grown so used to doing that with Misty that he had mistaken May for her. Old habits were hard to break.

He lowered his hand without showing his surprise. “It's all right if nothing comes to mind yet. These things take time. After we've traveled together longer, you'll gradually start to see more.”

“I… I understand.”

He had spoken naturally, but May lowered her head, blushing. His hand had rested on her hair for only a moment. Even so, her cheeks had turned pink.
