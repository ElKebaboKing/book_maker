Leaving the forest that held the Battle Tower behind, Ethan and May traveled out of eastern Kanto. Several days on the road were uneventful without being dull, and by the time they reached the central part of the region, both were covered in travel dust.

Compared with the rural landscapes elsewhere, this area was far more prosperous. Tall buildings, bridges, and parks were everywhere. Saffron, Vermilion, Celadon—Kanto's major cities were concentrated here.

They did not pass through the cluster of cities, though. Instead, they turned north. After more than half a day's walk, around their lunch break on the fourth day, they arrived in a place called Mulberry City.

Mulberry was called a town in the directions they had heard, but it was large enough to feel like a small city. Streets and rivers crisscrossed it, high-rises stood above the crowds, and people streamed past in every direction.

It had not been on their route. A few Trainers who had challenged Ethan in the wild had told them that a Pokémon Contest would soon be held here, so they had made the detour.

With the Grand Festival approaching, fewer and fewer towns still had Contests scheduled. That meant little to Coordinators who had collected five Ribbons years ago. For May, who had begun collecting Kanto Ribbons partway through her journey, and for newcomers or Coordinators who had lost several times, the deadline was pressing.

At the Pokémon Center, Nurse Joy told them that the upcoming Mulberry Contest was the last remaining Contest where a Coordinator could earn a Ribbon before the Grand Festival. May could only be grateful they had come.

She patted her still-developing chest and let out a long breath. “I'm so glad we got here early. If we'd been two days later, I really would have missed the Grand Festival!”

“You would have…”

Once that final opportunity passed, May would have to wait until the following year to challenge the Kanto Grand Festival. She would surely be stronger and better prepared by then, but she would no longer even be in Kanto. She would miss a chance to grow at a crucial point in her journey, and returning a year later would not feel the same.

After a short rest at the Pokémon Center, they went straight to the Contest Hall. It stood in the middle of Mulberry, a familiar half-dome of blue and white that looked unremarkable beside other venues.

The crowd outside was another matter. Perhaps the city's size and population helped account for it, but there were nearly a hundred Coordinators and their Pokémon gathered there. Apart from major venues such as the Saffron Contest Hall, Ethan had never seen so many entrants in one place.

That was only part of the reason. Coordinators who, like May, still needed one more Ribbon had crossed the length of Kanto to enter. With one glance around the crowd, May spotted several opponents she had met before.

Everyone who had yet to collect five Ribbons wanted one last chance to reach the Grand Festival. However many of them entered, only one would leave with the Ribbon.

“There really are a lot of people…” May said.

They worked their way through the crowd, registered at the service desk, and took a look at the stage so May could get familiar with it. Then they left the hall.

They had not gone far when they saw that persistent figure again: Harley.

May muttered, “Seriously, why is he everywhere we go?”

As if he had sensed her gaze, Harley spotted them at once. This time, instead of avoiding them, he strode openly up to the pair.

“Well, it's been a long time, you two.” His pleasant mask was gone. He narrowed his eyes at May and spoke with open mockery. “If you're here, collecting Ribbons can't have gone well. What, after all this time, you still haven't got five?”

Harley traveled alone and could devote himself to entering Contests wherever he found them. May had fewer opportunities and competed less often.

“What's it to you?” She rolled her eyes. “You're here too, aren't you? That puts us in the same position.”

After spending so much time with Misty, May was beginning to sound a little like her.

“The same position?” Harley gave a smug smile and pulled open one side of his coat. Five glittering Ribbons hung inside. “I collected mine ages ago. I only came today to watch you make a fool of yourself!”

How he had been so certain May would enter the Mulberry Contest was anyone's guess. Coming here simply to gloat, though, was entirely in character.

May was about to answer when Ethan stepped forward. Harley recoiled as if he'd been startled by an arrow and took a step back.

“W-what do you want?”

He hated reacting that way in front of a crowd. It made him look timid. But his earlier defeat had left him so frightened of Ethan that he feared the man might start a fight at the slightest provocation.

Ethan merely gave him a cool glance. Without saying a word, he led May away.

Harley froze, then flushed red. Ethan had dismissed him without even bothering to respond. He opened his mouth several times, but anger did not make him brave enough to call after them. All he could do was clench his fists and watch Ethan's back recede.

He dared to provoke May because he knew her temperament. Outside a Contest battle, she would not do anything to him. Ethan was different. When Ethan decided to act, he really acted.

That lack of restraint, combined with Ethan's strength, was why Harley could only swallow his anger. He had deceived May time after time, but after his crushing defeat and humiliating departure from their last battle, he could not manage a single boast in Ethan's presence.

That did not mean he intended to leave things alone. Their feud had become irreconcilable. Apart from Contests, finding some way to get back at Ethan occupied Harley's thoughts more than anything else.

Then his eyes happened to settle on another group he could not stand.

It was Team Rocket—the three who had posed as Jessibella and her attendants at the Saffron Contest and tricked him.

After James and Meowth had conned Harley out of his Pokémon, he had chased them a long way before finally getting both Pokémon back. He had given all three a beating for it, too.

The encounter had taught him who they were. Jessie, James, and Meowth belonged to Team Rocket, a criminal organization. They habitually opposed Ash and were hostile toward his friends. That included May and Ethan.

In that case… Harley's eyes shifted. He already had an idea.

Replacing his dark expression with a smile, he walked over to the trio. Jessie was still dressed as Jessibella, apparently unwilling to give up her dream of winning a Contest. James and Meowth followed her, again dressed as her manager and assistant.

The three had just come from the registration desk when they met him face-to-face.

“You!?”

Recognizing the man who had punished them last time, Team Rocket jumped and immediately turned to leave. They had only taken a few steps when Harley called out.

“Wait.”

“What do you want?” Jessie turned back and glared at him. “I've got a Contest to enter. I don't have time to stand here talking to you!”

“That's right, meow!” Meowth added. “We haven't tricked you out of any Pokémon this time!”

“You've misunderstood.” Harley smiled. “I'm here to help you.”

“Help us?” Jessie frowned. “What do you mean?”

Harley took out two Poké Balls and waved them before the trio. “I'll lend you my Pokémon, just like last time, so you can win the Contest.”

“What!?”

Jessie, James, and Meowth exchanged bewildered looks. What was he plotting?

“You'll lend us your Pokémon?” James asked. “Why would you do that? What's in it for you?”

Harley answered with a question of his own. “You and that boy Ash are sworn enemies, aren't you?”

“That's right!” Fire seemed to spring into Jessie's eyes at the mention of Ash. “We're going to get that twerp's Pikachu, and we'll teach the twerp a lesson too!”

“Then his friends are your enemies as well?”

“Of course!”

“There you have it.” Harley smiled. “We share an enemy, which makes us friends. My target is that girl May. Oh, and Ethan too.”

Ethan!?

All three felt their hearts sink. Ethan was nowhere in sight, yet even hearing his name sent a chill through them. Harley wanted to pick a fight with him? Was the man tired of living?

They kept those thoughts to themselves. “Even so,” James said, “haven't you forgotten that we stole your Pokémon last time? After that, you'd still lend them to us?”

“I haven't forgotten.” Harley was still angry about it, but he kept his voice as steady as he could. “I'm willing to make a concession to defeat our common enemies. To make sure this goes smoothly, though, lending you my Pokémon comes with a condition.”

“A condition?” James grew wary. “What condition?”

Harley spread his hands. “To prevent another unfortunate incident like last time, one of you stays with me as a hostage.”

A hostage? The three looked at one another, then put their heads together right in front of Harley and began whispering.

He was close enough to hear them breathe and strained to catch a word, but their plotting remained inaudible. He frowned and was about to interrupt when they broke apart and turned to him with broad smiles.

Rubbing his hands, James asked, “So which of us would you like to keep as your hostage?”

Harley suspected they had something up their sleeves. Even so, he was confident he could keep these simple-minded fools under control and let the whispering pass. He looked from one to another and finally fixed his gaze on Meowth.

“That Meowth.”

Of the two people and one Pokémon, Jessie had to compete and could hardly be left behind. James, being human, might find some clever way to cause trouble. To Harley, Meowth was the safest and most suitable hostage.

A talking Pokémon was exceedingly rare. Meowth might not be the only one in the world, but he was valuable enough that Jessie and James would never abandon him. Keeping him would prevent a repeat of their last trick.

What Harley did not know was that Meowth was exactly the one they had hoped he would choose. When he was not looking, all three wore the same sly grin and exchanged another glance.

Harley intended to use Jessie to stop May and get at Ethan. Team Rocket intended to use this opportunity to swindle Harley again.

As for going up against Ethan? What a joke!

To them, Ethan had become practically invincible. He was a champion in three regions, commanded Legendary Pokémon, and even their boss Giovanni preferred not to provoke him.

They also knew that Ethan saw them as too weak to pose a threat. As long as they behaved themselves around him, he would leave them alone even if they met face-to-face. Why would the three of them ruin such a favorable arrangement?

Only fools would do that.

Harley wanted to send them to their deaths? Then they had no reason to hold back from tricking him again.
