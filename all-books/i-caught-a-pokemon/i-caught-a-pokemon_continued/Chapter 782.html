Shizuko took out her third Poké Ball and sent out Houndoom.

After so many encounters with Team Magma and Team Aqua, Ethan knew Houndoom well enough to fight one with his eyes closed. He glanced at Blaziken. It was still in good condition and gave him a nod. Smiling, he left it on the field.

In the stands, Misty laughed. “Looks like Ethan wants Blaziken to take down three in a row!”

Ash and Morrison sat up. Sweeping three opponents was difficult, especially here. They had just fought their own match and knew how strong the Trainers in this round were. A sweep demanded the right field, favorable matchups, a Pokémon in good condition, and exceptional strength. It could happen in an early qualifier. Against stronger opponents later in the tournament, the challenge grew much greater.

Ash and Morrison had each knocked out two Pokémon with one of their own. Because their abilities were so close, neither had managed three.

Shizuko did not know if Ethan intended a sweep, but she felt slighted. Blaziken had already beaten two of her Pokémon. Did he think it could simply stay in and defeat another? Her anger rose, and her manner grew fiercer. When the round began, Ethan heard it in her louder commands.

A Pokémon battle was not decided by which Trainer sounded more imposing. It came down to the Pokémon's strength and condition.

Houndoom used the Rock Field's uneven terrain to circle Blaziken and search for a blind spot. Blaziken simply jumped onto a rock. From above, it could see Houndoom wherever it moved. The ambush was over before it began.

Shizuko changed tactics and had Houndoom attack from below. Blaziken's powerful kicks turned those approaches away, landing several blows of their own. She tried matching fire with fire instead. Flames heated the rocks red and split them apart, but they still could not overcome Blaziken.

Only then did Shizuko understand how difficult Ethan was to fight. It always felt as though she might win, yet every route was closed. She was being suppressed at every turn.

Houndoom could not break through and was in danger of falling. Shizuko took a deep breath, calmed herself, and hurriedly recalled it.

Ethan raised his brows as Houndoom vanished. From the way she battled, he had expected Shizuko to keep attacking until the bitter end. She knew when to switch after all. It was the right choice. If a Pokémon could not win, changing the lineup in time was better than waiting one move too long and regretting a knockout.

Shizuko opened another Poké Ball. Claydol appeared before Blaziken.

Many spectators, and even many Trainers, were unfamiliar with it. Claydol was a Ground and Psychic type that resembled a black figure molded from mud or clay. It stood about one and a half meters tall, with short limbs, many eyes, and mysterious markings across its dark body. It floated in midair, an eerie sight.

It was said to be a mysterious Pokémon born from clay dolls made by a flourishing ancient civilization twenty thousand years ago. According to one account, strange rays had brought those dolls to life. Another story held that ancient people had shaped them in imitation of something that fell from the sky.

Claydol moved by floating with psychic power and even slept while hovering. Its two detached arms flew under its mental control and could fire beams. Water would dissolve its body, so in rain it wrapped itself in psychic energy for protection.

Its types gave it a strong advantage over Blaziken. Ethan was skilled at fighting poor matchups, though, and did not recall his partner. Houndoom had taken a little more of Blaziken's stamina, but as long as it still wanted to fight, Ethan would trust it.

In the stands, Ash and Morrison disagreed about whether Blaziken could complete a three-Pokémon sweep now that it faced a type disadvantage.

“That won't be easy,” Morrison said, folding his arms. “Blaziken has fought nearly three rounds already. Now it has to face something strong against it. It must be tired, however powerful it is. I don't think it can do it.”

Ash privately agreed, but this was Ethan's Blaziken. He hesitated. “I don't think Ethan can beat this one either. This… this…”

“Claydol,” Max supplied, pushing up his glasses.

“Right! Claydol. That's what I was about to say!” Ash looked awkward and quickly went on. “Ethan would be fine against another opponent, but Shizuko reached the main tournament. She's strong too. She won't give him the opening.”

Misty snorted at their confident analysis and did not bother arguing. Did they really think Ethan fought at their level? How naive.

While the referee prepared the round, Ethan considered his approach. Ash and Morrison were right about one thing: Blaziken was tired, and this matchup was awkward. Its strength and fighting spirit had not faded, but it had fought many exchanges. Ethan's job was to turn that disadvantage into an opportunity.

The referee waved the flag. Ethan attacked first. Sitting back under pressure would only invite damage; he needed to seek an opening himself.

At his command, Blaziken ran through the Rock Field's complicated terrain, weaving among the stones as Houndoom had done. It gave Claydol no easy chance to catch it with Confusion.

Shizuko copied the answer Ethan had used against Houndoom. She sent Claydol higher into the air to attack from above.

But Blaziken was not Houndoom.

It fired Flamethrower into the sky. Flames engulfed Claydol and blocked Shizuko's view. At the same time, Blaziken sprang up level with Claydol and drove a kick into the center of the blaze.

Unable to see what lay beyond the fire, Shizuko chose caution. Rather than order a blind Teleport, she had Claydol shield itself with Confusion. A barrier seemed safer than appearing somewhere else only to be struck by Blaziken from an unseen direction. It could hold off the flames while she watched for the next move.

That was precisely the choice Ethan had hoped she would make. Claydol raised its psychic defense just before Flamethrower wrapped around it. Fire burned against the barrier but could advance no farther. The blast came quickly and faded quickly too. As it covered Claydol, its trailing flames were already dying away.

Shizuko looked to the place where Blaziken had fired and saw no sign of it. Where had it gone? She searched the open field and behind the rocks. Nothing.

Oh no. She looked up. Blaziken burst out of the flames and kicked Claydol's barrier.

The shield was strong enough to stop Flamethrower, but it was not unbreakable. After bearing the fire, it shattered under Blaziken's airborne kick. The kick continued through and hit Claydol itself.

Caught by surprise, Claydol flew backward. It lost its balance and crashed heavily to the ground. The force of the blow was plain to see. The spectators cheered. This was more like the spectacle they had wanted after the quieter opening rounds.

Amid the applause, Shizuko's face reddened. Ethan had predicted her thinking. She had been careless. Her careful defense had been broken by one brutal kick. Fortunately, it was a Fighting-type strike. Though strong enough to break the barrier, it dealt only half damage to Claydol itself.

Even so, the pressure on her grew. Blaziken could perform like this against a Pokémon that should counter it. How was she supposed to guard against every trick?

The battle had to continue. If Claydol could not beat Blaziken, Shizuko would be in real trouble. She ordered it to attack as soon as it recovered.

Psychic attacks came in quick succession. Despite its earlier success, Blaziken had to give ground and hide behind a rock. Claydol's type advantage began to show. It floated toward Blaziken's shelter, changing its attack angles and forcing it farther back into Ethan's half of the field. Soon Blaziken was near the boundary, with little room left to retreat.

Shizuko did not relax. She grew more cautious. And, just as she feared, Blaziken counterattacked when Claydol next used Confusion.

A hot Flamethrower swept over the space before Claydol. Remembering the last trick, Shizuko immediately ordered Teleport. Claydol moved back to a safe distance, preventing Blaziken from launching another attack from inside the flames.

It returned to Shizuko's half of the field. The fire died out. Blaziken did not follow. Puzzled, she looked down and found it standing exactly where it had been. It had done nothing else.

She had been fooled.

Before she could dwell on the mistake, Blaziken leaped back toward the middle of the field. It reclaimed all the ground Claydol had just forced it to surrender. One false attack had erased her progress and reset the battle.

What a frightening man.

Shizuko prepared to order Claydol forward again and drive Blaziken back to the boundary. This time she resolved not to retreat from another Flamethrower, even if Claydol had to take the hit. She would knock Blaziken out.

Ethan began his counterattack before she could speak.

As Blaziken's stamina fell, it seemed to find another reserve of power. Its steps quickened, and it ran toward Claydol faster than others of its kind.

The change in pace put Shizuko on alert. Ethan had beaten her first two Pokémon by changing the rhythm of the fight. Whatever Blaziken planned, she could not let it succeed.

“Stop it!” she shouted.

Claydol tried to catch Blaziken with Confusion. It had to aim, release the psychic force, and wait for it to reach the target. Blaziken was already gone by the time each attack arrived. Several tries missed. Claydol changed methods and used a Rock-type move to block its path.

Stones formed in the air and hurtled toward Blaziken.
