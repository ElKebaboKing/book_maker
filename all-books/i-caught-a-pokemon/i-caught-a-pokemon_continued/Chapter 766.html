One brilliant exchange could not decide a whole battle. Eevee had performed beautifully, and Blaziken had followed its lead, but Swellow and Alakazam still held type advantages over Blaziken. The fight had only begun. Vito did not let the setback trouble him.

What did need attention was Eevee's position. It had closed in on Alakazam, denying the Psychic-type Pokémon the safe distance it needed to attack. Most Psychic types had few effective options at close range unless they were among the rare ones skilled at physical combat. Alakazam needed space again.

For it, gaining that space took only a Teleport. At Vito's signal, Alakazam vanished and appeared on the far side of the field.

It had barely reappeared when another torrent of flame swept over it.

“What?!”

Everyone stared. The announcer blurted, “Ethan predicted Alakazam's next move and had Blaziken attack in advance! But how did he know where it would appear?”

It seemed almost impossible. The battlefield was an open rectangle, neither enormous nor small. Picking the exact place where Alakazam would reappear was unlikely enough; launching an attack there with such confidence was something few Trainers would dare. If Ethan had guessed wrong, he would have sent his flames into empty space and looked ridiculous.

The announcer and the crowd were astonished. The competitors watching from the stands imagined themselves in Vito's place and were just as amazed. This was one Trainer outplaying another.

Prediction itself was nothing miraculous. Any Trainer with experience had anticipated an opponent's move once or twice. But this was no cornered Pokémon with limited ways out. On an open field, Ethan had guessed both that Alakazam would Teleport and exactly where it would emerge. Then he had acted without a moment's hesitation. The certainty of his decision alone put him beyond most Trainers.

No one was more shocked than Vito. He had ordered Alakazam to Teleport at once, choosing its destination on the spur of the moment. He had only just made the decision himself. How could Ethan have known?

That was beyond merely impressive.

Alakazam cried out from inside the flames, snapping Vito back to the battle. He hurriedly called for Swellow to beat its wings and blow the fire out. As it did, Eevee closed in again and struck Alakazam with Swift.

Anyone could see that Ethan was concentrating on Alakazam. Swellow stayed high in the air, where it could evade most attacks; defeating the Pokémon closer to the ground first made sense.

Alakazam had barely escaped the flames when Swift hit it. Worse, the fire had burned it. Dark red marks covered its body, and it looked both hurt and miserable.

Vito regretted sending out Swellow. A different Pokémon on the ground might have screened Alakazam or shared the pressure, sparing it this string of attacks. Regret came too late. One more hit like that and Alakazam would be the first to fall.

He kept Swellow closer to Alakazam now, ordering it to guard its partner rather than range high and far. The change protected Alakazam for the moment, but it robbed Swellow of its mobility and handed Ethan the initiative.

Vito had little choice. The burn was hampering Alakazam's use of its psychic powers. He needed to buy time until it passed.

For some reason, Ethan did not press the attack. Vito finally had a chance to breathe.

In the stands, Ash and the others could not understand why Ethan was letting the opportunity go.

“Why isn't he attacking?” Ash frowned. “That was a perfect chance. If he waits until Alakazam gets over the burn, won't all that work be wasted?”

The others were puzzled too. Tyson nodded. “He's missed the best moment. I'd keep up the pressure and knock Alakazam out in one go.”

“Did he fail to seize it?” Morrison asked. “Or didn't he react in time?”

Misty trusted Ethan so completely that she assumed he had a reason. Of the others, only Brock had an inkling of what it might be.

He looked at Blaziken and Eevee, whose movements had grown more fluid as the match continued. Then he looked around at the cheering crowd. Perhaps Ethan was using the battle to settle his Pokémon into the atmosphere of the Conference.

No wonder he had won in two regions. He was thinking further ahead than most people.

Brock hesitated, then kept the thought to himself. This was something the younger Trainers needed to recognize on their own. If Ash, Tyson, and Morrison saw it, they might use these early, less intense matches to help their own Pokémon adjust. If they did not, perhaps they had not yet reached that point in their understanding.

They watched for some time without grasping Ethan's purpose. Brock shook his head. It was hardly a disaster; many Pokémon could perform wonderfully on this stage without such preparation. Still, Ethan's decision showed that he considered things other Trainers overlooked. Settling his Pokémon early would not make him champion by itself, but it could improve his results. It gave him a stronger foundation and a higher ceiling.

Once Blaziken and Eevee had grown comfortable with the Conference atmosphere and warmed up fully, Ethan decided it was time to end the match.

At his command, both Pokémon suddenly quickened their attacks. Vito was caught off guard.

From the stands, spectators had thought Ethan was missing chances to finish the fight. On the field, Vito had felt no such ease. He knew only how troublesome Eevee and Blaziken were, and how hard he had to work to hold them back.

You could not appreciate their strength until you faced them. Their speed, their reactions, and the power of their moves ranked among the best Vito had seen from Pokémon of their kinds. Holding them off had already taken everything he had. Now that they had changed pace, the pressure doubled.

Alakazam, only just recovering from its burn, broke first. Eevee struck it with Quick Attack and knocked it to the ground.

Swellow fared a little better in the air, but not for long. Blaziken leaped high and drove it down with Double Kick. Eevee was waiting on the ground and landed another blow. Swellow fell as its partner had.

The referee raised a colored flag to signal Ethan's victory.

Only then did the spectators fully realize that the match was over. Eevee's opening display had drawn cheers, but the middle of the battle had settled into a quiet stretch with little to excite them. Just when they expected the match to fade out after its strong start, it had ended in a sudden burst of action lasting little more than ten seconds.

The abrupt finish took them by surprise. Scattered applause began only after a pause, then spread through the arena.

Morrison and Tyson came away with a different opinion of Ethan. At first, both had thought him an exceptionally dangerous opponent. After watching him pass up so many chances, they judged him indecisive. That eased their worry. If that was truly his weakness, each felt confident he could beat Ethan when they met.

With his second-round win, Ethan needed only one more victory to reach the final tournament.

Since Morrison and Tyson had come to watch his match, Ethan returned the courtesy by watching one of Tyson's. Tyson revealed little, easily defeating his opponent with his hat-wearing Meowth and Sceptile.

The win earned Tyson more support from the crowd. He was presentable, strong, and, above all, a Trainer from Hoenn. That last fact alone won him favor. At a Hoenn tournament, many spectators naturally wanted a local competitor to become champion.

Morrison's match ran at the same time as Ash's, so Ethan went to watch Ash instead. Perhaps because his previous match had been so difficult, Ash fought beyond his usual level and overwhelmed his opponent. Their impressive performances kept the spotlight from settling too firmly on Ethan.

That gave Gabby an opening.

After the day's matches ended, she slipped into the competitors' dormitories alone at eight that evening. She had already learned Ethan's room number. Finding the hallway empty, she hurried to his door and knocked.

Ethan was still awake, reviewing the day's match and considering what he needed to improve. He assumed the visitor was Misty. When he opened the door and saw Gabby instead, his brows rose, and he immediately grew wary.

Given a choice, he wanted nothing to do with these self-styled uncrowned kings of the press.

Gabby noticed that he was alone and smiled. “Sorry to disturb you, Mr. Ethan. Is this a convenient time for an interview?”

Perhaps to help her plan succeed, she had dressed carefully despite the late hour. Her makeup was immaculate, and she wore perfume that seemed to drift toward him whenever she moved.

Ty, the cameraman usually at her side, was nowhere to be seen. Ethan grew more suspicious.

“Didn't I tell you I'd wait until I reached the final tournament? I still have another match tomorrow. You're early.”

Gabby smiled wider. “A little early, maybe, but I think it's worth it. Aren't you going to invite me in?”

The implication was plain. Ethan finally understood and glanced down at her. Her outfit was daring: a low neckline revealed a glimpse of her chest, and a short skirt left her legs bare. She had come dressed to tempt him.

So that was her plan. Seduction?

Gabby took a step toward the room. Ethan stayed in the doorway and did not let her pass.

He was not blind to the invitation. He simply could not afford to be careless around reporters. A gift that fell into your lap might be a carefully laid trap.

Perhaps Gabby really meant to trade intimacy for an interview and a better position at work. But she might also be carrying a hidden camera, ready to record whatever happened next. If those images got out, his reputation could collapse. To keep them private, he might have to submit to her demands.

He could be wrong about her. Yet what if he was right? Reporters knew how to use such material, and some would even use their own looks to get it. Ethan would not take the risk, however small the chance.

Of course, the main reason—he cleared his throat—was that Gabby simply was not tempting enough.

Ethan admitted he was hardly iron-willed when it came to women. What had happened with Maya, and his time with Erika, had shown him how easily he could be drawn in. But the temptation had to be strong enough.

Consider the women in his life: Sabrina, Erika, and Maya of the Whirl Islands. Every one of them was beautiful. They had standing, looks, strength, and status, and each was devoted to him.

Beside them, Gabby's appearance held no attraction for him. An ordinary man might find her beautiful, but Ethan felt no interest at all.
