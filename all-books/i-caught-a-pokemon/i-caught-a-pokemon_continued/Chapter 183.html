The woman looked about twenty-five. Her long reddish-brown hair was gathered into three tails, and she wore a long skirt with a black strappy top. She was not strikingly beautiful, but Ethan took her at once for someone who had spent years on the sea.

She came closer and looked up at the boy still circling overhead. With a weary sigh, she asked, "Senta, what trouble have you caused now? I told you to clean your room. Why did you sneak out again?"

"Sis! Save me! They came to challenge the Gym!"

His sister. So she was the Mikan Gym Leader.

Ethan expected her to rescue him first. Instead, she took one look, seemed to grasp what had happened, and turned her attention to Ethan and Sabrina.

"I'm sorry. I haven't introduced myself. My name is Cissy. I'm the Mikan Gym Leader and the Star of the West in the Orange Crew."

The Orange Crew consisted of the local Gym Leaders. Its name referred to the Southern Cross, a bright constellation that was difficult to see from many other regions. Here in the islands, it was a familiar sight, and the title cast the Leaders as the region's brightest stars.

Cissy stood with her hands on her hips, showing no sign that she meant to shake hands, so Ethan stayed where he was.

"I'm Ethan. I've come to challenge you. This is my companion, Sabrina."

"Ethan?" Cissy frowned and studied his face. Then recognition dawned. "Of course! You're this year's undefeated Indigo League winner."

Ethan had not expected to be recognized this far from Kanto. His victory's reach extended beyond the mainland after all. "That's me," he said, seeing no reason to hide it.

"I didn't expect you to come here after winning the Indigo League," Cissy said with a smile. "I'm honored that a small league like ours would be your next goal."

"There's no need to put it that way. A good Pokémon challenge isn't measured by the size of its league. If I think it's worthwhile, I'll cross an ocean for it."

Cissy clapped. "Spoken like a champion."

Then her expression turned apologetic. "My little brother has caused you trouble. I've scolded him many times, but he won't stop playing tricks. Whatever he did just now, I apologize. Would you let him down, please?"

She complained about Senta, but there was affection in her voice. He was her brother, and she had asked sincerely. Ethan signaled Sabrina to lower him.

"You should take his behavior seriously," Ethan told Cissy. "He was lucky he ran into us. Someone more vicious might have done worse to him."

Cissy gave an embarrassed smile and nodded. "I'll keep a closer eye on him."

Senta's feet touched down, but his legs gave way. He dropped to the ground and vomited. The spinning had left him as dizzy as if he had ridden a fairground swing dozens of times in a row.

When he had recovered enough to look up, he stared at Sabrina in horror. She was even scarier than his sister.

Cissy led them on toward the shore and into Mikan Gym.

Ethan looked around. The building resembled a warehouse outside, and the interior was just as plain. Beside the elaborate Gyms of Kanto, it looked almost shabby.

Sabrina and Senta moved away from the competition area. Ethan faced Cissy. "What are the rules here?"

"Good question. Orange League Gym challenges are quite different from those in Kanto. I've had plenty of visitors who know only standard battles and demand one before learning how things work here."

She took out a remote and pressed a few buttons. A partition slid open to reveal a row of empty cans. The floor of the arena parted down the middle, exposing a rectangular swimming pool below.

When the pool was fully open, Cissy released a Seadra into it.

"Indigo League champion or not, you're here to challenge me. I'm Cissy, the Star of the West, and I accept."

"This challenge has three stages. For the first, we each send out a Water-type Pokémon. They'll use Water Gun to hit the cans in order. Whoever hits the most wins the stage."

From the stands, Sabrina frowned. This counted as a Gym challenge? It sounded like a game for small children. No wonder she thought the local trainers weak.

Ethan saw it differently. The task looked simple compared with a battle, but it demanded accurate control. A Pokémon that had never trained its aim—or had not mastered the move—could struggle with it.

"All right. This is my choice."

He opened a Poké Ball. A flash of blue light gave way to Blastoise in the pool.

Only Blastoise and Gyarados among his Pokémon were Water types. Gyarados was excellent at breaking things, but poor at delicate work. For this challenge, Blastoise was the clear choice.
