While Misty, May, and Chloe explored Cerise Park, Ethan went into the laboratory.

He knew the layout well enough to find his way without help. He passed through the halls and went straight to the work area.

Professor Cerise, Ren, and Parker were gathered around several instruments, deep in discussion. None of them noticed him arrive. Ethan stopped and looked around.

Quite a bit had changed since his last visit. Equipment had been moved, and there were more machines than before. The laboratory was becoming more complete with every passing month. The sight left him oddly wistful. He had not been gone that long, yet it felt as if an era had passed.

Rather than interrupt their animated discussion, he stood and waited. Ren happened to glance his way. Her eyes lit up.

“Huh? Ethan? When did you get back?”

She had been the first to notice. At her voice, Professor Cerise and Parker turned. Surprise and delight spread across both their faces.

“Ethan?”

“Our champion's come home!”

The three set aside their work and came over. “Professor, Ren, Parker. It's good to see you again.” Ethan hurried to hand them the gifts he had brought. “These are just a little something for you. Please take them.”

Being a champion in three regions had not gone to his head. He treated them as he always had. A few titles would not make him act like someone else.

Though they had not seen him in some time, the Professor and his assistants felt no distance between them. He had come back as the same Ethan they knew.

“I watched every match of yours I could,” Parker said with a smile as he set his gift aside. “You were as strong as ever, just like at the Silver and Indigo Conferences. None of them could stand up to you!”

He pointed toward his desk. Ethan followed the gesture and spotted a figurine of himself.

Good grief. They were making those already?

Parker was more than a supportive friend. He truly enjoyed watching Ethan battle. Of the three researchers, he was the only one with a serious interest in Pokémon battles. If footage of one of Ethan's matches was available online, Parker had almost certainly seen it. Having watched his strength grow, he never worried much that Ethan would lose. What better experience could a fan ask for? Every match was a pleasure to watch.

“That's right,” Ren said teasingly. “He even took time off work to watch your final. Afterward he said, ‘That's it?’ He was disappointed your opponent went down so easily.”

Parker readily admitted it, right in front of his boss. “I wasn't wrong! Compared with the Silver and Indigo Conferences, this opponent was much weaker. It didn't feel like a final. It felt like a round-of-sixteen match!”

Well, of course it had. Ethan had eliminated the strongest opponent earlier, and even Hua had lost heart and failed to show his true strength.

To be fair, though, the average Trainer at the Ever Grande Conference had been a little stronger than at the Silver or Indigo Conferences. It was only the strongest contenders who had fallen short.

Professor Cerise kept smiling through the exchange, though his eyelid twitched a few times when he heard Parker had been watching matches on the job. He said nothing about it. Once the greetings were done, he asked Ethan, “What brings you back this time? Did you just come to visit?”

“Not entirely. I have another reason.” Ethan told them he planned to challenge the Battle Frontier.

“The Battle Frontier?” Professor Cerise stroked his chin. “I've heard of it. If I remember correctly, there's a facility near Vermilion City…”

He went to a computer and worked for a moment. A map of Kanto appeared on the large screen, marked with several locations. As he had said, one facility lay between Vermilion and Saffron City. Five others were marked as well; only the seventh facility's location was hidden.

“This map is a huge help.” Ethan photographed it with his phone. “Thank you, Professor. It'll save me a lot of wandering.”

…

When Ethan left the laboratory and entered Cerise Park, he found the three girls resting on a stretch of level grass. His Pokémon were nearby, as were Misty's and May's, all playing happily together. The girls stood when they saw him.

“Are we leaving?” Misty asked at once.

It was not that she was eager to go home. Being around Chloe had begun to weigh on her. Every time Chloe mentioned something about Brother Ethan that Misty herself did not know, she felt another prickle of irritation.

Ethan shook his head. “I'm afraid we'll have to leave tomorrow. Professor Cerise invited me to dinner at his house tonight and asked me to bring all of you. I couldn't turn him down, so we'll stay another day.”

Their reactions could not have been more different. Misty looked disappointed, May did not seem to mind, and Chloe was delighted.

Ethan took in their expressions, shook his head helplessly, and let it go. He went deeper into the park, released the Pokémon he had been carrying so they could roam freely, and visited his other partners who lived here.

He had only recently parted from most of them, so those visits were brief. He spent longer with the gigantic red Gyarados and Crystal Steelix, whom he had not seen in quite some time.

Clouds drifted overhead. Sunlight poured through the dome's glass and fell across the trees, casting long shadows. Ethan lay back on the grass and gazed up lazily.

It had been a long time since he had enjoyed an afternoon like this. No Gym battle to plan, no road to cover. He could simply be with his Pokémon and listen to the clear voices of the three girls talking nearby. The respite might be temporary, but he felt utterly at ease.

Now this was living.

It was still morning, though, with most of the day to go before dinner. He could not bring himself to spend all of it lying here. Then he remembered Misa, Masha, and the biker gang out on the edge of Vermilion City. Shelly and Courtney had been here from Hoenn for almost a month too. How were they getting on? He ought to go see them.

Ethan got up, brushed the dirt from his clothes, and told the girls he was heading out. Misty rose immediately.

“I'm coming with you,” she said. “I'm bored here, and it's been ages since I've seen Shelly and Courtney. I'd like to see how they're doing.”

Ethan saw through that at once. Misty had met the two women, but she was hardly close to them. She just did not want to stay in the park.

As soon as Misty spoke, May and Chloe stood as well.

“I'll come too.”

“Me too.”

So much for going alone and giving the girls more time to get acquainted. Now all three were coming, including Chloe.

Ethan left the other Pokémon in the park. He recalled only Gardevoir to her Poké Ball and called Gengar back into his shadow. Then he set off with the girls toward the outskirts of Vermilion City.

They had not gone far when he spotted a familiar dark-skinned boy approaching, one hand in his pocket. It was Chloe's childhood friend, Goh. Ethan did not particularly dislike him, but he had to wonder: why did they run into each other every time he came back?

Goh was heading toward Cerise Park with a mysterious smile and a spring in his step. When he drew near enough to recognize Ethan, the smile vanished. Of all the people he could have met, it had to be him.

Goh was asking himself much the same question. Why was this man always here whenever he came to see Chloe? And he had no desire to see Ethan's Pokémon either. That awful Gengar and terrifying red Gyarados had each left him with memories he would never forget.

He wanted to pretend he had seen nothing and walk straight past. Chloe was there, though. He could not ignore her.

“Ch-Chloe. What a coincidence…” Goh forced out a greeting, trying to look normal.

“Goh?” Chloe looked surprised. “Where are you going?”

“I… I was just taking a walk around here. Anyway, I should go. See you when school starts. Bye!”

Goh kept glancing at Ethan as he spoke, then hurried away as if fleeing a monster. Ethan watched him go and turned his attention elsewhere. Goh did not want anything to do with him, and the feeling was mutual.

No matter how often they met, they remained strangers. Their temperaments and beliefs simply did not fit. Even if Goh eventually traveled the world with Ash, Ethan doubted the two of them would become friends.

The encounter was a small interruption. Ethan put it out of his mind as soon as they left. They crossed the busy city and found the bikers' base on its outskirts.

Calling it a base made it sound grander than it was. It consisted of a motorcycle repair shop and a bar.

After Ethan invested in them, the gang had banded together and learned to run a business. Once a group of people down on their luck, they had begun to make something of themselves. Business was picking up too.

Rows of motorcycles stood outside the bar and inside the repair shop. At the very least, the bikes showed how far the gang had come. They had traded up from their bicycles and become bikers in earnest.

Several members were at work in the shop. When they saw Ethan, delight broke across their faces, and they gathered around him.

“Boss!”

“Boss!”

Ethan had never agreed to be their boss, but they had decided he was anyway. He had grown used to it. One of them ran ahead into the bar to tell the others, while the rest ushered Ethan inside as though he were a star.

Masha and Misa had already heard the news. They emerged from different rooms with a crowd of bikers and surrounded him so eagerly that Misty, May, and Chloe were pushed aside.

“Boss!” Masha's eyes shone. “You finally had time to visit! When did you get back to Kanto?”

“That can wait!” Misa shoved her younger brother aside with her hip. “We watched you win the championship on TV. You were incredible, Boss!”

The brother and sister were not the only ones talking. Everyone had something to say, and the bar filled with noise. Ethan spoke up to calm them.

“Easy, easy. Don't scare my friends.”

He called the girls over. Once the crowd made room, he finally had a chance to look around the bar. It resembled the American country bars he had seen on television: bottles behind the counter, a dartboard on the wall, and a pool table. The one unusual feature was the collection of Ethan posters covering the place.

It was a strange feeling. Other bars might display photographs of beautiful women. This one had photographs of him, stared at every day by a gang of men. Without knowing the story, a visitor might wonder about the gang's tastes.

A handful of other customers were there as well. They watched him curiously.

Knowing this was no place for a private conversation, Ethan caught Misa's eye. She understood, sent the gang on their way, and led him and the girls into a room that looked much like a meeting room.

When the door closed behind them, the whole world seemed to fall quiet at once.
