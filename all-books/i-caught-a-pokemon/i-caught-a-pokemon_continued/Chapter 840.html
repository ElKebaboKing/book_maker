Anyone who followed current events, especially in Kanto and Johto, had probably heard Ethan's name. The Pokémon Leagues drew enormous attention. Even if someone else had won, people would still know the champion's name.

Lucy and the three women worked in Pokémon battling. Of course they had watched the tournaments that drew the eyes of an entire continent. But months had passed, and their attention had been on the Pokémon rather than their Trainers. Like many people, they had forgotten what Ethan looked like and had failed to recognize him at once.

Now that Lucy had identified him, the three women finally understood.

“Oh! So it's you!”

“I watched the battle where you won the championship. You were incredible!”

“Hey, Mr. Champion, how about an autograph? Right here on my chest. Do you have a pen? If not, your finger will do!”

They really did say whatever came to mind.

At the discovery that he was the champion, their eyes lit up again. They crowded around him more boldly than before and even pushed May aside. Trainers arrived to challenge the Pike all the time, but a champion at their door was a rare sight.

Hearing the commotion, several more women dressed much like them came out of the facility. They gathered around Ethan and inspected him as though he were a rare Pokémon.

It reminded him of Erika and the Celadon Gym. Both places had a female Leader and female staff who liked to gossip together. The chatter could get noisy. The women here, though, were far less restrained.

Some men might enjoy being surrounded by women, but Ethan neither liked the attention nor took pleasure in the praise. He would have preferred it if Lucy had never recognized him and had treated him like an ordinary challenger.

There was little he could do about it. Fame and achievements beyond most people's reach came at a price. He had faced this before, more than once, and would face it again. Someone who enjoyed the attention might see it differently, but Ethan did not.

Lucy was a Frontier Brain, and she had no intention of letting her underlings carry on. One sharp order sent every woman surrounding Ethan back to her place.

Ethan's opinion of her rose. These women were hardly meek or obedient by nature. Whatever their usual relationship with Lucy, the fact that they obeyed a single command when it mattered showed how well she led them. She deserved her title.

With that settled, Lucy and the others led Ethan and May into the Battle Pike.

Beyond the doors stretched a vast chamber that resembled the inside of a pipe. Ethan looked around and saw how the facility had earned its name.

The battle arena stood at its center. Instead of a plain field built around one type of terrain, it combined shrubs, soil, a winding stretch of water, more than a dozen platforms above the pool, and small islands. Pokémon could battle on land, in water, or in the air. It felt more like a real patch of wilderness than the bare arenas he was used to.

Of all the Frontier facilities Ethan had visited, this was the most varied battlefield.

He and Lucy climbed the high steps to the Trainer positions on opposite sides of the arena. From there they faced each other across the field.

Lucy might have had a gentle-sounding name, but her bearing was that of a boss. Merely standing there, she was impossible to dismiss.

A woman with short blue-green hair and a matching strapless top stepped onto the referee's platform.

“The Frontier battle between Indigo League Champion Ethan Vale and Pike Queen Lucy is about to begin! Each Trainer may use two Pokémon. The first to win two rounds wins the match. Trainers, send out your Pokémon!”

Lucy was called the Pike Queen because of her strength, not merely because she ran the facility or because of her looks and bearing. Challenger after challenger had come through her doors, and she had won far more often than she had lost. Her skill let her rule this place like a queen.

There was no doubting her ability. Facing a League champion like Ethan, however, she still felt considerable pressure.

He stood there doing nothing, making no unnecessary movements. He almost seemed to leave himself open everywhere. Yet Lucy felt a suffocating force coming from him.

The faint resignation he had shown when her women surrounded him had vanished the instant he took his place as a Trainer. A flash of confidence appeared in his eyes, and the pressure he gave off made everyone take notice.

So this is a champion?

No—this was a man who held the championship titles of three regions. He was nothing like her previous challengers.

Lucy kept her expression composed despite the difficulty she felt ahead. She calmly took a Poké Ball from her coat. Almost at the same moment, Ethan took one out as well. When they released their Pokémon, everyone watching saw the first choices of the match.

By sheer coincidence, two identical Milotic appeared in the water before their respective Trainers.

Neither had expected the other to choose the same Pokémon.

“Huh? They both sent out Milotic?” The women watching could hardly believe it.

With so many kinds of Pokémon, matching choices were uncommon but hardly impossible. For both Trainers to send out a rare Milotic in this particular match, though, was an extraordinary coincidence.

Ethan and Lucy were just as surprised as the spectators.

Lucy meant to give an opponent like him her full effort. Alongside her signature ace, Seviper, Milotic was one of her main Pokémon. It was a natural choice for an opening round in which she could test him.

Ethan, meanwhile, had been concentrating on training his own Milotic. With water covering more than half the field, sending it out made perfect sense to him.

Each had a reason. The result was this unexpected mirror match.

“I didn't realize you had a Milotic too.” Lucy sounded as steady as ever despite her surprise. “Good. Same Pokémon, same type. This is the perfect way to measure our skill. Now show me what you can do.”

Seeing was better than hearing about it. Ethan had won three regional tournaments and enjoyed a formidable reputation, but Lucy had seen those victories only on screens. She would have to face him herself to learn whether he lived up to it.

More than anything, she wanted to battle him and discover the difference between them.

The two Milotic swam to their starting positions. Under the lights, with ripples reflecting around them, both Pokémon looked exceptionally beautiful.

Once they were ready, the referee raised her hand.

“Begin!”

Both Pokémon moved as her call ended. In near-perfect unison, they opened their mouths and fired Hydro Pump at each other. The two torrents crossed the arena, collided in midair, and burst into a shower of spray.

Identical moves had canceled each other out. But neither Trainer had ordered that exchange. It was an instinctive test at the start of the battle; now they took command.

“Milotic, use Twister!”

The order came from Lucy, not Ethan.

That caught him off guard. Twister was one of his favorite moves whenever he battled with a Water-type Pokémon, and he often combined it with other techniques. This time someone was using it against him.

But because he knew Twister so well, he knew how to answer it.

“Milotic, use Twister too!”

Two spiraling winds rose one after the other. They grew larger as they swept toward the middle of the arena. Although they spun in different directions, their power was evenly matched. Instead of crashing apart as the Trainers might have expected, the two Twisters merged into an even greater vortex.

The larger vortex pulled harder. Pool water, loose shrubs, and stones tore free and whirled upward. A murky waterspout took shape, blocking each Trainer's view of the other side.

Here the difference in their styles became clear. Lucy had used Twister as a straightforward attack. After launching it, she waited for her opponent to expose an opening.

More than ten seconds later, she stared in astonishment as Ethan's Milotic rode the rising water into the air, then dropped rapidly from above. Its Iron Tail struck her Milotic with full force.

Smack!

Lucy’s Milotic had not anticipated an attack from the sky. The blow landed cleanly and sent it plunging into the pool.

“Milotic!”

The cry came from the women watching, not from Lucy. They all sprang to their feet.

They had stood by Lucy since the Battle Pike opened. When had they ever seen her Milotic put at such a disadvantage right at the start? This was the Pike Queen's second-strongest main Pokémon, surpassed only by her ace Seviper. It was no ordinary Trainer's casual choice.

The descending Iron Tail had caught it completely off guard. Yet wasn't that proof of Ethan's more inventive approach?

Fortunately for Lucy's Milotic, the water absorbed some of the impact. The hit still hurt, but after a few seconds beneath the surface, it burst out in a spray and drove its head toward Ethan's Milotic.

The counterattack missed. Even so, the sight of Milotic back in action let Lucy's supporters breathe again.

Her Milotic pressed on without a pause.

“That's better.” The women settled back into their seats, their brows relaxing. “Now she's got it back to the boss's usual style. Attack, attack, and keep attacking. Nobody can stand up to that!”

Ethan was too far away to hear them, but May caught every word.

She knew the title Pike Queen meant Lucy was strong. She still disagreed completely with what the women were saying. There were no absolutes in a Pokémon battle.

Nobody can stand up to her attacks? That's because you haven't met Ethan yet!

May was not like Misty, who acted as though she were the world's biggest Ethan fan and believed him unbeatable. Still, she knew he had faced many difficult opponents since becoming a Trainer and had yet to lose an official match. An attack that supposedly no one could withstand would not be enough to stop him.

She kept the thought to herself. That was more her way than arguing with strangers.

And the battle proved her right.

Lucy's Milotic kept attacking with Iron Tail and Tackle and even tried Wrap. Apart from its Water-type moves, it used nearly everything it knew. Under Ethan's direction, his Milotic answered each attack.

Ethan's sharper reading of the battle let him defend and counterattack. He broke Lucy's rhythm, then had his Milotic land another Iron Tail before hers could react.

The women in the stands fell silent again. May felt a private satisfaction and cast them a faintly triumphant glance.

See? There is no such thing as an attack nobody can withstand. Now you know what Ethan can do.
