When Ethan and Sabrina entered the Orange League headquarters, a staff member approached them in the lobby. He looked to be in his sixties, with short silver-gray hair and a small mustache.

"Good morning. What brings you here? Are you sightseeing, or would you like to buy a souvenir?"

Ethan stepped forward. "Hello. I'd like to challenge the Orange League. Could you register me?"

The man's manner grew warmer at once. "Welcome! It's been almost a month since we last held a Winner's Cup match. It's wonderful to have a new challenger. First, though, the rules require me to see your proof of eligibility."

Proof? He must mean the four badges from the Orange Crew Gyms. If nearly a month had gone by without a challenge, the Orange League's competitive scene seemed to have stalled. That could hardly be good for its growth.

Ethan opened his bag, took out his badge case, and showed the man four different shell-shaped badges.

The staff member passed a small scanner over each one. A moment later, Ethan's details appeared on the computer screen. They even check for counterfeits, he thought. He glanced over and saw his photograph, his home region of Kalos, and his Indigo League championship listed there.

"Oh! You're the Trainer who just won the Indigo League?" the man said, delighted. "I thought you looked familiar."

People who worked for a League kept up with Pokémon news. They heard who had won in each region, which competitors were favorites, and which Trainers had become famous. The larger regions crowned champions every year, but no champion had ever come here to challenge the Orange League.

"That championship is in the past," Ethan said with a wave. "Right now I'm an ordinary challenger, starting on the same footing as anyone else. Please don't make too much of it."

The man did not take the modest words literally. Ethan was hardly an ordinary challenger. The Orange League was smaller, more remote, and less populous than the major Leagues. Its influence and viewing figures lagged well behind theirs.

An Indigo League champion entering the Winner's Cup was news worth publicizing. If Ethan won, his reputation would be confirmed. If he lost, it would show that the Orange League could stand against a major League. Either result would draw attention here.

The man's smile widened. "How rude of me. I forgot to introduce myself. I'm Carl, vice president of the Orange League."

Vice president? Ethan had assumed he was a receptionist. When he had first walked in, he had wondered why an older man was handling arrivals rather than the young women he usually saw at front desks. Now he understood.

"The vice president himself is registering me? I'm honored." Ethan held out his hand.

Carl shook it firmly. "We don't get many challengers. I can handle the desk by myself, so there's no need to keep a large staff here."

He smiled again. "You've had a long journey. The Orange Islands have warm sunshine and beautiful scenery. If you have time, I hope you'll enjoy them."

"I already have," Ethan said. "I've seen scenery I wouldn't find elsewhere, and caught some strong Pokémon. It's a lovely place to travel."

Carl was pleased. Then his gaze shifted to Sabrina. "And the young lady with you?"

"She's my traveling companion, not a challenger." Ethan turned to introduce her and found her staring at a Dragonite statue beside them.

The statue looked as though it had been cast in iron. It was a dark metallic green, carefully made, with Dragonite's wings spread and its claws extended to either side. Its eyes faced straight ahead as though it were guarding something.

Seeing their interest, Carl said, "The people here have long revered Dragonite as the island's guardian. Our current Supreme Gym Leader happens to have a powerful Dragonite himself. Since taking the position, he has never been defeated in the Winner's Cup."

Pride filled Carl's voice when he spoke of both the Pokémon and its Trainer.

Undefeated? Ethan thought. What a coincidence. So am I.

Carl entered a few details into the computer. "That's your registration complete. Under the rules, your match will be tomorrow at ten in the morning. Before then, let me show you the venue."

"I'd appreciate that."

Carl came out from behind the desk and headed for the door. Ethan looked at the empty counter. "Is it all right to leave? What if another challenger arrives while we're gone?"

"Don't worry." Carl gave a small smile, confident but faintly rueful. "As I said, we haven't had a challenger in a long time. A few hours away from the desk won't matter."
