In a lounge at the Battle Arena, Ethan, Misty, and May sat across from Bea. No one else was there. Greta and Scott had both stayed away, apparently understanding that the old friends would appreciate time to talk.

Tea and a plate of fruit stood on the table. The room was small but comfortably furnished. Ethan and Misty were already friends with Bea and felt at ease. May was meeting her for the first time. Once the introductions were done, though, May simply lowered her head, swung her legs, and quietly ate the fruit one forkful after another.

Bea glanced away from May's stomach, which somehow stayed flat no matter how much she ate, and looked at Ethan. After a moment's hesitation, she said, “I haven't congratulated you yet. Congratulations on winning the Ever Grande Conference.”

“It wasn't such a big deal.” Ethan waved a hand and smiled. “The tournament went smoothly. Winning felt like the natural conclusion. Besides, congratulations don't have a deadline. You're not too late.”

Misty brightened at once. “You should have seen him, Bea! From the qualifying rounds through the main tournament, Brother Ethan won by huge margins. The other Trainers couldn't keep up with him at all!”

Bea almost said something, then kept quiet. Although she had left Hoenn, she had watched every one of Ethan's matches on television. She was embarrassed to admit it.

Ordinarily, she paid little attention to other people's affairs and devoted herself to training. Yet during the tournament, she had found herself stopping her practice whenever Ethan had a match. Each time, she went to a Pokémon Center to watch the broadcast.

She had been unsettled lately. The memory of that night kept intruding on her thoughts, making it hard to concentrate on training. She understood little about romance, but she knew something had changed in her state of mind.

She had come to the Battle Arena hoping to recover the feeling of a good fight and push those distracting thoughts aside. Instead, as if fate had arranged it, she had met Ethan again. She could hardly have expected that. Far from clearing her mind, the meeting had left her even more confused.

At least no one else knew.

With Misty leading the conversation, they covered everything from Ethan's championship victory to Bea's recent travels. The atmosphere in the lounge grew easy and cheerful.

At noon, Greta and Scott came in and invited them to lunch. By the time they finished eating, Greta's Pokémon had completed their treatment and recovered their strength. Everyone gathered at the battle arena again.

In the meantime, the students had looked into both visitors. Scott had already introduced Ethan as the winner of the Indigo, Silver, and Ever Grande Conferences. An online search revealed that Bea was no ordinary challenger either: she was an official Gym Leader in Galar.

The students might not have known much about Galar, but they knew what a Gym Leader was. No wonder Greta had lost. Her earlier match had been more than a challenge from an unknown Trainer; it had been a contest between a Gym Leader and a Frontier Brain. An official Gym Leader had proved herself the better fighter.

Ethan and Greta returned to the wheel in the open-air practice ground, surrounded by the others. Greta folded her arms. “You know the rules. Go ahead and spin it.”

Ethan nodded and gave the lever a casual pull. The wheel spun, slowed, and stopped with the pointer on a red section marked with two dots.

Two. He would use two Pokémon. It was a good enough result. He would not get three rounds, but he could at least face Greta's two strongest partners.

The group went back to the battle hall. Ethan and Greta took their places in the Trainer areas, facing each other across the field.

Unlike Bea, Ethan projected no fierce presence. He simply stood there, looking like an unusually handsome Trainer. Nothing more. Yet no one in the hall dared underestimate him.

They took his easy smile for the composure of someone who had no need to show his strength. Once the battle began, they knew he would show no mercy.

As before, Greta released her Pokémon first. Two flashes of blue light revealed Hariyama and Medicham, both of which had fought well against Bea. Ethan had expected them after waiting for their treatment. He released the two partners he had already chosen: Blaziken and Scizor.

Since the Ever Grande Conference, the Pokémon Ethan had caught in Hoenn, including Blaziken and Milotic, had received plenty of training. Their tournament experience had raised their strength another step.

If he kept traveling with them alone, ordinary battles and ordinary Pokémon would offer them little room to grow. Only unusually strong Gym Leaders and Trainers could still test them properly. He would need to catch some new Pokémon.

That explained why Ash started building a new team whenever he reached a new region. As a Pokémon grew stronger, wild opponents and everyday incidents no longer helped it improve as much.

Ethan made finding new partners a priority, then brought his thoughts back to the field. Bea's match had shown that Greta's two Pokémon were capable and could still give his partners useful practice. But he would have to act on his new plan soon.

Greta sent Hariyama out first. Ethan looked it over and marveled again at the world's technology. Only hours ago, Pangoro had knocked it out. After treatment and rest, it stood before him lively and ready to battle. The efficiency was remarkable.

Ethan lifted his chin slightly. Blaziken strode into the battle area with firm steps.

The Ever Grande Conference had changed Blaziken in ways visible beyond its improved strength. Its movements were steadier, its expression calmer. It had battled from the qualifiers all the way to the final, before enormous crowds. Experiences like that had made it grow.

It was one reason Ethan's Pokémon stood above so many others. Having reached the summit once, they knew what the view from it looked like.

The students stirred at Blaziken's entrance. Earlier they had known little about this particular partner, but during Ethan's break they had hurried through footage of his Ever Grande matches. They had seen how strong Blaziken was and watched its spectacular three-Pokémon sweep. Now it was standing in front of them.

They were excited, though not simply because they feared for Greta. Facing a three-time conference winner, they had moved past taking offense whenever their teacher lost. Seeing Ethan and one of his championship Pokémon battle was enough. Win or lose, they would have something to talk about for days.

Greta had not studied the footage herself, but the students' reaction and Blaziken's bearing told her this Pokémon was special. She glanced at Bea. Blaziken gave her much the same impression as Bea's partners: it would be difficult to handle. Perhaps that was why Ethan had won three conferences.

Scott watched Blaziken without blinking. “We haven't even started, and you can already feel how strong it is. No wonder Mr. Ethan has accomplished so much!”

He turned to Misty. “He didn't have this Blaziken at the Silver Conference, did he? He caught it in Hoenn?”

“That's right.”

“Impressive,” Scott said. “If a newer partner is this strong, I can't imagine how powerful the Pokémon he caught earlier must be by now.”

How powerful? Misty considered it seriously and found she did not know the answer herself. Gardevoir, Gengar, the gigantic red Gyarados, and Crystal Steelix came to mind. She doubted any of them would lose even to an Elite Four member's ace.

And Brother Ethan's strongest partners had never been the ones he displayed at conferences. There were the three Legendary birds and two Legendary beasts. Then there were Deoxys and the black Rayquaza, whose power and capacity for destruction she had seen with her own eyes. They could only be described as disasters.

When Misty did not answer, Scott merely smiled. He assumed she might not know much about Ethan's older Pokémon. Besides, if those Pokémon no longer traveled, surely their strength had stopped growing.

The same older student stepped in as referee. At his signal, the match began.

Blaziken surged toward Hariyama. It used no fire. It seemed intent on testing the larger Pokémon at close range, which suited Greta perfectly.

At her command, Hariyama braced itself. It raised its thick palms and watched Blaziken's every movement.

The gap between them vanished in a few seconds. Just before reaching Hariyama, Blaziken accelerated again. Its orange form became a blur.

“Block it!” Greta shouted.

Hariyama swung both huge hands. Neither it nor Greta had judged Blaziken's speed correctly. In a flash, Blaziken seemed to disappear from directly ahead. It appeared at Hariyama's side, leaped, and drove a powerful kick into its body.

So fast! Greta had barely time to gasp before Hariyama staggered backward.

Its height, bulk, and toughness kept it standing, but the kick had done real damage. One strike was enough to put Greta on alert.

She was a skilled karate fighter as well as a top Pokémon Trainer. From either perspective, she could appreciate the precision of that kick, and she now understood Blaziken's strength more clearly.

Frowning, she ordered Hariyama forward again. The two Pokémon fell into a close-range struggle. Unlike the clash with Bea's Pangoro, this was strength against speed.

Hariyama had the clear advantage in raw power. Blaziken was faster and dared not meet every blow head-on. Yet whenever Blaziken slipped around behind it, Hariyama could not afford a moment's carelessness.

After several exchanges, keeping up with Blaziken was becoming harder for Greta. She looked across the field. Ethan stood as calmly as before, giving only the occasional short, precise command. He looked as though the entire battle was under control.

The contrast frustrated her. They had only just begun, and the outcome was not settled. Why did he look so certain he would win? Yet she could not deny what was happening. She had tried everything she could think of, and Hariyama still had no answer to Blaziken's speed.
