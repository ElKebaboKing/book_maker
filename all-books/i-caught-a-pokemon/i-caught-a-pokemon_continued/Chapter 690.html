The waves spread across the entire pool, reaching Walrein and Kingdra at the far end. Kingdra was swept along by the current, unable to hold its position. Even Walrein, standing on a platform, was battered by the surging water.

Both were Water-type Pokémon, at home in the sea, but these waves were too violent for them to keep their balance.

Ethan slipped his hands into his pockets. There was an old saying about fishing in troubled waters. His two Pokémon faced unfavorable matchups, so he had upended the entire field. Now no one could fight comfortably. In the confusion, he could look for a way to win.

He had plenty of experience with chaotic battles, whether people or Pokémon were involved. Juan was older, but Ethan doubted he had faced many situations like this.

Sure enough, Juan frowned. For all his composure and elegance, he could hardly believe what he was seeing. What sort of tactic endangered both sides? Was this how a normal Trainer battled? Twister was supposed to attack an opponent, not turn the whole battlefield against everyone. It was anything but graceful.

The two waterspouts had reached their full strength. They roamed unpredictably around the pool, throwing up spray and churning the currents. Neither distinguished friend from foe. Together, two uses of the same move had become far more destructive than either alone.

Water rained down on the field. It soon soaked Juan's elaborate suit, fitting his mood. He looked at his Pokémon. In a pool this enclosed, simply holding their positions was difficult, let alone attacking. All the plans he had made before the match dissolved like the water falling back from the spouts.

Things had changed faster than his plans could. Seen another way, though, Ethan's approach was clever. Juan himself would never have made such a decision. His opinion of the challenger shifted again.

Through a gap between the waterspouts, he saw Ethan standing calm and almost enjoying himself. The young man was difficult to read. But Juan could not stand there reflecting while the field spun out of control.

“Walrein, use Aurora Beam!”

Aurora Beam was a ranged Ice-type attack that could badly hurt Dragonite. Juan, however, aimed it at the nearest waterspout. With both spouts in the middle of the field, even standing still exposed his Pokémon to their force. He needed to stop them and calm the pool before he could fight on his own terms.

Walrein's beam could freeze water. If it froze a waterspout solid, it would be the quickest, simplest solution available to him.

Walrein opened its mouth. A blue-green beam shot out between its long tusks and struck the closer spout almost at once. Ice crackled over the point of impact and spread through the moving water. Given enough time, the whole column might freeze.

Before Juan could relax, the second waterspout drifted near. Its pull tore at the growing ice. Thick pieces broke away and crashed into the pool.

Juan's brief smile froze. The idea had not been wrong, but Aurora Beam had barely been enough for one waterspout. It could not freeze both together.

Still, the effort had not been wasted. The spout Walrein had struck had visibly weakened. Juan prepared to try again.

Before he could give the order, the water beside Walrein's platform burst open. Milotic surged up, leaped high, and rammed Walrein off its footing. Both crashed into the pool.

At almost the same moment, Dragonite beat its wings overhead and fired Dragon Rage at Kingdra. While Juan concentrated on stopping the waterspouts, Ethan had sent one Pokémon through the air and the other beneath the surface to attack from opposite directions.

The currents troubled Ethan's partners too, but they had experience fighting amid disorder. Ethan only had to account for the spouts as another hazard. Juan, by contrast, was so intent on ending them that he had diverted his attention from the opposing Pokémon.

Falling spray obscured his view, and he had to command two partners at once. Those pressures gave Ethan's attacks their opening.

Walrein splashed into the water. Once submerged, it was far less agile than Milotic. Milotic kept it occupied, preventing it from surfacing, escaping, or using another Ice-type move to freeze the waterspouts. The two struggled beneath the pool, and Walrein lost the advantage it had held on the platform.

Kingdra dove beneath Dragonite's Dragon Rage. Juan considered sending it deeper to help Walrein. Together his Pokémon might surround Milotic and change the battle's course. In a Double Battle, helping a partner in trouble was usually the right choice.

But the water below was as turbulent as the surface. Walrein might not be fast enough to catch Milotic and set up a two-on-one attack. The currents could also draw either of Juan's Pokémon into a waterspout and fling it into the air. Their sturdy bodies would not protect them from that.

If Kingdra stayed underwater, Dragonite would have the air to itself. The next time Kingdra surfaced, it could be attacked from above without restraint. Worse, Ethan might simply raise another waterspout when these died away and keep Juan trapped in the same situation.

Juan made his choice. If Ethan could fight beside the spouts, then the Leader of a Water-type Gym could too. He had resolved to teach the challenger a lesson and would not retreat now, least of all in front of Lisia. Watch me, little Lisia. I'll show you how elegantly your teacher fights.

He kept Kingdra at the surface rather than sending it after Walrein. “Kingdra, use Hydro Pump!”

A heavy jet of water shot from Kingdra's mouth. It swept an arc across the air, tracking Dragonite's flight. Ethan understood Juan's decision at once: he would meet him head-on.

Kingdra's attack looked imposing, but taking down Dragonite would not be so easy. Dragonite had traveled with Ethan for a long time, fought countless battles, and competed at the Silver Conference. It had grown to roughly the strength Charizard possessed before training in the Charicific Valley.

Among Ethan's second-tier Pokémon, Dragonite held the leading place and was nearing the level of his top team. It was not yet in the extraordinary class of his red Gyarados or Crystal Steelix, but it could defeat the ace Pokémon of most Gym Leaders. Tyranitar and Scizor were at about the same level as Dragonite, and all three were central to Ethan's plans for the Ever Grande Conference.

If Juan thought Dragonite's type disadvantage made Ethan the weaker side, he was badly mistaken.

Dragonite dodged left and right in the air until Kingdra's Hydro Pump ran out. Then it turned sharply and streaked toward Kingdra.

“Dragonite, use Dragon Rush!”

Dragonite accelerated again. It folded its wings, wrapped itself in a surging wave of power, and flew straight at Kingdra like an arrow loosed from a bow.

It's fast! Juan's eyes widened. “Dive, quickly!”

His warning came too late. Dragonite skimmed so low over the water that its rounded belly seemed to touch the surface. In a blink, it was face-to-face with Kingdra. Kingdra felt the heat of its breath and the terrifying Dragon-type energy gathered around its smooth head.

Bang! Dragonite struck with the force of its full speed. The super-effective blow knocked Kingdra out of the water. It skipped across the surface four or five times before flying toward the waterspout behind it.

Kingdra slammed into the rotating column. The current caught it, carried it higher and higher, and hurled it toward the ceiling.

Juan looked up in alarm. Kingdra shot free of the spout and fell back toward the pool like a kite whose string had snapped. Dragonite had already turned and was racing after it.

Only now did Juan realize how powerful Ethan's supposedly ill-chosen Dragonite was. That was the confidence behind Ethan's decision. Juan had understood it too late.

Kingdra had no way to evade in midair. Dragonite caught it with Wing Attack, changing its course once again and driving it down toward the water. Kingdra had been reduced to a helpless target by the chain of attacks.

The battle should never have become so one-sided. Juan's initial misjudgment had started a series of mistakes that Ethan kept exploiting.

Smack! Kingdra struck the surface. From that height and at that speed, the water might as well have been solid ground. It floated unsteadily, dazed by the impact, and did not recover for some time.

Juan watched anxiously. Dragonite hovered above Kingdra and began gathering energy in its mouth. Juan recognized Dragon Rage, the move Kingdra had dodged once before. Against Kingdra, it was the last attack he wanted to see coming.
