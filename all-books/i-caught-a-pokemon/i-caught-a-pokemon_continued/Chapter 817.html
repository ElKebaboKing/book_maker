After the three of them exchanged a few compliments, Sergio returned. Noland took two badges from the gift box he carried and handed one each to Ethan and Ash.

“Please accept these. They're Knowledge Symbols, proof that you defeated me.”

Ethan and Ash thanked him and took their Symbols.

Like the two Ethan had already collected, each was made of solid gold. It was a little larger than a bottle cap and heavier than it looked, with three diamond shapes stamped into its gleaming face.

Ethan already had two Symbols and remained calm. This was Ash's first, won against an exceptionally strong opponent. He could hardly contain his excitement. He held it high and struck a victory pose for May and the others.

“I got the Knowledge Symbol!”

May, Max, Brock, and Scott applauded. They chose to overlook how smug he looked.

Evening had come without anyone noticing. Ethan and Ash had accomplished what they came for, and it was time to leave. Articuno departed first.

Its visible injuries had been treated. After two battles in succession, Noland's Articuno seemed satisfied. Whether it had recovered its strength or not, it nodded to Noland, gave a high cry, and flew into the distant sky.

Articuno was fond of Noland, and the Battle Factory was somewhere it liked to visit. Noland was its friend. But this was not its home. It might return from time to time, yet it would never live here for long.

That was for the best. A wild Pokémon was free. By staying away most of the time, Articuno also made it harder for outsiders to connect it to the Battle Factory and discover where to find it.

Only after it vanished from sight did Ethan and the others say goodbye to Noland, Scott, and Sergio.

“Where will you two challenge next?” Scott asked.

Ethan and Ash exchanged a look. The nearest Frontier facility for Ash was the Battle Arena. Ethan had already challenged it and would ordinarily go somewhere else. But May's Saffron City Contest was about to begin. Ethan needed to take her there in time.

“We're heading to Saffron City,” Ethan said.

“Saffron City?” Scott looked puzzled. “Mr. Vale, wasn't the Battle Arena your first challenge? Why go back there?”

When Ethan explained that May was entering the Contest, Scott understood.

“I see.” He smiled at May. “If I get the chance, I'll come and cheer for you.”

May knew it was a polite offer. She smiled back. “Thank you.”

It was getting late, so the group said their farewells and set off. Scott and Noland watched them go.

“Looks like those two might conquer the whole Battle Frontier,” Scott said. As its owner, he could not help feeling moved by the arrival of such powerful challengers.

Noland glanced at him. He had little doubt that Ethan would keep winning, but he had not expected Scott to rate Ash so highly. Beating Articuno proved the boy was strong. Still, Noland was hardly the strongest Frontier Brain. Wasn't it too early to predict that much?

While they spoke, Team Rocket's trio lay bored on the Battle Factory roof, watching Ethan's party disappear into the distance.

“Say…” James chewed on a blade of grass. “Was it really all right for us to lie here the whole time?”

“Exactly!” Jessie complained. “Articuno flew away, and the twerp and Pikachu left too. We didn't do a thing! What happened to giving Articuno and Pikachu to the Boss?”

“You two idiots!” Meowth stared at them. “How were we supposed to pull anything off with that man around? We have to wait until he and the twerp split up before we make our move, meow!”

“But…” James looked toward the sky. “Articuno's already gone. Haven't we missed our chance with it?”

Meowth waved that aside. “So what? I'd rather do nothing than run into that man, meow!”

…

It was nearly dark when they left the Battle Factory, so instead of pushing on, the travelers spent the night at a Pokémon Center. Once Charizard's injuries were healed, Ash sent it back to Liza in Charicific Valley.

Charizard had done what Ash had called it here to do. Its priority now was to continue training in the valley until it could become as strong as Ethan's Charizard. Every extra second away meant another second of training lost. Ash said nothing about that comparison, but he had it in mind.

The night passed quietly. After breakfast the next morning, they resumed their journey to Saffron City.

At Ash's suggestion, they took a mountain route instead of the road they had traveled before. It looked shorter on a map, but it was no real shortcut. The level road would have been faster and easier.

They had already walked those roads many times, though, and repeating the same route would add little to their travels. That was why Ash chose the difficult mountain path. To him, travel was more fun when it tested you.

Ash went wherever the mood took him, with little advance planning. It made his journeys full of surprises and considerably more tiring. Sure enough, they had scarcely entered the mountains when, well before noon, he dropped onto a bare boulder in exhaustion.

Ethan, May, and Max were less tired than he was. It was rare to see someone with Ash's seemingly boundless energy worn out. Perhaps his first Symbol had made him so excited that he had spent too much strength on the climb.

They had no choice but to rest. Mountains stretched all around them, with no trees or other vegetation in sight, only exposed rock.

Ash and the others let out their Pokémon and set up camp with practiced ease. Some looked for firewood and water while the rest arranged the cookware. Everyone knew what to do, leaving Ethan with nothing to prepare. He took charge of watching the Pokémon instead.

Ash's Pikachu, Swellow, Grovyle, Corphish, and Phanpy; May's Combusken, Squirtle, and Munchlax; Brock's Forretress; and Ethan's Eevee gathered together to play. They made a cheerful, noisy crowd.

Ethan finished brushing Eevee's fur with a wooden comb. Then he leaned against a rock and found himself watching May's Munchlax for some time.

May noticed when she returned with water. She handed the bucket to Brock, who was cooking, then sat beside Ethan and looked at Munchlax too.

“What is it, Ethan? Is something wrong with my Munchlax?”

“Of course not.” Ethan laughed and shook his head. “Seeing it just makes me wonder when I'll catch a Snorlax or Munchlax of my own.”

“Oh? You like Munchlax too?”

“I do. They're strong and adorable. Apart from eating and sleeping a lot, what is there to complain about?”

May had never realized he liked this sort of Pokémon. In her mind, Ethan's Pokémon were all extraordinarily powerful, while she could hardly imagine describing her Munchlax that way.

“That reminds me,” Ethan said. “Misty told me you'd learned a Pokéblock recipe?”

May blushed. Pokéblocks were colorful, square treats made from blended Berries. They could fill a Pokémon's stomach and provide nutrients it needed, rather like vitamins. That sounded excellent in principle. May's Pokéblocks were another matter.

“Well…” She hesitated, embarrassed. “Mine might taste a little off. Munchlax doesn't mind, but the other Pokémon don't seem to like them much.”

May was putting it gently. Ethan knew her Pokéblocks were a catastrophe. What she made was painful to behold, with an effect to rival the dreadful meal Misty had once cooked when Brock fell ill during her travels with Ash. May's might even have been worse.

She called her creation “May's Delicious,” but neither Brock nor Max could stomach it. Every Pokémon the group had with them had taken a single bite and immediately fainted from the taste. That was how potent it was.

Only May's Munchlax, perhaps because it would eat anything, had finished its share and looked quite happy afterward.

May and her friend Misty had more in common than their cooking. Both had also had bicycles damaged by Pikachu. Still, their food did have one admirer. Jessie had tasted both Misty's cooking and May's Pokéblocks and found them delicious.

Jessie had an extraordinary talent of her own. Either her tastes were unique, or she really was a human-shaped Pokémon.

“What's wrong with that?” Ethan said, choosing not to mention how bad the Pokéblocks truly were. He smiled encouragingly. “People improve. How can you succeed without failing along the way? I'm sure more and more Pokémon will come to love your Pokéblocks.”

Those few words moved May deeply. She felt that Ethan believed in her, and in her heart she grew closer to him.

After a short rest, they set out again. For Ash and the others, the trip proved unusually smooth, apart from the hard walking. In a little over half a day, they crossed the mountains and a stretch of forest and reached Saffron City.

Ethan's presence was the reason. Team Rocket's trio dared neither approach nor cause trouble. With no one blocking the way, reaching the city was simply a matter of time.

They went straight to the Saffron Contest Hall. It shared the city's name with Saffron Gym, but the two stood in different places.

The Contest Hall occupied the center of a huge park within the city. Broad lawns, leafy woods, and a clear lake made the grounds beautiful and inviting. The hall itself was a grand, domed building.

Before they even entered the park, they saw a huge billboard outside its gates. It advertised this year's Saffron Contest. Several sleek, brightly colored Pokémon with carefully groomed coats posed around a giant Ribbon medal. Against its golden border, the Ribbon shone brilliantly.

“A Pokémon Contest…” Ash looked from the billboard to May and grinned. “It's your turn to show us what you can do!”

May smiled with confidence and was about to answer when she caught sight of someone familiar.

“Huh?” She pointed. “Isn't that Drew?”
