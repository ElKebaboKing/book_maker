The people of Sootopolis whom Zinnia and Ethan had mentioned were not the city's ordinary residents. They were the people who had lived in seclusion there before Sootopolis existed and had made a pact with the Draconid people.

Like the Draconids, they were guardians whose traditions had survived since ancient times, hidden from the wider world. The two groups existed alongside each other and protected the secrets of Rayquaza together.

There was one difference: the Draconids revered Rayquaza and wished to capture it, while the people of Sootopolis guarded the barrier.

Outsiders knew even less about the people of Sootopolis than they did about the Draconids. They were more mysterious still.

The only one Ethan knew by name was the Water Prince himself, Wallace. Very few people could know about that side of him. Perhaps even his niece Lisia had no idea.

If Ethan had not learned it from the games, how could he have guessed that a man who attracted attention everywhere he went was secretly a guardian?

Before Zinnia could recover from her surprise, he went on. “That's not all I know. I even know what the top of Sky Pillar is called: the Dragonhark altar.”

That put an end to Zinnia's suspicion that he was trying to draw information out of her. He knew far too much for an outsider. And she was certain the people of Sootopolis had not told him.

They only tended the barrier. They had never entered Sky Pillar and would not know about the Dragonhark altar inside it.

Then who had told him? Had he really been there?

Zinnia fell silent. After a while, Ethan asked, “Do you believe me now?”

She took a deep breath and forced herself to calm down. “What exactly do you want?”

“What I said before.” Ethan's tone did not change. “I wanted to work with you and ask you to help me find Rayquaza. Clearly, that was wishful thinking. So I'll have to go to the top of the tower myself, find Rayquaza, and catch it.”

Distraught, Zinnia missed the flaw in his claim. If reaching Sky Pillar were that easy for him, why had he gone to the trouble of calling her? She never thought to ask.

“That's impossible,” she said grimly. “Reaching the Dragonhark altar doesn't mean you'll see Rayquaza. It only answers the call of the Rainbow Meteorite, and that meteorite was lost long ago. You'll have made the trip for nothing.”

She was right about the missing meteorite. Without it, even the Draconids could not summon Rayquaza from the altar. That was why, when Ethan caught Deoxys, Zinnia had tried so desperately to get close to Rayquaza. It might have been her only chance to see it.

“Maybe,” Ethan said lightly. “Perhaps your people need the Rainbow Meteorite to see Rayquaza. But you're forgetting that I have Deoxys.”

Zinnia understood at once. Calling Rayquaza was not the only way to see it. They could attract its attention. A Pokémon as powerful as Deoxys could surely do that, especially so close to Rayquaza at Sky Pillar.

Ethan's bluff began to shake her resolve. She did not want an outsider to enter Sky Pillar. Yet if Deoxys drew Rayquaza down to the Dragonhark altar, she might see it again. She could not miss such a chance.

“I understand.” Zinnia finally gave in. “Where are you? I'll come to you.”

A smile of triumph crept onto Ethan's face, though his voice remained steady. “I'm in Lilycove City. How long will it take you to get here?”

She considered it. “I'm quite far away. I can't leave until tomorrow morning, and even then it'll take at least three days.”

Three days. Ethan nodded. That was more than enough time for a round trip to LaRousse City.

“All right. We'll meet in Lilycove in three days.”

He ended the call and returned to Ash and the others. They were all looking at him. He nodded to them.

“Looks like I have two days to relax after all.”

...

Morning sunlight reached into the boys' dormitory at the Pokémon Center, and Ethan opened his eyes. He had not wanted to wake yet. Noise had done it for him.

The clock on the wall showed a little after six. The source of the racket was, predictably, Ash. He and Max were arguing about something, and Ash's loud voice carried across the room.

Ethan sighed. Children were children in any world. How could they have this much energy so early?

It reminded him of his first school outing when he was young. He had probably been just as excited.

He stared at the ceiling, his mind drifting to memories of Earth. He found himself missing home.

After a few more minutes in bed, he pulled himself up, dressed, washed, and went to join the others in the dining area. He had slept for more than eight hours, but he still felt drained.

Everyone else was ready. Their luggage sat at their feet, and they could leave at any moment.

“You're so slow, Ethan!” Misty, sitting beside May, made a face at him. “Whenever we all stay at a Pokémon Center, you're the last to get up.”

Ethan yawned widely, ignored her, and sat down to eat breakfast.

At least there was good news about the trip to LaRousse. They would not have to walk a long way. A light rail train ran straight from Lilycove, so he could rest while they traveled. Every bit of strength he recovered might let him last a few more minutes in the coming battle.

He finished breakfast at his own pace while the others watched expectantly without daring to hurry him. At last he picked up his backpack and waved an arm. “Let's go!”

They left the Pokémon Center and went to the light rail station downtown. The trains had only two cars, but so many visitors seemed to be heading to LaRousse that one departed every ten minutes.

They bought their tickets and boarded. They had barely taken their seats when the train pulled out.

At first they chatted idly. Once the train climbed onto an elevated bridge, everyone fell quiet and looked out the windows.

The broad windows gave them a view over much of Lilycove. From up here the city looked even more prosperous, and the people below were no larger than grains of rice. It was a new way to see the place.

Beyond Lilycove, the train picked up speed. In about half an hour it had passed through thick forest and reached the area around LaRousse.

The city stood on a large island in the middle of a river, with Lilycove behind it and the river mouth not far ahead. Its position made travel easy and had helped the city grow.

Before they reached the island, they could see dozens of wind turbines in the distance, their blades turning without pause. Beyond the rows of turbines rose the technological city.

Strangely shaped high-rises filled the view. A place known as the city of technology surely had industries to match.

Ash and Max pressed their faces against the window, as though they wanted to get off at once for a closer look. Ethan glanced out and turned away. The sight impressed him a little, but he had seen plenty of such things before. It no longer held his attention.

Max's repeated cries of “Wow!” rang in his ears. He was as noisy as a frog.

When the train finally stopped and the doors opened, Ash and Max rushed out. By the time Ethan and the others stepped onto the platform, the two boys were already on the escalator, almost out of the station.

Misty and May exchanged helpless smiles. “Why are they in such a hurry?”

Ash had always been like that. Max used to be more sensible, but after traveling with Ash for so long, he seemed to be picking up the same habits. Ash's enthusiasm was contagious.

Ethan and the others followed them down the escalator. Before they could leave the station, a floating Block Bot made of small green blocks approached.

“Welcome to LaRousse City!”

A smile appeared on its lowest block as a bright voice rang out. While it spoke, the blocks shifted, and the words “Welcome” appeared on its display.

“This city really is high-tech!” Max adjusted his glasses. “What an amazing robot.”

Misty and May nodded. Brock folded his arms and looked faintly disappointed. “I'd rather have a pretty young lady welcoming us.”

The robot floated closer and extended a camera. It took several photographs of their faces.

May was first. She had not expected to be photographed and flinched in surprise, twisting her face into an awkward expression. The robot captured it.

After photographing everyone, the robot made a few noises and dispensed several passport cards.

“These are your city passports. You can use them to shop and travel. Please keep them safe!”

“Passports?” Ash and the others accepted the cards with fresh curiosity. Everyone else's photo looked normal. May's showed her startled, contorted face. Ash and Max immediately laughed at it.

The robot turned and floated off to greet other visitors. May stared at it, then at the passport in her hand.

“I want to take the photo again...”

It was too late. With a sigh, she followed the others out of the station, where they finally got a proper look at the city.

Even the nearest trash can was electronic. It recognized different kinds of rubbish and opened separate compartments to sort them.

“Wow, that's incredible...” Max had lost count of how many times he had said something like it.

Ash was no longer paying attention to the trash can. He ran a few steps ahead and looked around. “Where's that famous Battle Tower? Why can't I see it?”

As he searched the skyline, Ash failed to watch his feet. He stepped onto a moving walkway and was carried away.

Only then did the others notice the walkways running across the ground in every direction. That was how people traveled around LaRousse.

Startled, Ash tried to run back toward Ethan and the others. The walkway moved so quickly that even pumping his arms and legs could not bring him closer. It carried him farther away.

His strange struggle drew attention. Several fashionably dressed girls nearby covered their mouths and giggled. When it came to embarrassing himself, Ash never disappointed.

Ethan was about to help when another group came toward Ash on a neighboring walkway, traveling in the opposite direction.

One boy among them apparently could not bear to watch. He tossed a Poké Ball and released a Blaziken. In a few leaps, it reached Ash, caught him, and lifted him off the walkway.

“Whew... I'm saved.” Ash wiped the sweat from his brow and looked gratefully at the boy. “Thank you so much.”

“Don't thank me.” The boy barely looked at him. With a cool wave of his hand, as if the rescue had been nothing at all, he added, “I didn't save you for your sake. I just couldn't stand watching someone act so stupid.”
