When Ethan came ashore, Sabrina greeted him with a smile. A few seconds later, Cissy arrived on Blastoise, jumped onto the sand, and joined them.

"Congratulations. You've won the final race and earned Mikan Gym's Coral-Eye Badge!"

She took a pink shell from inside her clothes and offered it to him. Ethan looked closely. It had been polished smooth and gleamed in the sunlight. A blue stone set into the shell looked like an eye.

So this was the Coral-Eye Badge. Once it was in his hand, Ethan realized the blue piece was glass rather than a gemstone. The shell itself belonged to a kind found only around the Orange Islands, known as a sakura shell.

Its materials might not be rare or valuable, but the badge had been made with care. It was delicate and attractive. A girl would probably like something this pink.

He handed it to Sabrina. Her expression barely changed, but she kept turning it over in her hands. She liked the little ornament more than she let on.

Seeing their interest, Cissy explained, "All four Orange League Badges are made from shells. Each Gym uses a different kind."

Ethan nodded. He checked the time on his phone, then turned back to her. "It's getting late. We've done what we came for, so we'll be on our way."

"Wait." Cissy took out a small, finely made case and gave it to him. "This holds the Badges you'll collect. Take it as an apology for what happened earlier."

Ethan realized he had no place for his new Badges apart from the case he used for the Indigo League's. "Thank you. I'll put it to good use."

He stowed the case and called to Charizard, which had been sunning itself on the Gym roof. It swooped down low enough for Ethan and Sabrina to climb onto its back. Once they were seated, it beat its wings and carried them away from Mikan Island. Cissy watched them go.

They flew east over the sea, passing Mandarin Island North without landing. With daylight left, Ethan decided to continue toward the next island on their route.

By the time they reached Sunburst Island, evening had fallen. Ethan brought Charizard down by the shore, as he had before, where it would draw little attention.

On the beach, they saw a girl of five or six with two pigtails throwing glass bottles into the water one after another.

Charizard's descent startled her. She cried out, stepped back, and flung the rest of the bottles from her arms into the sea. Then she ran away without looking back, as though Ethan and Sabrina had interrupted something private.

Sabrina let go of Ethan's waist and climbed down. Watching the girl disappear, she tilted her head. "What was she doing?"

"I don't know," Ethan said. "We could pick up a bottle and find out."

He waded a few steps into the water and retrieved one that had not yet drifted away. In the last light of the sun, its glass shone orange. Sabrina came closer and saw a rolled sheet of paper inside: a message in a bottle.

"This isn't a deserted island," she said. "Anyone can make a phone call. Does someone still send messages this way?"

"Maybe it's a child's wish. She might hope the sea—or whoever finds the bottle—will help make it come true."

Ethan had remembered a story from the animated series involving a message in a bottle. To test his guess, he pulled out the cork and unfolded the note.

If anyone knows anything about a Pokémon called the Crystal Onix, please tell me. I would be very grateful. —Marissa of Sunburst Island

It was the story he remembered.

"Marissa must be the girl we saw," Sabrina said, looking the way she had gone. "But a Crystal Onix? Is it made of crystal? Does such a Pokémon really exist?"

Ethan studied the uneven letters, large and small, like a beginner's handwriting. "Of course it could. This world still has wonders we haven't found. Now that we have a lead, why don't we look for it?"

Sabrina gave him a helpless look. "A little girl's note might be nothing more than something she imagined. Is it worth spending time on a rumor we can't verify?"

Ethan had never forgotten the Crystal Onix from the series. He still vaguely remembered its story and where it lived.

"Don't worry. I happen to know a little about it myself." He rolled up the note, put it back in the bottle, pressed the cork into place, and threw it into the sea. "We won't be searching for nothing."

Dusk was fading. Through the sparse trees behind them, lights had begun to appear in town. They stood together for a little while, looking west over the water. When the sun had vanished below the horizon, they headed into town.

Sunburst Town was the island's main settlement and a distinctive center of the Orange Islands. It had no Gym or Pokémon Center, and few trainers visited. But it was famous for glasswork. Many islanders made their living as glass artisans.

Shops along the streets displayed glass objects of every shape. Visitors stopped to admire them, just as Ethan and Sabrina did.
