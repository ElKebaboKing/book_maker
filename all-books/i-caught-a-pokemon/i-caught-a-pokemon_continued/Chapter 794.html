Hariyama's defeat gave Bea the first round. Ethan, Misty, and May applauded from the side, and even Scott clapped with a smile.

As the man behind the Battle Frontier, Scott was happy to see either a Frontier Brain win or a challenger succeed. What mattered to him was a fine performance that showed the spirit of the Frontier.

While Ethan applauded, Bea stole a quick glance at him as though it meant nothing, then put her attention back on the match. No one, Ethan included, noticed.

Greta was disappointed, but she accepted the result. The first bout had been a straightforward contest of strength. Her Hariyama had lost because Pangoro was stronger, and there was nothing to argue about.

She recalled Hariyama, considered her remaining partners, and sent out Medicham for the second round.

Medicham was a humanoid Pokémon. Its head, legs, and lips were bright red with pale yellow spots; the rest of its body was white. It had a slender upper body, a fuller lower half, and long arms. The coloring made it look a little like a yoga practitioner or a Latin dancer.

After difficult yoga training, a Medicham could use its developed psychic power to predict an opponent's movements. It often went without food for long periods while meditating. By concentrating deeply, it could perceive an opponent's aura. Some accounts even claimed meditation raised its inner energy, sharpened its sixth sense, and let it blend into mountains and fields until its presence vanished.

As a Psychic- and Fighting-type Pokémon, Medicham fought with more technique than Hariyama. It could evade attacks with graceful, dance-like motions, then strike hard when it saw an opening.

Bea studied it, thought for a moment, and withdrew Pangoro. She sent Hawlucha into the battle area instead.

It was a questionable choice. Pangoro's Dark typing would have made it immune to Medicham's Psychic-type attacks. By replacing it, Bea had given up that advantage.

Still, Pangoro had taken damage in the first bout and lost stamina. No one knew whether it could finish another full round. Resting it would leave Bea a reserve for later. And unlike her other remaining partner, Grapploct, Hawlucha at least had the Flying type to help it fight back against Medicham.

At the referee's signal, the second round began. Knowing the matchup was unfavorable, Hawlucha attacked first.

Despite the “hawk” in its name and the cape-like feathers on its body, Hawlucha did not truly fly. It was light enough to leap high and use its wing-like cape to adjust its position, hanging in the air for a moment. That made its attacks hard to predict.

Medicham's flexibility served it well, though. With broad, supple movements, it slipped past Hawlucha's varied attacks. Hawlucha raised its two talons and lunged again, only to miss. It was visibly growing impatient.

Greta seized the opening. Fighters like her and Bea were quick to spot the smallest mistake. In an instant, Medicham used psychic power to make Hawlucha seize up, followed with a Fighting-type strike, and brought it down with surprising ease.

It was not simply a matter of overall strength. Medicham's pliant body had answered Hawlucha's forceful attacks perfectly, and its Psychic typing gave it another edge. The score was tied at one apiece.

Greta's students cheered. Ethan and the others watched Bea's calm face and knew the match was far from over.

She soon proved that all her travels and training had not been wasted. For the third round, Bea kept Pangoro in reserve and sent out Grapploct. Though it too faced an unfavorable type matchup, its four upper tentacles gave Medicham's flexible body nowhere to escape. Grapploct caught it, held it fast, and tightened its grip until Medicham fainted.

That put Bea ahead two to one. She was not finished. When her rested Pangoro returned to face Hitmonchan, it overwhelmed the Pokémon reputed to punch faster than a bullet.

One blow shattered Hitmonchan's hopes of winning. A second shattered Greta's hope of a comeback. With a long bamboo leaf between its teeth, Pangoro was a fearsome sight.

Bea won the match three to one, and made it look almost easy.

The students scratched their heads, unable to hide their disappointment. Ethan's group, by contrast, applauded quietly.

“I lost.” Greta shook her head ruefully and faced Bea. “You've trained your Pokémon exceptionally well. I couldn't match you.”

Though she was a Frontier Brain, Greta did not cling to her pride as some Gym Leaders might. She could admit defeat without making excuses. Unfortunately for her, Bea was not the best person to receive a gracious compliment.

Bea nodded once. “I lost one round. That proves I still haven't trained enough.”

Greta had no idea how to answer. If one lost round meant Bea lacked training, what did three lost rounds say about her? What would it say about the students she taught?

Bea's stiff delivery gave Greta a sense of her character. She shook her head and let it pass. Going over to Bea, she took a round, golden coin-like Symbol from inside her clothing.

“This is the Guts Symbol, awarded for defeating the Battle Arena. Please accept it.”

Bea thanked her, placed the Symbol in her backpack, then stepped aside and looked toward Ethan. He rose with Misty and May and approached Greta.

“Congratulations,” he told Bea first. “That was an excellent battle.” He turned to Greta, but before he could issue his own challenge, she shook her head.

“I'm sorry. I'd like to battle you now, but you've just seen all my Pokémon go down. I can't fight you until they've recovered.”

“Huh?” Misty blurted out. “Are those the only Pokémon you have, Greta?”

“Of course not.” Greta smiled. “I have plenty of others. But if I'm battling a champion, I want to use my strongest team. As you saw…”

So Hariyama and Medicham were among her best. Ethan wanted to face them at full strength too. With both injured, he would have to wait.

“All right. I'll wait.”

Greta looked relieved. Many Gym Leaders and Frontier Brains would welcome a serious battle against someone of Ethan's caliber. Win or lose, it would be valuable training for their Pokémon. She did not want to miss the chance.

Having settled that, Greta turned back to Bea. “May I ask you something? You're a skilled karate practitioner, aren't you? Would you be willing to spar with me, without our Pokémon?”

Every eye in the hall returned to the two women. A karate match between them would be a rare sight.

Bea paused in surprise, then nodded slightly. “Yes.”

Greta gave her Poké Balls to a student to take for treatment, straightened her clothes, and faced Bea. Her presence suddenly grew fierce. Ethan led Misty and May back to their places, clearing the floor. Scott even took off his sunglasses and fixed his eyes on the match. The students hardly dared breathe.

Bea met Greta's gaze without flinching. Her own fighting spirit rose. The two women gave low cries together and took their stances.

They circled and tested each other, searching for an opening while guarding against an attack. The tension filled the hall before either had thrown a strike.

Ethan knew nothing of karate or its finer terms. He was an outsider watching for the spectacle. In the time it took him to blink, Greta stepped forward and struck with both hands. Bea blocked and counterattacked. It happened so fast that after a second or two, they had already exchanged blows and separated again.

To Ethan, it looked even. The students' startled cries suggested Bea had gained the better of that exchange.

Misty leaned toward him. “Brother Ethan, can you tell what's happening?”

He shook his head. “I've never studied it. I don't even know the rules. Let's just enjoy watching. We'll know the result when it's over.”

Anyone could recognize an obvious advantage. In a brief exchange like that, though, who had landed a strike or earned a point was beyond him. It was the kind of moment that separated experts from beginners and those who only knew a little. Ethan did not need to be an expert to appreciate it.

Having gauged each other's skill in the first exchange, Greta and Bea stopped probing. They launched into a fierce contest, hands and bare feet flashing through a succession of short, violent clashes. They looked every bit as formidable as the Pokémon they had just commanded.

Ethan and the girls were engrossed. Even Greta's students stared; they could rarely have seen a karate match fought at this level. Then, without warning, the women stepped back at the same time and bowed to each other.

Was that it? Ethan could not tell who had won. Greta supplied the answer with a smile.

“People have called me a rare talent in Kanto, and I thought I was quite good. Today I met someone better. Thank you for holding back at the end.”

Talents seemed to be everywhere these days. Greta was a rare talent in Kanto; Bea had been called a once-in-a-century talent in Galar. The word was beginning to feel overused. Still, if Greta was thanking Bea for holding back, Bea must have come out ahead. Perhaps that extra century really did make a difference.

“You're too kind,” Bea said. “You took my attacks. I would call it a draw.”

“Come on.” Greta held out both hands, showing everyone her reddened palms. “Look at these. That last kick was so strong that even blocking it with both hands felt as though the bones might break. If we'd kept going, my hands would have swollen first. I lost.”

Now everyone understood. The exchange had looked even, but Greta's body had been the first to reach its limit. Her students stared at Bea in fresh amazement. She looked about Greta's age, yet she had even greater strength. Where had this formidable woman come from?

And Greta had lost at karate as well as in the Pokémon battle. In every contest today, the challenger had surpassed her.
