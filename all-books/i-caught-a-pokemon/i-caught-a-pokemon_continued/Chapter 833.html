Neither Ethan nor May had entered a race like this before. It did not take long for them to realize that keeping up their present speed would be unwise.

The problem was not how quickly they were tiring. The path itself had grown difficult. After the paved stretch near the start, a narrow trail wound through the forest, overgrown with weeds and scattered with stones that could trip an unwary runner.

They had no choice but to slow from a fast run to a jog. Their Pokémon had energy to spare. Eevee and Squirtle even found time to play as they ran, though it was mostly the restless Eevee pestering Squirtle.

Otherwise, the race went smoothly. Their first destination was Lunatone Rock, on the eastern side of the island. According to the map, it stood on a high, rocky hill with a lookout over the whole island. A great stone statue of Lunatone marked the checkpoint.

At the foot of the hill, the slope steepened. Ethan and May finally pulled away from the other contestants on their route. Their years of travel showed: accompanied by Eevee and Squirtle, they climbed nimbly and soon left the runners behind.

At the summit they found the statue, several meters high. Ethan could not tell how old it was. Wind and sun had worn the stone until, from a distance, its surface really did resemble the face of the moon.

The stamp sat on a platform beside it. Short on time, they gave the view below only a quick glance, went to the platform, and marked both maps with the Lunatone stamp.

That was their first checkpoint complete. They took another path down the hill, and May glanced at Ethan with a smile.

“Looks like we're tied so far.”

His long legs helped him cover ground, but May took quicker steps. Each had an advantage, and she had kept pace with him all the way.

“Don't get ahead of yourself,” Ethan said. “That was only the first checkpoint. The race has barely begun.”

“True. I wonder how everyone on the other routes is doing. If they finish ahead of us, that won't be good.”

Unlike in the anime, Ash and his friends were not among the entrants, and the Team Rocket trio were not around to cause trouble. That seemed to remove some strong opponents and distractions. But Ethan and May were visitors. Local contestants might know shortcuts and trail conditions they did not. They still had to give the race their best effort.

They hurried downhill without stopping and headed straight for Rainbow Falls. It was the checkpoint farthest from the Pokémon Center, but from Lunatone Rock it lay conveniently along their route.

A waterfall poured from the mountains into a pool below. A lovely rainbow shone within the falling water and never seemed to fade, like a bridge made of color.

Remoraid were firing Water Gun to form arches of spray. Ethan and May ran beneath them to a platform at the foot of the falls, found the stamp, and put their second mark on their maps.

They encountered several other contestants there. Ethan could not tell which routes they had taken. Everyone looked hurried, and each team departed in a different direction. At least they all followed the rules; no one tried to hide the stamp. Perhaps the cameras installed somewhere around the course helped discourage that.

On another day, Ethan and May would have stopped to admire the waterfall. Today they left almost at once for the next checkpoint: a cape overlooking the sea.

To reach it, they followed the riverbank. Ahead lay a lookout facing the ocean, with two upright pillars of rock nearby. The stamp platform stood at one side of the lookout. They marked their maps with its Kingler design.

The cape was another beautiful sight, but they had no time to linger. They set off for the fourth checkpoint.

By now May's youth and shorter experience of traveling were catching up with her. After running so far, her energy was failing and her pace slowed visibly.

Ethan realized she was no longer beside him. He looked back. She followed in silence with her head down, sweat on her forehead and at her temples. She could not keep this pace much longer.

He slowed at once. When she caught up, he ran beside her again.

May looked up, surprised. She bit her lip. “Why are you slowing down? This is your chance to get ahead of me.”

“How could I leave you here on your own?” Ethan smiled. “Winning a race and a pile of fruit matters less than the person I'm traveling with.”

He had put friendship before competition in a single sentence. May was deeply touched, and her cheeks flushed.

From then on, she no longer cared about beating Ethan to the finish. She stayed beside him with a lighter, happier heart.

For the rest of the course, he helped her over fallen logs and narrow crossings. They waded slowly through water together. When they grew hungry, they paused for energy bars. Though May was tired, Ethan's help kept her from falling farther behind.

There was a saying that work felt easier when a man and woman did it together. May did not know the phrase, but she felt its truth. With Ethan beside her, the race began to feel more like an outing in the wild. Her weariness seemed to lift, leaving her full of energy again.

In that mood they reached the fourth checkpoint, a giant tree three hundred years old. It was the tallest in the surrounding forest, its spreading branches thick with leaves and full of the presence of living nature. Beneath it they found the stamp and marked their maps.

Only one checkpoint remained. So far, everything had gone well. They knew nothing of the runners on the other routes, but no one on their own path had overtaken them. Even if they were not the fastest, they ought to be near the front.

Then they spotted Nurse Joy in the forest. Apparently she was competing too. She had helped organize and officiate the race, and now she was taking part in it herself.

They passed at a distance without calling out and continued toward the final checkpoint: a rope suspension bridge.

It spanned a deep river gorge. To reach the stamp, contestants had to slide down a long rope to the ground below, cross the hanging bridge, and reach a high stone pillar standing amid the river. The height and distance would be daunting to anyone afraid of heights.

Ethan and May were not troubled by it. They slid down the rope, landed lightly, and ran across the creaking, swaying bridge. At the pillar they pressed the fifth and final stamp onto their maps.

“We did it!” May lifted her right hand and jumped with delight.

Ethan watched her celebrate, smiling fondly. “It's a little early to celebrate. Let's get back to the finish.”

May stuck out her tongue in embarrassment. They could not let up at the last moment. She quieted down and followed him toward the Pokémon Center.

From some distance away, they could see a red ribbon stretched across the starting line. Crowds stood on both sides, apparently cheering for their return.

“We might actually be first. That's lucky,” Ethan said.

He had entered for fun, but a victory would make the day even more satisfying. May heard him and quietly eased her pace until she was half a step behind. She had wanted to compete with him before the race; now she was willing to give him the win.

Ethan reached back and drew her level with him.

“What are you doing?” he asked with a smile. “Why hold back when we're almost there?”

May understood. He wanted them to cross the line together, and the thought touched her even more.

Before, her feelings for Ethan had been a hazy admiration for his handsome face and his strength. Now his gentleness and consideration had won her over, much as they had won Misty over. They had not traveled together long, yet May was already falling for him.

She stopped hesitating and let him pull her along. With Eevee and Squirtle at their sides, Ethan and May broke the finish ribbon together.

“A new record!” Nurse Joy cried from beside the course. Somehow she had appeared there ahead of them. “Three hours and twenty-four minutes! Since the Potpourri Island race began, no one has ever finished in under three and a half hours. And these two crossed at exactly the same time! Congratulations to both of you!”

The spectators erupted in cheers. It might have been the first time they had seen two people finish together, and they studied the pair with curiosity.

May's face shone with happiness. She had hoped they would finish near the front. Breaking a record as well was a wonderful surprise. Surely no one could beat her and Ethan as a team.

She was pleased about the medals, too. It would be another honor to add to her Contest achievements, though the award was not what she cared about most. The plump, fragrant fruit was far more tempting.

May caught herself just before she began to drool.

A staff member came over to inspect their maps. After confirming all five stamps, he nodded to Nurse Joy. She smiled even more broadly, guided Ethan and May aside to rest, and returned to the finish to wait for the remaining contestants.

For Ethan and May, the race was over. It had added a bright memory to their long journey. For everyone else, it was still underway.

They waited more than two hours while the other teams gradually returned. Around noon, the oldest contestant reached the Pokémon Center with help from the staff. The Potpourri Island Pokémon Orienteering competition was finally complete.

Applause from Nurse Joy, the spectators, and the other entrants greeted Ethan and May as they stood together on the winners' podium. The event was held often enough that there were plenty of medals in storage, even though it had never produced two winners at once.

The fine fruit was another matter. It now had to be divided between two winners, and there did not seem to be enough to make two generous prizes. Fortunately, the winners were traveling together. Nurse Joy did not have to worry about how to divide the bounty between strangers.

She hung a gold Pokémon Teamwork Medal around each of their necks, then had the fruit brought over. Finally came the group photograph.

Eevee and Squirtle stood at either side. Ethan and May leaned close together, medals shining, and smiled toward the camera. With a click, the moment was captured.

The picture was printed almost immediately. May accepted it and studied the two of them, smiling so brightly and standing so close. They looked like a couple, she thought, even with their difference in age and height.

Her face reddened again. She gazed at the picture for a long while, smiling foolishly to herself, then carefully slipped it between the pages of the notebook she carried with her.

May's change of heart had reached its conclusion. Ethan had gained another devoted admirer.
