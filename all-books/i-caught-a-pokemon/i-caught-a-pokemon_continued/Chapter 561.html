Against Casey's Pidgey, Jolteon had a clear type advantage. But Ethan wanted his Pokémon to gain experience as they battled, so he took out its Poké Ball and recalled it.

Casey noticed.

She smiled. Despite losing the first round, she had chosen a Pokémon at a type disadvantage for a reason.

From recordings of Ethan's recent matches, she knew he rarely kept the same Pokémon on the field for consecutive rounds, regardless of the matchup. She had decided to take a gamble.

She had guessed right. When he saw Pidgey, Ethan withdrew the Jolteon that could have beaten it so easily instead of pressing his advantage.

Good! Without the type disadvantage, she had a chance to win.

A moment later, her smile vanished.

Ethan threw his second Poké Ball, and an elegant Pokémon appeared before Pidgey.

Casey recognized it at once: Ethan's Gardevoir.

Oh, this was bad...

Gardevoir was the first Pokémon Ethan had ever caught. Casey knew it was his ace and the partner he trusted most.

Sending a Pokémon like that against her Pidgey was almost unfair!

As Casey's delight turned to dismay, Ethan shook his head with a helpless smile.

His newer Pokémon from Johto needed to keep training through tournaments like this, but his long-standing partners from Kanto also needed an occasional battle to stay sharp. Every one of them deserved a chance on the field.

It simply happened to be Gardevoir's turn.

When the round began, Gardevoir fought as though it had decided that knowing its Trainer's opponent was no reason to hold back. It dispatched Pidgey cleanly.

The announcer and spectators could only imagine how helpless Casey must have felt.

Pidgey had no chance to fight back. Gardevoir used Teleport to evade its Quick Attack. When Pidgey launched Twister from a distance, Gardevoir dodged that too, dragged it to the ground with Confusion, and finished it with a rapid combination of attacks.

The gap between them was unmistakable. The round had been entirely one-sided.

The score was two to zero. Casey had lost two Pokémon without putting Ethan under any pressure.

"How can Casey turn this around?" the announcer asked, his voice subdued with concern for her.

Casey looked across at Ethan, drew a deep breath, and took out her third Poké Ball.

"Go, Rattata!"

...

"She's going to lose again..."

In a plaza outside the stadium, Ash, Misty, and Brock sat on a bench, each holding an ice cream. A nearby screen showed the match inside. Casey had now lost five rounds. Her last Pokémon, Beedrill, was fighting Ethan's Scizor.

Beedrill was in poor condition, its body covered in wounds. A moment's mistake let Scizor catch it in its great pincers. It looked close to defeat.

"Ethan's as strong as ever," Ash said enviously. "Casey can't fight back at all."

"Of course she can't." Misty tilted her head up proudly. "None of you competitors can beat him, if you ask me!"

Misty and Casey were about the same age, and Misty could sense Casey's vague affection for Ethan. Perhaps they bristled at each other because of that, or perhaps it was just their personalities. They had never quite gotten along since they met.

Brock glanced at Ash. "You know this isn't good news for you, right? If Ethan wins, then whoever wins your match with Gary will have to face him next."

"What?!"

Ash hurriedly pulled out the bracket the League had given him. Sure enough, his match with Gary sat beside Ethan's match with Casey. The winners would meet in the quarterfinals.

Misty covered her mouth to hide a laugh. "Fate really is playing tricks on you. Well, I'm looking forward to watching you do what you promised: beat Gary first, then knock Ethan out!"

Before Ash could reply, someone called his name from a distance.

"Ash!"

He turned and saw his mother, Delia, approaching with Professor Oak.

"Mom! Professor Oak!" He ran over to them in surprise. "What are you doing here?"

"We've come to cheer you on, of course." Delia looked at him with pride. "Reaching the final tournament is already a wonderful achievement. How could I miss the chance to support you in person?"

Professor Oak clasped his hands behind his back and smiled kindly. "I hear you and Gary have been drawn against each other?"

"Yes."

"Then both of you must do your best." Oak turned toward the screen. Ethan's Scizor had beaten Casey's Beedrill, and the camera had settled on Ethan's handsome face. "You're both excellent young Trainers, so I wouldn't be surprised whichever of you wins. Compared with him, though, you're still a little inexperienced. I hope you'll push past your limits and defeat him in the next round."

...

Ethan and Erika were leaving through the competitors' entrance when they met Ash's group coming toward them.

He was a little surprised to see Delia and Professor Oak there, but it made sense. With such an important match ahead, of course Ash's mother had come to watch.

"Professor, Aunt Delia. It's been a while."

"Congratulations on your victory," Oak said with a warm, grandfatherly smile. "You were remarkable. I still remember when we first met. You were so inexperienced then. After all your travels, you've grown tremendously. You're not only much stronger but more composed too."

They chatted briefly. Then, as Ash's match time approached, the group said goodbye and went into the stadium with him.

Erika watched them go. "Isn't Professor Oak Gary's grandfather? Why isn't he with Gary? He seems so close to Ash and his mother."

"Can't you tell?" Ethan laughed and repeated a joke he knew from his life on Earth. "Oak and Ash must be close because Oak's an old bachelor, and Ash's mother is a beautiful woman who's been single for years. Maybe he's hoping to court someone much younger!"

"Honestly." Erika rolled her eyes and gave his waist a light pinch. "At first I thought you actually knew something. Then you started talking nonsense. Don't joke about them like that. Imagine if they heard you! Delia seems such a kind, capable woman, and Professor Oak is so respected. Why would you think that about them?"
