On a stretch of level grassland outside Petalburg City, Ethan and Misty sat on a rock and waited.

After Kennedy's call, Ethan had looked at the city almost within reach and decided to put off his Gym challenge. Norman and his Gym would still be there later. First he would deal with Mr. Stone's request.

He wished he knew what was so urgent. Kennedy had told him nothing over the phone, only that someone would come to pick him up right away. The secrecy had made Misty curious too.

Just over twenty minutes later, the rumble of rotors sounded overhead. They looked up to see a medium-sized helicopter descending toward them.

A helicopter? Ethan had expected a car. Devon Corporation had sent an aircraft instead. He was impressed by the company's resources, and more curious than ever about what Mr. Stone wanted.

...

The helicopter landed on the rooftop helipad of Devon Corporation's headquarters in Rustboro City. As Ethan and Misty climbed out, Ethan spotted Kennedy waiting for them. After all this time, the secretary looked much the same, neatly dressed in a suit.

“Mr. Vale.” Kennedy glanced at Misty and gestured toward the building. “Mr. Stone is waiting for you. Please follow me.”

Ethan nodded and followed him toward the elevator without asking questions. Misty lingered a step behind to take one last look at the view from the roof before catching up.

She couldn't help being impressed. Ethan knew the president of a major company well enough for the man to send a helicopter for him. Traveling with Ethan felt nothing like traveling with Ash.

The elevator stopped on the fifteenth floor. Ethan had visited before, so he knew Mr. Stone's office was elsewhere. This floor housed the company's most tightly guarded research facilities.

Security was visibly stronger than on his last visit. Guards patrolled with Pokémon, cameras watched the corridors, and reinforced doors divided the area. Several guards glanced curiously at the unfamiliar man and girl Kennedy was escorting through.

“This looks better,” Ethan said to Kennedy with a smile. “It finally looks as though security means business.”

“We have you to thank for that.” Kennedy gave a rueful smile. “Team Aqua would have succeeded last time if you hadn't been here. Mr. Stone was furious afterward. He ordered a complete overhaul so it couldn't happen again.”

As they spoke, Kennedy led them into a large control room. Computers and monitors filled it. Among more than a dozen researchers, Ethan immediately spotted Mr. Stone.

The president came forward smiling. “Mr. Vale. We meet again!”

“Hello, Mr. Stone.” Ethan got straight to the point. “What's so important that you needed me here?”

Mr. Stone did not answer immediately. His gaze shifted to Misty. “And who is this?”

“Hello.” Misty introduced herself with easy confidence. “I'm Misty. I'm traveling with Ethan.”

Noticing that Erika was no longer with him, Mr. Stone asked, “Where is Miss Erika?”

“She went back to Kanto,” Ethan said. Why did he ask? “Is there something you need from her?”

Mr. Stone's brow creased. He looked disappointed, though he shook his head. “I wasn't looking for her specifically. I had hoped to draw on both of your strengths.”

Ethan understood that he meant their strength as Trainers. What problem required both him and Erika?

“Don't underestimate Misty because she's young,” he said with a smile. “Her sisters are the Gym Leaders of Cerulean Gym in Kanto. After this trip, she's going home to inherit the position. She's an excellent Water-type Trainer, and she's my best partner when I need help.”

Misty felt a rush of emotion. His best partner? Was that really how Ethan saw her?

“Oh?” Mr. Stone looked at her anew. “Then I misjudged you. I should have known that anyone traveling with Mr. Vale would be exceptional.”

He had not expected a girl who looked barely into her teens to be so capable, much less to become a Gym Leader. But he trusted Ethan's judgment. The young man's skill and character had both been proven. If Ethan said Misty was strong, she was strong. Mr. Stone relaxed.

Now Ethan was more puzzled than ever. “Mr. Stone, can you tell us why you called me here?”

“Of course.” Mr. Stone led them to a large screen and nodded to a researcher. After a few keystrokes, a video appeared.

It showed a satellite's view of space. Stars glimmered against the dark, and Earth filled much of the frame. Its oceans, continents, mountains, and snowfields were clearly visible.

“Is that our Earth?” Misty asked.

She used the same name Ethan had used for the planet where he had lived before coming to this world. Here, though, it meant the planet they lived on now.

“Yes,” said Mr. Stone. “That's our planet.”

Misty nodded, wondering why he wanted to show them a view of Earth. Then she realized that Earth was not the subject of the recording.

An enormous object raced in from deep space. It looked something like a meteorite, though not quite. In an instant it struck the satellite's solar panel and destroyed it. The force sent the satellite tumbling.

Through the spinning view, they saw the strange meteorite continue toward Earth and strike a continent. Then the recording ended.

Misty was bewildered, but she recognized the shape of the land where it had fallen. “That was Hoenn, wasn't it? Do you want us to find the meteorite?”

“Yes,” Mr. Stone said. “Though we've already located it. We ran into trouble when we tried to bring it back for study.”

“What kind of trouble?” Misty asked. “Radiation? Something else?”

By then, Ethan had a good idea where this was going. Meteorites were not particularly rare in the Pokémon world. Research institutes studied them in hopes of discovering something useful inside. But one kind of meteorite was unlike the others.

Such a meteorite often carried a Pokémon from space within it. It could serve as the Pokémon's home and its means of travel, carrying it across the cosmos until it reached some far-off destination or fell like a shooting star onto an unfamiliar planet.

That Pokémon was Deoxys, the Mythical Psychic-type Pokémon.

After a pause, Mr. Stone asked, “Have either of you heard of Deoxys?”

Ethan said nothing. Misty glanced at him, then shook her head. “Deoxys? Is that a Pokémon?”

“It is,” Mr. Stone said. “A friend of mine named Professor Lund encountered something extraordinary four years ago while conducting research in Antarctica. He had established a research station there when a meteorite landed nearby. A Pokémon no one had discovered before emerged from it. That Pokémon was Deoxys.”

Ethan knew the incident Mr. Stone meant. It was the story of one of the Pokémon movies from his previous life.

Deoxys were not vanishingly few on this planet, either. At least five had appeared across the animated series and films Ethan knew, and two had appeared together in one film. Those were only the ones people had discovered. More might have landed in uninhabited regions or in the sea.

“A Pokémon from space...” Misty nodded. “So there's a Deoxys inside that meteorite. You want us to catch it for you?”

“No.” Mr. Stone sighed. “I'm interested in the meteorite, not in catching a Pokémon. As you saw, it destroyed one of Devon's experimental satellites. The satellite is useless now, but that loss is minor. The meteorite's research value is what matters to me.”

He went on. “Rare elements, rare forms of energy—discovering and using them could teach us more about the universe and advance our technology. Once we identified the impact site, we sent armed teams several times to recover the meteorite. Deoxys defeated them all.”

“Why not ask the police for help?” Misty asked.

Mr. Stone glanced at her. “This is a matter of business competition. We received the information first and located the meteorite first. Naturally we want to retrieve it before anyone else. If we bring in the police, other interested parties will hear about it, and then...”

He did not need to finish. Misty understood. She had simply never had to think about such corporate maneuvering before.

“I see.”

Mr. Stone turned back to Ethan with a smile. “I imagine a Pokémon like Deoxys interests you too, Mr. Vale. So will you help us?”

“Of course I will,” Ethan said, smiling.

Deoxys was rare, and Ethan genuinely liked it. He could hardly ignore the chance to meet one. Mr. Stone had probably counted on that. A Trainer of Ethan's ability would not be tempted by an ordinary Pokémon, but Deoxys was another matter.

Still, one question occurred to him. “This is important. Why didn't you send Steven? I'm sure he could bring the meteorite back.”

Mr. Stone gave a strained smile and said nothing. Kennedy answered for him.

“We don't know where Master Steven has gone this time. We can't reach him by phone, and even satellite tracking has failed.”

Ethan shook his head. Steven was probably deep in some cave, digging for stones. No wonder no signal could reach him there. If he loved digging so much, why hadn't he become a miner?

“All right. There's no time to waste. I can leave now. Do you have a Poké Ball transfer machine here?”

“We do,” Mr. Stone replied. “What do you need it for?”

“To bring in some reinforcements.” Ethan smiled. “Deoxys won't be easy to face. If I'm going to beat it, I'll need some of my old team with me.”

...

Every minute of delay gave someone else a chance to reach the meteorite first. Ethan accepted the assignment, exchanged the Poké Balls he needed, and left promptly with Misty. Mr. Stone saw them off as the helicopter rose from Devon's roof and headed toward the impact site.

Mr. Stone also supplied them with new equipment for the operation. Deoxys could alter magnetic fields, rendering ordinary communications and other devices useless nearby. Even many Electric-type Pokémon had been affected to some degree. The helicopter would have to land before it reached the worst of it.

Without equipment designed for those conditions, Ethan and Misty might find themselves living like modern-day primitives before they even found Deoxys.
