With a long blast of its horn, the great luxury liner left port and headed into the deep blue sea.

Once aboard, Ethan and Misty went to the passenger cabins to put away their luggage. They would be at sea for two days and two nights. It was a long journey, but a comfortable one: they could rest, train, or battle other passengers along the way.

On an upper deck, they found their room by its number. Ethan swiped the key card and opened the door. Misty immediately spotted the bright floor-to-ceiling window. Beyond it lay the wide sky, the endless ocean, and an unobstructed view of the deck.

“Wow!” She dropped her bags, hurried to the window, and leaned against it to look out. “What an amazing view!”

“Of course.” Ethan smiled. “I paid a lot for it. It's not the best suite on the ship, but it's far better than a standard cabin.”

The room was luxurious. It had a living room, bathroom, bedroom, and kitchenette, along with appliances, a massage chair, and everything else they needed. With that view, it could have passed for a room in an expensive hotel.

Why spend so much? They had more than two days at sea ahead of them. A comfortable place to stay would make the trip more pleasant. Ethan considered it money well spent.

Misty had moved on to the wide, soft bed and was rolling across it with a delighted expression. Ethan shook his head, put away their luggage, and beckoned her to join him. Together, they explored the ship.

It was the most luxurious liner Ethan had ever boarded—bigger, taller, and grander than the S.S. Anne or any other ship he had traveled on.

The cabins were nearly full. Four or five thousand people shared the vessel, making it feel like a small city. Passengers filled its cabins, decks, swimming pools, ballroom, playground, movie theater, and gym. Everywhere was lively.

The largest crowd gathered around a Pokémon battle on deck. A man and a woman were directing their Pokémon while spectators watched. Ethan and Misty stopped for a while, but the Trainers were not especially skilled, and neither showed anything remarkable. The two moved on.

The liner sailed steadily onward. Behind it, the harbor had shrunk to a tiny dot.

After their tour, Ethan kept the promise he had made the night before. He and Misty found an empty pool toward the stern and began training.

Ethan released Feebas first. It swam contentedly around the pool. When Misty released her Staryu, the two Pokémon played together in the water for a while.

Misty looked up at Ethan. “How do you want to train it?”

“By battling, of course. But Feebas only knows Tackle and Splash so far. Staryu will need to go easy on it and give it attacks it can learn to answer, a little at a time.”

“Got it. I'll hold back. Let's begin!”

...

Over the next few hours, several of Misty's Water-type Pokémon took turns as Feebas's training partners. Even while holding back, they were much stronger than Feebas could handle.

By the end of the morning, Feebas was exhausted. It floated motionless on the water like a dead fish.

Ethan found the sight funny but did not press it further. He understood that rushing would only make matters worse. Training required rest as well as effort.

By the time they had eaten lunch, the ship had completed a third of its voyage. While they stood at the rail afterward, looking out over the sea, Misty noticed something like a wreck in the distance.

“What's that?” she asked.

“Sea Mauville, I think,” Ethan said.

“Sea Mauville? What is it? It sounds as though it has something to do with Mauville City.”

Ethan searched his memory of the games he had played before coming to this world. “It used to be some sort of large research facility. It was abandoned for reasons I don't remember. Now anglers love it. The currents there are complicated, and people travel all the way out from the mainland to catch rare Water-type Pokémon.”

Someone leaned against the rail beside him. A woman's voice said, “You know quite a bit. Not many people remember Sea Mauville now.”

Ethan turned. The stranger wore a red leather jacket over a bandeau top, shorts, and knee-high boots. Her long blonde hair fell in styled curls. Heavy makeup made her exact age hard to judge; she looked somewhere between her late twenties and early thirties. She was fairly attractive, and there was a faint scent of perfume and tobacco about her.

Ethan and Misty exchanged a glance. Neither had met her before, and they did not know why she had approached them. Misty was especially wary of a pretty woman dressed so boldly coming up to Ethan.

But the stranger showed no sign of being interested in him that way. Looking toward Sea Mauville, she said, “What you said isn't quite right. Sea Mauville was an undersea energy extraction platform owned by Greater Mauville Holdings. When the New Mauville project was canceled, the company suffered a severe blow and eventually went bankrupt. The platform was abandoned.”

She continued, “Before it could be dismantled, investigators found that it had developed a unique ecosystem. They preserved it as a nature reserve. Over the years, the ruins and their surroundings became part of the same landscape. That unusual sight led to its later renovation as a tourist attraction.”

Misty was surprised by how much she knew. “How do you know all that?”

“My father worked there before he died.”

That brief answer explained it. As they spoke, the liner drew close enough for them to see the whole site from the upper deck.

The broken structure did look like a drilling platform. Its rear half had sunk beneath the sea, leaving only part of the ruins above the surface. Many Water-type Pokémon lived in the shallows nearby, treating the place as their own refuge.

Human construction had changed the ocean. In turn, the ocean had changed what people had built.

The liner continued on, and Sea Mauville slowly disappeared from view.

The woman turned back with a smile. “I'm Diane, a magician's assistant. You two are Trainers, aren't you?”

“Huh? How did you know?” Misty asked. She glanced at their clothes. Nothing about them seemed to give it away.

Diane shrugged. “I saw you at the pool on the aft deck this morning, training Feebas. I figured only Pokémon Trainers would use battles to train it like that.”

“Oh, I see.” Misty's eyes brightened. “You said you're a magician's assistant? I've loved magic shows since I was little! I didn't expect to meet someone from one today. You must be really good!”

“The truly talented one is over there.” Diane tipped her chin toward a deck chair in the distance. A silver-haired man lay in it, sunbathing behind oversized sunglasses. “He's my partner, Butler—one of the finest magicians in the world.”

Butler? The man was reclining and wearing sunglasses, so Ethan could not see his face clearly. The name meant nothing to him. He knew little about stage magic and had no reason to know its stars, though Butler must be accomplished to have such a reputation.

“Where are my manners? I'm Ethan Vale, and this is Misty. You saw correctly: we're Trainers, preparing for the next part of our journey.”

Misty asked, “Where are you and Butler going?”

“Forina. We're meeting the rest of our troupe there before a performance.”

Forina was another unfamiliar name to Ethan. From the map, he thought it lay somewhere inland in Hoenn, but he knew little else about it.

What caught his attention was Butler. When Ethan introduced himself, the magician had stolen several looks at him. Ethan was certain he had felt the man's gaze—and something faintly hostile in it.

It made no sense. If Butler was annoyed that Diane had approached Ethan, it was hardly Ethan's fault. She had come to him.

Then Ethan reconsidered. Butler had seen him talking to Diane earlier without reacting. It was only after Ethan gave his name that the hostility appeared. Butler must know who he was, or at least have heard something unfavorable about him.

Perhaps Diane's approach had not been accidental either. Ethan had no proof; it was only a suspicion. Still, it was enough to put him on guard.

He kept smiling and gave no sign of what he was thinking. The three chatted casually for a while. Everything looked ordinary. Butler, too, turned away and stopped watching them, as though nothing had happened.

After they parted, Ethan took Misty back to their cabin. As soon as the door closed, he called Gengar out.

“What is it?” Gengar had been reading a comic. It drifted out of Ethan's shadow and looked at him in confusion.

Ignoring Misty's equally puzzled expression, Ethan asked, “Can you temporarily draw a person's soul out of their body?”

He remembered Ash's visit to the Pokémon Tower in Lavender Town. Hoping to catch a Ghost-type Pokémon to face Sabrina, Ash had encountered Haunter and Gengar. They had separated his spirit from his body, and he had spent a long time in the tower that way.

“Of course I can,” Gengar said. “But why? It's dangerous. If anything goes wrong, you could really die. The ability is unstable during the day, too.”

Ethan told Gengar and Misty what he had noticed and what he suspected. “I want to find out what's going on. If those two are planning something against me, I'd rather know in advance.”

“So that's it!” Misty said, only now putting the pieces together. “I had a feeling Diane might have an ulterior motive. If you're right, they could be hiding something.”

They both looked at Gengar. It hesitated, then nodded. “All right. I'll be careful.”

Ethan took a deep breath, told Misty to watch over his body, and lay down on the bed without changing his clothes.

Before closing his eyes, he turned to her uneasily. “Stay here. Don't let anyone come in. And...don't do anything strange to my body.”

“What kind of person do you take me for?” Misty waved him on. “Leave this to me. Go find out what those two are doing!”

Ethan almost said something else. Had he seen a glint in her eyes? Never mind. He pushed the thought away, relaxed, and closed his eyes.

Gengar floated over his head and reached down. The instant it touched him, Ethan felt weightless. Then his soul drifted out of his body, beyond his control.
