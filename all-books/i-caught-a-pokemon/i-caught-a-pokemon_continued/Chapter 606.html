Roxanne took out her second Poké Ball. A flash of blue light revealed a Nosepass in front of Ethan.

It was an unusual Pokémon, as if a huge boulder had come to life. Its whole body was blue, with arms, legs, and a large red nose.

Nosepass was a pure Rock type. The magnet in its nose never failed and always pointed north. If someone became lost in the mountains, one look at a Nosepass's nose would tell them which way to go. That was how the Pokémon got its name.

There was another curious fact about the species. Because like magnetic poles repelled each other, two Nosepass could not bring their faces close together. Wild Nosepass were said to move only one centimeter in a year, though they could spin and dive underground in an instant when threatened.

A Nosepass could remain perfectly still and draw prey toward it with the powerful magnetism its body gave off. That magnetism grew stronger in colder seasons. Sometimes, however, it attracted a predator instead: Metang.

Ethan called Eevee back to his side and studied the Nosepass.

He remembered Roxanne's Nosepass well from the games. It was her signature Pokémon and had left a strong impression on him.

It was no pushover. When he challenged her in the games, a team without sufficiently strong Pokémon or a type advantage could easily need several Pokémon just to beat it.

Like Geodude, this Nosepass had a cold, stony body and looked difficult to damage. Eevee might not even be able to break its surface.

Ethan did not want Eevee to batter itself against a rock like that. After a moment's thought, he decided to answer Nosepass with a Pokémon that could meet its toughness head-on.

Before he had quite settled on his choice, something brushed against his trouser leg. He looked down. Eevee was gazing up at him, eager to go.

He raised an eyebrow. “What? You still want to battle?”

Eevee nodded emphatically and cried out.

The great stone Pokémon was much larger than Eevee, and Eevee's claws and teeth might not be able to scratch or bite through it. But Eevee was not afraid.

Ethan knew Eevee was unlikely to win. Even so, he agreed to let it take the field again.

Earning a Gym Badge was only one purpose of this challenge. More than that, he wanted to develop his Pokémon's abilities.

Even if Eevee lost and got hurt, it could still learn something from the defeat.

As soon as Ethan nodded, Eevee ran back to the middle of the field.

Roxanne had seen their exchange and understood what Ethan intended. She said nothing, merely smiling a little.

Once the battle began, she held nothing back and focused on directing Nosepass. Ethan, by contrast, fought much more cautiously.

Under his commands, Eevee gave everything it had. It raced around the rocky field, attacking from different angles and trying to bring Nosepass down. But it could not overcome the type disadvantage. At last, Nosepass used Rock Tomb, and Eevee fell.

The student serving as referee raised a colored flag. “Eevee is unable to battle! Nosepass wins the second round!”

With Eevee's defeat, the score stood at one to one.

Misty watched without much concern. Eevee was unevolved, had limited ways to attack, and faced a poor type matchup. It was never likely to hurt Nosepass badly. Its defeat had been predictable, but she knew Ethan would send in a stronger Pokémon to turn the battle around.

From his Trainer's area, Ethan recalled the unconscious Eevee.

The match had been brief, but it had not been pointless. Eevee had gained more battle experience, and Ethan had learned something about Nosepass that would help him in the next round.

He returned his attention to the field. This time he did not hesitate. With a sweep of his arm, he sent out Tyranitar.

Like the red Gyarados and Crystal Steelix before it, Tyranitar threw back its head and let out a deafening roar the moment it appeared.

“Raaah!”

Several of the more timid students went pale and stepped back. Even Misty, sitting in the stands, covered her ears.

No matter how many times she saw a fierce Pokémon like this one, its temper still frightened her.

Its strength aside, that sheer presence was impressive.

Roxanne nodded when she saw Ethan's choice.

Unlike Eevee, Tyranitar had the type advantage. Its Ground-type moves could hit Nosepass hard, and its armored body would not fare badly against stone. It was a sound choice.

Even so, Roxanne knew the match would be difficult for her.

She had watched Ethan's Silver Conference battles and seen how well this Tyranitar performed.

She could not be confident against a Pokémon capable of competing at that level. Perhaps her strongest Pokémon could give it a fight, but Nosepass...

Nosepass had helped her defeat many challengers, yet Roxanne knew it would probably struggle against Tyranitar.

It was not fear before the battle. It was the conclusion her experience and judgment had led her to.

The referee raised the flag again, and the third round began. If Ethan beat Nosepass, he would earn his first Hoenn Badge. If he lost, he would have to return and try the Gym again tomorrow.

Nosepass remained motionless. Tyranitar stamped a foot. Energy surged, and more than a dozen sharp stones burst up from the ground and thrust toward Nosepass.

Almost at the same instant, Nosepass used Stone Edge too. One wave of stone spikes raced from east to west and the other from west to east, until they crashed together.

Bang, bang, bang!

The two Stone Edge attacks shattered against each other, spraying fragments into the air. The chips fell back onto both Pokémon, striking their hard bodies with a series of little clinks.

As the dust and stones cleared, both Pokémon opened their mouths almost at once and launched their second attacks.
