After thinking it over, Ethan guessed May had chosen Grass- and Fire-type Pokémon to make a combination stronger than either could manage alone. Whatever her plan, the teams were set. He could only hope she performed well.

Vivian gave the signal. The timer started, and so did the match.

Drew attacked first. Ethan noticed a pattern. May had also been a beat slow to act against Harley and Anthony, surrendering the initiative and leaving herself to defend and counterattack. It was a poor habit. An early attack could score the first points and put the other Coordinator on the defensive. May had not had enough time to polish away habits like this. Another region of travel would teach her a great deal.

Roselia used Petal Dance. Flygon darted through the shower of petals and flew at Combusken and Skitty. May reacted calmly despite going second. She ordered Skitty to use Assist, which borrowed Combusken's Fire Spin. Flames caught the petals and dispersed them. Combusken then leaped with Sky Uppercut, forcing Flygon to pull back. Its jump fell a little short, though, and the punch missed.

May and her friends in the stands all winced at the missed opportunity. Drew was untroubled and prepared another attack.

This time Flygon used Flamethrower while Roselia used Solar Beam. The two powerful moves rushed toward May's team. There was little time to dodge. May ordered Combusken to answer with Fire Spin.

On its own, Fire Spin should not have been strong enough to hold back both attacks. Yet when the moves met, they formed a larger whirlpool of flame and light. Its regular rotation resisted Flamethrower and Solar Beam, preventing either from advancing. The crowd watched in astonishment.

Drew was surprised too. In his brief hesitation, May called out again. Combusken poured more fire into the spinning column, which threw the incoming attacks back toward their owners. Flygon and Roselia were hit by the rebound. An explosion filled the field with smoke, and a large section of Drew's yellow point bar disappeared.

“Yes!” Ash punched the air. “May got the first big hit! Maybe she really can beat Drew!”

Misty and the others were delighted too. A few more exchanges like that could carry May into the semifinals.

Ethan hated to dampen their excitement, but he said, “May and her Pokémon are doing well. I still think Drew will win.”

“What?” Ash stared at him. “But May's performing better!”

“She just sent their moves back at them,” Misty added.

The others could not see why Ethan sounded certain. Brock thought for a moment. “Do you mean Drew hasn't been fighting seriously yet?”

Ethan nodded. Brock's level head served him well when he was not distracted by an attractive woman.

“Look at Drew's face. He's been composed from the beginning. He's not underestimating May or acting out of arrogance. He's testing her without feeling pressed.”

“That doesn't mean she'll lose,” Ash objected.

“No. But look at his Pokémon. They took that counterattack and still haven't panicked. They seem ready for more.”

Ash and the others studied Roselia and Flygon. Ethan was right. Neither appeared badly hurt, and both still looked eager to fight.

“Drew sees May as a worthy rival,” Ethan continued. “He wants to know how much she's grown. I think that was his test. Now he'll give her everything he has.”

It was the sort of observation a champion made. As Ethan finished speaking, the match changed. Drew's attacks came faster.

May could not adjust at once and fell behind. Time ticked away. Flygon and Roselia attacked repeatedly, while Combusken and Skitty defended and seized what chances they could to counter. The struggle remained fierce, but the balance was shifting toward Drew.

Even spectators who could not follow every exchange saw May's yellow point bar shrinking toward the edge. Ash and the others grew quiet.

0:45... 0:44...

May's Pokémon did not yet look exhausted, but her point bar had only one segment left. Drew still had nearly half of his. One more hit might eliminate her.

May knew it. When Drew ordered Flygon to use Flamethrower again, she launched a final counterattack.

The flames shot straight at Skitty and Combusken. Combusken leaped into them. Wrapped in fire, it rose beneath Flygon and delivered a Sky Uppercut. The punch scattered the flames and struck Flygon hard in the face. The audience answered with applause and cheers.

It was a brilliant move, but May could not turn the match around. As Flygon landed, Drew countered at once. Roselia released Stun Spore, paralyzing Combusken and Skitty. Flygon followed with Steel Wing.

Bang!

Unable to dodge, both of May's Pokémon were knocked backward. Her point bar reached zero. The clock stopped at 0:03.

Only three seconds had remained in the five-minute match. Even without Drew's final attack, however, May would have lost on points when time expired.

The crowd cheered Drew's victory and applauded May too. She had given them a wonderful battle despite losing. After a brief moment of disappointment, May comforted her Pokémon, stood, and bowed to the spectators still calling for her.

Ethan and his friends rose from their seats and went to the competitors' exit. They met May as she came off the stage.

“You were amazing, May!” Max said.

“You did so well!”

Caroline, Misty, Ash, and Brock joined in. None seemed disappointed that she had been eliminated; they were sincerely proud of her. Ethan stood at the back of the group and applauded quietly.

Reaching the top eight was an excellent result. May had started among 247 Coordinators, all of whom had earned five Ribbons. This was her first event on such a scale, and she had advanced farther than most. She had even outdone Ash's result at his first Indigo League tournament—but Ethan decided not to bring that up.

They left the arena together and ate lunch at a nearby restaurant, watching the remaining matches on television. Drew defeated another opponent to reach the final, where Robert awaited him. One of them would win the Grand Festival.

Ash sipped his juice. “Who do you think will win?”

“Robert,” one friend answered.

“I think Robert too. He's so strong.”

Ash had not expected such agreement. “Really? I thought some of you would say Drew!”

“Drew's excellent,” Brock said, “but Robert is a little stronger. I don't think the outcome is much in doubt.”

Ash turned to Ethan, hoping for a different answer. “Robert,” Ethan said. “They've already given my reasons.”

The television proved them right. Drew fought with everything he had, but Robert's Milotic and Claydol seemed a level above his team. With more than two minutes left, Drew's point bar had fallen to its final segment while Robert still held most of his. The result was almost certain.

Drew refused to give up. He fought until the clock ran out, his two Pokémon collapsing only at the very end. Amid a roar of cheers, Robert became the Grand Festival champion.

May watched Drew's disappointed face and clenched her fists. Drew had beaten her convincingly, only to lose to Robert in much the same way. The gap between them felt like a chain she could see but could not yet cross.

The group stayed with the broadcast through Robert's award presentation and the Festival's closing. As at the opening, there was a performance. To Misty's displeasure, the singer was Lisia.

Misty watched her charm the crowd. Gradually, her gaze hardened. She had made a decision.

...

At eight that evening, an enormous outdoor party filled the Grand Festival plaza. The closing banquet was a Festival tradition, held to thank every contestant for their hard work and celebrate the new champion. Only after everyone had eaten and enjoyed themselves would the event truly be over.

The food was served buffet-style and was so good that Ash and the others had wandered off in different directions. May sat at a long table and finished the last bite on her plate. She was about to take a walk when Misty came over.

“May, will you walk with me?”

May paused. There was something unusual in her friend's voice. Had something happened? She did not ask. She stood and nodded.

“Of course.”
