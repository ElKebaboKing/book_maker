As expected, Feebas lost its battle against Corphish.

But Ash showed no joy at winning. If anything, he felt as though he'd been tricked.

Feebas had used a fish Pokémon's natural agility to dodge many attacks in the water, and it had landed several Tackles on Corphish. Even so, Corphish's two large claws quickly brought it down.

The match had gone so smoothly that Ash felt little satisfaction in his victory. A faint disappointment lingered. This was nothing like the battle he'd imagined!

“Ethan, your Feebas is so weak! That wasn't hard at all. This isn't like you!”

Ash grumbled, and he had a point. In his experience, every one of Ethan's Pokémon was immensely strong. Ash had to give everything he had to beat even one of them, and compared with those Pokémon, this Feebas was hardly worth mentioning.

“Like me?” Ethan, on the other hand, was perfectly at ease. He smiled. “What's strange about it? Every Pokémon starts out weak and grows stronger. Feebas might not look like much now, but perhaps the next time you meet, it'll surprise you.”

Ash thought about it and had to agree. Every Pokémon went through a stage like this. He stopped dwelling on the match, and his mood brightened.

Besides, whatever else had happened, he'd defeated one of Ethan's Pokémon. Most people couldn't say that!

And yet...

No matter how he tried to console himself, he couldn't quite shake his disappointment.

Ethan saw his expression and smiled. “If that battle wasn't enough for you, how about I give you another?”

Ash's eyes lit up. He nodded eagerly. “Sure! Bring it on! This time I'm really going all out!”

Seeing Ash fired up again, Ethan pointed toward the open ground beside them instead of taking out another Poké Ball. “Let's change fields. My second Pokémon isn't a Water type, so we won't need the pool.”

Ash had no objection. Ethan's serious preparations only made him look forward to their second battle more. On a different field, Ethan certainly wouldn't use Feebas again. Ash went to the ordinary battlefield and took his place opposite Ethan in the other Trainer's box.

Under Ash's expectant gaze, Ethan unhurriedly took a second Poké Ball from his waist pouch and threw it.

Blue light flashed. A Pokémon resembling a stone gauntlet appeared before Ethan, floating in midair.

What was that? Ash's eyes shone. He had never seen this Pokémon either, and he immediately pulled out his Pokédex.

It soon told him that the unusual creature was Beldum, a Steel- and Psychic-type Pokémon.

Steel and Psychic? Ash nodded. Psychic Pokémon were often powerful, and with Steel as well, Beldum could surely attack from a distance and defend itself up close. This one had to be something special! His anticipation for the second battle grew.

That pleasant feeling lasted only until he actually fought Beldum.

Ash sent out the Treecko he had caught recently. Its Grass type put it at a disadvantage against Beldum's Steel type, but Ash wasn't worried about that.

Once the battle began, though, something felt wrong again.

Beldum hovered in midair and looked impressive, yet all it seemed able to do was Tackle. It had even fewer options than Feebas! And the open field made its straight-line charges easy for Treecko to block or dodge. Beldum couldn't make any use of its type advantage.

Wait a minute...

Ash frowned. He was about to ask why Beldum seemed so weak too when Treecko knocked it down before he could speak!

Even Ash now understood what had happened.

“Hey!” He jumped up in anger, waving his arms like a child. “You tricked me again! This wasn't what I expected!”

“Hahaha!” Ethan couldn't help laughing at his reaction. “How did I trick you? I only said we'd have another battle. I never said which Pokémon I'd use. You're the one who made an assumption. How is that my fault?”

Ash opened his mouth to argue, then realized Ethan was right. He had never promised to use a particular Pokémon. Ash had simply assumed that, after Feebas, Ethan would send out someone stronger.

He'd been had again!

How annoying!

“Those two...”

Brock, who had seen exactly what was going on, shook his head helplessly. Unlike the oblivious Ash, he knew Ethan was using him as a practice partner, giving Feebas and Beldum battle experience. Only someone as straightforward as Ash would fall for the same trick twice.

Still, Ethan had a childish side too. He really ought to act a little more mature.

Just then, Beldum, lying on the field but still conscious, was enveloped in white light.

The glow drew all three of their eyes. Even the two little boys battling on the other field turned to look.

Brock recognized it at once: the light of evolution.

A battle gave the winner plenty of experience, but the loser gained some too. If a Pokémon was close enough to evolving, even a defeat could push it over the threshold.

Ethan's Beldum had evidently reached that point. He watched it evolve with a pleased smile.

Ash, in stark contrast, stood there with a long face, torn between anger and the miserable feeling that he'd been used.

That was all thanks to me!
