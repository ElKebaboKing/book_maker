When Ethan left the arena after his match, he ran into Ash and the others hurrying toward it.

Misty was still talking animatedly beside him about the excitement she had seen in the stands. In the middle of her story, Ethan stopped. She looked up in surprise and saw Ash's group approaching.

“Huh? Your match is over already?” Ash asked, disappointed. “What a shame! We came as fast as we could to watch, but we're still too late.”

Morrison and Tyson looked disappointed too. They had already marked Ethan as a difficult opponent and hoped to see what he could do.

There was no helping it. Ethan's match had been scheduled late, and he had spent a long time in the arena, but the venues were far apart. They could not cross from one to another in a few minutes.

Ethan waved a hand and smiled. “You didn't miss much. It was an ordinary battle. You can see how long it took me to come out.”

As he spoke, a solitary figure emerged from the competitors' passage and trudged away.

Ash watched him go. “That's Anthony, isn't it? He was your opponent, Ethan. What happened to him?”

Anthony looked as though he had been through some terrible ordeal. All the energy had gone out of him.

Max pushed up his glasses. “Isn't it obvious? Mr. Ethan beat the fight right out of him!”

“What? Really?” Ash scratched his head. “I've battled Anthony too. He used underhanded tricks to beat a lot of challengers, but he's still strong. What kind of battle would leave him like that?”

That only made Morrison and Tyson more curious.

Misty had learned over their long travels that some things were best left unsaid in front of others. Before Ethan could answer, she smiled at them. “It wasn't anything special. The battle just dragged on. Anthony couldn't stand the atmosphere, and it got to him.”

Morrison and Tyson had questions, but accepted her explanation. They had felt the pressure of this stage themselves. It was nothing like an ordinary battle or a Gym challenge, and it had affected their Pokémon badly. They could perform at barely more than half their usual strength.

Fortunately, most of the competitors faced the same problem. With nearly everyone struggling, it had not seemed unfair.

Ethan asked how their matches had gone. As expected, all of them had advanced.

Ash had made it through by a narrower margin. Perhaps his opponent had been unusually strong, or perhaps he had chosen a poor pairing. Each side had lost a Pokémon in a clash that left both badly hurt. Corphish's fine performance and sheer persistence had finally carried Ash to a hard-fought victory.

May and the others worried that he might fall before the final tournament. Ethan was more confident. He knew Ash better than they did, and he was certain Ash would make the top thirty-two.

That certainty did not come from having watched the anime. Ethan could sense it in Ash himself: the makings of an excellent Trainer.

At the end of the first round, half of the 256 competitors were out. The remaining 128 were matched again within their respective groups for the second round.

Ethan's next opponent, also assigned to Arena F, was a Trainer named Vito.

At first the name meant nothing to Ethan. Then he saw the family accompanying Vito and heard Ash explain who they were. Vito belonged to the Winstrate family, whose string of victories had made them fairly well known in Hoenn.

The family's grandmother, father, mother, son, and daughter all loved Pokémon battles. They would challenge passing Trainers and prove so persistent that they would hardly let anyone leave without battling the whole family. Over time, their habit had earned them a measure of local fame.

Vito was the strongest of the five.

Ash had encountered the family during his travels, but Vito had not been home then. Ash had heard that he was away training for the Ever Grande Conference. Now Ethan had drawn him as an opponent. What a coincidence.

Ash's story stirred Ethan's memory. The Winstrates had appeared in the anime and in the games. To a player, though, the five were not especially strong. With a few extra levels, they were easy to defeat and good for experience points.

How would the strongest of them perform in this real world?

...

After a night's rest, Ethan arrived early at Arena F the next morning. His match was the first of the day, which finally gave Ash, Tyson, and Morrison the chance to watch him together.

With half the competitors gone, the day's schedule held half as many matches. The competitors' dormitories were growing quieter, while the stands grew fuller and the atmosphere more heated. Even for the morning's first match, the seats were packed.

Some spectators had never seen Ethan battle. Others had watched him the day before and come back early, drawn either by his appearance or by his strength. He was beginning to attract attention at the Ever Grande Conference.

To the crowd's cheers, Ethan and Vito entered the battlefield from opposite sides.

Vito was about twenty-four or twenty-five, with green hair tied in a ponytail. He was of average build and pleasant enough to look at. His manner seemed gentle, even a little hesitant, rather than forceful. Ethan would not underestimate a member of the Winstrate family on that account.

While Ethan studied Vito, Vito studied him. Though he had come to compete at Ever Grande, he was the odd one out in his family: battling did not interest him as much as other ways of living.

Ethan was plainly better-looking than he was, and Vito found himself taking to him. Compared with a heavily muscled Trainer who looked rough, a handsome man ought to be more civilized. Perhaps the battle would be gentler too...?

Cheers for both competitors rose from the stands. At the referee's signal, Ethan and Vito took out their Poké Balls. Blue light flashed as their Pokémon appeared.

Vito sent out Alakazam and Swellow. Ethan answered with Blaziken and Eevee.

Yesterday, Ethan had helped Metagross and Milotic adjust to the crowd. Today it was these two Pokémon's turn. His newer partners needed time in the arena, and even the older ones would rotate in to recover the feel of competition. He intended to give them all a chance.

The battle began, and both Trainers issued their commands.

Alakazam's Psychic type and Swellow's Flying and Normal types each posed a threat to Blaziken. Once again, Ethan's opening matchup was slightly unfavorable. Two such starts in a row might have frustrated another Trainer. Ethan barely gave it a thought.

If Vito relaxed because of his type advantage, even for a moment, he would soon be the one in trouble.

Swellow climbed high, searching for an opening. Alakazam floated cross-legged and raised both spoons toward Blaziken and Eevee.

Unlike Anthony the day before, Vito did not attack at once. He seemed poised to unleash a storm of attacks at the first mistake.

Ethan acted as if he had noticed nothing. At his order, Blaziken sent Flamethrower across the field, while Eevee ran around the flank toward Alakazam.

In the stands, Tyson and Morrison sat up almost together, holding their breath as they waited to see Ethan's strength. Ash watched just as closely. He wanted to see what these Pokémon could do—and whether the gap between himself and Ethan had widened or narrowed.

May and Max had never watched Ethan battle in person either. May knew his strength mostly from Ash's stories, while Max had seen only a little of him in televised recordings. Now they could judge for themselves whether he was as formidable as they had heard.

For a moment, everyone, whether they cared about Ethan or not, focused on the battle.

Blaziken's flames reached Alakazam. It calmly lifted a spoon, forming an invisible barrier before it. The fire struck hard but could not break through.

Eevee raced in from the side. Vito and Swellow both saw it. Without waiting for an order, Swellow dived to intercept. Alakazam raised its other spoon and aimed it at Eevee.

Swellow's outstretched talons drew so close they seemed about to touch Eevee. At the same instant, Alakazam's psychic power swept toward it. One attack came from above, the other from the ground. The two Pokémon had caught Eevee between them.

Their teamwork needed no instruction from Vito. Compared with Anthony's pair yesterday, they were on another level. From experience, Vito could already picture Eevee falling to their combined assault.

Then Eevee turned sharply away from Swellow's dive. It burst forward with Quick Attack, slipping past Alakazam's invisible Confusion and slamming into the Psychic-type Pokémon.

“Beautiful!”

The announcer cried out without thinking, and the crowd erupted. Who would have expected such a reaction from an Eevee that looked so ordinary?

There was more. The impact broke Alakazam's concentration. Its barrier shattered, and the remaining flames of Blaziken's Flamethrower poured through and scorched it. One move from Eevee had reversed the exchange.

Tyson and Morrison each had their own thoughts, but both were astonished by Eevee. Its performance alone suggested that Ethan really was as capable as they had heard.

Ash exchanged a look with Pikachu. What Eevee had done looked simple: dodge, then strike. In truth, the timing had to be exact. A second too early or too late, and it would not have escaped Swellow, much less Alakazam's unseen psychic attack.

Ash had seen that kind of agility only in his own Pikachu, after three regions of hard travel. Eevee had been on the road for much less time. How had its reactions become so sharp already?
