It was not long before Ash and the others returned to the ground floor of the lodging building.

Ash's broad smile was visible even before he came through the door. He had reached the quarterfinals, his best result in two League tournaments. More than that, he had finally beaten Gary, his longtime rival, for the first time. Winning on a stage like this meant something special.

"Congratulations." Ethan applauded with a smile. "Just as I 'predicted,' you won."

"Thanks." Ash scratched his head bashfully at the praise. Then he remembered who his next opponent would be and grew serious. "Ethan, we battle the day after tomorrow. I'll do my best!"

Ethan's expression turned peculiar.

He had come over to congratulate Ash, and Ash had responded by announcing his determination to beat him. Ethan did not know whether to laugh or sigh.

Well, Ash was a child and the story's chosen hero. Ethan would let it pass.

A celebration meal for both their victories was only natural.

Besides their own group of five, Ethan invited Delia, Professor Oak, Jimmy, Marina, Misa, and Masha. They crowded into a private room at a nearby restaurant and ate their fill. Afterward, Oak, the eldest among them, offered to pay despite the pained look on his face. He discovered Ethan had already settled the bill.

Oak stood before the smiling server and sighed to himself. What a thoughtful young man.

That night, after everyone returned to their rooms, Ethan stood on the balcony and happened to see Ash leave the building alone. He headed toward the lake beside Silver Stadium.

"What are you looking at?" Erika came up behind Ethan and slipped her arms around his waist. Following his gaze, she said, "Oh, isn't that Ash? Where is he going so late?"

"He's going to find Gary. Who else?"

"Gary?" Erika was surprised for a moment, then nodded. "Their relationship is complicated. I suppose Ash wants to comfort him."

Ethan turned and wrapped his arms around her, breathing in her perfume. "If Ash had been eliminated, Gary as he is now would have gone looking for him too. After tonight, I think Gary will entrust his dream to Ash."

"Why do you say that?" Erika had scarcely finished speaking when Ethan opened her collar and slid a hand beneath her clothes. Her face reddened.

Ethan offered little explanation. Caressing her, he said with a smile, "If things go as I expect, Gary will turn to scientific research. I doubt we'll see him competing in future League tournaments. Anyway, it's a beautiful night. Let's celebrate properly."

...

The next day, Ethan and Erika had no match of their own. They stayed in their room.

The day's battles played on the television while they used the computer to review Ash, Jon Dickson, and several other possible opponents. They went through the Pokémon those Trainers had used, concentrating on each one's abilities so they would know what to expect.

As on the previous day, four matches would determine the remaining quarterfinalists. Of the Trainers Ethan knew, only Jimmy and Marina still had a match today, and they would have to face each other.

To anyone else, their pairing might have looked ordinary. To Ethan, who knew a little about their feelings for one another, it was emotionally harsher than Ash and Gary's match.

Once the battle began, it fell into three distinct stages.

At first, they tested each other carefully. Perhaps neither had worked through their feelings or knew how to face the other. They held back and fought so cautiously that it hardly looked like a match between the final sixteen.

The announcer was baffled, and the spectators began murmuring. Some even wondered if the match was fixed.

The conflict on Jimmy's and Marina's faces was plain on the screen. Fortunately, it did not last long. Marina seemed to make up her mind and launched a fierce attack. Jimmy responded in kind, and soon both were fighting freely, exchanging blows.

That second stage settled the crowd. At least now there were moments worth cheering for.

After the halftime break, they stopped holding back entirely and fought with everything they had.

Jimmy was the stronger of the two. In the final exchange, his Typhlosion defeated Marina's Feraligatr despite its type disadvantage, securing his place in the quarterfinals.

Unlike Ash, Jimmy showed little excitement at advancing. Marina, though she had lost, seemed happy for her childhood friend.

Ethan sighed. What a complicated relationship.

One more match in their half of the bracket followed. It was far less tense; the gap in strength was considerable. A Trainer from Hoenn named Harrison won decisively.

With that, all four quarterfinalists in Ethan's half were set: Ethan, Ash, Jimmy, and Harrison. Ethan would face Ash, while Jimmy would face Harrison. The winners would advance to the semifinals.

In their room, Ethan and Erika spent the rest of the day preparing. They drew up several plans based on their opponents' Pokémon and made the final choices for Ethan's team tomorrow.

...

The quarterfinals followed the same knockout-tournament rules as the round of sixteen.

Each match would be a full six-on-six battle. The battlefield and first Trainer to send out a Pokémon would be drawn beforehand. Once either Trainer had lost three Pokémon, there would be a five-minute halftime break.

The only real difference was how fiercely people online debated who would make it through and eventually reach the final.

Ethan's half of the bracket had left a strong impression on the spectators. He led the polls by a wide margin, but Ash and Harrison had plenty of supporters too. Jimmy came last, largely because he and Marina had held back during the first half of their match.

In the other half, Jon Dickson was the only standout. Most people expected the final to be between him and Ethan.

After a night's rest, Ethan and Ash stood opposite one another inside Silver Stadium at nine the next morning.

Ash looked well rested. His eyes were determined, and he seemed eager to begin.

Beating Gary yesterday had given him a great deal of confidence. He was ready for today's match.

Two matches were scheduled today: Ethan against Ash in the morning, then Jimmy against Harrison in the afternoon. By day's end, their half of the bracket would have its two semifinalists.

Once both Trainers were ready, the referee called, "We'll now draw the battlefield for this match!"

Ethan and Ash looked up together. The wheel spun on the large screen and, when it stopped, pointed once again to the rock field.

That was good news for Ash. He had fought on the rock field only yesterday and performed well there. Familiarity with the terrain gave him a slightly better chance.

The next draw decided who would send out a Pokémon first. Once again, Ethan had the bad luck to be chosen.

He looked untroubled. By now, he was used to it.

Before the watching crowd, the battlefield changed. Ethan's match against Ash had begun.
