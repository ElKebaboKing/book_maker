Bang!

Fireworks burst over Ecruteak City, announcing the start of the battle.

Beneath their shifting colors, Articuno, Zapdos, and Moltres closed in on Ho-Oh and attacked.

Ho-Oh, like Moltres, was Fire and Flying type. Of the three legendary birds, only Zapdos had a type advantage against it. Moltres and Ho-Oh would resist each other's Fire attacks, while Articuno was vulnerable to Ho-Oh's flames.

That was the theory. A real battle was rarely so simple.

After so much time training and fighting together, Ethan's three birds worked as a team. When he faced a powerful opponent, he sent them out together.

He could have challenged Ho-Oh with Deoxys, Rayquaza, or even his newly trained Charizard. But a fight with any one of them would cause an even greater disturbance. He was not trying to catch Ho-Oh, so there was no need to go all out.

Besides, these were the birds Ho-Oh had fought when it restored the three legendary beasts, ruining Ethan's attempt to catch them. The birds deserved a chance to settle that score themselves. That was why they had glared at Ho-Oh the moment they appeared.

Ethan was not the only one who resented that encounter. The three birds had their pride too. They were all great birds of the sky. Why should Ho-Oh stand so far above them? Tonight they meant to bring it down from its lofty perch.

Articuno fired an Ice Beam carrying a bitter chill. Moltres launched a blazing Flamethrower. Zapdos unleashed a powerful Thunderbolt. Three differently colored attacks streaked toward Ho-Oh from three directions.

Ho-Oh's strength lay beyond any one move. Its vast reserves of energy and the ways it could wield them set it apart. In a game, Pokémon might be measured by numbers. In the world before Ethan, a being like Ho-Oh could exercise powers ordinary Pokémon simply did not possess.

The three birds were formidable in their own right, but Ho-Oh still had an edge. An unseen force surged from its body and met all three attacks at once, stopping and canceling them.

Boom!

The explosion drowned out the fireworks. Its report carried all the way toward Ecruteak. People watching the display heard it and assumed an unusually large firework had gone off. They searched the sky, saw nothing to account for that particular sound, and soon returned their attention to the colors overhead.

Ho-Oh counterattacked at once. It climbed sharply, cried out, and poured Sacred Fire down across the sky. The flames spread wide enough to engulf all three birds.

The night turned red. From the roof of Bell Tower, the descending fire looked like an enormous net falling over the tower itself.

For the first time, Miki and her sisters saw the power of a legendary Pokémon at close range. They worried that the flames would reach everyone on the roof.

Each of the three birds used its own power to fend off the broad attack. None was hurt. But broken remnants of Sacred Fire kept falling toward Bell Tower.

Gardevoir raised both hands. An invisible barrier appeared above the tower and caught every remaining flame before it could reach them.

Fire spread across the barrier, briefly hiding the battle from view. When it cleared, four great bird Pokémon had closed in and were fighting at close quarters.

Ho-Oh was strong enough to hold back the three birds with raw power. At close range, however, the balance shifted. It was not poor at fighting up close; it simply had only two talons and one beak against three opponents.

It drove Moltres away, only for Articuno to rake its back. When it turned on Articuno, Zapdos darted in at high speed to harry it. By the time it shook Zapdos off, Moltres had returned.

The three birds had grown since Ethan first caught them. Now at the height of their strength, they pressed Ho-Oh so hard that it could scarcely fend them off.

Ho-Oh circled Bell Tower, struggling to draw them out and make space. Its powerful body let it break free at last. Having learned what the birds could do up close, it kept moving and fought them with attacks from a distance.

That made the battle far more conspicuous.

In truth, Ho-Oh's first wave of Sacred Fire had already caught Ecruteak's attention. The enormous blaze had lit much of the night sky and even outshone the fireworks. At first, people thought the city had arranged another spectacle for the festival. A sky full of flame at this hour was certainly impressive.

Then observant residents and Pokémon Trainers began to doubt it. The lights above Bell Tower looked less like a planned show than Pokémon battling.

Many residents were content to watch from where they stood. Trainers were different. Whether it was a battle or a rare Pokémon, they wanted a closer look. At least several dozen people hurried toward Bell Tower.

One Trainer rushed past a detached house. His pounding footsteps and the shouts of nearby residents disturbed a little boy inside. The boy opened his window and saw fire fading from the sky.

In the combined light of fireworks and flame, his face was clear. It was the boy Ethan had met on the food street with the Ho-Oh kite.

“What's that...?”

He peered toward Bell Tower. Several colorful, glowing shapes like birds seemed to be chasing and grappling with one another above it. Why were Pokémon flying there so late? They were distant and hard to make out, but one of them looked terribly like Ho-Oh.

He remembered the man at the night market and what he had said. The boy's expression changed.

Could he have been telling the truth? Had Ho-Oh really appeared? And what were those other bird Pokémon around it?

“Grandpa!”

He ran into the house. A moment later he returned, pulling a thin, silver-haired old man along with him.

“Kuriu, what is it? Slow down, slow down!” the man protested.

Kuriu paid little heed to his grandfather's aging legs. He pointed at the sky. “Grandpa, look! It's Ho-Oh! The real Ho-Oh!”

“Ho-Oh?” The old man shook his head with a weary smile. He knew his grandson too well. He had noticed the boy's efforts to cheer him up, including the Ho-Oh kite he had secretly made. The care behind them touched him deeply. But he was old now, and that impossible dream ought to be left behind...

His eyes turned toward Bell Tower. They widened at once.

“It... it really is Ho-Oh?”

...

Thoom!

Back over the tower, a blast of Ho-Oh's fire tore through Articuno's icy power and shot straight at it.

Articuno had traveled and fought beside Ethan for a long time. It had faced opponents of this caliber before. Rather than linger after its own attack, it beat its wings and rose, escaping the flames.

Zapdos and Moltres moved into the space it left and renewed the assault. The legendary Pokémon tangled together once more. This was no battle that would end in moments.

Then Ethan sensed something and looked toward Ecruteak. From the height of the tower, by the flashes of fireworks and the city's lights, he could see figures racing through the woods of Bellchime Trail toward them.

People had noticed the fight and come to investigate. Most were probably energetic fellow Trainers. With a battle of this scale, their arrival was inevitable. Ethan had prepared for it.

He calmly took an Ultra Ball from his belt and pressed its button. Deoxys appeared beside him.

The sisters instinctively stepped back. In the darkness, the unfamiliar Pokémon's red-and-blue face and expressionless stare were unsettling. Fear came easily when faced with something unknown.

They knew it belonged to Ethan, though, and it merely floated there without threatening them. Their alarm faded into curiosity, and they began to study it.

Ethan pointed toward the approaching Trainers. “See those people coming from that direction? Stop them. Don't let them interrupt the birds' battle. Use the same trick your counterpart used in LaRousse City.”

Deoxys made a string of sounds Ethan could not interpret and turned toward Ecruteak.

Copies began separating from its body. In moments, dozens surrounded it. Without any further visible command, they flew together toward the approaching Trainers. The original Deoxys remained beside Ethan. There was no need for it to deal with these people personally.

Barely a dozen seconds later, the Trainers met the copies. They had come to watch the spectacle and perhaps seize an opportunity. The strange figures startled them, and when the copies attacked without warning, they had to fight back.

These Trainers had noticed the battle quickly and made it this far; they were capable enough to destroy copy after copy. But the swarm seemed endless. One fallen copy was replaced by two. After they defeated those two, four more appeared.

The battle in the sky was tantalizingly close. No matter how hard they fought, the Trainers could not advance another step.

Ethan turned his attention back to Ho-Oh and the three birds. He had stopped the first visitors, but more would notice once the fireworks ended. The battle needed to finish soon.

He raised his voice toward the sky. “Enough playing around! Use Tri-Bird Assault!”
