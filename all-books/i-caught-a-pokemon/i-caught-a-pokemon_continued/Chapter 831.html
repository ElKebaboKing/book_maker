More Beedrill kept joining the swarm. Before long, there were two or three hundred of them. Even from a distance, the hum of their wings was audible.

According to a study someone had once done on the dangers people most dreaded encountering in the wild, a Beedrill swarm ranked near the top. These cold, aggressive forest hunters lived in colonies and could be deadly to a new Trainer or an ordinary traveler who wandered into their territory.

Metagross and Claydol were being pursued, but Ethan, May, and Spenser held to the rule they had agreed on. None of them interfered or offered help. They watched to see what would happen.

Either Pokémon could have driven off the Beedrill easily if it had been free to concentrate on them. Instead, each had to watch for attacks from its opponent while avoiding being surrounded. The danger multiplied.

Even so, they were not merely fleeing. They struck back as they retreated. The situation was unfavorable, but neither seemed in immediate peril.

The fight drew closer to the hill. The buzz of Beedrill wings grew clearer. Ethan and Spenser kept their composure, while May grew visibly tense.

She could not face a threat like this without concern. She knew Ethan was beside her and would take care of it if things went wrong, but she still measured her own abilities against the swarm. Combusken, Squirtle, and Munchlax together could not have held off so many Beedrill. If Ethan had not been there, she would have hidden until they passed.

On the screen, Beedrill fell now and then, but their numbers scarcely seemed to diminish. Then a disturbance broke out on both sides of the pursuing swarm. The referee robot swung its camera around.

Dragonite and Scizor had arrived. They must have heard the battle and hurried over. Their presence also confirmed what Ethan had expected: each had won its own fight.

With those two powerful Pokémon joining in, the situation changed at once. Beedrill fell in great numbers. The rest saw the danger and fled; in moments, the swarm was gone. It had arrived quickly and departed faster still.

Only the unconscious Beedrill on the ground and the damaged trees showed that a battle had taken place.

With the swarm gone, Metagross and Claydol stopped retreating and faced each other again. Dragonite and Scizor approached, and Claydol grew uneasy. It could feel how much stronger these Pokémon were than the wild Beedrill. Metagross alone had matched it; two more opponents would be overwhelming.

Dragonite and Scizor had no interest in ganging up on it. They stopped a short distance away and left enough room for the other two to continue their battle.

Spenser looked at Ethan with fresh respect. When the young man proposed a three-on-three free-for-all, Spenser had assumed he meant to concentrate his forces on one of Spenser's Pokémon, gain a numbers advantage, and win from there.

Apparently, that had never been the plan. Ethan simply found three separate one-on-one matches too troublesome and preferred to send everyone out at once. Dragonite and Scizor's refusal to attack Claydol made that clear. The younger generation could be formidable indeed.

Seeing that they were only blocking its escape routes, Claydol put Dragonite and Scizor out of mind and focused entirely on Metagross.

Claydol was one of Spenser's main partners and had been with him for years. Metagross had performed impressively at the Ever Grande Conference but had not made Ethan's first-string team. Ethan judged them to be on much the same level, and their battle bore that out.

They resumed fighting as evenly as before. No contest could remain tied forever, though. Both fired Hyper Beam, and the two blasts met in the forest. When the explosion cleared, the result was plain on the screen: Metagross and Claydol lay amid the debris, both battered and unconscious. They had knocked each other out.

It was a surprise, yet it made sense. Ethan's Pokémon were powerful in their own right, but his judgment and commands were a major reason they won so often. Without his guidance, Metagross had to rely on its own considerable abilities. Holding Spenser's Claydol to a draw was a good result.

Ethan was satisfied. May shook her head slightly, disappointed that Metagross had not managed a clear victory. Spenser had his own regret that Claydol could not stay standing. At least it had defeated an opponent; he had not lost all three without an answer.

“Well, I managed to take one of your Pokémon down,” Spenser said lightly. “Perhaps this old man hasn't lost his touch!”

He had nearly suffered a complete defeat, but his spirits were as good as ever.

“More than that,” Ethan said with a smile. “Your Pokémon is truly strong. Mine had to give everything it had just to reach a draw with Claydol. I still have work to do.”

Spenser laughed. Many challengers had fallen to his Claydol, and he knew its strength better than anyone. Dragonite and Scizor had plainly been stronger than Metagross. If one of his main partners had only tied with a Pokémon outside Ethan's leading lineup, Spenser understood the difference between them. If Ethan kept improving, even that less favored Pokémon might become more than Spenser could handle.

The referee robot began retrieving Spenser's fallen Pokémon. He had given it their Poké Balls at the start of the match. It recalled Claydol first, then flew off to find Shiftry and Venusaur.

Dragonite picked up the unconscious Metagross. With Scizor beside it, it headed back toward the starting arena.

After a few more words with Ethan, Spenser placed a gleaming gold Symbol in his hand.

“Please accept this. It's the Battle Palace's Spirits Symbol, proof that you defeated me.”

Like Ethan's earlier Symbols, it was made of gold. Its design was rather plain: a square block marked with grooves.

…

Ethan and May returned from the Battle Palace to the quiet town on Metallica Island. The challenge had gone more smoothly than they expected. It was only noon.

Ethan had barely felt involved. He had watched the whole contest while his Pokémon fought on their own, and they had earned him another Frontier Symbol. Of all the Battle Frontier facilities he had challenged, this one had been the easiest for him.

That did not mean it would be easy for anyone else. Defeating Spenser's Pokémon without giving a single command would be difficult for most Trainers. Ethan's team had often fought independently and had been trained by that experience. Ash or another challenger might have struggled badly.

A win was a win, and the journey had been worthwhile. He had seen spectacular scenery too. Now it was time for their next destination.

Metallica Island's remoteness presented a problem. When Ethan asked at the little dock, he learned that the next passenger ship would not arrive for three days. Getting here had been easy; leaving was another matter.

He brought out his second flying mount. Dragonite appeared before him and May again. It had just fought a battle, but the difference in strength had kept it from taking any injuries. Its time resting in its Poké Ball had restored its energy as well.

“Thanks for helping us again,” May said with a smile. Ethan helped her onto Dragonite's back, then climbed up behind her. Once they were both settled, Dragonite beat its small wings and rose high into the sky.

The breeze felt pleasant, and May narrowed her eyes in contentment. As Dragonite leveled out, she looked back. Metallica Island was shrinking beneath them and soon fell far behind.

It had been some time since Ethan last traveled through the air. He had had two flying mounts on his journey. The first was Charizard, now far away in the Charicific Valley. The second was Dragonite beneath him.

They flew differently. Charizard felt powerful and steady when carrying Ethan, though it could not match Dragonite's speed. Dragonite was livelier, rising and dipping as it pleased and often skimming the water. Neither was a better flyer; each had its own style.

Their next destination was near Fuchsia City, not terribly far from Metallica Island. Dragonite was much faster than a ship, but by the time dusk approached they still had not reached the mainland. They landed on a nearby island to find somewhere to spend the night.

Fortunately, this island was larger and more prosperous than Metallica Island, perhaps because it lay closer to the mainland. It had less forest and more mountains. Its town was bigger, with more people, cleaner streets, and a lively air.

Dragonite came down in the town. Ethan and May asked some residents where they were. The island was Potpourri Island, and the town was Wisteria Town, one of the largest settlements in the surrounding waters.

They also heard that a Pokémon Orienteering competition would be held the following morning. It was an occasional event in which a person and a Pokémon partnered up and raced through five locations around the island. The first team to reach the finish would win a Teamwork Medal and a prize of Potpourri Island's famous fruit.

An orienteering race? Local fruit? The mention of food immediately caught May's interest.

Their timing was fortunate. If the Palace challenge had taken longer, they would not have reached Potpourri Island on the eve of the race, and they would have missed it altogether.

They followed the streets to the Pokémon Center outside town. Adults and children alike were talking about tomorrow's competition, making May even more eager to enter.

At dinner, she finally said it aloud. “Ethan, let's take part in the orienteering race tomorrow!”

Ethan chuckled. “Is it the medal you're after, or that local fruit?”

May gave a sheepish grin, but admitted it freely. “I want both! I heard this isn't a test of battling strength. It's just a race across the island. That means I have a chance to beat you!”
