Early the next morning, Ethan and Erika visited the Petalburg Gym.

The Gym stood on the outskirts of the city, backed by two green mountains. It looked like an old Japanese estate, enclosed by tall white walls. Without the sign reading “Petalburg Gym” over its entrance, most people would never have guessed it was a Pokémon Gym.

They climbed the steps. The doors were open and no attendant was in sight, so Ethan walked in.

Inside was an enormous traditional dojo. It was deserted and very quiet; the only sound was Ethan and Erika's footsteps echoing on the floor.

“Hello? Is anyone here?”

Ethan called out. After waiting a while without an answer, he raised his voice and called again.

At last, someone came running from the other side of the building.

But Ethan saw that it was only a boy of about seven. The child was short, with black-framed glasses, bushy eyebrows, and medium-length hair. He wore a T-shirt and shorts and looked like the sort of youngster who knew every bit of Pokémon theory.

He looked almost exactly as he had in the anime. Ethan recognized him at once: May's younger brother and Gym Leader Norman's son, Max.

“Coming! Coming!” Max ran up to Ethan. As soon as he saw Ethan's face clearly, he pointed at him and cried, “Oh! It's you!”

Ethan tilted his head. “You know me?”

“Of course! I've been watching the Silver Conference for the past two days—the live matches and the replays. You're Ethan, the Champion!”

Ethan was a little surprised that Max recognized him, but it made sense. The boy loved learning and knew a great deal about Pokémon. In the anime, he had recognized Ash the first time they met and even known which match had knocked Ash out of the tournament.

“That's me.” Ethan smiled. “Is Mr. Norman here? I've come to challenge the Petalburg Gym.”

“Challenge my dad...” Max started to answer, then remembered something and smiled sheepishly. “Oh, I almost forgot. Dad went out early this morning. He might not be back for several days.”

“What?” Ethan stared at him. “He's gone?”

“Yeah. He left only half an hour ago. He told me to close the front doors and put a notice outside, but the match I was watching was getting exciting and I forgot.”

Well, that was unfortunate.

Ethan and Erika exchanged a helpless look. Half an hour earlier, and they might have caught him.

But while Ethan looked disappointed, Erika shot him a sharp glare.

Whose fault was this?

His, of course. They had both woken early and were almost dressed for breakfast when he had suddenly felt amorous and insisted on pulling her back into bed.

If he had not kept them there for so long, they would have arrived in time.

Ethan understood her look. He gave a rueful smile and shook his head.

He had meant well. Calling at the Gym too early might have disturbed Norman, so he had deliberately delayed them. Who could have predicted Norman would leave?

“I see.” Ethan sighed. “Did Mr. Norman say what he was doing, or where we could find him?”

“Well...” Max scratched his head. “I'm not sure exactly. My sister might know. I'll go get her!”

He dashed across the Gym and vanished through a door before Ethan could respond. The boy really was eager to help him.

Before long, Max returned, using both hands to pull a pretty young girl into the dojo.

She was about Misty's age. Shoulder-length brown hair curled outward at the sides, and her lovely features promised that she would grow into a beautiful woman.

Ethan knew her immediately. She was May, the popular traveling companion who would join Ash in the anime's third generation after Misty left.

May was every bit as pretty as Misty, but quieter without being dull. She could be quite clever too.

Ethan had reached Hoenn a few days before Ash. May would ride her bicycle to Professor Birch's laboratory in Littleroot Town to receive her first Pokémon around the same day Ash arrived.

They would meet there as though by fate and begin traveling together.

Ash was still two or three days away. That was why Ethan could meet May here now.

Max had dragged her toward the battlefield so suddenly that she did not know what was happening. She was about to scold him for rushing around when she saw the man and woman waiting there. Understanding the situation, she composed herself.

“I'm sorry.” May bowed lightly to Ethan and Erika. “The Pokémon League called my father away on urgent business. Before he left, he said he might not return for three days.”

Three days...

Ethan could not wait here that long. If he left, though, he would have to visit other cities first and come back to challenge Norman after his return.

“There's nothing for it, then. I'll come back another time.”

He shook his head and was about to turn away when Max called out again.

“Mr. Ethan, wait!”

Ethan and May both turned to look at him.

“What is it?” Ethan asked.

As if by magic, Max produced a pen and notebook from behind his back. He held them out hopefully. “Could I have your autograph? I'm a fan!”

Ethan laughed. He had not expected his Johto victories to win him a fan all the way in Hoenn, let alone a boy who would become an important character in the anime.

“Of course.” He took the pen and notebook. Although he already knew, he asked, “What's your name?”

Max straightened to attention. “How rude of me! I'm Max, and I'm seven. I want to travel like you and become a great Trainer like my dad!”

“That's an excellent dream. Mr. Norman is strong and respected, so you couldn't ask for a better example. I'm sure he'd be proud to hear you say that.”

Ethan signed the notebook's first page with a few quick strokes. When Max opened it, he read: May you always remember why you began, and may your dream come true. Your friend, Ethan.

Max trembled with excitement.

Ethan handed back the pen and was about to leave when he noticed May looking down curiously at his Torchic. An idea came to him.

“Do you like it?”

May glanced at Ethan, then back at Torchic. She neither agreed nor disagreed.

“It's cute. What is it called?”

Despite being a Gym Leader's daughter with a younger brother who loved Pokémon and knew so much about them, May knew surprisingly little herself.

After receiving her starter from Professor Birch, she would encounter a wild Pokémon and naively throw a Poké Ball at it, thinking she could catch it without a battle.

In battle, she would also fail to understand type matchups, leaving her Pokémon knocked down. Ash, a master of fighting against type disadvantages, would have to explain the basics to her from his own experience.

It was quite remarkable.

And May's first Pokémon would, of course, be a Torchic too.

“It's called Torchic. It's a Fire-type Pokémon.”

“A Fire-type Torchic...” May nodded without entirely understanding. “Its feathers look so fluffy and warm. I bet it feels nice to touch.”

“It's strong too. You're about the right age, and you're a Gym Leader's daughter. You'll be getting your first Pokémon and setting out soon, won't you?”

May's eyes brightened at the mention of travel. The prospect of seeing the world clearly appealed to her more than Pokémon did.

“That's right. Once Dad comes back from his League business, I'll go to Littleroot Town and see Professor Birch.”

“I thought so.” Ethan nodded. “Treecko and Mudkip are good choices too, but if you can't decide, consider Torchic. Believe me, you won't regret it.”

“Torchic...”

May looked again at the little orange chick beside Ethan's feet.

Whatever its type, its appearance suited her tastes.

Yes, like that man's Torchic. It could be a good choice.

After Ethan and Erika left the Gym, May gathered her thoughts. Turning around, she found Max grinning foolishly at his notebook.

“You asked him for an autograph. Is he famous?”

“Come on!” Max shot her an exasperated look. “You don't know him? He won both the Silver Conference and the Indigo Conference! He overwhelmed his opponents with sheer strength, and you don't know who he is?”

“Really? He's that strong?”

May pictured Ethan again. To her, he had looked like a handsome, gentle man. She could hardly connect him with a Champion.

“Oh, right,” Max said. “What did he tell you?”

“Nothing much. He suggested that I choose Torchic as my first Pokémon.”

“Torchic...” Max frowned and thought it over. “He can't be wrong. I was going to recommend Treecko, but if Mr. Ethan says that, maybe Torchic really is stronger. And Fire beats Grass, so when you choose...”

He trailed off, looked at his sister again, and thought about her personality. Then he nodded firmly.

“I see it now. Why didn't I think of that? Maybe Torchic really would suit you better.”

...

Outside the Gym, Ethan and Erika paused by the entrance and brought up a map on their phone.

If they followed the route they had planned, missing the Petalburg Gym now was no great problem. They would circle Hoenn and return here eventually; Ethan could challenge Norman then.

It would change the order of his Gym battles, but there was nothing to be done.

They put the disappointment behind them and headed out of Petalburg City.

Like Oldale and Littleroot, Petalburg was no major tourist destination and had no especially famous scenery. With the Gym challenge postponed, they had no reason to stay.

Next was Rustboro City to the north, where another Gym awaited Ethan.

Getting there would take them a long way past the sea and through the famous Petalburg Woods. It would not be an easy trip.

But that was what traveling meant: covering dull stretches of road while learning about the world, meeting new people, and training Pokémon.

Leaving Petalburg, they continued west along the main road. Before long, they reached Hoenn's long, winding western coast.

Ethan did not know what lay across the water. But as he looked over the broad blue sea, his regret at missing Norman that morning faded away.

Golden sand stretched along the shore, and many Trainers were out on the beach. Erika suggested, “Shall we walk by the sea? We might find a Water-type Pokémon native to Hoenn.”

Ethan nodded. He had no objection.

They slowed and headed toward the sand. In his mind, Ethan ran through all the Water-type Pokémon of Hoenn.

Except for Kyogre, they seemed common enough. None struck him as worth searching for specifically.

He glanced at Erika and saw how interested she was. Smiling, he let the thought go.

Forget searching for Pokémon. He could spend time with the woman he loved and relax.
