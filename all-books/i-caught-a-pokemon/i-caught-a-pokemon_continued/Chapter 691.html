“Hurry, dodge!” Juan shouted to Kingdra.

Kingdra heard him, but it was too dazed from the fall to control its body. It could neither dodge nor sink beneath the surface in time. Dragonite gave it no chance to recover.

Dragon Rage swept down in a blue-green wave and struck Kingdra.

Boom! Water exploded around it. The shock wave spread across the pool and collided with both waterspouts. Their energy had already begun to fade after several minutes of violence; the blast broke most of what remained. Only weak, scattered gusts continued to turn.

All the water they had lifted into the air fell back into the pool at once, raising an even larger wave. The Trainers' elevated platforms protected Ethan and Juan from the spray.

A minute earlier, Juan would have welcomed the end of the waterspouts. Now he watched their last traces disappear without relief. Kingdra floated unconscious on the surface. He had lost one Pokémon, and the battle was in worse shape than before. His promising opening position had been overturned completely.

One thing offered him a little comfort. While both Trainers had focused on the fight above the pool, Walrein had used its greater battle experience to break free of Milotic underwater. It had hurt Milotic and resurfaced.

Juan's first judgment about Ethan's team had been partly right: Milotic was much weaker than Dragonite. But Walrein had escaped too late. If it had surfaced sooner, it might have helped Kingdra defeat Dragonite before turning on Milotic. Regret could not change the result.

For Ethan, the situation was straightforward. Two against one was an advantage he had no intention of wasting. Milotic's injuries were manageable. On his command, it and Dragonite attacked Walrein from the water and the air.

Walrein was an evenly capable Pokémon, without a glaring weakness in its abilities. Juan's was also strong. It hauled itself onto a platform and defended from both directions, holding out longer than Ethan might have expected.

But Dragonite was stronger, and Milotic kept pressing it too. Walrein blocked a close-range Thunder Punch from Dragonite. As Dragonite flew past and climbed, Walrein prepared to counterattack—only for Milotic's Water Gun to strike it in the back.

It could not defend both sides forever. The combined assault soon brought it down after Kingdra.

Sebastian announced in his aged voice, “The challenger wins the first round!”

Misty smiled in the stands. She did not celebrate openly; she only gave Lisia a sidelong, triumphant look. To her surprise, Lisia showed no disappointment at her teacher's defeat.

Misty's smile faded. “It seems you were worrying for nothing. Ethan fought as steadily as ever. We won the first round.”

Lisia turned toward her, eyes shining with an expression Misty knew too well. She had seen that look from Melody, the festival maiden on Shamouti Island, and from Casey, the young Trainer at the Silver Conference. Alarm bells rang in her mind.

“He's amazing!” Lisia said, excited admiration spilling out of her voice.

Misty's eyelid twitched. She no longer wanted to discuss Ethan at all, but Lisia was too absorbed in the battle to notice.

“I've never seen anyone beat my teacher so easily! Even my uncle couldn't do that. Is this what winning two regional tournaments looks like? And those two Twisters becoming waterspouts—I had no idea you could battle that way! It opened my eyes. It was so exciting!”

Juan had lost on the battlefield. Misty was the one suffering in the stands.

“It wasn't anything special,” she said, though she did not believe it. She hoped to cool Lisia's sudden admiration. “Lots of Trainers use unusual tactics. One of my old traveling companions did something unexpected in almost every match. If you met him, you'd really see something new.”

Lisia hardly heard her. She rested a hand under her chin and nodded to herself. “So that's what a two-region winner is like. He's given me an idea, too. Perhaps I could make a beautiful effect with two Twisters in my next stage performance.”

Oh no. Misty rolled her eyes. Juan charmed the middle-aged women of Sootopolis, and Ethan seemed to charm girls wherever he traveled. Lisia was beginning to fall for him too.

That could not continue. Misty made a firm decision: from now on, she would watch every pretty girl they met on the road much more closely. And as soon as this Gym challenge ended, she would get Ethan away from Sootopolis.

She had wanted to stay for the festival in a few days. Now Lisia made the city feel far too dangerous. They could not stay here and let her follow Ethan around. We have to leave. Right away!

...

On the field, Juan recalled both of his defeated Pokémon without a trace of expression. The first round had gone nothing like he expected, and the second was about to begin before he could reflect on it.

He still had to face Dragonite and Milotic. What if Ethan used Twister again, raising two more of those dreadful waterspouts? Juan could almost imagine surrendering rather than fighting through that a second time.

Yet he could not ask Ethan to stop using a move. A Gym Leader who demanded such a condition would lose face. Worse, little Lisia might think he was afraid of the challenger. That would sting more than any embarrassment before the women who admired him.

Juan took a breath and sent out his next two Pokémon. If he wants another chaotic fight, I'll give him one.

Blue light flashed from the Poké Balls. A Politoed and a Whiscash appeared on the field.

Misty straightened at the sight of Politoed. She had one herself and wanted to see how Juan's compared with her own.

Ethan glanced at Politoed, then studied Whiscash. This was the first time he had encountered one in person.

It resembled a large catfish. Its back was dark blue, its belly yellow, and its body stood less than a meter high. Sponge-like pale blue lips framed its mouth. Two long yellow whiskers hung from the corners, and a W-shaped mark sat on its forehead. Altogether, it looked rather vacant.

Whiscash were said to be fiercely territorial. They nested at the bottom of swamps and claimed the surrounding waters. When something intruded, they could shake the ground violently before attacking.

During the day, they often slept motionless in the mud while waiting for prey. They ate almost anything. Their eyesight was poor, so they sensed vibrations in the water with their whiskers to locate food. They might then shake the ground to frighten an opponent and swallow prey from the swamp bottom, mud and all.

Some people saw groups of Whiscash leaping at the surface and took it as a warning of an earthquake. Over time, the story spread until people believed the Pokémon caused the quakes themselves.

Ethan examined Juan's Whiscash carefully. Even in the games, he had rarely dealt with the species; few Trainers used it. He recalled that it was Water and Ground type, but he knew little about its Abilities. He would have to learn as they fought.

At his signal, Dragonite and Milotic returned to their positions. Before Sebastian could announce the start of the second round, Ethan looked over at Juan. The Leader's grave expression told him exactly what was worrying him.

“Don't worry,” Ethan said with a smile. “I won't make waterspouts again this round.”

Juan stared. Why would Ethan give up an advantage voluntarily? Yet he seemed sincere. Perhaps Juan had misjudged him. Perhaps he was a decent man after all. Juan felt a little more respect for the challenger.

That feeling vanished as soon as the round began.

Ethan kept his promise: he raised no waterspouts. Instead, before Juan could issue an order, Milotic dove out of sight. Juan expected an attack from below. A long moment passed with no sign of it.

Then the water in the center of the pool began to turn. At first Juan did not grasp what Ethan was doing. The rotation grew faster until a vast whirlpool opened before him.

No waterspouts—just a giant whirlpool! What difference did that make? One disruption had risen above the surface, and the other churned below it.

Juan was about to protest. Ethan had said he would not use that move! But he stopped himself. Both effects came from Twister, yet a whirlpool was not a waterspout. Ethan had kept the exact promise he had made. Somehow that made it more infuriating.

Juan still had a battle to fight. He had encountered whirlpools before and could deal with one more easily than with the twin waterspouts. This time he ignored Dragonite at first and ordered Politoed and Whiscash to attack Milotic together.

They knocked Milotic out. Only then did Juan turn both Pokémon against Dragonite. For a while, the decision worked. The balance shifted toward him, and another strong push might have won the round.

But he had underestimated Dragonite again. Even facing two opponents alone, it held its ground. The struggle lasted a long time. Then Dragonite surged forward and, in one fierce stretch of fighting, defeated Politoed and Whiscash both.
