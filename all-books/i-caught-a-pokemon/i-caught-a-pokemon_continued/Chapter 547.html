Ash had arrived late, so his room was on the top floor of the competitors' hotel. The view was better up there, though getting in and out would be less convenient.

Once they had put away their luggage, Ash and his friends took the elevator back to the lobby to meet Ethan. Together they headed to the outdoor battlefields for the screening trials.

The trials gathered more than two hundred registered Trainers for an initial selection, reducing the field to forty-eight. They were group matches held before the preliminaries, away from the public tournament. Each entrant had three chances to battle different opponents. Judges assessed their performance, so even someone who lost a match might still reach the preliminaries.

After Ethan explained the rules, Ash nodded. "So if I do well enough to impress the judges, I could advance even if I lost two matches?"

"That's right," Ethan said with a smile. "You can use only one Pokémon in each match, though, which makes it easy for a battle to go wrong. If you want to get through, you have to give every match your best."

Every entrant had to take part. Trainers would reach Silver Town on different days, so the Pokémon League had scheduled the trials for the day before the tournament opened.

Ethan, Ash, and all the other Trainers had to compete. Even though Ethan had arrived well ahead of time, he could only take part today alongside everyone else.

At the venue they found more than a dozen battlefields enclosed by wire fencing, lined up side by side. The other two hundred or so entrants were arriving too.

At the service desk, Ethan drew his match assignments.

He looked down. He was in Group A, scheduled for matches two, four, and ten.

All even numbers. He wondered whether that was a good sign.

"Let's wish each other luck."

Ethan and Ash went to their separate fields. Erika naturally accompanied Ethan, while Brock went with Ash. Misty looked between the two, silently apologized to Ash, then followed Ethan.

The three reached the Group A field at an easy pace. The two Trainers ahead of Ethan were already battling. They were young men of about the same age. One had sent out Nidoking, the other Hitmontop.

The Pokémon seemed evenly matched, trading attacks while neither gained a clear lead. Both Trainers shouted encouragement at the top of their lungs, determined not to lose their first match.

After Ethan watched for a while, Hitmontop landed a Rolling Kick squarely in Nidoking's chest and knocked it down.

Before Nidoking's Trainer could return his Pokémon, the referee signaled Ethan and his opponent to enter.

"Good luck, Ethan!" Misty said, raising a little fist to cheer him on.

Ethan smiled at her and walked onto the field, studying his opponent.

It was a boy of about ten, wearing a blue baseball cap and dark clothes, with a Pikachu at his side. If Ethan had not just parted from Ash, he might have thought Ash was standing opposite him.

The boy's face and expression looked remarkably like Ash's, and he even had a Pikachu. He resembled Ash more than Richie did.

Once both Trainers had reached their positions, the referee raised a colored flag. "Next is Group A's second match: Ethan versus Travis!"

Travis?

Ethan had heard that name somewhere.

Wait...

A memory flashed through his mind. "Travis, are you from the Orange Islands?" he asked.

"Huh?" Travis looked at him in surprise. "Yes. How did you know?"

"So it's you." Ethan smiled as he realized who the boy was. "What a coincidence. When I challenged the Gyms in the Orange Islands, I met your mother on Kumquat Island. She asked me to tell you, if I happened to meet you in Johto, that she misses you and hopes you'll come home soon."

Misty nodded. "Luana asked Ash and me too. I never imagined you'd be here at the Silver Conference!"

"Mom..." Travis's thoughts flew back to the island where he had grown up. He could almost see his mother. His eyes grew damp, and he nodded gratefully to Ethan. "Thank you for telling me. I really have been away a long time. Once I'm done with the Silver Conference, I'll go home!"

Ethan had not expected much to come of the request. Johto was vast; how was he supposed to find someone he did not know? Now that he had delivered Luana's message, he felt the satisfaction of fulfilling a promise.

He had never imagined that Travis would be his first opponent in the screening trials. What a twist of fate.

Travis collected himself and looked at the handsome man before him again. He was grateful for the news of his mother, but he would not hold back in the battle ahead. He had left home to get away from a comfortable life and achieve something through his own efforts.

His gaze turned resolute, and he took a Poké Ball from inside his jacket.

"That's the look I like to see..."

Ethan met his eyes and took a Poké Ball from his belt pouch.

At the referee's signal, they threw their balls together. Blue light flashed, and two Pokémon appeared on the field.

Travis had sent out Kingler, a Pokémon commonly found along the coast.

His Kingler was unusual, though. It stood about a meter and a half tall, a full size larger than most of its kind, and looked powerfully built.

Ethan had chosen Dragonite.

"Please be careful," Travis said. Perhaps he felt a little guilty about battling Ethan so soon after receiving his kindness. "Apart from Pikachu, Kingler was the first Pokémon I caught. It came with me from the Orange Islands, and it's very strong!"

"Is that so?" Ethan had some idea of what Travis was feeling. He smiled. "Then you'd better be careful too. My Dragonite is no pushover."
