Drake recalled Onix and threw his third Poké Ball. Blue light flashed, and a deep-purple Gengar appeared, hovering above the field.

The announcer took a long breath and recovered a little from his disappointment. "Just as in his previous matches, Drake's third choice is Gengar! This is a powerful and dependable Pokémon. How will Ethan respond? Will he keep Venusaur in while it's still in good condition, or choose someone else?"

Even the announcer failed to notice that his voice no longer held the confidence it had at the start of the match.

In the Trainer's area, Ethan saw Gengar and recalled Venusaur. He had several Pokémon he could send against it, but he had made a plan the night before. There was no need to hesitate. He turned to the companion beside him.

Haunter turned toward him at the same moment. Their eyes met.

"Heh, heh, heh! Let me take this one!" Haunter laughed before Ethan could speak.

"That's what I had in mind." Ethan smiled and nodded. "I'm counting on you."

"Don't worry. I won't lose!"

Haunter drifted slowly out onto the field under thousands of watching eyes. The announcer sounded incredulous.

"What? Ethan is sending a Haunter against Gengar?"

The spectators were just as confused. Haunter was Gengar's unevolved form. Surely a Pokémon that had not evolved would be weaker than its fully evolved opponent in both ability and strength. Why choose it when Ethan had a formidable Gyarados and a Venusaur fresh from victory? Had two wins made him overconfident, or did he trust this Haunter that much?

Whatever the answer, they thought it was good news for Drake. At last he should win a round.

Drake was not so sure. The two Ghost Pokémon were pulling faces at each other, laughing, and chasing each other about as though they were old friends. Yet his research on Ethan told him that this was not a Trainer who would give away a match. Haunter must have something special for Ethan to choose it now.

When the referee restarted the battle, Haunter showed everyone what that something was.

Who said an unevolved Pokémon could not defeat its evolved form? A born prankster with power far beyond its opponent's, Haunter toyed with Drake's Gengar throughout the match.

They traded Shadow Ball, Dark Pulse, and Hex, but whatever Gengar used, Haunter answered with greater force and broke through it with ease. When Gengar grew tired and used Destiny Bond, Haunter saw through the attempt. It simply waited for the move's effect to end, then finished Gengar with a grin.

Haunter had made it look effortless. Drake's Gengar had not touched it once.

The referee raised the green flag and announced the result. The spectators stared at one another in silence. It took the announcer a long moment to find his voice.

"That... that was far too easy. No, the difference in strength was too great!"

The score was three to zero. Was this really the Drake the Orange Islands knew? Before the match, League officials had promised a fierce, evenly matched contest between two powerful Trainers. What the crowd had seen was nothing like that.

Ditto, Onix, and Gengar were all Pokémon whose strength the spectators had witnessed before. Today, against Ethan, they seemed to be fighting on an entirely different level.

Across the field, Drake looked at his unconscious Gengar and shook his head. The defeat had been absolute. Most troubling of all, Ethan had not given a single command. Haunter had fought freely and won through its own power and experience.

Drake quietly recalled Gengar.

"What a powerful Haunter," the announcer said. A question still bothered him. "But with strength like that, why hasn't it evolved? If it became Gengar, surely it would be even stronger!"

Haunter glanced toward the commentary booth.

Evolve? That's easy. What's so difficult about it?

It would show these people who thought they knew everything.

"Heh, heh, heh!" Haunter grinned widely. White light suddenly shone from its body. When the glow faded, it had taken on an entirely new form before the astonished eyes of Drake and the audience.

"It... it evolved?!" the announcer cried. He sounded baffled. Haunter had not appeared to gain enough experience during that short battle. How had it evolved now?

Ethan reacted differently. He covered his face with a hand. He understood Haunter's reason all too well. The show-off had chosen this moment just to see everyone's shocked expressions.

In its new body, the Pokémon strutted back to him. "Well? I didn't embarrass you, did I?"

It was Gengar now. Round and plump, it still looked mischievous but somehow a little cuter too. It did not seem used to its body yet, stretching its feet and turning its hands over to examine them.

Ethan studied its new shape. "You did well. Just don't do that again."

Gengar blinked and scratched its head. "What? I didn't make a big enough impression?"

"No." Ethan smiled ruefully. "You made too big an impression. Now there's no mystery left to my last trump card."
