Misdreavus came straight through Ice Beam, from the front of the attack to the back. Its body was covered in wounds, but it did not slow. It kept charging at Glalie.

That only confirmed Katie's plan for Ethan. It was painfully obvious.

Ash still had not worked out her strategy. He clearly did not know what made a Ghost-type Pokémon so dangerous. All he saw was a rare opening, and he did not hesitate to order Glalie to use Headbutt.

Glalie rushed forward at full speed and slammed into Misdreavus. Misdreavus made no attempt to dodge.

Bang! Even from the stands, they could hear the impact.

The blow threw Misdreavus backward. Just before it fell, a purple light flashed in its eyes. Anyone who was not watching closely could have missed it. The light vanished, Misdreavus hit the ground, and its eyes rolled back. It was unable to battle.

Ash thought he had been handed an easy win. He raised his right fist and cheered, delighted to have pulled back another round.

The next moment, purple light flew out of Misdreavus's eyes and into Glalie's. Pain crossed the floating Pokémon's face. It dropped from the air and lay still, also unable to battle.

“What?!” Ash stared, unable to understand what had happened. The announcer's voice rang across the field.

“Misdreavus is unable to battle! Glalie is also unable to battle! This round is a draw!”

In the stands, May looked just as lost. She had never seen anything like it. “Why did Glalie fall too? Misdreavus had already lost.”

Max pushed up his glasses. “Was that Destiny Bond?” he asked, sounding uncertain.

He knew the move from studying Pokémon, but recognizing one in a real battle was harder. Several moves had similar looking effects. A brief flash of light was not much to go on.

“Yes, it was Destiny Bond,” Ethan said with a nod. “It's a Ghost-type move that takes the opponent down with its user. If an opponent's attack directly knocks out the Pokémon that used it, the attacker faints as well. It's a way to force a one-for-one trade.”

“It's that powerful?” May asked. “Then couldn't you use it to take down any opponent?”

“It has serious limits,” Ethan said. “Using it twice in a row fails. It also fails if the opponent doesn't attack soon enough. An experienced Trainer may spot what you're trying to do. Still, against an opponent you can't otherwise beat, it can turn a battle around.”

No one had tried Destiny Bond against Ethan, but a Trainer's intentions could sometimes be plain to see. Katie's had been. If she had used it against him, he would not have given Misdreavus the opening. Against Ash's style of battling, it had caught him by surprise.

Only a skilled Ghost-type specialist could use Destiny Bond with real finesse. Yet against someone like that, Ethan would already be on guard. For all its power, pulling it off was far from easy.

Brock rubbed his chin. “Why would Katie use it here? Maybe she wanted to change the pace, but trading one Pokémon for one doesn't gain her much.”

The exchange had certainly startled the crowd and given them plenty to talk about. Whether it had improved Katie's position was another question.

“She must have her reasons,” Ethan said. “Maybe none of her remaining Pokémon had a reliable way to beat Glalie, so she chose the safer trade. Or perhaps she thought Glalie was Ash's ace and wanted it gone quickly.”

He could only guess. Katie alone knew what she had been thinking.

With Ash's third Pokémon down, the match reached its intermission. Katie still held a lead of one Pokémon.

Ethan reached for more popcorn and found the bag empty. When he turned, May was tossing the last kernels into her mouth one by one. She tilted her head at his look, apparently unaware she had eaten his popcorn.

Well, fine. She could have it.

The short break passed quickly. While the Trainers rested, the field wheel settled on the Water Field for the second half.

That was good news for Ash. His Corphish was strong, and the water would give it room to show what it could do. As soon as the match resumed, he sent Corphish out. Katie answered with Golduck, which had performed so well earlier.

The two Pokémon attacked at once. They fought evenly at first, but Katie soon read Ash's straightforward approach. Golduck caught Corphish with Confusion and held it helpless in midair. Golduck threw it into the pool, only for Corphish to seize its chance, burst out of the water behind Golduck, and land a heavy blow.

The battle began to shift Ash's way. When they met at close range again, Corphish clamped Golduck's claw in its pincers and hurled it away. It immediately gathered a Bubble Beam and fired, defeating Golduck.

It had been one of the few rounds in this match that went back and forth instead of favoring one side throughout. Corphish had simply performed a little better, earning Ash the win.

“They're finally tied,” Max said, letting out a long breath. “Now they're back at the starting line.”

“There's more to it than that,” Ethan said. “Ash fights on momentum. Once he gets a win, it's much easier for him to find his rhythm.”

Corphish was still waving its pincers triumphantly. Katie's expression barely changed, but Ethan could see a hint of urgency in it.

She sent Venomoth back out. Ash recalled Corphish and brought out Swellow, turning the round into an aerial battle.

People jokingly called it “Bug Swellow”: whenever it appeared, it was bound to take down at least one Pokémon. The nickname said plenty about its strength. Ash had even chosen a favorable matchup this time, sending a Flying type against Venomoth's Bug type. Such a deliberate type advantage was unusual for him.

Ash finally showed the skill everyone expected of him. Venomoth used Disable again and sealed one of Swellow's moves, but Swellow's greater strength and relentless attacks carried it to a comfortable victory.

Three down for Ash, four for Katie. Ash had won another round.

On the big screen, Katie's brows drew together. The “Cool Beauty” of Lilycove City looked far less cool than her reputation suggested.

Ash now had the initiative and showed no sign of switching. Katie took out her fifth Poké Ball. A Scizor appeared in front of Swellow.

“Scizor?” May asked. “It's a Bug type too, like Venomoth. Is it really a good choice against a Flying type like Swellow?”

“Flying is strong against Bug,” Ethan said, nodding before shaking his head. “But Scizor is also a Steel type. Flying-type attacks don't do much to Steel.”

May studied its body again. It really did look like steel. A bird Pokémon would probably hurt its beak pecking at that armor.

When the round began, both Pokémon used Quick Attack and charged. They clashed five or six times in a matter of moments, neither gaining an edge. They changed moves and traded attack for defense. Even after a prolonged battle of Double Teams, they remained evenly matched.

At last, Swellow caught the briefest opening and struck with Aerial Ace, defeating Scizor.

“Bug Swellow” had lived up to its name again. Its strength had brought Ash two wins.

Ethan smiled. If Ash kept growing like this, his talent with Flying-type Pokémon might one day make him an excellent Flying-type master. And what would that make Ethan?

As a Scizor Trainer himself, he could see that Katie's Scizor was quite strong, even if it did not match his own. It could have beaten Swellow. It had ended its Double Team first, though, and that small mistake had cost it the round.

The score stood at three to five. Katie was on the edge of elimination. One more loss would end her tournament.

Under the eyes of the whole stadium, she solemnly sent out her final Pokémon. Blue light flashed, and a Walrein appeared in the pool. It lifted its head and roared.

“That's quite an entrance.” Ethan raised his brows and smiled at the others. “I'd say Walrein is Katie's ace.”

“Her ace?” May asked.

“Yes,” Misty said. As a Water-type Trainer, she agreed with Ethan. “Look at its condition and its muscles. You can feel how strong it is.”

With both of them saying so, Max and the others had no reason to doubt it.

“Ace or not, Ash still has Swellow and two other Pokémon,” Max said with a grin. “He can wear Walrein down if he has to. There's nothing to worry about.”

…

“Corphish is unable to battle! Walrein wins!”

As the announcer called the result, Max stared from the stands, his mouth hanging wide enough to fit two eggs.

He had just said Ash could not lose. Now Walrein had defeated Swellow and Corphish one after the other. For a moment, Max wondered if he had jinxed them. How else had such a promising position turned so suddenly?

Ash looked grim too. It was hard for anyone to stay calm after the same opponent knocked out two of their Pokémon in succession.

He had not expected Walrein to be this strong. It had beaten Swellow and Corphish with startling ease. Both had used some energy in earlier rounds, but defeating them back to back still showed how powerful Katie's ace was.

Worse, Walrein had built up its own momentum and was almost unhurt.

Ash had one Pokémon left. He could still give the last round everything he had. He gritted his teeth and threw his final Poké Ball.
