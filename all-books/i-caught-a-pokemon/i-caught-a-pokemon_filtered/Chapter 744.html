Chapter 744: The Wrong Person

Dinner was finally ready around seven that evening. The elderly innkeeper, who was also the cook, filled more than half the square table with a generous meal.

Muscle Island was far from the mainland, with fewer vegetables available, so most of the dishes were seafood. Perhaps Ethan had paid well. Whatever the reason, the woman's decades of cooking experience showed in every plate. Fresh seafood, enticing colors, careful presentationeven familiar ingredients smelled so good that the three guests wanted to taste them at once.

Misty did not wait. She stole a bite before the meal began and gave the cook an enthusiastic thumbs-up.

Mm! It's delicious!

Bea stared at the crowded table, plainly surprised by its size. Ethan smiled. Help yourself.

She hesitated. Do you usually eat this much?

Of course not. Most days we keep things simple. When we meet a friend, though, we sometimes have a feast. It's become a habit.

Bea finally picked up her utensils. Rather than the seafood, her eyes went to the desserts. Ethan and Misty exchanged a surprised look.

Anyone who saw how Bea dressed and knew she specialized in Fighting-type Pokemon might assume she followed a strict training diet, avoiding sugar and fat in favor of protein. Instead, she loved sweets and ate them without restraint. The pleasure on her face after each bite left no doubt.

Ethan and Misty let her enjoy them and turned to their own plates. After a tiring day, a fine meal was an excellent reward.

Neither Bea nor Misty drank alcohol. Ethan poured for himself and had several cups of sake. The food called for something to accompany it, and he had reason to celebrate. Necrozma had taken Maxie and Archie, but Ethan had stopped Groudon and Kyogre. Team Aqua and Team Magma on the Hoenn mainland would be swept away regardless.

The meal lasted until nine. They ate and talked, with Misty doing most of the talking, and all three enjoyed themselves.

After washing up, it was time to sleep. Standing in the bedroom together, however, they found the mood had grown a little delicate.

The old Japanese-style inn was small, with only one guest room. There were no beds. The guests would lay bedding on the tatami and sleep on the floor.

That would not normally have been a problem. Ethan and Bea, however, had not grown close enough for sharing a room to feel entirely natural. They had once sheltered from rain together in a cave and sat together wearing little more than their clothes, but that had been an emergency. Travelers made allowances when they had to.

Here, in a quiet, comfortable room, it felt different. Misty's presence kept things from being too awkward, yet Ethan found himself thinking of them as ordinary people rather than Trainers roughing it outdoors.

While he worried, Bea knelt and spread out her bedding. There was not a hint of embarrassment on her face. Ethan realized he had been the only one fretting. Bea was direct by nature. They were friends, all three would be present, and their bedding would not even touch. Once the light went out, what did it matter?

Her calm helped him relax. It was not merely that he was less decisive than Bea. He already had several women in his life and had to consider how things might look. He also owed Bea respect for her reputation.

Misty laid her bedding beside Bea's. Ethan spread his a little farther away. The sake was beginning to catch up with him. He yawned, slipped under his covers, and fell asleep within seconds.

Misty and Bea were not so quick to sleep. They had eaten too much, and their unsettled stomachs kept them awake. After the light went out, Misty drew Bea into another conversation.

Crack!

A great roll of thunder sounded overhead. Heavy rain began to drum on the roof and pour into the courtyard.

Rain? Misty turned toward the window in the dim light. I like falling asleep to it. It's been stuffy all evening. Maybe this will cool things down.

She wished Bea good night and had barely closed her eyes when something cold touched her face. Her good mood vanished.

What? Is the roof leaking?

She sat up and held out a hand. Several drops fell into her palm. Bea sat up too. Even in the dark, her movements made it clear that water was falling on her bedding as well.

Misty sighed and switched on the light. A wide patch of ceiling above them was wet, and water dripped steadily from it.

What do we do?

The two girls, both in their nightclothes, looked at each other helplessly. Ethan was still sound asleep, and the roof above him was dry. The old inn had evidently needed repairs; tonight's downpour had found the weak spot. There was no one to fix it now.

They looked at the open floor on either side of Ethan. We'll have to move.

Without waking him, they rolled up their bedding and carried it over. Where exactly to put it was another question, particularly for Bea.

Misty had slept beside Ethan many times, in tents and elsewhere. She was used to it. Bea would be much closer to him than before. Would she be able to sleep? They could wake Ethan and ask him to move, but neither wanted to disturb him while he slept so deeply.

After a moment's hesitation, Bea laid out her bedding beside Ethan's. Misty felt a vague unease, then reasoned that they would still be under separate covers. Ethan was asleep anyway. What could happen? She settled on his other side.

Rain continued to fall outside. The light went out, leaving the room dark again.

Bea lay stiffly on her back, listening to the water rush past the window. She could not settle. Her body felt tense, and her heart beat faster than usual. For all her composure, she was lying beside a man. She could hardly be indifferent.

Her regular schedule eventually caught up with her. After some time, her breathing slowed, and she fell asleep.

The rain did not let up. Its steady patter filled the quiet room, along with the faint sound of three people breathing.

Much later, Ethan woke in a haze. He was thirsty.

Perhaps the innkeeper's homemade sake had been stronger than it tasted. Even after sleeping, his head swam. He noticed nothing unusual around him. In the dim light, he climbed out of his warm bedding, found a cup of water on the table, drank, and crawled back.

He changed position to go to sleep again and sensed someone in front of him. Half opening his eyes, he made out a person lying with her back to him. The figure beneath the covers was a woman, with short hair.

Erika? What's she doing here? Am I dreaming?

The room was too dark for him to notice the difference in hair color. Sleep and drink muddled his thoughts, and he did not question why Erika would be beside him.

Missing her, he left his own covers and drew the woman into his arms. He buried his face in her hair and reached for her without thinking.

Then the touch made him pause. He knew Erika intimately. She always smelled faintly of flowers, her body was soft, and her figure was unmistakable.

This woman felt different. He touched her again, unable to believe it. She was smaller. Her scent was soap and shampoo, not flowers. And beneath his hand, he felt firm abdominal muscles. Her whole body was taut, like someone who trained every day.

Trained?

The thought struck him like a bolt of lightning. He snatched his hand away, retreated to his own bedding, and finally saw who was beside him. It was Bea, not Erika.

What had he done? Ethan looked at his hand, then at Bea, cold sweat forming on his forehead. If she noticed, she would think he had touched her deliberately. How could he explain it?

But why was she beside him? She had spread her bedding on the other side of the room. Had he returned to the wrong place after getting water?

He started to rise and find his own bedding, then noticed Misty lying on his other side. He was in the right place. Both girls had moved while he slept.

A soft dripping sound came from elsewhere in the room. He understood. The roof had leaked over their original bedding.

His heart pounded. He waited, watching Bea. She neither woke nor turned on him. He let out a silent breath. She must have slept through it. Thank goodness. He really had touched her; if she knew, he would have no way to defend himself.

He resolved to act as though nothing had happened. Quietly, he turned toward Misty and lay down again.

At least there was no danger of mistaking Misty for Erika. Misty had none of Erika's curves, and Ethan would never pursue her while she was underage. Even if he accidentally touched her, their closeness meant she was unlikely to hold it against him.

Bea still had not stirred. Reassured, he closed his eyes.

What Ethan did not know was that Bea had awakened the moment he buried his face in her hair. She was facing away, so he had not seen her eyes snap open. She had felt his hand slip beneath her covers and into her clothes.

No man had ever touched her that way. Shock left her unable to think or even breathe for a moment. By the time she understood what had happened, Ethan had already let go and pulled away.

That surprised her too. She remained still. He had been close enough for her to smell the sake on his breath. He must have been confused in his sleep.

He probably mistook me for his girlfriend.

Bea had learned at dinner that Ethan had a girlfriend. Misty had made a point of mentioning it. Her reason, to an outsider, was obvious: she wanted Bea to know Ethan was already involved with someone, and to prevent anything from developing between them.

For all Misty's calculations, she had not imagined Ethan would skip straight past that warning in his half-asleep state and reach for Bea.
