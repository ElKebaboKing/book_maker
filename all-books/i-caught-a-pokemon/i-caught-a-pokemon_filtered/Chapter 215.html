Chapter 215: Seven Legendary Pokemon Clash

The four legendary Pokemon already fighting noticed the three birds entering the sky above them.

The island's three gods stopped first. They watched their counterparts warily, unable to tell where the newcomers had come from or what they wanted. Lugia used the pause to catch its breath.

Ethan's birds offered no greeting. As soon as they reached the others, they attacked.

The three island birds were furious. Had these strangers come to seize their territory or simply pick a fight? Either way, they were fiercely territorial. They left Lugia alone and met Ethan's birds head-on.

Fire faced fire, ice met ice, and lightning sought out lightning. Six bird Pokemon chased one another across the sky, their moves bursting into brilliant light whenever they met.

The gods of the Orange Islands were formidable. The collector had captured them with surprising ease, but their attacks were devastating. After only a few exchanges, Ethan's Moltres was holding its own while his Articuno and Zapdos were slowly being pushed back.

Ethan was neither surprised nor alarmed. This was no formal match. His Pokemon could work together, draw enemies away, and change tactics whenever they needed to.

He surveyed the battlefield and settled on the God of Ice. In a group fight, it made sense to concentrate on the opponent who offered the best opening.

That did not mean the island's Articuno was weak. All three gods were immensely powerful. But Ethan's Moltres was the strongest of his birds, and its Fire-type attacks gave it an advantage against an Ice-type opponent. With help from the others, they might defeat the God of Ice quickly.

Ethan released Gardevoir. The battle was too far away for his shouted commands to reach the birds, so he needed her to carry his thoughts directly to them.

Gardevoir rested one hand lightly on his arm and fixed her gaze on Ethan's three birds.

Moltres, leave the God of Fire and attack the God of Ice. Zapdos, help Moltres. Your Electric-type moves will work well against its Flying type. Articuno, hold off the other two. Don't let either of them interfere until we've beaten the God of Ice.

The birds changed targets, each taking up its assigned role.

"Connect me to Lugia too," Ethan told Gardevoir.

She nodded and turned her attention to Lugia.

Lugia, I'm the Trainer of those three birds. I need your help. Please work with my Articuno to keep the Gods of Fire and Thunder away for now.

A neutral voice answered inside Ethan's mind. Lugia was speaking through telepathy of its own.

I see. Are you the commander?

No. I'm a Trainer who happened to come to this island. The commander is probably my friend. He's gone to Ice Island for the last treasure. This isn't the time to argue over titles, though. Even an ordinary person can do something in a crisis.

Lugia was silent for a moment. You're right. I'll help hold back the Gods of Thunder and Fire and buy time for the commander's return.

With Ethan's birds in the fight, Lugia no longer faced all three opponents alone. It could turn more of its strength toward attacking.

Energy gathered in its mouth. A huge Hydro Pump surged toward the God of Fire.

Bang!

The attack was too fierce to meet directly. The island's Moltres tried to evade, but Lugia was close enough that the water clipped one side of its body. It cried out, shuddered with pain, and climbed higher.

One strike had wounded the God of Fire. Water's advantage over Fire helped, but Lugia's power clearly surpassed that of any one bird on the field.

Elsewhere, Ethan's Moltres and Zapdos had caught the God of Ice between them. Moltres fired Flamethrower again and again while Zapdos struck with repeated electric bolts. The island's Articuno had to defend in two directions at once.

It fought fiercely, but it could not watch both attackers every instant. Flame or lightning found its mark whenever its attention shifted, and its strength began to flag.

The God of Thunder tried to go to its aid. Ethan's Articuno intercepted it halfway.

Ethan glanced across the sky and nodded. If they could defeat the God of Ice first, they could take control of the battle.

He found a moment to check on Ash. Ash had taken a plank from the wrecked sailboat and was riding it like a sled toward Ice Island, pulled by Charizard. He kept having to steer around the stray attacks from the battle, so he was moving more slowly than Ethan had hoped.

Still, the fight was turning in their favor. Ethan felt less desperate for Ash to return with the last treasure immediately.

Then dozens of iron rings flew from the frozen crater on Lightning Island. They closed around Lugia, crackling with electricity, and bound it in midair within seconds.

The collector!

Ethan had not forgotten him through carelessness. Everyone had been focused on escaping the airship before it exploded, and they had assumed the collector might die in the crash. Somehow he had survived. Seeing Lugia appear, he had made his move.

Lugia screamed and struggled, but it could not break free of the rings.

Ethan's brow tightened. With Lugia unable to move, the battle could reverse in an instant. He already knew how tough the rings were. Sending his birds to destroy them would mean surrendering their advantage over the God of Ice, and the three island birds would hardly stand by while they tried.

Possibilities flashed through his mind. There was no time to hesitate.

Zapdos! Articuno! Moltres! We have to defeat at least one of them before the three gods regroup. It's time to stop holding back. Use that big move!
