Chapter 338: Puzzles and Mysterious Writing

News of the secret chamber caused an enormous stir and quickly spread throughout the Ruins of Alph park. People stopped working and gathered outside the stone room, craning their necks to glimpse whatever secrets lay within.

Seeing the crowd, Foster hurriedly had the barrier moved forward. This kept bystanders away, both to prevent anyone from being trampled and to protect the ruins. Once that was done, he and Ethan entered the hidden chamber through the newly opened passage.

The room was noticeably darker than the outer chamber. It was still empty and looked little different from the others, but they discovered a stone picture on the far wall.

The image carved into it remained clear. Strangely, however, the slab had been divided into nine sections and arranged without any apparent order. It was impossible to tell what the full picture depicted.

Foster stepped closer and studied it with a frown. After a moment, realization dawned.

"This looks like some kind of puzzle!"

Ethan nodded.

Foster was right. Just as in the games, the wall before them held a picture puzzle whose pieces had been scrambled.

From a modern perspective, puzzles were merely educational toys. In that ancient era, however, something like this would have been an impressively sophisticated mechanism.

The sight made Ethan breathe a little easier.

He had worried that all their effort would lead nowhere. Now they had finally found something and had not wasted their time.

Foster put on gloves and tested the puzzle. The pieces could not be removed, so he had to slide them one by one, arranging them in the proper positions like a mechanical riddle.

Researchers needed sharp minds. After only a little experimentation, Foster assembled the picture.

When its complete shape appeared, everyone realized that it depicted Kabuto.

"A picture of Kabuto?"

Foster was surprised at first, but it also seemed reasonable.

Kabuto must have been common in that era. Using one as decoration would have been perfectly natural.

But was that all?

Was it truly nothing more than a simple puzzle?

They waited. Nothing happened. Foster was about to speak when the picture gave off a faint glow. Several short lines of writing appeared across the stone.

The symbols did not belong to any modern language. They resembled English letters, but every character contained a round shape like an eye, giving the writing an eerie appearance.

"It's Unown script!" Foster exclaimed.

Ethan knew exactly what it was, but he played along and looked surprised. "Unown script?"

"That's right!" Foster nodded. Knowing Ethan might understand nothing about it, he volunteered an explanation. "Unown script is often found in ruins. It's an ancient form of writing and the predecessor of the letters we use today. According to legend, its characters evolved from the shapes of a Pokemon!"

"Do you know what it says?"

"Give me a moment. I'll try to translate it!"

Foster hurriedly took a handbook from his trouser pocket and began comparing its entries with the symbols.

After more than ten minutes of work, he opened a notebook and slowly wrote the translation on the paper:

Our words shall remain here for the ages.

The short sentence left everyone looking at one another.

Foster scratched his head. "What does that mean?"

"I think..." Ethan had no intention of revealing too much and answered vaguely. "It may only be a ceremonial phrase."

Nothing else happened after the writing appeared. Foster was deeply disappointed. He tapped one place, then another, unwilling to accept that the discovery ended there.

No matter what he tried, there was no response.

"Don't rush." Ethan took out the radio again and waved it before Foster. "There should be other chambers. Let's keep searching."

"Other chambers?" Foster could no longer contain his questions. "Mr. Vale, you mean there are more? How do you know? And how did you think of using a radio to search for the signal in the first place?"

Ethan could not possibly explain. "It was only a sudden inspiration. Come on, let's go back outside."

Since Ethan did not wish to say more, Foster had no choice but to swallow all his questions.

They left the chamber. When Ethan switched on the radio again, the nearby transmission had vanished, perhaps because they had solved the mechanism. A faint signal still came from elsewhere.

That made Foster realize Ethan was right. There had to be more than one secret chamber.

The group set out again. With their earlier experience to guide them, Ethan carried the radio and located hidden rooms. Whenever he found one, Foster directed the workers to excavate a passage while Ethan moved on to search for the next.

Working together, they quickly discovered three more hidden chambers.

Inside, they found puzzles depicting Aerodactyl and Omanyte. In the final chamber, the picture before them was Ho-Oh.

The first few images had drawn interest, but when Ho-Oh appeared, Foster and the other researchers began breathing heavily with excitement.

"It's Ho-Oh!" Foster said in disbelief. "Then the mysterious bird cry we heard through the radio must have been Ho-Oh's voice!"

After hearing that, Ethan also felt the cry likely belonged to Ho-Oh. But he had never actually heard Ho-Oh's voice and did not dare draw a firm conclusion.

Solving the three mechanisms revealed three more passages of writing. After that, every sound from the radio suddenly disappeared.

No matter where they went or which frequency they tried, the speaker produced only endless static.

It seemed the mysterious signal had vanished completely.

"What do we do now?"

Foster was anxious. Everything that had happened today was more astonishing and inexplicable than anything he had experienced since arriving at the Ruins of Alph. He desperately wanted to uncover the final secret.

Ethan frowned too.

In the games, this event had been only a short sequence, little more than a cutscene or a simple process. This was reality, and Ethan did not know what came next.

As he thought, his gaze moved across the locations of the four hidden chambers. Suddenly, his eyes lit up.

In his mind, he connected the four positions with lines and identified their point of intersection. He hurried over and soon reached an ordinary-looking stone slab.

He stamped hard. A hollow thud echoed beneath his feet, proving there was another secret chamber below.

Foster could swear that the researchers had walked through this spot countless times and that the slab had never been there before.

Solving the puzzles must have triggered some mechanism and caused it to appear.

He excitedly waved to the workers and ordered them to open the slab. When they lifted it away, a pitch-black chamber appeared below.
