Chapter 196: Navel Gym

Danny faltered. His smile stiffened.

As a Gym Leader with no shortage of charm, he had always found it easy to strike up conversations with women on Navel Island and elsewhere in the Orange League. He had been turned down now and then, but never ignored so completely.

Meeting someone as beautiful as Sabrina had seemed like a stroke of luck. He had not expected her to look straight through him.

He glanced at Ethan. The younger man was better looking, and perhaps carried himself a little better too. Did Sabrina like him? Were they already a couple? Danny had not noticed any particularly intimate gesture between them.

No matter. She did not know he was the Navel Gym Leader. When she found out, she would surely be impressed and change her attitude.

The three followed the path toward the Gym, with Danny leading. He filled the walk with stories of Navel Island and things that had happened to him there, trying hard to be entertaining.

Sabrina showed no interest. Even Ethan yawned, offering an occasional response only so Danny would not feel as if he were talking to himself.

Still, Danny could not shake an odd feeling. Something seemed to be pressing against him, a prickling force that almost felt like psychic power. It was as if someone objected to his being there.

Ethan must feel threatened and want him away from his companion. Danny scratched at the uncomfortable sensation and stole a look back.

Ethan seemed bored. He watched the distant scenery or spoke quietly with Sabrina. He was doing nothing unusual.

Then Danny met Sabrina's eyes. A cold glint flashed in them. Despite the summer heat, sweat broke out across his back. He blinked and looked again. Her expression was perfectly ordinary.

Had he imagined it? He could make no sense of what he had felt.

After a long walk, they reached an arched iron gate at the foot of the mountain. Stone walls stretched away on either side, hiding everything behind them.

Danny turned with a smile that held a touch of relief. "We're here. This is the entrance to Navel Gym."

He and Ethan pushed the heavy gate open together. Beyond it lay a broad clearing with a wooden hut. A stopped cable car stood behind the hut, while a steep mountain path with a railing ran toward the volcano.

Except for the three of them, the place was empty. Apparently Danny had no other staff. Ethan wondered how one person kept such a large site in order.

Danny guided him to a sign beside the railing.

Welcome, challengers, to Navel Gym. Your first task is to climb the mountain. You must do so by your own strength. Anyone who uses a Pokemon's help during the ascent will be disqualified.

A smaller line at the bottom read: Companions may use the cable car.

"What now?" Sabrina asked softly.

Ethan glanced at Danny. He stood with his arms folded, waiting for Ethan's choice.

"No one else is here, and I don't see any cameras," Ethan said jokingly. "Maybe I should take the cable car with you and go straight to the top."

"Don't do that!" Danny blurted out.

Ethan and Sabrina turned to him. Danny cleared his throat. "The climb is an important part of Navel Gym's challenge. The Leader is watching from somewhere, even if you can't see him. He'd know if you took the cable car."

Ethan had only hoped the remark would prompt Danny to admit who he was so they could discuss the Gym challenge openly. Danny was still keeping up the act. Ethan let him.

"I see," he said with a theatrical sigh. "Then I'll follow the rules. You brought us here because it was on your way, Mr. Danny. You don't need to stay with us now. Go ahead with your own business."

Danny could hardly leave. For his own reasons, he was not ready to reveal his identity, and he worried Ethan might choose the easy ride up if no one watched him.

"I'm heading to the summit too," Danny said. "Let's climb together. We can look out for each other."

Ethan handed Sabrina his extra equipment and watched her board the cable car. He tightened his shoelaces, then followed Danny along the mountain path to the foot of a steep rock face.

Looking up, Ethan guessed the volcano rose at least several hundred meters. Climbing without safety gear would test a challenger's nerve as well as their strength.

The face was less treacherous than it first appeared. Numerous projections offered handholds and footholds. It also slanted rather than rising straight up, giving climbers a way to keep their balance.

Danny started climbing first. Every movement was practiced and sure. The innkeeper had been right: however flirtatious he seemed, he was genuinely skilled at outdoor sports.
