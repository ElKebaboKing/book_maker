Chapter 778: An Arrangement of Fate

Under the crowd's gaze, Ash's final Pokemon appeared: Grovyle.

Both Trainers had only one Pokemon left. Whoever won this last round would advance to the next stage and join the final sixteen.

Even Max and the others were tense. They stood with their fists clenched and called out to Ash.

Come on, Ash!

Don't lose!

As their friends cheered, May quietly asked, Brother Ethan, do you think Ash can make it through?

It was not that she lacked faith in him. At this point, a single mistake could send him out of the tournament. Nobody could know the winner until the last moment.

Watching Ethan battle felt entirely different. They never worried that he would lose. They only wondered how much of his strength an opponent might force him to show. Ash's matches could make them anxious even when he had the advantage, as though defeat might come at any moment.

Trust him, Ethan said with a light smile. Ash is the kind of Trainer who handles pressure best when it matters most. Give him a match where he's comfortably ahead, and he may not know what to do with it. Put him in a hard fight like this, and you'll see what he can really do.

May looked toward the Trainer's area. Ash's face was serious. Thinking over the way he fought, she nodded.

Somehow, calling him Brother Ethan lately had made May feel closer to him. No matter how nervous she felt, hearing his voice now seemed to calm her. It was strange.

On the field, Grovyle and Walrein had begun their fight.

Walrein was an Ice and Water type, while Grovyle was a Grass type. Each could strike the other's weakness for double damage. That mutual threat promised a fast battle.

Grovyle had not yet evolved into Sceptile, but it was arguably Ash's strongest Pokemon at present. People jokingly called it the number one member of his team. Sending it out last showed how much confidence Ash had in it.

Two aces were facing each other. It was bound to be a fine match. Ethan sat up straighter.

Come on, Ash. Show me how much you've grown.

Walrein opened with Ice Beam. Grovyle dodged and countered with Leaf Blade, but Walrein dived into the water. Grovyle's attack missed, and its target disappeared beneath the surface.

Walrein used the field to its advantage, surfacing to attack and then vanishing again. For a while, Grovyle could hardly return a blow. Ash soon found an opening, though. He used the patches of water frozen by Walrein's own Ice Beams to jolt it out of the pool.

Walrein had used so many Ice-type attacks that the whole surface was becoming a hard sheet of ice. It had trapped itself above the water and could no longer dive away. Grovyle had more chances to attack.

The ice slowed Grovyle somewhat, but it was still fast enough to close the distance. With Quick Attack it suddenly accelerated and struck Walrein in the neck before it could react.

Walrein fell heavily. Then it revealed its own surprise. It sprang back up and, imitating Grovyle's movement, charged at tremendous speed and slammed its head into Grovyle. Caught off guard, Grovyle stumbled back more than a dozen steps before regaining its footing.

The spectators realized what they had seen. Mimic was a Normal-type move that let a Pokemon copy the last move its opponent had used and make it its own for the battle. Even a move outside its usual strengths could become a sudden weapon.

Walrein now had Quick Attack, at least temporarily. In a way, it had made up for its lack of speed.

After a moment's surprise, Ash stopped trying to outpace it. He ordered Grovyle into a close-range fight. Katie told Walrein to meet it head-on.

That was exactly what Ash wanted. As the two drew close, Grovyle abruptly sprang backward and fired Bullet Seed. The attack struck Walrein.

Max and the others gaped. Ash had used a feint! He had fooled Katie, the announcer, and the audience alike. It had looked as though Grovyle would settle the round at close range. Instead, it had drawn Walrein into an attack. Ash really had grown.

Grovyle could attack from a distance and hold its own up close. Though unevolved, it was performing almost as well as Tyson's Sceptile. Once it evolved, it would surely become stronger still.

The successful strike gave Grovyle the initiative. It pressed its advantage and controlled the pace. In their final exchange, it dodged Walrein's Quick Attack by the narrowest margin and struck a vital spot with Leaf Blade. Walrein fell.

The match was decided. The referee announced the result at once. Walrein is unable to battle! Grovyle wins! The winner of this match is Ash!

The match had swung back and forth, with several excellent rounds. The crowd had enjoyed every turn and now gave Ash a roaring cheer. Max and the others stood, shouting something toward him.

Their voices vanished beneath the noise. Even someone sitting beside them could barely make out their words, let alone Ash down on the field.

Ethan remained seated, but he applauded quietly. He had seen real progress from Ash. His basic style had not changed, yet his Pokemon were stronger and he had added some strategy to his instincts. He had come a long way since the beginning of his journey.

That made Ash more dangerous, though to Ethan he was still a little inexperienced.

When Ash disappeared into the competitors' tunnel, Ethan and the others left the stands to meet him outside. They had barely approached when Ash waved at them with a broad grin.

Congratulations, Ash! Max and May hurried forward. You're through to the next round too!

That was a good battle, Ethan said. You've grown a lot.

Ash's grin stretched nearly from ear to ear. Heh, of course! I'm pretty good. How could I go out in this round?

Misty gave his boasting no mercy. Listen to you. If I hadn't watched the match, I'd think you beat her easily. You only just scraped through in the final round.

Ash had long since learned to fear Misty's sharp tongue. Apart from Ethan and May, she could be just as cutting with anyone. His smile stiffened, and his pride faltered.

All right. Ethan rubbed Misty's head. You two can stop bickering. Katie was strong. Against someone other than Ash, she might well have advanced.

Ash brightened instantly and shot Misty a triumphant look. See? Even Ethan says Katie was strong. Beating her proves I'm stronger!

Misty had not truly meant to pick a fight, especially on a happy day like this. After traveling with Ash for so long, teasing him had become a habit. Seeing him back to his old self, she smiled at the corners of her eyes, snorted, and turned away.

Another match passed after Ash's. Morrison's match came in the final time slot of the day. He won by a wide margin, easily defeating his opponent, and Ash watched his rival with envy.

Ash had struggled to overcome Katie. Watching Morrison win so comfortably made him feel as though he had been outdone.

By the end of the first day's matches, Ash and Morrison had both advanced. Tyson alone had been eliminated by Ethan. With the two friends still in the tournament, the prospect of a battle between them seemed more real.

That evening, Ash suggested celebrating the three of them reaching the final sixteen together. May enthusiastically agreed. They found the highest-rated pizza restaurant they could and sat down to a feast.

Its most popular dish was an authentic pizza baked in a stone oven. The crust was crisp, the cheese melted in their mouths, and the toppings included roasted meats, seafood, and fruit. Everyone ate their fill.

Ash and Morrison even turned dinner into another contest, each determined to eat more than the other. Ash's appetite was no match for Morrison's enormous stomach, though. He lost by a narrow margin, sighed in frustration, and, with his belly stretched full, declared he would beat Morrison next time.

While they ate and laughed, the draw for the final sixteen was released.

When the notice arrived, nobody felt like eating any longer. Ethan paid the bill, and they hurried back to the competitors' hall. The trip took long enough that, by the time they arrived, the big screen already showed every pairing.

Ash and Morrison looked up. Both froze, then turned toward each other. Their portraits stood side by side in the first pairing of the first row.

They had just shared a meal. Tomorrow they would have to decide which of them was stronger on the battlefield. Their faces went still, their eyes full of complicated feelings.

They had known that as the field narrowed they might eventually meet. Neither had expected it so soon. One would win; the other would leave the tournament with regrets.

Ethan looked away from them and searched for his own pairing. He found his portrait in the last group of the second row. His opponent was a young woman with a ponytail and thick black-rimmed glasses.

He tried to recall her matches. With so few competitors left, he had some impression of nearly all of them. In the round of thirty-two, this woman had fought with a ferocity completely at odds with her unremarkable appearance. She had crushed her opponent in a one-sided match.

Interesting. Ethan rubbed his chin thoughtfully.

He had drawn Tyson in the first round, then the strongest of the remaining competitors in the second. Was he going to face the strongest Trainer in the final eight as well? He did not dwell on it. Perhaps his luck was simply like that.

Even if he had known the Pokemon League had arranged the draw deliberately, he would only have laughed. Against overwhelming strength, such little tricks were useless.

He turned back toward Ash and Morrison just as Morrison ran through the doors. Ash stood stunned for a moment, then chased after him. May and the others watched in confusion.

They... May began.

Leave them be, Ethan said with a wave. It's a quarrel between two boys. They can work it out themselves. Let's return to the dormitory and wait.

As Ethan saw it, Ash and Morrison were friends as well as rivals. They had to face each other tomorrow. If they wanted to preserve their friendship through the battle, only they could speak honestly with each other. Nobody else could do it for them.

May and the others understood. They nodded, left the boys alone, and followed Ethan back to the dormitory.

After they were gone, Goodshow stood behind a glass window on the hall's second floor. He watched Ethan's retreating figure in silence for a long time.

Yes, he had arranged this opponent too. He had chosen the strongest Trainer left in the field.

Ethan was already among the final sixteen, and that was dangerous. If Goodshow could not stop him in the next round, Ethan might reach the final eight, or even the final four. Then everything Goodshow wanted and hoped for might truly fail.
