Chapter 769: Another Sure Win?

True to its name, Sceptile might have been modeled on a lizard and a crested dinosaur. The Hoenn Grass-type starter was green across most of its body, with a broad tail like a mass of leaves and sharp, blade-like leaves growing from its wrists. The round seeds on its back held nutrients that could bring trees to life in an instant.

Pokemon researchers had observed Sceptile tending the trees in forests and basking in the sun to regulate its body temperature. It was especially capable in rainforests, where it could race through the jungle, leap between branches, and pounce on an enemy from above or behind, finishing the attack with the leaves on its arms.

It was a powerful species and a favorite of many new Trainers. No wonder Hoenn's Pokemon Professors recommended it as one of the region's starter Pokemon.

Ethan had faced Sceptile before, but Tyson's looked stronger than any he had encountered. He wanted to see what it could do against Blastoise.

Whatever happened, Ethan had great faith in the Blastoise he had caught near the start of his journey. It had not competed for a long time and faced a type disadvantage, but it was strong and had fought in two Conferences already. Ethan trusted it to win.

Once both Pokemon were ready, the referee gave the signal. The match that would decide which Trainer reached the top sixteen was underway. Their friends and the crowd were watching closely, and the Pokemon League had its own special interest in the result.

The referee's flag fell. At their Trainers' commands, both Pokemon moved at once.

Sceptile was a Grass type built for close combat. It bounded over several rocks and closed rapidly on Blastoise.

Tyson expected Blastoise to dive beneath the water to avoid that approach. Instead, it floated where it was and fired a Water-type attack. A powerful Hydro Pump burst from one of its cannons, aimed at the spot where Sceptile was about to land.

Tyson had traveled long enough to know how to fight a Water type, and Sceptile began to dodge without waiting for an order. It had scarcely moved when the Hydro Pump struck.

Huh?

Tyson stared. Blastoise's aim was unusually good. It had predicted the landing point of his swiftly moving Sceptile and hit it squarely.

Whether Ethan was as strong as Team Rocket's Meowth had claimed, Tyson could not yet say. His Pokemon's fundamentals, at least, were solid. Sceptile resisted Water-type moves, so the hit did limited damage. Even so, the force of the stream stopped its advance. Sceptile flipped in the air and landed on a rock below.

Tyson had been braced for a difficult match, so the surprise did not shake him for long. He ordered Sceptile forward again. Its type advantage meant little from a distance; with few ranged attacks to use here, it needed to get close. This time Tyson was prepared. Blastoise could not possibly catch Sceptile the same way again.

Sceptile sprang toward another rock. Blastoise fired Hydro Pump once more.

In midair, Sceptile changed tactics and used Double Team. Four images split away from it, evading the water and confusing its target.

Before Tyson could relax, Ethan said calmly, Blastoise, hit the one on the far left.

Tyson's heart jumped. He studied the images. Even he could not tell which one was the real Sceptile. Had Ethan worked it out? How could a person see through something the Pokemon could not?

Blastoise fired another Hydro Pump from its other cannon. The stream crossed the short distance in a heartbeat and struck the Sceptile on the far left, knocking it straight into the water.

What?! the announcer cried. Blastoise found the real Sceptile! How? Was that luck, or did Ethan see something the rest of us missed?

Ash and the others were stunned too. Morrison, who had doubted Ethan earlier, could not deny what he had seen. That's incredible. How did he tell? They all looked exactly the same! I couldn't have done that.

None of his companions could answer the question.

That's why I said no one here can beat Ethan, Ash said. He sighed, but a fire burned in his eyes as he clenched a fist. Everyone who faces him notices how strong his Pokemon are. Hardly anyone realizes that Ethan himself is the hardest part of facing him.

Brock nodded. It's Ethan's mind and his commands that make his Pokemon seem so unbeatable.

Listening to them, Morrison studied Ethan again. The man seemed more complex, and more mysterious, than he had a few minutes earlier.

May was even more affected. Before she learned much about battles, Ethan's two Conference titles had told her he was strong, but she had never known what that strength meant. After becoming a Coordinator and competing in the Grand Festival, she could appreciate it. This handsome man with the gentle smile was far stronger than even Robert, the Grand Festival champion.

The battle continued. Sceptile had fallen into the pool with its Double Team broken. Ethan gave it no chance to climb out. Blastoise dived and swam after it.

The mood in the stadium tightened. Even spectators who rarely watched battles knew the danger of a land Pokemon trapped in the water with a Water type. Some of the more timid ones closed their eyes.

Underwater cameras showed Blastoise as a massive shadow. Despite its size, it was closing on Sceptile at remarkable speed. Sceptile flailed briefly, righted itself, and swam toward the nearest rock. It could not match Blastoise in the water. Within moments, its pursuer was behind it.

Sceptile! Behind you! Tyson shouted.

Sceptile saw the rock almost within reach. It felt the surge of water at its back and had time only to turn before Blastoise's round head slammed into it.

Bang!

Blastoise drove Sceptile toward the bottom of the pool. Sceptile was not a Water type, but it did not fear water. It had taken a breath before going under. Instead of rushing to the surface, it raised both arms and watched carefully for the next attack.

What came was no ordinary strike. A huge shell spun toward it, sending ripples through the water.

Sceptile might have blocked another Pokemon with its leaf-bladed arms. How was it supposed to stop a spinning shell? Even touching it looked painful. The water kept Tyson's commands from reaching it, so it had to respond on its own. There was no time to escape.

Sceptile released a stream of bubbles, adjusted its angle, and crossed its arms. It used X-Scissor against the oncoming shell. Two slashes cut through the water and struck it, but had no effect.

Before Sceptile could try another move, the shell hit it full on.

Tyson waited anxiously for Sceptile to surface. With his Pokemon underwater, he could follow the fight only on the big screen, like the crowd. Seeing Blastoise use Withdraw and drive its shell into Sceptile again made his heart sink.

He looked across the field. Ethan's expression had not changed. He showed no pleasure at gaining the advantage. That composure made him especially difficult to face. Tyson would get no opening from overconfidence or carelessness.

At least Sceptile was still standing. It used the impact to push itself up and out of the water, landing back on a rock. But it was covered in injuries, and much of its strength was gone.

It had not touched Blastoise once. If Tyson changed nothing, defeat was almost certain. He was not too stubborn to act, nor too concerned about looking weak before the crowd. He raised his Poke Ball and recalled Sceptile.

Cheers broke out in the stands. Misty's voice was the loudest. Though she was far away, Ethan thought he could hear her.

Strength earned respect. There had been no knockout, but Tyson's decision to switch first showed that he had lost the opening exchange. Some spectators wished the round had lasted longer; most were pleased with what they had seen.

In the main grandstand, Goodshow sighed as he watched Tyson retreat. It could be a tactical adjustment, nothing more. Still, he sensed a pattern he did not like. Was this heading toward the very outcome he feared?

Amid the spectators' different reactions, Tyson sent out his second Pokemon. Blue light flashed, and Metagross appeared on one of the rocks.

Ethan raised his brows. They shared a Pokemon species. He had not intended to use his own Metagross today; otherwise, he could have watched two of them face each other.

He recalled Blastoise and took out his second Poke Ball. Dragonite appeared in the sky.

In the stands, Ash nodded again. Dragonite. Ethan's got this round too.

That Dragonite really is strong, Brock agreed.

Morrison glanced at them. Why did they declare Ethan the winner no matter which Pokemon he sent out? Did they think so little of Tyson? Or did they honestly believe every Pokemon Ethan owned was that powerful?

Raising a whole team to ace level took more than training. It required countless real battles. Most Trainers poured all their effort into one or two aces, a few solid mainstays, and a handful of weaker Pokemon. That was the best ninety-nine percent of them could hope to achieve.

Blastoise had looked like an ace. If Dragonite was just as strong, Ethan had shown his best cards in the first two exchanges. What would he do later? Tyson's strongest Pokemon was his Meowth. If Tyson kept that ace fresh until Ethan's best were exhausted, how would Ethan answer?

Ash and the others seemed entirely unconcerned. Morrison grew more confused. Had they missed the problem, or was he overthinking it? Did Ethan have more Pokemon he had not seen?

As he wondered, the second exchange began and drew his attention back to the field.
