Chapter 860: The Samurai Boy Is Still the Same

While Matori returned to Team Rocket's secret base to seek Giovanni's instructions, leaving the entire organization on alert, Ethan and May knew nothing of it. They checked in at the Pokemon Center and washed away the dust and fatigue of the road. Before dark, Ethan took May to the Pewter Museum of Science. It was a chance to walk off dinner and broaden her horizons, though she did not seem very interested.

They stayed one night. After breakfast, they left Pewter City for the forest nearby.

Mountains surrounded Pewter to the northwest, north, and northeast. They were bare and rocky rather than green, their dark gray slopes matching the city's name. Forest covered their lower reaches, lending the foothills a more lively appearance.

Ethan and May walked a long way north among the trees. The sun had climbed high, but the Battle Pyramid was nowhere in sight.

Ethan, are you sure it's here? May asked. We've walked for ages. It hasn't flown off again, has it?

Ethan checked the map Scott had sent him and shook his head. It's still where it was before. If the map is right, we'll reach it in another ten or twenty minutes.

As he spoke, something rustled in the bushes ahead. May thought a wild Pokemon was about to appear. Her eyes brightened, and she gestured to Eevee. It understood and leaped into the undergrowth.

May did not mean to catch anything. Combusken, Munchlax, Squirtle, and Eevee were still growing, but the four of them were enough to take her through the Grand Festival. She saw a playful encounter with a wild Pokemon as a chance for Eevee to practice.

Eevee vanished into the bushes. The leaves shook, and then a frightened boy shouted from inside.

Oh. That wasn't a Pokemon!

May hurried forward, alarmed that she had scared someone. Before she reached him, an older boy in samurai armor stumbled out of the bushes and fell to the ground. Eevee sprang out behind him.

I really did frighten him... she murmured. She was about to apologize when Ethan spoke.

You?

The armored boy looked up and recognized him. You're...that Trainer from before?

It was the Samurai boy Ethan had met during his first journey through Kanto. He looked just as he had then, wearing the same armor and carrying the same bug-catching net on his back.

May was relieved that they knew each other. Perhaps the mistake would be less awkward to settle.

I'm sorry, she said with an apologetic smile. I didn't mean to scare you. Are you all right?

Of course. A little thing like that doesn't bother me. Samurai got to his feet and brushed the dirt off his armor. Next time you send a Pokemon into the bushes, look first. You might frighten someone.

He sounded so composed that Ethan might have believed himif he had not heard the shout a moment ago.

The last time Ethan saw him, he and Chloe had been on their way to the Indigo League. They had met Samurai and the ninja Aya in Viridian Forest. After so long, the boy was apparently still wandering Kanto's woods. He might as well have grown roots in the forest and merely moved from one patch to another.

What was his name again? Ethan could not remember asking. Since the boy had never offered it, he saw no reason to ask now.

Samurai turned to him. I've been training ever since you beat me. This time I'm going to settle the score!

How long had it been since someone challenged Ethan so boldly? His thoughts drifted back to the start of his journey. Time had passed quickly.

May leaned toward him and whispered, Why does he sound so angry with you? What did you do?

It had been so long that Ethan could not recall every detail of their meeting. They had certainly battled. Probably nothing much. Maybe I beat him a little too thoroughly.

A memory stirred, and he smiled. Do you remember what I told you back then? he asked Samurai.

Of course! You Trainers from Pallet Town are a difficult bunch. I met several more after you, and every one of them beat me. Samurai drew himself up. But that's in the past! I've trained since then. I'm not the boy you defeated before!

Ethan remembered now. After beating Samurai, he had played a trick on the Trainers from Pallet Town by encouraging the boy to challenge them. Samurai had done exactly that and, just as in the animated story, had suffered one defeat after another. Even against Ash, he had managed little more than a draw.

May looked puzzled. She leaned close again. Since when are you from Pallet Town?

It was a little joke I made back then, Ethan whispered. I didn't expect him to take it seriously.

The exchange was brief, but Samurai felt slighted. Hey! What are you two talking about? He pulled a Poke Ball from his clothes and released his Pokemon. Send yours out! Today you'll find out what a crushing defeat feels like!

A flash of blue light revealed a Butterfree.

It looked different now, but Ethan was sure this was the Caterpie Samurai had owned before, the one that had become a Metapod and traded Harden after Harden with Ash's Metapod in the forest. Its evolution showed at least some progress.

Then again, the boy was badly out of touch. If he watched Pokemon tournaments, he would know what Ethan had accomplished. Someone who knew Ethan had won the Indigo League would hardly challenge him with such confidence. Perhaps ignorance made him fearlessor perhaps he had that much faith in his own growth.

Ethan looked into his waist pouch, sighed, and raised his head. I beat you once already. If you want another battle with me, you'll have to beat my assistant first.

Assistant? Samurai followed Ethan's gaze to May. This girl? He did not especially want to battle her, but he could see no way around it. Fine. I'll beat her and then I'll beat you!

May had no idea when she had become Ethan's assistant, but she understood what he wanted. She stepped in front of him beside Eevee.

That's right. You can't challenge Ethan just because you feel like it. Beat me first!

Eevee gave an excited cry and jumped toward Butterfree. Since hatching, it had played and tussled daily with Ethan's Eevee. May's partner was just as mischievous, energetic, and curious, and its battle style was growing similar too.

Ethan offered one warning. Watch out for Butterfree's powder moves.

May nodded and focused on the fight. They were in the wild with no referee. As soon as both Pokemon were ready, the battle began.

At their Trainers' commands, Eevee and Butterfree moved almost together. Butterfree flapped its wings and sent a strong Gust toward Eevee. Eevee slipped behind a large tree and avoided it easily.

The wind shook the tree and set its leaves rustling. Bushes shuddered as well. With whole stretches of forest in motion, Butterfree could no longer see clearly where Eevee had gone.

Samurai caught a glimpse of movement off to one side. He turned in time to see Eevee spring onto a tree trunk, launch itself upward, and slam into Butterfree.

Butterfree! Look out!

His warning came too late. Eevee struck, and Butterfree reeled in midair.

Samurai had expected Butterfree's height to give it the advantage over a Pokemon on the ground. Instead, the complicated forest terrain had helped Eevee hide from its sight. Without Butterfree flapping, the Gust died away.

That first exchange told him the pretty girl was no easy opponent. She was more capable than the Trainers he usually met wandering through the forest.

Samurai had spent years here and seen many Trainers and tactics, though. He did not panic. Once Butterfree steadied itself, he attacked again.

Butterfree shot a white thread of silk toward Eevee. It moved so quickly that it caught Eevee's ankle just as it landed from its leap.

May had an answer ready. Calmly, she led Eevee to a nearby tree. Eevee circled the trunk several times, winding the silk around it, then pulled hard. Anchored by the tree, it dragged Butterfree down toward the ground. Samurai had to order Butterfree to break the thread.

Ethan found a rock and sat down to watch. Samurai's first two attacks had achieved nothing. There was a gap between his battle sense and May's.

It was not a matter of how many Trainers he had faced. A good Trainer read a complicated battlefield and adapted to danger instead of trading attacks mechanically. Samurai still lacked that flexibility. His years in the forest exposed him to new Pokemon and opponents, but his habits limited what he learned. Without entering tournaments or watching high-level battles, genuine growth would be difficult.

Unless something changed, May would beat him soon. The battle unfolded as Ethan expected.

Once May understood Butterfree's strength and habits, she settled into a counterattacking rhythm. Trees and bushes gave Eevee cover for both defense and quick movement, letting it evade Butterfree's attacks while creeping closer.

Butterfree finally released Stun Spore. Eevee used Sand Attack to scatter much of the powder. It darted through the gap it had opened and struck Butterfree with Quick Attack, knocking it out.

Butterfree fell with a thud directly in front of Samurai.

Damn it... He clenched his fists at the sight of his unconscious partner. He had not expected to lose even to this girl. How could he get revenge on Ethan now?

Well? Ethan smiled. My assistant is pretty good, isn't she?

Samurai reddened, unwilling to accept it. I was careless. I want another battle!

Another one? Ethan raised an eyebrow and looked at May. She nodded. He turned back with a smile. All right. That's fine with us.

Delighted, Samurai took out the Poke Ball holding his strongest partner. He had saved this Pokemon to challenge Ethan, but after losing one match, he had to win one back first.
