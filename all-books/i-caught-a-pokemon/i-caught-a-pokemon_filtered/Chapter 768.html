Chapter 768: You Have No Idea How Strong He Is

While Ethan was giving his interviews, Tyson was on the outdoor training ground at the other end of the competitors' dormitories. He was putting his hat-wearing Meowth and the rest of his team through their final practice before facing Ethan.

No matter how much he trained, one problem refused to go away: his Pokemon's condition.

They prepared thoroughly in private and practiced every day. Yet the moment they entered an arena, it was as if an invisible shackle fell over them. They could not use their full strength. Their jumps, runs, and moves became unsteady. More troubling was their state of mind. Tyson felt a little nervous himself, and that nervousness was enough to make his Pokemon's movements slightly awkward.

It was hard to avoid under the eyes of tens of thousands of spectators, with everyone's attention fixed on the battle. Perhaps they would need a few more matches to grow accustomed to it.

His thoughts returned to tomorrow's opponent. Tyson had watched only one of Ethan's matches, but Ethan's Pokemon had seemed untouched by that pressure. Ethan himself had looked composed, as though such crowds were nothing new to him. That puzzled Tyson and made him wary.

Even so, Ethan's commands had seemed indecisive. His Pokemon had performed well, but he had let chances to win slip by and dragged out a match he could have ended much earlier. Tomorrow might be difficult, Tyson thought, but he would win.

He turned his attention back to training. Before he could speak to his Meowth, it bristled, bared its claws, and stared toward the entrance.

Tyson looked around in alarm. Another Meowth stood beneath a tree, watching them. He recognized it at once: the Meowth that traveled with Team Rocket's trio.

Since meeting Ash, Tyson had encountered the trio several times. He had no reason to be friendly with people who so often did wrong. Their shared interest in Meowth had given them something to talk aboutbut really, it was the two Meowth that had anything in common.

Why was this one here? Before Tyson could ask, his own Meowth went over and said something to it.

Team Rocket's Meowth glanced at Tyson and the Meowth in the hat, then turned away, leaving them to look at its back.

I came to warn you, meow. Don't underestimate that man!

What do you mean? Tyson asked. Is he that strong?

Meowth did not turn around. It lifted its eyes to the night sky and spoke gravely. You can't even imagine how strong he is. Team Rocket is the strongest organization in the world, and even we couldn't beat him. And those Pokemon he hasn't shown yet? One look at any of them would make you shake. You can't stand against them!

Tyson was bewildered. Was Ethan really so powerful? Trembling at the mere sight of a Pokemon sounded absurd. Ethan was a man, not some spirit or god.

He understood that Meowth meant well, but dismissed the warning as an exaggeration. He was about to point out that he was strong too when, in the blink of an eye, Meowth vanished.

Tyson's words stuck in his throat. That strange departure made him take the warning a little more seriously. Could Ethan truly be as formidable as Meowth claimed? Tyson could see no sign of it. Still, caution cost nothing. He decided to raise Ethan one level in his estimate.

...

On the roof of an office building at the Ever Grande Conference, Pokemon League president Charles Goodshow stood with his hands behind his back and watched the night sky.

Footsteps approached. Without turning, he seemed to know who had come.

Beautiful night, isn't it?

It is. John, the live broadcast director, came to stand beside him with a half-smoked cigarette in his mouth. But I like the view ahead of us even more.

Goodshow lowered his gaze. The main stadium glowed in the distance, and beyond it the food stalls bustled beneath the lights.

After a long silence, he asked, You disagree with my decision too?

Only the League's senior officials knew what he meant. In the final tournament draw, they had used unusual means to target one competitor: Ethan.

He had won championships in Kanto and Johto and crossed the sea to pursue another in Hoenn. Goodshow had intervened to make that third title harder to win. The League had arranged for the strongest remaining entrants to face Ethan instead of eliminating one another in other matches. If one of them beat him early, they would avoid the embarrassment of one Trainer collecting every Conference title.

John blew out a ring of smoke and shook his head. I don't know. The investors and the board care about ratings. Give them those, and they won't mind who wins. The audience likes a star they can follow, too. And if Ethan really is strong enough to win again, putting more strong opponents in his path won't stop him. He'll beat them, reach the final, and take the title.

Goodshow was silent again. At last he sighed. Do you think I don't know that? But how can we leave it untested? One man taking every championship can't be healthy for the League. I can't watch it happen while I'm president.

Stubborn old man, John thought. He decided to try once more.

I see it differently. He dropped his cigarette and crushed it under his shoe. One Trainer winning every Conference wouldn't harm the League. It could give everyone a standard to aim for. A Grand Slam champion who stands apart from the field would inspire other Trainers to work harder and find ways to surpass him. The League wouldn't sink into decline because of him.

Goodshow was getting old and could be set in his ways, but he was not shortsighted. He had held the presidency for years. He had simply never faced a challenger as strong as Ethan, and now he could not tell which course would serve the League best.

John's argument affected him. Goodshow was not entirely convinced, but he had to admit it made sense. If events unfolded that way, the future might look very different.

After a third long silence, he shook his head. Perhaps. But we've already interfered with this draw. Let's see what happens. If Ethan defeats everyone we put in front of him and wins anyway, he'll have proved he's someone worth holding up as an example. Then we'll consider what you've said. For now, let's deal with the tournament before us.

...

Nine the next morning. The Ever Grande Conference's main stadium.

The final tournament had moved into the Conference's largest venue. Although this was only the opening round, all eighty thousand seats were filled. The smaller arenas' atmosphere could not compare. A single burst of applause from a crowd this size seemed to shake the earth. For the competitors, it was another challenge to their composure.

Ethan walked calmly from the competitors' passage into the cheers and faced Tyson across the field. One match had already been held here, warming up the crowd. Theirs was the second of the day.

They stood on a raised platform. A cover concealed the battlefield between them until it was time to begin.

As announced earlier, the final tournament used full six-on-six battles. Trainers could switch Pokemon during a match. A wheel would select one of four fieldsgrass, rock, water, or iceat random. After either side lost three Pokemon, there would be a brief break and a second field would be chosen for the rest of the battle.

The format was much like the Indigo and Silver Conferences Ethan had already competed in.

The announcer briefly introduced both Trainers, then called out, They're ready! Let's see which field they'll fight on in the first half!

A large wheel appeared on the stadium screen, marked with symbols for the four fields. It spun rapidly, slowed after a dozen seconds, and stopped with its pointer on a water drop.

The Water Field! Ethan and Tyson will battle on the Water Field for the first half!

Water. Ethan gave the choice some thought. Fewer of his Pokemon could fight comfortably there than on the other three fields. It limited his options somewhat. He was not worried. He trusted his team to win.

The cover in front of them opened. Gears turned beneath the platform, and a pool rose into view. A dozen or so rocks broke the surface of the water.

Under the eyes of the crowd, Ethan and Tyson each took out a first Poke Ball.

Before either released a Pokemon, Ash and the others began guessing in the stands.

Who do you think will win? Morrison asked.

Ethan, of course, Misty answered at once. I don't care how strong Tyson is. He won't beat Ethan.

Morrison dismissed her answer. He did not know she was a Trainer from a Gym family. To him, she was just a pretty girl traveling with Ethan, and her opinion carried little weight.

He turned to Ash, only to hear the same prediction.

I say Ethan too, Ash said. He's the strongest Trainer I've ever met. No one has ever beaten him in a six-on-six battle!

My pick is Ethan as well, Brock said with a smile. In fact, I don't think anyone here can beat him.

Come on. Morrison shook his head. Why does everyone choose him? Tyson has looked stronger so far. I think he'll win, though it won't be easy. It might come down to their last few Pokemon.

As they argued, the two Trainers made their opening choices. Tyson sent out Sceptile.

Ethan was mildly surprised. Tyson had given up the field advantage to start with a Grass-type starter. Then again, perhaps he expected Ethan to use a Water type and had chosen Sceptile to counter it. It was a clever predictionand a correct one.

Ethan opened his Poke Ball. Blue light flashed, and Blastoise appeared in the water.

Ash, Misty, and the others nodded knowingly. Blastoise? Then Ethan's got this.

Morrison was even more confused. Hold on. Yes, it's the Water Field, and Blastoise is a Water type, but Sceptile has the type advantage. Why are you so sure Blastoise will win?

Ash grinned. Because you have no idea how strong Ethan's Blastoise really is. Watch it fight, and you'll understand why we said that.

Huh?

Morrison could not imagine what kind of strength would make all of them disregard a type advantage and believe so firmly that Ethan would win.
