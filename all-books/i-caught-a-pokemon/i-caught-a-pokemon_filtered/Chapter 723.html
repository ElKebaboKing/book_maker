Chapter 723: Revived at Last

Late that night, when most of the city was asleep, Ethan looked at Ash and the others sleeping soundly in their beds and quietly left the room.

The dormitory was still. He could just make out someone snoring somewhere.

He passed a yawning Trainer who had arrived to check in at this hour, pulled his clothes a little closer around himself, crossed the empty dining room, and slipped out the Pokemon Center's back door.

Gengar had been waiting outside for some time, peering about furtively.

After Ethan sent it to follow Yuko, he had been busy in the forest directing Deoxys against Rayquaza and the other Trainers. He had missed his chance to meet Gengar then. Once he returned to the Pokemon Center, dinner and a long chat with Ash's group kept him occupied. Only now, in the dead of night, could he get away to hear its report.

Gengar looked as though it had come to a secret rendezvous, and Ethan couldn't help laughing.

You're a Pokemon. Why do you look more like a spy now?

A spy? That sounds awful. Gengar gave a wicked chuckle. I'm a Pokemon agent with a one hundred percent success rate at tailing people!

Enough bragging. What did you find?

Gengar shrugged. I've had plenty of time to locate the laboratory and learn its layout. I found the Deoxys core you mentioned, too. But there are cameras everywhere. Anything visible will be spotted. I can get around them, but for anyone with a physical body, slipping in unnoticed would be nearly impossible.

What condition is the core in?

Gengar thought back over what it had seen. It's lying inside a sealed case, surrounded by equipment. I tried getting closer, but there's an alarm by it. I couldn't approach without setting it off, and I didn't want to alert them, so I came back.

Still only a core?

Ethan rubbed his chin. He remembered it regenerating into Deoxys in the latter half of the movie. Why was it still a core now?

Was it too early? Or was some other trigger missing?

Never mind. If he got the core to Deoxys, surely it would know how to restore its companion.

What about security? he asked.

I only saw a few people on patrol, and they didn't look particularly tough, Gengar answered at once. But like I said, cameras are everywhere. The moment you go in, they'll see you. Even if you take the core secretly, you'll trip the alarm.

Ethan trusted its assessment. If Gengar said the guards weren't much of a threat, they probably wouldn't endanger his plan. The cameras were another matter.

Professor Lund's laboratory was different from a Team Rocket facility or some other criminal organization's hideout. It was a lawful research center under official protection. Even the core was something Lund had found himself, making it his lawful property. Ethan couldn't simply invade the place in the open. He couldn't even let his own Pokemon be identified. Otherwise, the police would follow the clues straight to him.

That made this a new kind of challenge. He would have to rely on Pokemon no one had seen him useor Pokemon that didn't belong to him at all.

His mind made up, he told Gengar, Come on. Let's meet our new friend first.

...

Across the river from LaRousse City, in a forest on the far bank, Ethan climbed down from Dragonite into a small clearing.

Two Deoxys floated there, watching him in silence. The cool moonlight made them look even colder and more uncanny.

Luckily, this was the world of Pokemon, and one of them was his own partner. If he had found a sight like this in the wilderness back in his old world, he would have jumped out of his skin and thought he had wandered into a supernatural film.

He approached the wild Deoxys. I found your companion. Getting it out will be difficult, though, so I'll need your help.

Deoxys made a sound he couldn't understand. He hardly needed a translation to guess that it agreed.

His own Deoxys nodded when he looked its way. It seemed the two had become friends and could now get along peacefully. That was good news.

He had left his Deoxys here partly to give it time with one of its own kind, and partly to keep an eye on the wild one so it wouldn't leave or cause more trouble.

There was no time to waste. Ethan released Gardevoir and left her and Dragonite to protect his body. Then he turned to Gengar. All right. Let's do it. Pull my soul out.

He found a level patch of ground, lay down, and closed his eyes. Gengar came to his side, set a hand on his head, and pulled upward. For the second time since their experience at sea, Ethan's soul left his body.

He had been through it before, but seeing his own body lying there still felt strange. For an instant, it seemed unfamiliar, as though it belonged to someone else.

He shook off the feeling and urged Gengar on. Time's short. Let's go.

Only Ghost-type Pokemon could see him in this state, so Gengar would have to relay all his orders and directions. Gengar could also turn invisible. Neither it nor Ethan's soul had to fear the cameras.

As for the two Deoxys, Ethan had no intention of hiding them.

They only needed to avoid being seen before they reached the laboratory. Once inside, they could take the core. It didn't matter if anyone spotted them then. The whole city had watched them fight Rayquaza above the forest that day. People would assume they were acting on their own. Who would imagine a Trainer was directing them?

Under the moon, the two Deoxys streaked across the city toward the northern part of the island. In the darkness, no one noticed them arrive.

Professor Lund's laboratory stood beside lush flowerbeds. The building looked modern, but it had no sign and attracted little attention. Someone passing by without knowing what it was would never guess it housed a research center.

Careful, aren't you?

They landed by a small side door. Gengar and Ethan, still in his disembodied state, passed straight through the wall. The two Deoxys smashed the reinforced door and followed them inside.

The alarm sounded almost at once, its shrill call echoing through the quiet building.

This was a scientific facility in the middle of a city. Its designers seemed to have planned for petty thieves, not Pokemon breaking down doors. Even after the alarm had rung for some time, the guards were slow to arrive.

Two men carrying batons finally confronted Deoxys with a Pokemon each. They could do nothing to stop them. The Deoxys merely floated past, and both men and both Pokemon collapsed unconscious without even forcing the intruders to make a move.

They met no further resistance. Ethan knew the police must have been alerted, though. Perhaps they were already on their way.

With Gengar leading, they went straight through the building. Within minutes they reached its central laboratory. The door looked thick and formidable, but Deoxys's power made short work of it.

Inside, beyond the core in its sealed case, two people were still sitting there: Professor Lund and his assistant, Yuko.

They hadn't left, even at this hour? Ethan hadn't expected that, but it made little difference. They were ordinary people. Whether they were present or absent would not change what happened next.

His part of the job was finished. Now it was up to Deoxys.

Professor Lund rose and faced the doorway.

You finally came.

He sounded calm, as though greeting an old friendor as though he had expected this all along.

Ethan kept his face still. He knew Lund wasn't speaking to him. The professor was addressing Deoxys. Humans and Pokemon other than Ghost types could not see Ethan's soul. He could dance right in front of Lund without being noticed.

Even so, Lund's perception astonished him. Two Deoxys had come in, yet the professor had picked out the one he had encountered four years ago. That was absurdly sharp.

The wild Deoxys paid him no attention. It flew straight to the middle of the laboratory and looked at the core.

Lund might have spoken as though they knew each other well, but as far as Deoxys was concerned, it had scarcely seen the man. At most, it had caught a glimpse of him four years ago. Why would it remember an unimportant human?

Lund seemed to expect no reply. He looked at the core he had spent four years trying to revive and said, When I saw you today, I knew you'd come here. I thought this day might arrive eventually. I just didn't expect it so soon.

Deoxys ignored him again. It swept out a hand, and the sealed case around the core shattered.

Lund's hands clenched so tightly that his knuckles whitened. Yuko gave him an anxious glance, looking as though she wanted to speak but could find no words.

He drew a deep breath. I've actually made a breakthrough in reviving it. I've mastered the technique. If I can replace a few more parts and have a little more time, I can

Before he could finish, Deoxys extended a hand. Energy flowed from its core to the other one. The core visibly began to grow, rapidly building a body around itself. Within a few seconds, a new Deoxys had taken shape.

Lund's unfinished sentence died on his lips. At last, he let out a helpless sigh.

Four years of work, surpassed in seconds. What had all that time, money, and effort been for? What was the point of working late in the laboratory night after night?

Ethan felt sorry for him. But effort did not always bring a reward. Lund had no malicious intent; he had simply wanted to restore Deoxys. Although its revival had happened differently from the movie, the result was the same. Years of painstaking work had come to nothing, a harsh outcome for the professor.

There was no other solution Ethan could see. The movie had ended much the same way, and he had no wish to change it.

As he reflected on that, the invisible Gengar drew close to his ear. It's getting late. We need to leave. If your soul stays away much longer, your body will be in trouble.

It was right. He had been away from his body both too far and too long. The work was done, and there was no reason to stay.

Ethan nodded. He took one last look at Lund's dejected face, sighed, and led the three Deoxys back the way they had come.

Only when they left the building did he see that the return trip might be less simple. The alarm had brought LaRousse's police to the laboratory. Worse, the city's defense system had activated. Countless smart blocks assembled into a huge robot that stood menacingly in their path.

It was only a slight complication.

Against three Deoxys, the officers and their Pokemon were routed at once. The robot, the ultimate defensive weapon of this city of technology, lasted barely a dozen seconds under their attacks before it broke into fragments scattered across the ground.

The defenders could only watch, helpless and frustrated, as the three Deoxys flew out of the city and disappeared above the forest.
