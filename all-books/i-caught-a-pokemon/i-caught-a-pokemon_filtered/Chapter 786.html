Chapter 786: The Final

Ethan and the others left the stands and waited by the competitors' tunnel. At last Ash came out, his shoulders low and his steps heavy.

Seeing his face, Max and May swallowed the words of encouragement they had prepared. He looked too downhearted for any of it.

Ethan was about to speak when Misty strode past him. Though she was only ten, she walked with the force of someone much older. Everyone turned toward her. With a stern face, she raised her right hand and swung it at Ash's cheek.

What? The others stared. Ash froze in shock, too startled even to dodge.

Misty's palm stopped a few centimeters from his face. She had not struck him after all.

M-Misty, what are you doing? Ash asked. With her temper, he had thought she might really slap him.

There, she said, narrowing her eyes. You can make another expression. I thought you'd left your spirit on the field.

Ash flushed. I... I was just...

Just what? You lost. Why wear that miserable face? So you were eliminated. Your skills weren't enough this time. If that's the problem, work harder and try again!

Perhaps a sharp remedy was what he needed. Under Misty's blunt words, Ash's face went from embarrassed red to angry purple and then back to normal. His gloom faded with it. Max and the others breathed easier. May even gave Misty a quiet thumbs-up. Only Misty would have dared to do that.

She's right, Ash. Cheer up, Morrison added. Remember how you talked to me yesterday? Why are you the one falling apart now?

Ash stared for a moment. He was behaving exactly as Morrison had after his own loss. If Morrison could recover, why couldn't he? Ash let his disappointment and frustration go with a sigh. Light returned to his eyes, and he nodded to them.

You're right. One defeat doesn't decide everything. My journey is only beginning! Next time, I won't stop at the final eight.

That's the Ash we know, Max said with a grin.

...

With Ash eliminated, Ethan was the only one of their group still in the tournament. Calling him a lone seedling hardly fit. He was more like a great tree, sturdy and towering, sweeping aside opponents as he dominated the Ever Grande Conference.

Next came the semifinal. By coincidence, Ethan drew the blue-haired young woman who had eliminated Ash. He avenged his friend with another easy six-to-zero win. He seemed unstoppable.

Ash's feelings were complicated. He had given everything and failed to beat her. Against Ethan, she could not knock out even one Pokemon. The gap between him and Ethan was dispiritingly wide. They had not fought each other since the Silver Conference, but he could see how much more he still needed to improve.

Having won the semifinal, Ethan was first through to the final. He waited to see who would emerge from the other match.

The two Trainers there fought under a peculiar strain. Both wanted to win, advance, and claim the greater honor. Both dreaded what advancing meant: facing Ethan. Would they reach the final only to be easily beaten in front of everyone? Yet neither could bear to fall one step short. They fought with that conflict hanging over them.

In the end, a Trainer named Hua proved a little stronger and reached the final. He had earned a place opposite Ethan, though his feelings about it were mixed.

When spectators compared their performances, most shook their heads. The gap looked too large. Nearly everyone expected Ethan to win the title easily. Only the most reckless gamblers would stake everything on Hua.

The semifinals were followed by a rest day. Under the Conference's arrangements, Ethan and Hua each filmed promotional material for the final. Publicity spread across Hoenn. The Pokemon League wanted even remote towns, isolated islands, and sparsely inhabited deserts to hear the news and have a reliable signal to watch.

Online support matched the opinion in the stadium: ninety-seven percent for Ethan and three for Hua. Ethan led by 152.78 million votes. In image, presence, and strength, the two scarcely seemed comparable. It might have been the most lopsided show of support in Ever Grande Conference history.

The public seemed to agree on one thing: Ethan winning would be the natural result.

After a busy half day, Ethan began receiving calls. Sabrina, Erika, Maya, Professor Cerise's family, and people calling from Kalos all contacted him. So did Anabel, Looker, Lance, Clair, Lisia, and many others close to him, whether by phone or message.

Some could not cross the sea to Hoenn because of work or distance. Others had their own reasons for missing the final in person. They all wished him well and would follow the match on television.

Their blessings and reminders warmed Ethan. This was what it felt like to have people care about him. Without noticing how it happened, he had made so many friends in this worldand girlfriends too.

He had formed ties here that mattered deeply. If he were ever offered a return to his old world, he would hate to leave. He thought he would refuse. Too much of his life was here now for him to let go.

...

After a night's rest, Ethan's strength and focus had returned. The final battle arrived.

Salutes thundered. Balloons and streamers rose from the stands. Before the match, Pokemon League President Goodshow spoke beside the ceremonial flame. He thanked the staff, then the Trainers for their efforts. The announcer stirred up the crowd, and at last the finalists entered through opposite tunnels under thousands of eyes.

Ethan waved to the cheering spectators as he walked calmly to his Trainer's area. Hua looked far more nervous. It was his first final, and the cheers for Ethan were overwhelming. He had prepared himself as well as he could, but everything beyond the field seemed against him.

He knew the crowd thought he could not beat Ethan. He ought to use their doubt as fuel, to prove them wrong... but even he suspected he was no match for this man. He could only give it everything he had.

The announcer gave both finalists a grand introduction. The field wheel began to turn. Hua watched tensely; Ethan barely reacted. The first-half field was the Grass Field.

It was a fairly even setting. Fish and jellyfish Pokemon could not fight there, but most others had room to show their strength. For Hua, that was welcome news.

At the referee's signal, both Trainers took out Poke Balls. The cheering climbed to its peak. Hua made no move to throw his, so Ethan sent his out first.

Blue light flashed. Blaziken appeared, and the crowd shouted itself hoarse for the Pokemon that had swept three opponents in the previous round.

Hua's face fell. He remembered what Blaziken had done to Shizuko. He had not been on the receiving end, but putting himself in her place was enough to make him feel trapped. While studying the recording, he had tried several tactical simulations with both Shizuko's team and his own. None had changed the outcome.

Was he about to experience the same thing? What a way to start the final.

At least he had expected Blaziken might lead. The Poke Ball in his hand held a Pokemon it could not counter easily. He threw it, and his partner appeared.

It's Slowbro! the announcer cried. Hua has opened with a Slowbro he hasn't shown in any previous match! He's kept a surprise for the final!

So that was his plan. Ethan smiled. Slowbro was a Water and Psychic type. Both Blaziken's Fire- and Fighting-type attacks would deal only half damage. As a type matchup, it was a sound choice. Hua had brought a Water type onto a Grass Field because he had correctly guessed Ethan would open with Blaziken.

Whatever Hua's chances, that preparation was worthy of a finalist. Ethan was measuring him by his own standards. By ordinary standards, Hua was very strong. If Ash had faced him, Ethan would have given Ash only about a forty percent chance to win. Yet Hua still stood opposite Ethan feeling that victory was distant.

Slowbro's appearance made the match more interesting. As Slowpoke's other evolution alongside Slowking, it might look vacant and slow, but it was no easy opponent. Its Water and Psychic types gave it many ways to attack.

Slowbro was pink and walked on two legs. Its head resembled Slowpoke's, and a cream-colored stripe crossed its belly. A spiral-shelled Shellder clung to its tail. Its appearance suggested several real animals: an otter, a hippopotamus, even a platypus.

Standing upright, it had longer limbs than Slowpoke. Slowpoke evolved into Slowbro when a Shellder bit its tail while it was hunting in the sea. The sweet taste seeping from the tail entranced Shellder. Greedy for more of that flavor, it refused to let go and even changed its shell into a spiral shape as it kept feeding.

According to observations, if the Shellder fell off during a fierce battle, or if the tail was severed and the Shellder came away, Slowbro would revert to Slowpoke.

Slowbro's reactions were sluggish, but that could also help it. Toxins from the Shellder on its tail dulled its sense of pain. It could be struck and only slowly realize what had happened. That delay might let it keep fighting a little longer.

It sounded like a minor trait. In the right moment, those extra seconds could turn a battle around.
