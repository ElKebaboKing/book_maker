Chapter 803: You Put on Your Show

Huh? Ethan stared at Misty. All of Kanto knows? What do you mean?

Misty held up her phone and told him about the reports she had seen throughout the day.

I don't know what happened, but the television news and radio have been full of stories about your battle with Tucker tomorrow. My phone keeps sending me alerts too. It's only been half a day, and I've already seen hundreds of posts!

Misty did not understand it, but Ethan immediately had an idea.

Hundreds of posts? Had someone paid to push the story?

He could guess who. Tucker had seen Ethan register for a challenge and recognized the value of his titles. Now he wanted to use them to raise his own profile.

That was quite an ambition.

He had turned an ordinary challenge into a public spectacle, as though letting the opportunity pass would cost him millions. He had not even asked Ethan whether he agreed.

People like that existed in every world.

Of course, Ethan might be wrong. Perhaps Tucker was not behind it. But the possibility seemed remote. Tucker stood to gain the most from the match, and Ethan could think of no one else with a reason to arrange this.

It was a gamble, though. Was Tucker sure he could handle all this attention?

Ethan could roughly see his plan. Win or lose tomorrow, Tucker would become better known.

Winning, of course, was out of the question.

Now that Tucker had put the match before all of Kanto, Ethan could not afford to lose. He would fight with everything he had. As long as he performed steadily, a Frontier Brain had no chance of taking a point from him. Even one of the Elite Four would struggle.

Tucker probably understood that much. He would try to win, but he must have prepared another outcome.

Lose gracefully, then collect the popularity the broadcast brought him.

If Tucker had discussed it with Ethan beforehand, Ethan might well have helped him. He could train his Pokemon, win the Symbol, and let Tucker benefit from the occasion too. Even if they never became friends, there was no need for bad blood.

But now...

Ethan gave a quiet, humorless chuckle. He had made up his mind. He would use this match to show people that they could not exploit him at will.

...

After a night's rest, Ethan, Misty, and May finished breakfast and left the Pokemon Center for the Battle Dome.

When they had returned to their rooms the previous night, all three had seen the flood of stories about the match. Even Ethan had found them while checking his phone before bed.

An unseen hand seemed to be guiding the coverage, portraying Tucker as someone who could stand on equal footing with Ethan. Many people who knew nothing of either Trainer's actual strength took the promotion at face value. They expected a magnificent Pokemon battle and followed the news eagerly.

Some who lived close enough had even bought tickets overnight to watch in person. More than a dozen television stations were bringing equipment to broadcast the match across Kanto.

Naturally, Ethan's friends heard about it too. He received call after call that evening. Even Erika learned from the news that he had returned to Kanto. She was delighted, but she also complained that he had given her no warning.

What could he say? He had planned to appear in Celadon City unexpectedly and surprise her. Tucker's publicity had spoiled that plan and left Ethan in an awkward position.

It only deepened his irritation. Tucker's unilateral decision had thrown his own plans into disarray.

They could see the crowd long before they reached the Battle Dome. The line stretched for nearly a kilometer. People were clearly eager for the match.

Among them stood fans carrying banners for Tucker. Most were young women. They chatted and laughed as they waited, waving their fan merchandise and looking forward to his appearance.

Tucker certainly knew how to cultivate a following.

Ethan pulled his hat lower and started past the line. His phone rang. The caller was a member of the Battle Dome's staff.

No one wanted today's match to go ahead more badly than Tucker's people. They had agreed on nine o'clock, but before eight, someone was already calling to ask where Ethan was and urge him to attend.

When he hung up, Ethan sighed. Each new intrusion made Tucker and his staff harder to bear. They seemed to have no sense of boundaries.

At least there were plenty of Ethan's supporters in the line as well. He had won the Indigo Conference through battle after battle before a vast audience. Time had passed, but people still remembered his performances. Some admired his skill; others admired his looks. His fans were numerous, and as more arrived, they threatened to outnumber Tucker's.

Casual viewers could follow the match on television or online. The people who had traveled hundreds of miles to be here were mostly devoted fans of one Trainer or the other.

Ethan had never tried to cultivate fans, or even paid much attention to them. That did not mean they were not there.

Following the staff member's earlier directions, he led Misty and May through a side entrance, avoiding the crowd. They went straight to the challenger's lounge to wait for the match. It was their last chance to make any adjustments.

Misty looked around with interest. The Battle Dome was well equipped, and the lounge alone showed how much money had gone into it. There was even a Poke Ball transfer machine in one corner, like the ones at Pokemon Centers.

After exploring the room, she dropped onto the sofa and stretched. This place isn't bad. Brother Ethan, what Pokemon are you going to use against that man?

Ethan's reaction had soured both girls on Tucker. Misty would not even use his name.

Ethan laughed softly. Tucker has planned everything so carefully and arranged such a grand show. I wouldn't want to disappoint him. I'll help see his performance through to the end.

Misty and May heard the mockery in his voice. Both leaned forward eagerly.

So...?

Tucker thinks he gains something whether he wins or loses. But he's overlooked one thing. There's a world of difference between losing with dignity and fleeing in humiliation.

As the girls watched, a Poke Ball floated out of Ethan's belt pouch and settled in his hand. Gengar rose from his shadow and gave them a mischievous grin.

If Tucker is so sure we're on the same level, I'll show him what despair really feels like. He'll pay for what he's done.

...

Ladies and gentlemen! Thank you for waiting!

At nine o'clock, the hovering commentary platform appeared above the field, and the host's excited voice rang through the Dome.

As you all know, today's Battle Dome challenge is one everyone has been waiting for! Defending the Dome is none other than our own superstar, Tucker! And his challenger needs no introduction either: Ethan Vale, champion of the Indigo Conference, the Silver Conference, and this year's Ever Grande Conference!

The crowd roared. Cameras swung toward the field.

After letting the reaction build, the host continued, Both Trainers stand among Kanto's finest in strength and popularity! Now that they've met, which one will prevail? Before we find out, let's enjoy some highlights from their greatest battles!

The arena lights dimmed. One large screen showed Ethan's best moments from the Conferences. When his montage ended, the opposite screen played highlights of Tucker's performances in the Battle Dome.

Set to driving music, the editing was impressive. It could stir anyone's excitement. Tucker's footage even looked more spectacular than Ethan's.

The Dome's staff had clearly worked hard to present Tucker as Ethan's equal. Though he was a Frontier Brain, the publicity placed him on the same level as a champion of three Conferences.

The audience did not know any better. They expected a close, thrilling contest and responded enthusiastically.

After the montage, spotlights converged on a rising platform. Ethan slowly came into view. He faced the cheers with a calm, easy expression and offered the crowd a small wave. Their response was deafening.

Tucker's entrance was far more elaborate. Colored lights flashed across the field. Petals rained from above. Dressed in his flamboyant costume, Tucker sailed into the arena on a swing.

He swung back and forth a dozen times, then leaped down and landed neatly in the center of the field. As the spotlights found him, he bent at the waist and gave the crowd a dazzling smile and a deep bow, like a star taking the stage.

The entrance had been carefully designed, and it drew thunderous applause.

But when Tucker straightened and looked at Ethan for the first time, he found the man's expression unchanged. His entrance had made no impression at all.

Tucker frowned, displeased. He considered Ethan a rival of comparable stature. Why was Ethan so cold toward him?

He had planned to speak with Ethan in front of the crowd and make them look like worthy opponents who admired each other. That plan was falling apart. Something in Ethan's gaze even struck him as hostile.

Tucker felt more uncomfortable, but he dismissed the concern. To him, Ethan was a useful prop. If he would not cooperate, Tucker could still make use of him in the battle. He would at least say what the occasion required.

Ethan, he called warmly, welcome to the Battle Dome! I look forward to seeing a performance worthy of the Tactics Symbol today!

The Tactics Symbol was the prize for defeating him. Ethan merely gave a slight nod. He did not answer.

Tucker had been rebuffed again.

Ethan knew his silence might seem rude, even arrogant. At this point, he did not care.

You want to build a stage and put on a show? Then perform alone. You put on your show; I'll fight my battle. I came for the Symbol. If you expect me to play along, keep dreaming.

Tucker's staff had not expected such a cold response either. They had never met Ethan. Even if he did not share Tucker's love of showmanship, they had assumed a champion would at least exchange a few polite words.

Instead, Tucker was left talking by himself. How could that look like a clash between two great rivals? He looked more like a clown performing for a man who barely acknowledged him. It was almost as if Ethan did not take any of them seriously.
