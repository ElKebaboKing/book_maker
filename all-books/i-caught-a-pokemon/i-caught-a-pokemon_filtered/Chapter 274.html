Chapter 274: Dragonite vs. Charizard

Two dragon-shaped Pokemon, one yellow and one red, faced each other in the air above Pummelo Stadium, wings beating steadily.

The yellow one was Drake's Dragonite. Its round belly and broad body gave it an almost gentle look. Dragonite were rare, and the people of Pummelo Island revered one as their guardian. Spectators of every age loved the species.

The red Pokemon, though it looked more like the dragons of legend, was not a Dragon-type at all. Ethan's Charizard was leaner than Dragonite, with a fierce face, sharp claws and teeth, and a blazing tail. Each watched the other, eager to fight.

Beneath them, Ethan and Drake stood in their command areas with equally grave expressions. Neither could afford a lapse in attention. The stands had quieted too. Drake's supporters and the smaller number cheering for Ethan all knew how much this round meant.

If Ethan won, he would claim the Winner's Cup without losing a Pokemon and bring down the Orange League's undefeated champion. If Drake won, he would still have to defeat five of Ethan's Pokemon in good condition. The road ahead would be daunting, but a comeback would earn him extraordinary honor and give Orange Islanders something to boast about before Indigo League fans.

Some spectators who knew type matchups noticed that Charizard appeared to be at a disadvantage.

"Dragonite is a Dragon- and Flying-type," the announcer said, puzzled. "Charizard is Fire- and Flying-type. Dragon doesn't have an advantage over Fire, but Dragonite resists Fire-type attacks. This is the first time Ethan Vale has deliberately chosen an unfavorable matchup. Does he have a special plan?"

Casual spectators who rarely watched battles understood at last why the choice seemed odd. Given Ethan's earlier decisions and his calm expression, they assumed he must be thinking several steps ahead.

In fact, Ethan had no elaborate plan. He had a small team and did not want to send out a Pokemon he had already used. Dragonite could fly, so Charizard's ability to meet it in the air mattered more to him than the type disadvantage. Everyone was reading far too much into a simple choice.

Drake himself kept trying to guess Ethan's intention. When the referee declared the battle open and he still had no answer, Drake set the question aside and attacked first.

"Dragonite, use Water Gun!"

Dragonite could learn attacks of many types: Dragon, Flying, Normal, Water, Fire, Ice, Electric, even Psychic. That range had caught plenty of unfamiliar opponents off guard. Ethan knew enough to be ready.

"Charizard, dodge!"

Charizard rolled sideways in the air, avoiding the stream of water with ease. Dragonite used the moment to close in. Electricity crackled around one of its claws as it swung at Charizard.

Thunder Punch. So he wants a close fight?

"Charizard, answer with Dragon Claw!"

Blue light shone around Charizard's claws. The two Pokemon collided in the air, trading punch for claw. They struck dozens of times in only a few seconds, their limbs blurring and the wind whistling around them. The crowd roared its approval. At last Dragonite was giving them the kind of contest they had hoped for.

Both Pokemon took blows. Neither gained a clear advantage. Thunder Punch was doubly effective against Charizard, but Dragon Claw struck Dragonite's Dragon type hard in return.

Bang!

Their heavy tails collided, and the two separated, breathing hard.

Drake intended to keep up the pressure. Before Ethan could speak, he called, "Dragonite, use Thunderbolt!"

Bright electricity erupted from Dragonite's long antennae and spread outward, threatening to engulf Charizard. It was a powerful, wide-ranging attack. Charizard had no useful way to meet it head-on.

"Charizard, get down to the ground!"

Charizard plunged toward the grassy field and slipped beneath the spreading current. Yellow arcs hissed through the air and faded without touching it.

The thick grass, rooted deep in the ground, would not readily carry electricity. Ethan judged that standing there would reduce the reach and force of Dragonite's Electric-type attacks. Charizard would not be entirely safe, but that weapon was less threatening near the ground.

What a difficult opponent. Ethan and Drake reached much the same conclusion. They had traded attacks without either of them gaining the upper hand.

Looking at Dragonite, Ethan remembered what it had done in the anime. It could use more than a dozen moves across different types. It had beaten three of Ash's Pokemon in succession, only losing to Pikachu when its stamina finally ran out.

Drake's other Pokemon might be strong enough to reach the Indigo League semifinals, Ethan thought. This Dragonite could be strong enough to win the whole tournament.
