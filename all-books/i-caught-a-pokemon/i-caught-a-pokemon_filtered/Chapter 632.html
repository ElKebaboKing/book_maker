Chapter 632: Preparing for the Contest

At the front desk, Nurse Joy looked over the group before Ethan could explain what they needed. You arrived just in time, she said with a smile. We have exactly one men's dorm room and one women's room left. A little later, and you would have had to share with other Trainers.

That was welcome news. None of them wanted to stay with strangers. Sharing would make everything less convenient and less comfortable.

That's great, Ethan said. Could you assign us those rooms, please?

Certainly. Nurse Joy worked at her computer. Without looking up, she added, Judging by your group, you're here for the Pokemon Contest too, aren't you?

Ethan felt the atmosphere shift. Trainers who had been grooming or feeding their Pokemon in the lobby turned their eyes toward the group, trying not to seem obvious.

Misty and May, the only two girls among the travelers, noticed as well and instinctively moved closer together.

May glanced around. Why is everyone looking at us? she whispered.

Isn't it clear? Ethan replied without turning. There are already plenty of entrants, and the number of places is limited. They see our group and think the competition just got tougher.

May swallowed. Being watched by so many people made her more nervous than ever.

Fortunately, Brock had no sense of the mood. He leaned across the counter toward Nurse Joy, his face full of longing. You've misunderstood! Only May is entering the Contest. But if you wished it, beautiful Nurse Joy, I'd sign up just for you!

Ugh. Misty couldn't stand such a syrupy declaration. She grabbed one of Brock's ears to pull him awaythen realized Max had taken hold of the other.

Oh? Misty remembered May introducing her rather serious little brother at the cafe. Why was Max tugging Brock's ear too? The answer came to her at once: he was as tired of Brock's embarrassing declarations as she was. At least someone could take over this job when she wasn't around.

Misty and Max exchanged a grin. Together, they hauled Brock back into the group. His cry filled the lobby, distracting the other Trainers and allowing May to breathe easier.

...

After putting their luggage in the dorm rooms, Ash was eager to drag Ethan outside for a battle.

But seeing all those other entrants had weighed on May. She shook her head. You go ahead. I want to find somewhere to practice a little more.

She had prepared during their travels, yet with the Contest almost upon her, she felt less certain than ever.

I'll come with you, Misty said with a smile. I can already guess how their battle will end. Ash isn't going to beat Ethan. Besides, I'd like to see how someone trains for a Pokemon Contest.

May understood that Misty had offered to keep her company, and she was touched. Thank you.

So the group split up. Ethan, Ash, and Brock went to the battlefield behind the Pokemon Center for a battle between the boys. Misty and Max accompanied May to the beach to practice.

We'll see you later! Misty waved to Ethan. Come find us at the beach when you're done.

I will.

Ethan watched them go, then followed Ash and Brock through the Center's back door to the open-air battle grounds.

Except in very remote places, an outdoor battlefield was standard at a Pokemon Center. Even in an unfamiliar city, a Trainer could expect to find somewhere to battle there.

Because Slateport was a coastal city and many local Trainers had Water-type Pokemon, this Center also had a simple pool for water battles.

Only two children were using the grounds. They were battling on the ordinary field farthest away and seemed to be local kids playing rather than traveling Trainers.

That was one difference between Pokemon Contest entrants and League competitors. If this had been a League tournament, every battlefield would already have been occupied, with more battles breaking out in any open space nearby. Dozens of Pokemon would be taken to the treatment room each day before anyone called the place lively.

Contest entrants, by contrast, quietly attended to their own preparations. Those who wanted to practice, like May, often sought an unseen corner, unwilling to reveal what they had planned.

The children were too caught up in their battle to notice the newcomers. Ethan watched with interest. One boy commanded a Poochyena, the other a Zigzagoon. Perhaps they had little experience, or perhaps their Pokemon were too young, but their entire exchange consisted of Tackle, Growl, and Tail Whip. They shouted their commands with enormous enthusiasm all the same.

What a wonderful childhood.

Ethan shook his head and went to one side of the water field, facing Ash across it.

Huh? Ash looked surprised. We're having a water battle this time?

I caught a Water-type Pokemon recently, Ethan said. It hasn't had many chances to fight. This is a good opportunity to train it.

Sure, let's do a water battle. Ash took his place opposite Ethan, rubbed his nose, and grinned. Come on, Ethan! I'm ready!

Under Ash's watchful eyes, Ethan took a Poke Ball from his waist pouch and tossed it lightly. A yellowish-brown, remarkably ugly fish Pokemon appeared in the pool.
