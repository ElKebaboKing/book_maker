Chapter 849: I Admit I Took a Gamble

Anabel was a difficult opponent, both now and in her later life with the International Police. In the anime, Ash had lost to her once, trained specifically for a rematch, and only beaten her on his second visit. That alone said a great deal about her strength.

The young Anabel had another remarkable ability. Since childhood she had sensed what Pokemon were thinking. She could communicate with wild Pokemon, almost converse with them, and sometimes reach a state of complete harmony with them. In theory it was closer to unusually keen empathy than psychic power, but people still considered her a psychic.

Scott had said that when Anabel became excited during a battle, she could even convey her thoughts directly to her Pokemon. She could guide them without speaking, as if by telepathy. In those moments, Scott considered her the strongest Frontier Brain in some respects. That was his judgment; everyone had their own view of who deserved the top place.

Whatever the ranking, Anabel belonged among the strongest Frontier Brains. Ethan had never sensed that ability in the International Police Anabel. Perhaps passing through the Ultra Wormhole and losing her memories had cost her this gift as well.

He studied the Alakazam across the arena and took out the Poke Ball he had prepared. Blue light flashed, and Dragonite appeared.

Ethan had decided on Dragonite before arriving. It was a normal part of rotating his team; he would have chosen it even if Anabel had sent out someone else. At his level, type matchups were no longer the only concern. His Pokemon could fight through unfavorable situations. Giving each of them time to battle and keeping the team developing evenly mattered more to him.

Dragonite and Alakazam had no direct type advantage over each other. That made the match seem comparatively even.

The green-haired referee saw both Pokemon ready and signaled the start.

Dragonite beat its wings and rose into the air, looking for a chance to strike from above. Anabel directed Alakazam to defend and counter, meeting movement with stillness. The two styles made for a compelling battle.

Dragonite tested it with Wing Attack. Alakazam fired Psybeam, hoping both to hurt Dragonite and disturb its condition, but Dragonite was too fast for the beam to land. Alakazam then used Teleport to avoid the incoming wing.

One exchange was enough for both Trainers to recognize a serious opponent. Ethan had expected it and knew roughly how skilled Anabel was. She, meanwhile, was startled by Dragonite's reactions. This challenge would be hard.

Dragonite attacked again, much more fiercely this time. Alakazam's psychic power struck repeatedly. The force was invisible, making it harder to evade than most moves, but Dragonite's experience in the air let it slip away again and again.

Alakazam was experienced too. It was one of Anabel's main Pokemon, and its close bond with her sharpened their coordination. As it learned Dragonite's flight patterns, maneuvering around its attacks grew steadily harder.

Ethan changed course. Ranged attacks were no longer working, so he told Dragonite to close in and keep Alakazam occupied at close quarters. He did not expect Alakazam to be capable there too.

Most Psychic-type Pokemon would try to open the distance once an opponent got close, avoiding direct contact and wearing the foe down. Alakazam, with its slight frame, looked especially vulnerable to that kind of pressure. Yet as Dragonite came in for the attack, Alakazam answered with Focus Punch and drove it back.

Ethan's eyebrows rose. May looked equally startled from the stands. It was the opposite of the Alakazam they had expected. A less capable Trainer could have charged straight into that trap and lost the battle.

For Ethan and Dragonite, it was a surprise, not a defeat. Once Dragonite recovered, its own close-range ability began to win out. That difference lay in the Pokemon's bodies as much as their experience. Learning a physical move did not give Alakazam the strength to match a Dragonite built for fighting up close.

Focus Punch was Fighting-type, too, and Dragonite's Flying typing reduced the damage it took. Alakazam prolonged the fight with Recover, restoring much of its strength, but it ultimately fell to Dragonite's Thunder Punch.

Alakazam is unable to battle! The first round goes to challenger Ethan Vale and Dragonite!

Anabel shook her head ruefully. She had made no obvious mistake, Alakazam had performed well, and she had even brought their thoughts into harmony. Yet she had lost in a way that seemed almost ordinary.

The difference in raw strength was plain to her. She had been a Frontier Brain long enough to recognize it. Still, she had not expected one of her main Pokemon to be beaten like that. Since becoming a Trainer, she had never met anyone who left her feeling so helpless.

Who is he?

She looked Ethan over. The longer she studied him, the more familiar his face seemed. She lived far from cities and rarely met new people, though, and could not recall where she might have seen him. If she had encountered a Trainer this strong before, surely she would remember.

There was no time to solve that mystery. She recalled Alakazam and chose her second Pokemon: Metagross.

Ethan thought for a moment. Dragonite was still in good condition, but he returned it and sent out his own Metagross. Of the Pokemon he had brought, it was relatively less powerful. This was a chance to measure it against a first-rate Trainer's Metagross.

Anabel had not expected a mirror match. You have a Metagross too. She examined Ethan's Pokemon and nodded appreciatively. It's strong, and the metal on its body has a fine sheen. You've raised it well.

Ethan returned the compliment. As they spoke, both Metagross took their positions. The referee signaled the second round.

Same species, same evolutionary stage, and much the same collection of moves: individual ability and the Trainers' decisions would settle this one.

Ethan could tell Anabel's Metagross was powerful, another of her main Pokemon like Alakazam. His own did not yet match Dragonite. Anabel also had her silent way of communicating orders. Despite the apparent symmetry, he was the one at a disadvantage.

That made him focus harder. He had chosen this battle for his Metagross precisely because the opponent would push it.

Both Pokemon moved forward at the start. When they reached a suitable distance, they attacked almost together. Each opened with Psychic. Two invisible forces collided in midair and burst apart. The explosion raised no smoke, but sparks crackled like electricity.

Under that spray of sparks, both moved again. Their Trainers' different styles now showed in their choices.

Anabel's Metagross came in fast. Like her Alakazam, it was clearly suited to close-range fighting. Ethan's Metagross had not trained for that specifically, but its body was built for it. There was no reason to retreat from a direct clash. What mattered was how each Trainer handled it.

Ethan kept one precaution in mind. Rather than send his Metagross forward at once, he ordered Iron Defense.

As he spoke, Anabel's Metagross accelerated and slammed toward his with Take Down.

Her bond with her Pokemon was useful. She could send instructions straight to its mind without speaking, saving precious time and allowing instant changes in a fast battle. In that sense she could act almost as another Pokemon on the field.

But the speed had a drawback. If an opponent anticipated the order after it had been given and the Pokemon had acted, changing it could be too late. Anabel had only just conveyed Take Down when she saw Iron Defense and realized the danger. Her Metagross was already committed to the charge.

How did he know? she wondered. Who uses Iron Defense before the opponent has even attacked?

Her Metagross hit Ethan's with a clang that echoed through the hall. Take Down was powerful, but it also injured the user through recoil. Iron Defense had sharply raised Ethan's Metagross's resistance. Anabel's blow did less damage to its target than the collision dealt back to her own Pokemon.

Yes! May shouted from the stands. Ethan's prediction had been brilliant, as though he had seen straight through Anabel's plan.

He had no time to celebrate. While her Metagross felt the recoil, Ethan ordered Tackle. His Metagross struck and drove it away. Anabel's Pokemon had taken two hits in quick successionan unfortunate opening. At least the second blow carried it out of reach before Ethan could follow up further.

Once it was safe, Anabel breathed again. Dragonite's victory over Alakazam had shown her a gap in Pokemon strength. This exchange showed her a gap between Trainers.

Ethan eased his own tension when she withdrew. Iron Defense had involved some prediction, certainly. But even if he had guessed wrong, Anabel's Metagross would have attacked sooner or later. Using the move early would not have been wasted.

As it turned out, he had guessed correctly. Anabel was very strong. Compared with the Anabel he knew from her International Police years, however, she was still young and inexperienced.
