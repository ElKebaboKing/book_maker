Chapter 204: The Festival Ritual

Tens of thousands of visitors crowded Shamouti Island's long main street. Most had come from other islands to see the festival. In local costumes they had rented or bought, they were almost indistinguishable from the islanders.

Vendors had sprung up along both sides of the street and called out their wares. By the time Ethan's group arrived, the other people in bird costumes had dispersed. Only Carol stayed with them. Melody had vanished too.

The crush of people slowed them to a crawl. It took five minutes to cover barely a hundred meters.

"Come and look! These pearls are a Shamouti specialty. Every one is full and round!"

"Sakura shells, white conches, yellow ring shells! Jewelry made from every kind of shell!"

"Orange cakes! Try our local treat!"

The voices ran together into a noisy din. Night fell while they walked, and lights across the island made the celebration seem even brighter.

Ethan looked around in amazement. "What a crowd. There are nearly as many people here as at the Indigo League Conference."

Carol chuckled. "It's Shamouti Island's biggest festival. Nothing else all year matters quite as much. You won't find a celebration this grand anywhere in the Orange Islands, not even in Trovitopolis on Mandarin Island South!"

She was proud that her home could host such an event. Ethan could hardly blame her. Everyone wanted to see their hometown thrive.

At one stall, he bought several bird masks and put them on himself, Sabrina, and Misty. They had stumbled into a festival, so they might as well join in. Ash and Tracey could buy their own masks if they wanted them.

A long lantern procession opened a path through the crowd ahead of them. People carried lanterns of many shapes and danced toward the end of the street. The most striking was a huge Gyarados lantern. Several performers worked it up and down until it seemed that a fierce, living Gyarados was swimming past the spectators.

The group took in the sights as they made their slow way forward. When they finally left the main street, the crowd thinned at once. Carol led them farther on to a banquet hall built of straw and stone, with something of a Maya style to it.

Torches filled the hall with warm light. A lavish dinner had been laid out across more than a dozen long tables.

"Come and rest," Carol said. "Please help yourselves to the food. I'll arrange rooms for you after the banquet."

She gave Ethan's group a table of their own, then sat with Maren at another. After five years apart, the two friends surely had plenty to discuss.

Once no one else was close enough to hear, Tracey lowered his voice. "I wonder why they're treating us like this."

"Who cares?" Misty said. "The food and rooms are free. Let's enjoy them."

Ash was more interested in something else. He looked around the hall, eyes wide. "Where did that girl go?"

"Hold on." Misty stared at him. His interest was obvious. "You don't have a crush on her, do you?"

Ash's face turned as red as Misty's had earlier. "No! I'm just curious..."

That was what he said, but he was a ten-year-old boy who had just been kissed by a pretty girl. Of course it had stirred something in him.

Ethan left their teasing alone. He sat quietly, trying to recall the story of Shamouti Island and the three legendary birds. A crazed collector had attacked and captured the birds to obtain Lugia, the God of the Sea. Ash had stopped him in the end. Ethan could no longer remember the details, though, or how the collector was defeated.

No point forcing it. I'll deal with things as they happen.

Sabrina noticed his distant expression and glanced at him. "What are you thinking about? Don't tell me you're thinking about that girl too."

Ethan spread his hands. "Come on. I'm not a lovestruck kid like Ash. Why would I be thinking about a young girl?"

He was privately surprised. Sabrina rarely showed such concern. Was she jealous?

She turned away again. Ethan picked up a pear-shaped fruit from the table and took a bite. It was tender, juicy, and remarkably sweet. A region named for oranges knew how to grow fruit; it tasted better than what he usually found elsewhere.

"Try this. It's delicious!"

At his recommendation, Sabrina and the others each tried one. He was right.

A lovely flute melody drifted through the hall. Everyone turned toward the stage. Melody had changed into a long white dress, with a wreath of flowers on her head and a white veil over it. Moving in a gentle dance, she stepped onto the stage like a fairy.

She held an unusual instrument, and the sweet notes came from her playing it.

"Melody?"

The entire hall fell silent, Carol and Maren included, as everyone watched her dance and listened. Ethan's group had not expected a change of clothes to transform her manner so completely. The willful girl now seemed demure.

The tune was beautiful, soft and lingering, yet it left Ethan with a sense of loss he could not explain. He could appreciate it without liking it.

When the piece ended, Melody stepped down from the stage and approached their long table. With the grand gestures of an actor, she proclaimed:

"When the heavens and earth rage and the world stands at the edge of destruction, the God of the Sea will appear. Together with an exceptional Trainer, it will calm the gods' wrath!"

She knelt between Ethan and Ash and gazed earnestly at them. "Lord Ethan, Lord Ash, if either of you is that exceptional Trainer, please prove it to me!"

Ash looked completely bewildered. Melody found his expression funny. She leaned in and whispered, "Don't get the wrong idea. It's a ritual we do every year. Just play along!"
