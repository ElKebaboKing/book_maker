Chapter 674: Just as I Thought!

Vigoroth simply could not keep still.

Ethan noticed that every movementattacking, defending, or runningcame with something extra. Vigoroth paced, bounced on its toes, pawed at the air, scratched its cheeks, or thumped its chest. It was acting on instinct without realizing it.

He ordered Combusken to attack again and watched Vigoroth closely. Sure enough, those little motions followed a pattern. They were unconscious habits.

A plan formed at once. There was no time to weigh whether it would work. Combusken was already slowing down, so Ethan had to take a chance and test his idea.

Combusken, use Scratch!

He had deliberately chosen an attack Vigoroth could dodge easily. As Combusken raked its claws toward it, Vigoroth slipped aside and, just as Ethan expected, bounced twice on its toes.

Ethan seized the opening. As Vigoroth landed, he shouted, Behind you, at an angle! Double Kick!

Combusken was puzzled but obeyed without looking back. It kicked behind itself twice.

Thud! Thud!

Both kicks struck Vigoroth squarely in the chest. It staggered back, clutching the spot and grimacing in pain.

Norman raised an eyebrow. At first he thought Ethan had guessed right by luck. What happened next made him reconsider: Ethan repeated the same trick, and it worked again.

Another pair of kicks hit the same spot. This time Vigoroth broke out in a sweat from the pain.

Norman frowned. Ethan had clearly seen through Vigoroth's movements and anticipated where it would go. But how?

Across the field, Ethan smiled faintly at Norman's baffled expression. Sometimes the person closest to a thing was the last to see it.

Pokemon had been named for their habits and traits since people first came to know them. Vigoroth had earned its name because it was always in motion.

Norman knew his own Pokemon so well that he had grown used to its restlessness. Familiarity had made him overlook the important detail.

Ethan was surely not the first to notice. Other Trainers might have dismissed the habit, or seen it but lacked the ability to seize such a brief opening. Over time, even Norman had stopped treating it as a weakness.

His experience and keen eye soon served him. Norman followed Ethan's gaze, then studied Vigoroth and understood.

So that's it!

Yet understanding did not mean he could fix it. Vigoroth had been restless ever since it evolved. A few shouted commands would not change a lifelong habit.

Under Combusken's continued assault, Vigoroth could no longer hold out. One more Fighting-type strike landed, and its eyes rolled back. It toppled onto its back.

Vigoroth is unable to battle! Combusken wins!

The match had been evenly contested at first, then turned quickly once Ethan found the weakness. Without that discovery, Combusken and Vigoroth might have knocked each other out, as Slakoth and Metang had.

Even the Pokemon Norman used against ordinary challengers were formidable. His strength as a Trainer was plain to see.

Combusken was panting hard too. Ethan recalled it and waited for Norman's third Pokemon.

Norman did not reach for another ball immediately. He applauded. You anticipated Vigoroth's movements. Excellent observation!

It's hardly something to celebrate. Ethan shook his head regretfully. I still have a long way to go. If my observation were really that good, I'd have noticed it at the start of the battle. Things would have been much easier.

He wants to be better still? Norman studied him with mixed feelings.

Few people could win a battle and immediately reflect on their own shortcomings. Ethan had already won two regional League tournaments. If he kept growing like this, what kind of Trainer would he become?

Norman wanted to see how far he could go.

He put the thought aside and took out his third Poke Ball.

This is my last Pokemon. It's one of my stronger partners. Many challengers have lost their Gym battle to it.

Ethan fixed his eyes on the ball. Norman's first two Pokemon had increased the difficulty step by step. The last would surely be the strongest of the three. If Norman himself considered it powerful, Ethan expected a serious challenge.

Norman threw the ball. Blue light flashed, and a gorilla-like Pokemon appeared on the battlefield, lying on its side.

Ethan stared at it, speechless. He had guessed right after all: Norman's last Pokemon was Slakoth's final evolution, Slaking.

Norman was meticulous to a fault. He had Pokemon from all three stages of the same evolutionary line and had put real effort into raising each one. Just how much did he like this species?

Slaking had a pink, pig-like nose and beige skin. Brown rings surrounded its half-lidded eyes, giving it a lazy, sleepy look. Two white tufts of hair sprouted from its head, while a collar of white fur circled its neck and body. Brown fur covered its limbs. It reminded Ethan of a baboon as much as a gorilla from Earth.

He shook his head and rested a hand on the Poke Balls at his waist, considering what to send out.

Misty aimed her Pokedex at Slaking. She had the same question Ethan did about Norman's choice of Pokemon. She wanted to ask Caroline why he had raised all three forms, but it felt too personal, so she kept quiet.

Beep. Slaking, the Lazy Pokemon. Normal type. Slaking is the world's laziest Pokemon and spends most of its life lying down. It eats whatever food is within reach, including grass. Only when it has eaten all the grass around it will it reluctantly roll over and move elsewhere. This leaves regular circles of bare ground wherever it rests. Although it appears to be doing nothing, Slaking stores energy for a counterattack. Releasing that energy at once gives it astonishing power.

Misty was close enough that Ethan heard the entry too, though he already knew most of it. That knowledge was exactly why he understood how strong Slaking could be.

It really was powerful. It hit hard, defended well, and could endure tremendous punishment. For all its lazy appearance, it could move with surprising speed, keeping up with many quick opponents. A careless Trainer who underestimated it could be caught completely off guard.

Choosing a Pokemon to face it was the crucial question.

Ethan reviewed his team and ruled out the partners that were still too weak. That left only Dragonite, Tyranitar, and Scizor.

His third battler would have to be one of them...
