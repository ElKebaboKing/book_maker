Chapter 787: A Champion's Chant Across the Stadium

Blaziken and Slowbro began their battle. Hua went all out from the start, hoping to take control immediately. If he could suppress Blaziken before it found its rhythm, perhaps he could avoid the fate of Ethan's earlier opponents.

Hua had set himself a modest goal. If he could survive the first round without losing, he would count the final as a victory in his own mind. Ethan would no longer be unbeaten, and Hua could feel that he had earned his place here, even if he eventually lost.

He had a plan. Ethan had no intention of giving him that opening. Ethan had not set out to win the Conference without losing a Pokemon, but after coming this far against opponents who had not seriously threatened him, he saw no reason to let the chance go. Why not finish as well as he could? No one in the last several Ever Grande Conferences had won the title without losing a round. Perhaps he could be the first in years.

With their different aims, the Trainers ordered their Pokemon forward. Hua did not hold anything back for later. Slowbro fired Confusion and Water Gun in an unbroken barrage, determined to bring Blaziken down.

The type advantage made even Blaziken withdraw from those attacks for the moment. It was a direct, effective plan, and Ethan had no immediate way through. Invisible psychic force and long streams of water swept across the Grass Field. Blaziken ran swiftly among the thick grass, dodging them.

Seeing it forced to move, Hua grew more excited. Few Trainers had managed to put Ethan under pressure in this Conference. Even a temporary advantage gave Hua hope of winning the first round.

Then he began to notice the problem. Blaziken was too fast and could keep running for too long. Its legs moved so quickly it seemed to fly across the grass. Slowbro had not hit it once, while each failed attack drained Slowbro's stamina and energy.

The tall weeds hid Blaziken's orange-red form as it flashed past. Slowbro's small eyes lost track of it more than once. Without Hua's warnings, Blaziken might already have come up behind it. Hua realized the round would not be as easy as he had hoped.

Ethan saw that moment of hesitation.

He was bold but attentive, patient enough to read an entire battle and quick to catch a small opening. Once Hua gave him one, Ethan acted at once. Blaziken ducked under another Water Gun without slowing, then sprang high out of the grass toward Slowbro.

No! Hua saw the sudden change. Slowbro's ranged attacks and type advantage had held Blaziken back, but its close-range fighting was weak. Letting Blaziken reach it could be disastrous.

Don't let it close in! Use Psychic!

It was a sensible order. Psychic could hurt Blaziken and push it away to restore the distance. But Blaziken's leap was a feint. Halfway across, it dropped suddenly back into the grass. Psychic passed through the space it had occupied and vanished harmlessly.

As soon as it landed, Blaziken used Quick Attack. It raced low over the ground toward Slowbro, faster than before. The leap had drawn out Hua's defense and made him aim at an attack from the air. This was Ethan's real change of pace.

Hua's mistake had consequences. Only a second or two had passed, but that was enough in a battle. He could not give Slowbro a new order in time.

Blaziken's legs blurred. It was not usually known as one of the fastest Pokemon, but natural talent and Ethan's training had given it remarkable speed. It might not match the very fastest, yet ordinary Pokemon could hardly keep up with its footwork. It crossed more than ten meters in a blink.

Before Slowbro could react, Blaziken reached its round pink face. It lowered its head, raised a shoulder, and drove into that face with the force of Quick Attack.

Bang! Quick Attack was not a Fighting-type move, and Blaziken's speed made the impact severe. Slowbro rolled backward for more than ten meters before stopping just short of the boundary.

Slowbro! Hua cried. The blow had happened too quickly for him to respond. At least his Pokemon had not gone out of bounds.

Slowbro still lay there, staring blankly, while Blaziken turned and charged again. Get up! Hua shouted.

Slowbro's sluggishness was showing its other side. It lay motionless, a huge question mark almost visible over its head. It seemed not to know how it had ended up on the ground.

Don't just lie there! Blaziken is right behind you! Hua called, growing frantic.

That delayed response could be a double-edged sword. At times, it let Slowbro keep fighting after a hit and create a comeback. Now it held Slowbro back. Most Pokemon would already be on their feet. Hua could shout all he liked; Slowbro was still trying to understand what had happened.

Blaziken did not give it time to recover. Fire and Fighting moves were resisted, but they were not its only weapons. Its sharp claws were part of its body and useful weapons in their own right.

Blaziken reached Slowbro and raked its back with one claw. Then it used Slash, striking a vulnerable spot precisely.

Slowbro's face suddenly twisted with pain. The pain did not come from the fresh claw marks. The earlier shoulder blow had finally registered in its brain. It rolled on the ground, too distressed to get up and fight back.

Ethan would not miss the chance. First he had Blaziken position itself between Slowbro and Hua, preventing a recall beam from reaching it. Then another Slash struck Slowbro's head and finished the round.

Slowbro is unable to battle! Blaziken wins!

The referee raised the flag. Hua's hope of taking even one round from Ethan had failed. The crowd cheered.

Ash applauded too. Ethan is incredible at finding an opening. Hua made one small mistake, and Ethan beat him for it.

And Blaziken did it without relying on Fire or Fighting moves, Max added, adjusting his glasses. It's so fast, too.

Brock smiled. More than raw power, look at its speed compared with other Blaziken. It was quick to begin with, and Ethan's training has developed that strength. It's becoming a fast attacker. That's why its speed has stood out so much in these recent matches.

Fast attacker. May quietly remembered the phrase, though it had little to do with being a Coordinator. It made her think of her own Combusken. Lately she had felt it nearing a breakthrough. Soon it might evolve into Blaziken, and she could not help looking forward to what kind of Pokemon it would become. Might it have a talent for speed like Ethan's?

Since becoming a Coordinator, May had recognized many gaps in her skills. Appeals were a matter of ideas and cooperation with her Pokemon. The bigger challenge was battling. She traveled with Ash, but she could neither imitate his style nor learn much from it.

Combusken was her first Pokemon. With Ethan's Blaziken as an example, May had hopes for it. She had seen enough of Ethan's battles to know how strong he was. Traveling with him might improve her own battling as well. She could help Misty keep an eye on Ethan and gain something for herself too.

On the field, Hua recalled Slowbro with reluctance. It was the surprise he had prepared for Blaziken and had abilities it never got to use. The loss drained his spirits. Looking up, he saw that Ethan was not switching Pokemon either. He sighed. Why was the thing he feared most happening again?

Whether he had lost his nerve or decided that struggling would change nothing, Hua fought the second round without the drive he had shown in the first. He still ordered attacks, but Ethan could feel the difference.

Hua sent out Tauros. Without Slowbro's type advantage to protect it, Tauros met Blaziken's fire. Flames burst from Blaziken's mouth and, soon after the opening exchange, stray blasts ignited the surrounding grass. Fire spread quickly across the field.

Tauros was caught with nowhere to flee. When the flames died, it lay on the ground, blackened. Spectators nearby could even smell scorched beef.

Its chest still rose and fell faintly. At least its life was not in danger. Hua caught himself thinking that and stopped. Why at least again? He had worried about the same thing in the previous round.

Tauros had fallen easily, but Hua did get one piece of good news: Ethan recalled Blaziken afterward. There would be no second three-Pokemon sweep from it.

Hua felt relieved, then ashamed of that relief. He had come to fight for the championship. Why did a simple switch by his opponent feel like an achievement? He felt pathetic.

Nobody else saw him as a fool. Most spectators had expected this result before the final began. What they were seeing only confirmed it.

Champion!

Champion!

Champion!

The second round had barely ended, and the third Pokemon had yet to appear, but voices rolled through the stadium like a rising wave. At first the chants were uneven. Within seconds, they found one rhythm and echoed above the field.
