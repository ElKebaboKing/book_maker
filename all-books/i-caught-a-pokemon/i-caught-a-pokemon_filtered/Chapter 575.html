Chapter 575: Into the Final

Flames covered Blaziken from head to toe, making it look like a huge fireballa meteor ready to race across the ground.

Perhaps the heat of its own flames was too much even for it. Blaziken coughed out a wave of hot air. Then it fixed its gaze on Gardevoir and ran.

The last patches of ice and snow, and even the damp earth nearby, dried in an instant.

Blaziken had poured everything into this move. The strain on its body would be immense, but if it hit and happened to burn Gardevoir, an impossible victory might become possible.

Harrison placed all his hope in the attack. As Blaziken drew closer, his worry grew so sharp that he shut his eyes without meaning to.

Please, let it hit!

Across the field, Gardevoir could use Teleport again. It could leave the path of Flare Blitz whenever it wished. Out of respect for Harrison, Ethan chose not to do that.

The result seemed settled. At least he could let the final exchange be a worthy one.

At Ethan's thought, Gardevoir responded.

It lifted both arms and used Psychic to drive Blaziken back. Blaziken gritted its teeth and forced its way forward against the invisible power.

It looked ready to collapse, its awareness beginning to blur, but fierce resolve kept it moving.

"Persistent," Ethan murmured, shaking his head. He sounded impressed, not alarmed.

Gardevoir's eyes flashed. It drew on its power to the limit, and a brilliant light burst from its palms toward Blaziken.

Dazzling Gleama powerful Fairy-type move.

Even in daylight, the light blazed like the sun. It almost blinded Blaziken. Under its force, Blaziken felt as if its body might melt. Every step and every breath became agony.

Worse, it could not keep its eyes open or tell which way to charge.

It's over, Ethan thought with a sigh.

Gardevoir Teleported behind Blaziken and struck its back with Psychic.

This time Blaziken could not endure. It flew forward, losing consciousness before it hit the ground.

"Blaziken is down!" the announcer cried.

Harrison, who had been looking down with his eyes closed, snapped them open. His Blaziken struck the ground. Behind it stood Gardevoir, slowly lowering its arms.

He had not seen how Gardevoir avoided the charge or delivered the final blow, but the scene told him enough.

He sighed. He had not won. Even at the end, Blaziken had never touched Gardevoir.

"What a decisive victory!" the announcer said. "Congratulations to Ethan, the first Trainer through to the final! His opponent will be decided in this afternoon's other semifinal!"

Harrison recalled Blaziken, walked over to Ethan, and shook his hand.

He looked at the handsome young man, several years his junior, with sincere admiration. "You're incredible. I was no match for you. After fighting you, it feels as though we're on different levels altogether."

"It's not quite that bad," Ethan replied with a warm smile. "My Pokemon have been through trials most Trainers' teams haven't faced. They grew stronger from them, and they did their best today."

In truth, Harrison was probably a little stronger than Ash overall.

Their teams emphasized different strengths. Ash's Pikachu and Charizard stood apart from his other Pokemon, which were somewhat weaker.

Harrison's Blaziken was clearly his ace, but the rest of his team was strong too. His Pokemon were more evenly matched.

Ash had an extraordinary upper limit, though his performance depended heavily on the day. Harrison had a higher baseline. If he could defeat Charizard and Pikachu, he would likely beat Ash.

Against Ethan, that balance led to a different result.

Ash had made Ethan work for his win, while Harrison had been overwhelmed. Ethan's Pokemon were, on average, stronger than Harrison's.

There was another reason Ethan had lost two rounds to Ash: he had sent Dragonite and Scizor, neither of which had finished growing into its strength. He had not used his strongest partners.

Had he chosen veterans like Gardevoir or Jolteon for those rounds, Ash would have had no chance to win them.

Still, losses had value. Ethan believed Dragonite and Scizor would grow stronger because of what they had experienced at this tournament. One day, they might stand beside his veteran Pokemon as equals.

He turned his attention back to Harrison. They did not know each other well, so their conversation was brief. A photographer took a picture of their handshake, and then they went their separate ways toward the waiting rooms.

Inside, Ethan spotted Ash, Misty, and Brock beside Erika.

"When did you get here?" he asked, surprised. The route from the stands was much longer than his walk from the field. Somehow the three had arrived first.

"We ran the whole way!" Misty smiled brightly. "Congratulations on reaching the final, Ethan!"

"Heh, heh!" Ash was grinning too, with none of Misty's grace. "A feast! I can't wait!"

Ash knew that Ethan treated them to a hearty meal whenever he advanced. As soon as Gardevoir won, he had dragged Misty and Brock out of the stands, without even waiting for Ethan to shake Harrison's hand.

Ethan laughed at his eagerness. Nothing except a battle or a big meal could make Ash move that fast.

"Of course. Come onlet's eat until we're full!"

They found a nearby restaurant and ordered a generous spread. Ethan was not very hungry at midday. He watched the television and chatted with Erika, Misty, and Brock.

Ash and Pikachu were the only ones eating as fast as they could.

It was already one in the afternoon. The main lunch rush had passed, and the restaurant was fairly quiet.

The television on the wall showed the day's other semifinal. It had only just begun, with the competitors being introduced. Jon Dickson faced a Trainer named Yamashita from the opposite half of the bracket.

Jon stood with a confident little smile. Brock watched him and said, "Unless something unexpected happens, Jon should win this, shouldn't he?"

"I think so," Misty agreed. She glanced at Jon on the screen. "Then the final really will be Jon against Ethan, just as we thought. He can smile now, but he won't be a match for Ethan!"

"You..." Ethan lightly flicked her forehead. She stuck out her tongue, and he shook his head with a smile. "Don't tempt fate for me. If I lose to him after you say that, everyone will laugh."

Misty was unconcerned. "I believe in you!"

The match began as they spoke. Even Ash stopped eating and turned toward the television.

The field at Silver Stadium had been drawn as a Grass Field. Jon, sending out first, chose Exeggutor to take advantage of the terrain. Yamashita answered with Magcargo.

Judging by Jon's earlier dominance in his half of the bracket, everyone expected him to win by a wide margin, just as Ethan had. The opening round startled them.

Exeggutor used the thick grass and its Grass- and Psychic-type moves to give Magcargo trouble early on. But Magcargo's Fire type had the advantage. Once it counterattacked, repeated blasts of flame scorched Exeggutor badly.

Perhaps Jon had grown careless. Perhaps the League's promotion of him as Ethan's equal had made him underrate the other competitors. Instead of switching Pokemon, he tried to fight through the unfavorable matchup.

The decision cost him. Magcargo's flames knocked Exeggutor out.

The referee raised a green flag. Yamashita had taken a one-to-zero lead.

Even then, no one realized how serious Jon's trouble was.

The announcer sounded amused. "It seems Jon Dickson may have underestimated his opponent, and the type matchup worked against him. The first round went badly, but I'm sure he'll adjust as the match continues!"

One loss was surprising, nothing more. Jon's strength was well known, while Yamashita had shown little in earlier rounds. No one believed Yamashita could beat him.

Jon himself thought the same.

The second round astonished them again.

Jon took Yamashita seriously and sent out Ampharos, which was not at a type disadvantage against Fire. Yamashita trusted Magcargo and kept it on the field.

Jon attacked fiercely from the start. Under his commands, Ampharos dealt Magcargo considerable damage. Then Magcargo spewed flames across the Grass Field, setting the whole surface alight.

Fire surrounded Ampharos in an instant. Its cries carried through the blaze.

The grass fed the flames, and the flames spread through the grass.

That was fire's destructive nature. Electric attacks and streams of water would fade into the air or earth when they missed. A flame could catch on fuel and keep burning until that fuel was gone.

The Grass Field gave this blaze more than enough to consume.

Jon frowned. He had not foreseen this. He wanted to recall Ampharos, but his Poke Ball's red beam could not penetrate the fire. He could not even see where Ampharos was inside it.

His composure vanished. However badly he wanted it to stop, the flames spread until the entire field had become a sea of fire.

Heat reached the spectators nearest the arena. They leaned back in alarm, trying to escape the hot air blowing toward them.

When the flames finally weakened, Jon searched the field. Ampharos lay blackened and motionless. He rushed out to check on it.

The burns were severe. Ampharos could not move, and its life was in danger. Without prompt treatment, it might die.

A medical team with two Chansey began emergency care at once. Then they lifted Ampharos onto a stretcher and carried it to the nearby aid station.

After the frantic interruption, the match resumed.

The accident was unexpected, but it fell within the rules. Different fields brought different risks.

Jon had lost two rounds. Now he had to focus and treat Yamashita with real caution.

At last he understood that Yamashita's plain earlier performances did not mean he was weak. Every Trainer in the semifinals had earned a place there. Any one of them might have held back a surprise. Underestimating an opponent could end his own tournament.

Fortunately for Jon, there was still a difference in their overall strength.

He sent out his strongest remaining Pokemon and did not relax again. He won the later rounds and reached the final after all, where he would face Ethan.
