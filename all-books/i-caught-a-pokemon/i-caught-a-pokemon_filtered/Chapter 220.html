Chapter 220: The Moon Is Beautiful Tonight

"One more thing," Ethan said as he remembered it. "That collector started all this, and we don't know whether he survived. Could you go to Lightning Island and check on him too?"

"Of course. Leave it to us," Carol replied.

After she left, the mayor brought in musicians to thank Ethan and the others. Melody took the stage as well and played a tune on her flute.

She had changed back into her festival clothes. Beneath her white veil, she looked graceful and beautiful. After everything she had experienced with Ethan's group, her feelings toward them had changed. At the least, she now considered them friends.

The tune was nothing like Lugia's Song. Its quicker rhythm lifted everyone's spirits.

In such a festive mood, Professor Ivy set aside discussion of her paper and picked up a glass of wine. She could drink quite well. After several glasses on her own, her expression had not changed. Seeing Ethan and Sabrina sipping nearby, she joined them for a chat.

Ethan took a mouthful and considered the taste. It was not made from grain. Shamouti Island must brew this fruit wine locally. It was mild rather than harsh, with a sweet fruit flavor.

Sabrina tried a few sips too, but before she finished even half a glass, her cheeks had begun to flush. She rarely drankperhaps she never hadand was clearly unused to the taste of alcohol.

"You two are drinking so slowly," Ivy said with a laugh. "Don't tell me you can't hold your wine. There'll be plenty more occasions like this. If a little wine gets you drunk, you'll miss half the fun. Come on, cheers!"

Without waiting for an answer, she clinked her glass against Ethan's. He drank his wine in one go. Before he could put the glass down, Ivy filled it again.

"Professor Ivy..." Ethan shook his head with a wry smile. "I really can't drink much. This is too fast for me."

"That's why you need practice. Another toast!" She tapped her glass against his again.

Ethan sighed. She wanted someone to drink with her, and he could hardly escape. He emptied his glass, then covered its rim with his hand. Ivy gave him a sidelong look but did not press him further.

At the other end of the table, Ash watched the adults with longing. When he heard the fruit wine was weak, he tried to sneak a taste. Delia caught him immediately and forbade it. He put the glass down in defeat.

"Little boys should listen to their mothers," Misty teased him. Then, when she thought no one was looking, she stole a few sips herself. Her face turned red as an apple.

Melody happened to see her. Before long, they were bickering again, this time over how old someone ought to be before drinking.

The hall was full of noisy conversation. Ethan liked it. He surveyed the table and found that Tracey was the only one apart from the revelry.

Tracey admired Professor Oak so much that he sat beside him like a diligent student, notebook in hand, asking one question after another. From Oak's expression, Ethan suspected he wanted to try the fruit wine too. But with Tracey looking up to him, he was determined to maintain his image and patiently answered every question.

The burden of being famous. Ethan felt a little sorry for him. Perhaps he would face the same predicament one day.

Dinner continued as stars appeared outside. Once everyone was full, fatigue struck all at once. Fortunately, Melody had arranged rooms for them.

Ethan left first. In his room, he took a cool shower to wash away the day's exhaustion. He had changed into sleepwear and was about to go to bed when someone knocked.

"Ethan, are you asleep?"

It was Misty.

"Not yet. What is it?" He opened the door and found her waiting in the hall.

"Ash and I talked it over," she said hopefully. "We're planning to leave around noon tomorrow and head to the next island. When are you going? Would you like to travel with us?"

"I don't think so." Ethan considered it, then shook his head. "We're going to different places. Let's each follow our own route. Knowing our luck, we'll run into one another again soon."

"...All right." Misty sighed, unable to hide her disappointment. She turned and walked away.

Ethan was about to close his door when he noticed Sabrina leaning against the wall farther down the hall. It was dim enough that he had missed her before. She had surely heard his conversation with Misty.

Sabrina watched Misty disappear around the corner. "That girl really likes you," she said softly.

Ethan paused, choosing his words. "Maybe. I think of her as a little sister, though. Nothing more."

"I see."

Sabrina looked at him and began opening the door to her room. Before she went in, Ethan spoke on impulse.

"Would you like to come in and sit for a while?"

She turned back, unsure why he was inviting her into his room so late. Given their closeness, she saw no reason to refuse.

"All right."

When Sabrina entered, Ethan closed the door almost without thinking. Her hair was still damp from a shower. A faint trace of wine remained about her.

In the dim room, she looked beautiful. Ethan found himself watching her.

After a moment, Sabrina smiled. "What are you looking at?"

"I... The moon is beautiful tonight."

Sabrina glanced out the window. A cloud hid the moon completely. There was nothing to see. Ethan had plainly reached for the first thing that came to mind.

Her psychic senses told her the mood was different tonight. Ethan, too, seemed unlike himself. Perhaps something was about to happen. She fell quiet.

He kept looking at her. Though he had drunk water only a short while ago, his mouth felt dry. Perhaps the wine had made him less cautious. He moved closer.

Sabrina neither retreated nor pushed him away. She watched him calmly, and her stillness gave him courage.

Ethan stopped before her, close enough that she could feel his breath. He held her gaze for a long moment. Then he lowered his head and gently kissed her.
