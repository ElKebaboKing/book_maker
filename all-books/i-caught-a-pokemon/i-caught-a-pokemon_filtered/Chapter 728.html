Chapter 728: Caught!

Boom!

High above them, two terrifying bursts of energy collided. The shock wave swept every cloud from around Sky Pillar, clearing the sky for kilometers around the battlefield.

Even the tower trembled, the shaking particularly strong at its summit. For perhaps the first time since it had been built, the whole structure stood exposed, with nothing hiding it from view.

Thousands of years ago, the Draconid people had built Sky Pillar to be sturdy and protected it with a strange power. They must have expected battles here someday and wanted the tower to survive them. But they could hardly have imagined a Pokemon as strong as Rayquaza fighting against it.

Under the power of the two combatants, Sky Pillar stood like a lighthouse in a storm, enduring the thunder and wind while giving every impression that it might finally fail. The fight had pushed it past what it should have been able to withstand. Fortunately, the Dragonhark altar was not their main battlefield, so the tower took fewer direct hits.

Even so, Misty felt the floor shudder beneath her. She braced herself against a stone pillar and edged toward Dragonite. Being close to it made her feel a little safer. If the tower fell, Dragonite could rescue her at once.

Dragonite sensed her worry, turned with a smile, and gave her a thumbs-up.

Ethan and Zinnia barely noticed the shaking. Both stared at the battle above. It had reached its decisive point. Though the clash had been brief, its intensity had drained both Pokemon. Each had taken enough hits for its condition to deteriorate badly. They still attacked fiercely, but neither had the overwhelming presence it had shown at the start.

Ethan knew it was time to change tactics. Through Gardevoir's telepathy, he told Deoxys to shift from Attack Forme to Speed Forme. It stopped trading close-range blows with the black Rayquaza and began harrying it instead.

The change worked immediately. Rayquaza had grown used to its opponent's relentless offense. Deoxys's sudden burst of speed caught it unprepared, and for a time it could scarcely track where Deoxys went.

In Speed Forme, Deoxys's attacks were weaker, but only by the standards of Legendary Pokemon. They still struck hard enough to trouble a powerful attacker with limited defenses like Rayquaza. More importantly, Rayquaza had already lost too much stamina to adjust.

Whoosh! Whoosh! Whoosh!

Deoxys became little more than a blur. Under that constant pressure, the black Rayquaza began to lose ground. It roared and twisted its long body, seemingly trying to goad Deoxys into fighting it head-on again.

A wild Pokemon might have taken the bait. Ethan would not throw away the advantage he had worked to create. First, he had matched Rayquaza blow for blow with Deoxys in Attack Forme. Once Rayquaza was exhausted, Deoxys's speed let it stay beyond reach while landing hit after hit.

It sounded simple. Against a nearly equal opponent, guiding a battle to this point required a Trainer's full attention and careful choices. That was what a Trainer could offer: help a Pokemon perform beyond its usual limits, turn a balanced fight into an advantage, and make the impossible possible. It was one of the greatest differences between a wild Pokemon and one fighting beside a human.

Now it was time to finish the fight.

Deoxys made copies of itself and slipped past Rayquaza once more. The real Deoxys appeared behind it and struck with another powerful sphere of energy, hitting it squarely in the back.

The blast staggered Rayquaza. Pain crossed its face.

Zinnia's heart nearly leapt from her chest. Now! she shouted.

I know!

Ethan had seen the opening too. He pulled out the Ultra Ball he had prepared and hurled it skyward. Before all three of them, the spinning ball climbed more than ten meters and snapped open. Red light wrapped around the black Rayquaza and drew it inside.

Ethan's nerves tightened. He had caught many Legendary Pokemon, but Rayquaza stood near the top of the food chain. He could hardly remain calm at the prospect of catching oneespecially a black Shiny Rayquaza, the Pokemon he had dreamed of having.

The Ultra Ball fell onto the Dragonhark altar with a soft clack. It rolled several times, stopped, and shook violently. Rayquaza was making one last struggle inside.

But it was already injured. Even if it escaped, Deoxys would be waiting, along with Ethan's next Ultra Ball. The outcome felt inevitable.

Ultra Balls were one of the more expensive and prominent Poke Ball products. Like ordinary Poke Balls and Great Balls, they sometimes drew complaints about failed captures. That had more to do with how people used themand a measure of luckthan any lack of quality. Their success rate could be trusted.

This one gave Ethan reason to believe it. After several fierce shakes, the movement slowed. Each sway was smaller than the last. It might truly work.

Ethan's eyes brightened. Misty clenched both fists as if she could lend the ball her strength. Come on!

She knew there was much she had not seen or heard of, but she had never heard of anyone in her lifetime catching Rayquaza. This was no ordinary Pokemon. It was Hoenn's Legendary Rayquaza. Would Ethan be the first to accomplish it? Would she witness that moment?

They watched with hope while Zinnia looked on with mixed feelings. After only a few seconds more, the ball gave a final click, and its light went out.

Yes! Misty cried as soon as she saw the capture succeed.

She bounded over and threw her arms around Ethan's head. His face was pressed into her chest, and he felt its soft, still-developing curves. He pulled free so he could breathe again, looked at the Ultra Ball resting quietly on the altar, and laughed.

One ball. That was all it had taken. Perhaps there really was a reason Ultra Balls cost more.

He remembered how he had once needed his whole team to face Zapdos. Now Deoxys had beaten Rayquaza one-on-one. Rayquazahe had truly caught one! He had accomplished in this world something that had once been possible only in a game. Excitement surged through him.

He took a deep breath, eased out of Misty's embrace, and walked toward the ball.

Zinnia watched him pick it up. Her hands clenched into fists, then slowly loosened. The battle had shown her the power of top-tier Pokemon, a level beyond ordinary opponents and far beyond anything she could currently reach. If she had fought Rayquaza herself, even sending out her entire team might not have been enough. Ethan had defeated it with one Pokemon.

Monsters, she thought. Both Trainer and Pokemon.

Ethan knew nothing of her thoughts. The instant he held the Ultra Ball, his own emotions became surprisingly calm. It might have been his imagination, but the ball felt heavier now, as though he could sense the black Rayquaza's immense life force within it.

He did not release Rayquaza again. He put the ball straight into the pouch at his waist. At the same time, the Meteorite Key Stone he carried returned to normal. That gave him a bold idea, though testing it would have to wait for another opportunity.

Footsteps sounded beside him. Zinnia approached.

Guardian Rayquaza chose you, and you caught it. Congratulations.

The meteor shower had vanished sometime during the fight. By moonlight and the green flames around them, Ethan studied Zinnia's face. It was calm, revealing nothing.

Thank you, he said. You tried to tell me something before the battle. You can say it now.

It doesn't matter anymore. She glanced at the pouch, as if she could somehow see Rayquaza inside the ball. I wanted to tell you what I knew about its weaknesses. Clearly, you didn't need it.

Rayquaza's weaknesses? Ethan paused, then shook his head. The Draconid people might know something about its body, but its greatest vulnerability was simple enough: as a Dragon- and Flying-type, it was four times as weak to Ice-type attacks. Perhaps that was what she had meant to tell him. Most people knew too little about Rayquaza even to identify its types, much less plan around them.

Zinnia wasn't as stubborn as she had seemed. Once she knew she would not catch it herself, she had still meant to share what she knew rather than keep it to herself. Ethan's opinion of her improved. He had stopped her before she could speak, but the gesture still mattered.

And the Draconid people had spent thousands of years pursuing this goal without achieving it. How many of them had gone to their graves with that longing unfulfilled?

I understand how you feel, Ethan said after a moment. Well, I'm in a good mood today. Listen: the offer I made earlier still stands. When I can, I'll do my best to help you catch a Rayquaza and fulfill your people's wish.

Zinnia stared at him in disbelief. It took her a long time to find her voice. Why?

I told you. I'm happy today, and you didn't interfere with the battle. I appreciate that. Ethan changed tack. But perhaps you won't need my help. You just managed to summon Rayquaza on your own, without the Rainbow Meteorite.

Zinnia looked at the dull Key Stones scattered on the floor and sighed. It's not so easy. These weren't ordinary Key Stones. Our people spent years finding stones that reacted to Rayquaza. Each one took enormous effort to obtain. Now their energy is exhausted, and they're useless. Besides, the offering summoned Rayquaza, but that was all. It didn't choose me.

That's because you're too rigid, Ethan said. What does being chosen have to do with it? Beat Rayquaza with your own strength, throw a Poke Ball, and there you are. You've chosen it.

You don't understand. Zinnia sounded weary. This is our tradition. We aren't allowed to change it.

And that's why you still haven't caught a single Rayquaza.

His words struck hard. Ethan gave her one last look and beckoned Misty over. Once they were both on Dragonite, he looked down at Zinnia, much as Rayquaza had looked down on them earlier.

I've never let tradition decide everything for me. That's how I caught so many Legendary Pokemon in such a short time. I've said enough. I have other things to do. Think it over. When you're ready, contact meassuming I have time.

Dragonite spread its wings without waiting for an order and flew into the distance.

Ethan was gone, but his words continued to echo in Zinnia's mind.

That's why you still haven't caught a single Rayquaza.

The sentence struck like a hammer. Had they really followed their traditions too closely? Without the Rainbow Meteorite, they couldn't summon Rayquaza. Without being chosen, they couldn't carry on their role. Were those beliefs wrong?

No one was there to answer her. Zinnia stood motionless, watching Ethan disappear. Her heart pounded as his words and the image of him played over and over in her mind.
