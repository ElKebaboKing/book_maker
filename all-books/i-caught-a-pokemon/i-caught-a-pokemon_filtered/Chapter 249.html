Chapter 249: Camping by the Lake

"Mr. Quackenpoker, does the number of Magikarp affect when they evolve?" Tracey asked.

"It does," Quackenpoker said. "There's a mysterious power in this lake. The more Magikarp gather, the more likely they are to trigger it and evolve together. With too few, it can take a long time, perhaps until tomorrow."

"I see."

The sun was sinking toward the horizon. Since they would have to spend the night outdoors, everyone set to work before dark.

Quackenpoker continued operating his equipment and observing the Magikarp. Ash and Misty gathered firewood and lit a campfire. Tracey brought out the cooking gear and took charge of dinner. Ethan and Sabrina pitched their own tent and Quackenpoker's.

Tracey made curry rice with a fruit salad. The Pokemon gathered the fruit in the nearby forest; Ash and his friends had brought the curry, potatoes, rice, and other ingredients themselves. They camped and cooked far more often than Ethan, who tended to stay in hotels or eat at restaurants, so they traveled prepared.

"This is delicious!" Quackenpoker said after tasting the fragrant curry. The mild spice surprised him. "What luck! I was going to make do with instant noodles. I never expected a meal like this on an uninhabited island."

Across the folding table, Ethan took a generous spoonful of rice covered in sauce. It really was good, appealing in taste, smell, and appearance. Tracey was a capable cook, though still not quite as good as Brock, who usually handled Ash's meals.

They talked as they ate. The pleasant dinner stretched beyond an hour, and the moon was high by the time they finished. Misty brewed a pot of hot tea, and everyone gathered around the campfire.

Quackenpoker held his warm cup and looked at Tracey. "Young man, you're a Pokemon Watcher too, aren't you?"

"Huh? How did you know?"

"Never underestimate an experienced Watcher's eyes." Quackenpoker blew aside the tea leaves floating in his cup, looking pleased with himself. "I saw your sketchbook and binoculars down by the river. You look at things the way a Watcher does."

"So that's how." Tracey had never tried to hide his profession, and he did not mind Quackenpoker recognizing it.

After an evening together, the old man's manner toward all of them had softened. He smiled kindly and held out his hand. "May I look at your observation notes?"

"Of course." Tracey gave him his sketchbook and the notes he carried with it.

Quackenpoker read through them carefully. At last he nodded in approval. "Very good. You've studied all kinds of Pokemon, and your observations are detailed. Keep at it. I believe you'll become an excellent Pokemon Watcher."

The two discussed their knowledge and experiences. Tracey learned a great deal from the older Watcher. Quackenpoker did not leave Ethan and the others out. Over tea, he told them stories of unusual events from his travels. He had visited regions all over the world, and Ash and his friends listened in amazement to adventures they could hardly imagine.

More than a hundred Magikarp entered the lake during that time, but there were still too few to trigger the mass evolution.

After ten o'clock, everyone grew sleepy. Ethan and Tracey began discussing who would keep watch, but Quackenpoker refused before they could settle it.

"I'll stay up alone," he said firmly. "To be honest, I wouldn't feel comfortable leaving the Magikarp measurements to you."

He was blunt, but they understood. He had studied these Magikarp for three years and waited a long time for tonight. They might evolve at any moment. He wanted to observe them himself rather than entrust the crucial hours to anyone else.

Tracey first tried to persuade him to rest for his health. Quackenpoker would not change his mind, so the others let him sit beside the fire through the night. Ethan understood him. At a moment this important, he too would want to see it with his own eyes.

Ethan and Sabrina washed up by the river, then went into their tent. Ash and Tracey spread their sleeping bags at a comfortable distance from the fire and climbed in.

Misty alone stayed where she was. She looked at Ethan and Sabrina's tent, her expression shifting as a thought occurred to her.

Why were they sleeping in the same tent? She did not know exactly what their relationship was, but sharing a tent so openly suggested they were close. Could Ethan and Sabrina be a couple?

She made up her mind, lifted the tent flap, and ducked inside.

Ethan was spreading a blanket on the ground. Camping was colder than sleeping indoors, and without enough bedding they might wake shivering before morning.

He looked up in surprise when Misty entered. Sabrina frowned but said nothing.

"Ethan..." Misty smiled at him.

"What is it?" he asked.

She hesitated. Then, seeing that Sabrina was already in her sleepwear, she found the nerve to ask, "It's windy outside. Could I sleep in your tent tonight?"

Ethan smiled ruefully to himself. She had already come in; he could hardly send her back out. He did not want her left in the cold, either. Ignoring Sabrina's knowing look, he smiled at Misty.

"Of course. The tent's big enough for four people."
