Chapter 248: The Magikarp's Home Island

Ethan and the others followed the river up through the forest. After another ten minutes or more, they finally reached the summit of Rind Island's mountain.

The ground leveled out. Dense forest surrounded a broad, clear lake in the center, and water spilled from it toward the slopes below.

"Here we are. The Lake of Birth and Evolution!"

Quackenpoker set down his backpack. Only a few Magikarp were swimming around the lake, and the mass evolution had not begun. Relieved that they had made it in time, he relaxed.

Misty dropped onto a large rock with a long breath. She had climbed all this way carrying a heavy box. At last she could rest.

Everyone looked at the lake. As Quackenpoker had said, it appeared perfectly ordinary. Had they come upon it by themselves, they would never have suspected that anything remarkable happened here.

Sabrina glanced around. "It's strange. Why can't I see any other Pokemon?" she asked Ethan quietly.

He noticed the silence then. A forest this large ought to have wild Pokemon, and a lake should attract other Water types. Yet the entire summit was quiet.

"Maybe the Magikarp migration disturbed them," he suggested. "They might have moved away or hidden until it's over."

Sabrina nodded without committing herself. They had too little information to be sure.

Quackenpoker was already taking equipment from the boxes and setting it up beside the lake. There were devices shaped like cameras, a laptop, and something resembling a satellite receiver. Ethan and his friends gathered around. None of them knew enough to identify every instrument from its appearance.

"Mr. Quackenpoker," Tracey asked politely, "what do these machines do?"

Quackenpoker seemed pleased by the question. With no sign of evolution yet, he had time to answer. "They automatically analyze all sorts of data on the Magikarp: weight, growth patterns, bone density, muscle fatigue, and more. They're powerful tools."

Ten-year-old Ash was lost among the technical terms. He scratched his head. "That's amazing. I don't understand what you said, but it sounds impressive!"

Quackenpoker laughed. Praise from a child pleased him.

Splash! Splash!

Several red shapes broke the surface as more Magikarp came up the river and entered the lake. The group watched until the water settled. Then Ethan turned back to the old man, who handled his equipment with practiced ease.

"How long have you been a Pokemon Watcher?"

Quackenpoker thought for a moment. "I've forgotten the exact number of years. It must be fifty by now."

"Fifty?" Misty stuck out her tongue in amazement. To stay with a profession for fifty years took dedication. Even if he had started at fifteen or sixteen, as Tracey had, he would be in his sixties or older now.

It seemed hard to be out in the sun and wind at that age. What if he died on some deserted island? Misty caught herself and hurriedly shook away the disrespectful thought.

Quackenpoker was busy with his instruments and noticed nothing. Even if he had, his manner suggested he would have ignored it.

Tracey admired the weathered veteran. "That's incredible. How long have you been observing Magikarp?"

"Not long. Only three years," Quackenpoker replied lightly, as though the time were nothing more than a number.

Ethan looked at him with fresh respect. At Quackenpoker's age, many people would choose to stay home and enjoy their families' company. Few would still travel to remote islands to study Pokemon. A person's life held only so many years, and he had devoted three of them to these Magikarp. His love of Pokemon was plain.

Misty trained Water-type Pokemon, but even she could not imagine giving Magikarp that much attention. She admired him while still wondering whether such an ordinary species really needed three years of study.

Tracey remembered the colored rings. "What about the bands we saw on some of their tails?"

"I put them there," Quackenpoker said. "They identify individuals by the year they left the island. Red means one year ago, blue means two, and yellow means three."

"All these Magikarp were born here?"

"That's right. Magikarp born on this island swim out to sea as they grow. No matter how far they travel, they return at this time each year. Rind Island is their home."

Ethan had never known Magikarp could have a home island. They lived all over the world, though, so perhaps this migratory behavior belonged only to the ones in the Orange Islands.

The light of sunset fell across the group. More Magikarp were gathering in the lake, but none had begun to evolve.

"This isn't good," Quackenpoker said, checking the time again. "There are fewer Magikarp than I expected. It looks as though we'll have to camp here tonight."
