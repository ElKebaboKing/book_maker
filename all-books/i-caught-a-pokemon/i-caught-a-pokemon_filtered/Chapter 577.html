Chapter 577: Jon Is Stunned

Today's Water Field had a broad patch of land in the center, surrounded by a pool.

There was enough room on that central ground for Rhyhorn to run. That was why Jon had felt safe sending it out.

Against a Ground- and Rock-type Rhyhorn, Ethan chose Crystal Steelix.

Thud.

Crystal Steelix landed heavily to another roar from the crowd. Rhyhorn showed no fear of the enormous opponent. It scraped a front hoof across the ground, eager to fight.

Its confidence was not groundless.

With a body like a cross between a rhinoceros and a horned dinosaur, covered in armor, Rhyhorn looked tremendously hard. Once it got moving, the force of its charge would be frightening.

Jon smiled when Ethan brought out Crystal Steelix.

Just as he'd hoped. He had been studying Ethan's team for a long time. Every one of Ethan's Pokemon had looked strong in tournament footage.

He had never faced them directly, though, and believed he could handle them.

More importantly, Ethan had changed since the Indigo League. At the Silver Conference he seemed less interested in exploiting type advantages and more willing to meet opponents head-on. That gave Jon room to use his own strengths.

There was a reason he had opened with Rhyhorn. Now Ethan had responded exactly as he had expected, sending the rare Crystal Steelix for a direct contest of strength.

Steelix was huge, but Jon's Rhyhorn had trained for that kind of clash. It had never feared a test of raw power.

The referee checked both sides and swung his flag.

"The first round of the Silver Conference final begins now!"

At Jon's command, Rhyhorn started to run.

It earned its comparison to an armored vehicle. The ground trembled under its hooves.

Rhyhorn was not fast, but the momentum of its charge was imposing enough that Ethan took it seriously.

He had no intention of avoiding it.

Crystal Steelix feared no Pokemon in a contest of strength. "Stop it!" Ethan called.

Steelix lowered its center of gravity and coiled its body. Rhyhorn had built up momentum, so Steelix needed the friction of its coils against the ground to resist the impact.

Under the crowd's tense gaze, they collided.

Bam!

The force drove Steelix's front half backward. Its tail braced hard against the earth, though, and no matter how Rhyhorn pushed, it held its position.

Rhyhorn shuddered as though it had struck a small mountain. It could not advance another step.

Neither Pokemon had won the first exchange. Ethan remained calm, while Jon raised his eyebrows.

Even that charge had not moved Steelix. He had underestimated its strength. A pity, but the result was still within what he could handle.

"Rhyhorn, use Bulldoze!"

Rhyhorn lifted both front hooves to begin the Ground-type attack. Ethan did not give it the chance.

Ground-type moves were dangerous to Crystal Steelix. Even it could not take many direct hits.

"Don't let it. Use Iron Tail!"

Steelix swept its huge tail through the air with a rush of sound. It struck Rhyhorn with a sharp crack.

Rhyhorn staggered sideways. Its low stance let it catch itself before it fell.

Its thick hide did its job. The blow had interrupted it without dealing much damage.

Even so, Jon had been warned. He withdrew Rhyhorn to a safer distance and changed tactics.

From there, he ordered Earthquake. The whole field shook, and rings spread across the surrounding pool.

Now!

Crystal Steelix rocked with the ground and struggled to keep its balance. Jon saw his opening.

"Push it in!"

Rhyhorn charged again. Steelix had no time to brace as before. Rhyhorn drove it to the edge of the central ground, gave one more push, and sent it crashing into the pool.

Splash!

Water rose high and closed over Crystal Steelix. Its huge body vanished below the surface.

It worked! Jon celebrated inwardly. He tried to hide it, but he could not quite keep the smile from his face.

Most Ground-, Rock-, and Steel-type Pokemon could not swim. Anyone with a little knowledge knew that. Once they fell into water, they rarely got back out.

"This is bad!" the announcer cried. "Crystal Steelix has fallen into the pool around the field. Is Ethan about to lose the very first round?"

The spectators gasped. Ethan was the tournament favorite, with a huge following. Could he stumble at the opening of the final?

The referee looked over the water. Bubbles rose, but the splashing was fading.

He was about to call the result when something caught his eye. A vast shape seemed to be moving below the surface.

The stadium screens showed it too. The crowd quieted, staring in disbelief.

Jon noticed the change in mood and frowned. He looked carefully at the pool. A huge shadow really was swimming there.

No. Surely not.

Water suddenly erupted. Crystal Steelix's enormous head broke the surface. It coiled, pulled itself upward, and climbed back onto land.

Jon stood numb with shock.

How? Steelix could swimand climb out again?

Most of the audience was just as stunned. Even Professor Elm and the Kimono Sisters in the waiting room stared. Only those who knew Crystal Steelix well, like Ash, were unsurprised.

"Incredible," Elm murmured, adjusting his glasses. "That Steelix can swim. I never expected it."

Steelix did not give Jon time to recover. It rushed forward, wrapped its body around Rhyhorn, and lifted it from the ground.

"Oh no! Break free!"

Jon finally shook off his surprise. Without room to run, though, Rhyhorn was helpless in the coils.

Creak... creak...

Crystal Steelix tightened its grip. Rhyhorn's armorlike hide could withstand punishing attacks, even flames, but constant crushing pressure was another matter.

Its struggles grew weaker. Steelix squeezed harder. Rhyhorn's eyes rolled upward, and its body went limp.

The referee raised his red flag. "Rhyhorn is unable to battle! Crystal Steelix wins!"

Jon had gone from complete confidence to doubt in a single round.

He did not seem to realize he had lost until the referee reminded him to choose his next Pokemon.

Jon could accept a loss. He had lost the first two rounds against Yamashita, after all. This felt different.

"You can't understand how strong Ethan is until you've fought him yourself."

Jon remembered reading that online. The person who wrote it was a Trainer from the Whirl Islands. Ethan had apparently beaten him so thoroughly at a recent Whirl Cup that he had lost much of his desire to continue as a Trainer.

Jon had dismissed the comment as an excuse from a weak opponent. Now he began to understand it.

Ethan's match videos had not conveyed this pressure. Facing him in person felt suffocating.

Victory always looked possible, yet somehow slipped away. Why?

Jon recalled Rhyhorn. His expression heavy, he took out his second Poke Ball.

"Go, Golduck!"

He threw it with his usual motion, but his voice had lost its earlier certainty.

"After that first-round defeat, Jon Dickson is choosing a Water-type to use the field to his advantage!" the announcer said. "That's quite a change from his usual style!"

Jon had reached the final through skill, not luck. A Trainer with his record had pride. He had not usually bothered to build his choices around the field or a type matchup.

Now he had deliberately chosen a Water type on the Water Field. The match was changing him already.

Golduck was a pure Water type, but like Psyduck it could also use some Psychic-type moves. It was far more alert and imposing than its absentminded pre-evolution.

Ethan liked the species. He had never encountered a wild one, though, and had not caught one of his own.

His fingers passed over Jolteon's and Dragonite's Poke Balls. After thinking for a moment, he chose Blastoise.

Several days earlier, Ethan had asked Chloe to send him all his Pokemon from Cerise Park apart from the red Gyarados. He had kept them with him after each match instead of sending them back, ready for any matchup.

"Go, Blastoise!"

Blue light flashed. The larger Blastoise and smaller Golduck faced each other across the field.

"Two Water types!" the announcer said. "Now experience, stronger moves, and how well they use this field will decide it!"

As the battle began, both Pokemon attacked from a distance.

Blastoise fired Hydro Pump. Golduck did not answer with a Water-type move. It used Confusion.

Ethan almost laughed at the trick. He had brought a Water type for a Water-type duel, and Jon had chosen this?

Hydro Pump struck Golduck squarely. It endured the hit thanks to its own Water type. Blastoise, meanwhile, suddenly felt light as Confusion lifted it high into the air.

The red gem on Golduck's forehead flashed rapidly. It pushed both hands downward, and Blastoise plunged toward the ground.

It was a standard way to use Confusion here: hurt the target, lift it, then slam it down for a second impact.

An ordinary Pokemon might have had no answer. Blastoise had a shell.

Ethan did not need to give an order. After many similar experiences, Blastoise knew exactly what to do.

Just before it hit, Blastoise withdrew into its heavy shell, letting the armor take the force.

Crash!

A crater opened beneath it. A moment later, Blastoise poked out its head and shook off its dizziness. It had taken no meaningful damage.

Even so, Ethan did not want Jon to repeat the move. "Blastoise, jump into the water!"

Underwater movement and refracted light would make it much harder for Golduck to catch Blastoise with Confusion again.

Jon sighed when his surprise attack failed. He did not lose heart. He ordered Golduck into the pool as well.

If they were both Water types, they could settle the round in the water.
