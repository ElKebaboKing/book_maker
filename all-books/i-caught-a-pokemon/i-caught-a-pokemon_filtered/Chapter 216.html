Chapter 216: A Vision of the End

Ethan's three birds abandoned their opponents and climbed together into the clouds.

The island's three gods stared after them, baffled that their counterparts had all broken away at once. They did not give chase. Instead, they watched the sky warily.

Dark clouds gathered and thickened at a visible pace. Within moments they had covered the whole skynot merely over one island, but across more than two hundred kilometers of surrounding sea.

The fighting had already darkened the day. Now no sunlight reached them at all. A breeze stirred by the shifting air pressure lifted everyone's hair.

Misty pulled her clothes tighter around herself. The oppressive sky made it hard to breathe. "Ethan, what exactly are you doing?"

Melody and Tracey looked at him too.

Ethan patted Misty's shoulder. "I'm trying a combination move with a lot of power. The birds have practiced it a few times, but they're still working on it. They've only succeeded once. I wouldn't use it lightly if this weren't urgent, so you'd better protect yourselves."

That was hardly reassuring. Misty and the others sent out Pokemon to stand guard. Seeing that Melody had none, Misty brought out Starmie to protect her.

Snowflakes began to fall. A blizzard was coming. Thunder rumbled overhead without striking; terrible amounts of electricity seemed to be building inside the clouds.

A blizzard and Thunder together? Misty had seen powerful Pokemon moves alter the weather, but never over an area so vast.

Snow settled on the three island birds. Their body heat melted it into water that soaked their feathers.

Crack! Crack!

Lightning tore across the sky as the gathered power reached its peak. Instead of one bolt, more than a dozen smaller streaks fell at once toward all four legendary Pokemon on the battlefield.

"Here it comes," Ethan said quietly.

The three birds tried to evade, but they were exposed in the air, their feathers wet. The lightning found them as if guided to its targets.

The Gods of Ice and Fire cried out. The attack was divided among many bolts, so it was less devastating than it looked, but it still sent them falling toward the sea. The God of Thunder resisted the Electric-type strike and remained in better shape.

The lightning had barely faded when a fierce blizzard engulfed them. This time the God of Ice resisted the attack, while the Gods of Fire and Thunder cried out in pain.

They were legendary Pokemon with immense endurance. Though struck twice, all three held on. They were wounded, panting, and disheveled, seemingly near their limits.

"Too bad," Misty said with a sigh. She had hoped the two attacks would defeat them outright.

"Don't worry. There's one more strike."

Misty looked up. A huge ball of fire, almost like the sun, broke through the cloud cover and dropped straight toward the battlefield.

"What is that?" Melody asked. "A meteor?"

"No. It's Moltres using Burn Up. It started high above us, so the descent makes it look like a meteor."

By then Ethan's Moltres was only dozens of meters from the ice below. The injured island birds sensed the threat and tried to leave. They had barely begun to beat their wings when Moltres struck the God of Thunder.

Boom!

Flames exploded outward. Heat cracked and melted the ice, and a storm of steam, water droplets, and wind burst from the center.

The gale nearly knocked Ethan and the others down. Blown ice chips stung their faces and forced their eyes shut. Their Pokemon blocked the larger chunks and debris before anyone was hurt.

The wind reached far across the ice. When it finally eased, they looked toward the battlefield.

A gap several hundred meters wide had burned through the frozen sea. The God of Thunder floated on the water, unconscious and unable to fight. Ethan's Moltres hovered nearby, breathing hard.

Burn Up did not usually exhaust it so badly. The dive from such a height had increased its speed, but the impact had taken a toll on Moltres too.

Ethan had originally imagined this three-bird combination for an opponent as formidable as Mewtwo. If every part hit, it might even defeat a Pokemon as strong as Lugia in one blow. Using it to knock out the God of Thunder felt wasteful, but he had needed to weaken the island birds quickly.

The clouds broke apart, and the wind calmed. The Gods of Ice and Fire had been thrown clear by the blast and were struggling to rise. They had been farther from the center, and Moltres had aimed Burn Up at Zapdos, so they had taken little damage from the final strike.

Misty scanned the sky. "Where's Lugia?"

Ethan looked around. Lugia had vanished. "Maybe it went into the sea. The lightning struck everywhere, so it may have broken the rings holding Lugia. If so, it could have escaped then."

"I hope so." Melody clasped her hands at her chest. "I hope it's safe."

Across Shamouti Island, townspeople stood at their windows, watching the sky with grave faces. The distance hid the battle itself, but thunder, blizzard, and the falling fireball had terrified them.

Was this what the end of the world looked like?
