Chapter 754: Miserable Is the Only Word for It

Excitement gleamed in Harley's eyes. He could already picture Cacturne knocking Ethan down.

Then something unbelievable happened.

Cacturne had reached Ethan's back and brought its raised arm down. But a dark shape sprang from beneath Ethan's feet, putting itself between them like a wall. It lifted both hands, and a wave of black energy hurled Cacturne backward.

Harley's expression froze. It took him two seconds to understand what he had seen. Why was Ethan unharmed while Cacturne had been sent flying?

He looked again. The figure that had emerged from the shadow was a Gengar. Why had a Gengar been hiding in the man's shadow in the first place?

Keh-heh-heh-heh! Gengar lowered its arms and grinned at Harley. I've seen what passes for your cunning. Pretty pathetic. And trying a trick like that in front of me? You have a lot to learn!

Hearing it speak stunned Harley all over again. A Gengar that could speak human language? There were Pokemon like that in the world?

After a moment, he remembered to check on Cacturne. The blast had flung it dozens of meters into a wall. Cracks spread across the masonry, and Cacturne lay unconscious beneath them.

Sweat soaked Harley's back. He knew his own Pokemon better than anyone. Cacturne was his strongest and most trusted partner. It had ambushed a man from behind and still been defeated in a single blow. The gap between the two Pokemon had to be enormous.

That could not be right. Harley would rather believe Gengar had struck a vulnerable spot by chance than accept that it was so powerful.

Ash and the others finally relaxed.

That scared me. Ash breathed out. I forgot Gengar was there! With it guarding Ethan, nobody's going to hurt him.

Ethan had only paused. He glanced back at Cacturne, then at Harley's shifting expression, shook his head, and continued toward the battle field without a word.

Knowing what kind of man Harley was, the ambush had not surprised him. He had even suspected Harley might try it and felt no need for extra precautions. Harley could have brought several more Pokemon and still could not have reached Ethan under Gengar's watch.

Ethan's silent dismissal enraged Harley further, but it also cleared his head. His strongest Pokemon had fallen in one hit. What was he supposed to use now?

He watched Ethan walk away, mind racing. The failed ambush had put him in a humiliating position of his own making. Ash, May, and the others had come outside, so he could not try another cheap trick. Nor could he slip off unnoticed with Ethan ahead of him and the group behind. Uneasy, he followed them to the nearby outdoor battle field.

Ethan went straight to one of the Trainer's positions. He was here now; Harley could decide whether he dared accept the challenge.

Harley wanted to teach Ethan a lesson, yet Gengar's display left him wondering what he had gotten into. Still, he had come this far. He clenched his teeth and took the opposite position.

With his hands in his pockets and his eyes half-lidded, Ethan said, Come on. One battle or a full series, I don't care. Show me what you can do.

Harley's blood boiled. Ethan managed to hit a sore spot with even a casual remark. No wonder Harley had disliked him on sight.

He had to try, however little confidence he felt. Big words, Harley scoffed. I was about to say the same. Let's see whether you're as good at battling as you are at talking.

He paused and held up five fingers. If we're doing this, make it five against five. We each use a different Pokemon in every round, win or lose. After all five battles, whoever has more wins takes the match.

Harley was quick to scheme. He did not know the tale of Tian Ji's horse race, but he had arrived at a similar strategy. Whether Gengar had truly overwhelmed Cacturne or merely landed a lucky blow, it was plainly strong. Harley assumed it was Ethan's best Pokemon.

He could sacrifice his weakest Pokemon against Gengar, taking it out of the remaining rounds, then use his other partners to win the rest. He would lose one battle but win the series. The plan sounded excellent in his head.

It depended, of course, on his other Pokemon being stronger than Ethan's. Harley knew nothing about Ethan's team, and Ash's group had no intention of enlightening him.

Confident in his supposed advantage, Harley took out a Poke Ball. He did not release his Pokemon or name a first pick. He merely held the ball and waited for Ethan to show his hand.

Ethan saw through the little tactic. He shook his head, unwilling to bother arguing, and took a Poke Ball from his belt pouch. It made no difference how many rounds they played. His Pokemon's strength gave him every reason for confidence. Schemes could accomplish little against an overwhelming opponent.

Go, Ethan! Ash called.

You can do it, Ethan! May added.

The cheers darkened Harley's mood. Then Ethan's ball opened in a flash of blue light, revealing an Eevee.

Harley's mouth curled into a smile. Eevee was ordinary enough, often encountered in the wild, and this one had not even evolved. To him that meant weakness. He had traveled a long time without meeting a single Eevee that impressed him.

Surely Gengar really was this man's strongest Pokemon. Now Harley knew what to do. He threw his own Poke Ball, releasing Wigglytuff.

As Jigglypuff's evolution, Wigglytuff was twice as tall and considerably stronger. It was no longer quite as round, but its large eyes, soft pink-and-white fur, short limbs, and long ears still made it look harmlessly cute. A thin film of tears always covered its eyes. Beneath that gentle appearance, a Normal- and Fairy-type Wigglytuff could surprise anyone who underestimated it.

Ethan knew the species well and would not have let his guard down even if he had not. Harley's Wigglytuff, though, looked unusually sly. Its eyes and expression were fierce. It seemed to take after its Trainer.

The Pokemon moved to their respective sides of the field. With everyone watching, Harley made no further underhanded move. He believed an evolved Wigglytuff would beat an unevolved Eevee easily. Win the first round, and the advantage would be his.

There was no referee. Once both Pokemon were ready, they rushed together without waiting for a formal signal.

Almost at once, Harley realized something was wrong. Eevee looked like any other of its kind, yet it was far harder to deal with. Neither Wigglytuff's Body Slam nor its Double Slap could land. Eevee struck back with Tackle and Quick Attack, driving Wigglytuff steadily backward until it was panting.

Harley had underestimated Eevee badly. Worse, the first battle was slipping away from him.

Wigglytuff, what are you doing? he shouted. Stop playing around! Use Sing!

Despite its name, Sing was no cheering song. It could lull an opponent to sleep and turn a battle around in an instant.

Wigglytuff knew it was in trouble. It swung a hand to force Eevee back, leaped to a safer distance, and opened its mouth.

A spray of sand struck its face before it could sing a note. Sand filled its mouth, silenced the song, and stung its eyes. Harley had been naive to expect such an easy chance to put Ethan's Pokemon to sleep.

Eevee had interrupted Sing with Sand Attack. At Ethan's command, it took advantage of Wigglytuff's blinded state and struck with Take Down. Wigglytuff's eyes rolled upward, and its body flew back to land at Harley's feet. It was out.

Harley stared with his mouth open. For a long moment, he could not believe it.

I... I lost?

He had expected a certain victory. Instead, he had been on the defensive from the start and lost quickly without any real chance to fight back.

Ash and the others exchanged looks.

Um... Max hesitated. Does Harley know who he's battling?

I don't think so, Ash said, scratching his head. I haven't told him.

Neither have I, Brock said.

May shook her head. I haven't mentioned it either.

Max pushed up his glasses, an excited glint reflecting off them. So he thinks Ethan is just an ordinary Trainer?

Looks that way, Ash said. Should we tell him?

Why would we? Misty snorted. Harley seemed so sure of himself. I want to see how he feels after all five battles.

A Coordinator already fought at a disadvantage against a Trainer who specialized in battles. Harley's opponent was a man who had crushed challengers on the League tournament stage. After Harley's scheming and harassment, no one in the group felt much sympathy for him.

And now he could not even fight back. Miserable was the only word for it.
