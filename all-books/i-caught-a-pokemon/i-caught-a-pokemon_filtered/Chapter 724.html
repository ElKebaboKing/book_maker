Chapter 724: Keeping a Straight Face

Three Deoxys flashed through the night, the moonlight lending their bodies a faint silver sheen. In only a few minutes they left LaRousse City behind and landed in the forest clearing.

Everything was as Ethan had left it. No one had attacked the clearing, and no foolish wild Pokemon had wandered in. The only change was that Gardevoir had wrapped his body in psychic power to keep it warm through the night.

She really was thoughtful. It was a detail he hadn't considered himself, yet she had noticed it.

There was no time to dwell on that. He had gone a long way and stayed away a long time. His soul was starting to feel uncomfortable, as though its connection to his body had nearly broken. He hurried to lie above it, and in a moment of disorientation his soul slipped back inside.

When he opened his eyes and rose from the ground, his body felt warm. He didn't seem to have suffered any aftereffects.

Well done! he told Gardevoir with a smile.

Then he turned to the two wild Deoxys. He didn't know their sexes, or even whether Deoxys had sexes. What he did know was that this pair was unusual: they had come from the same meteorite.

Other Deoxys also traveled through space on meteorites, but usually there was only one in each. These two had shared a single rock. That was why the red-cored Deoxys had been so determined to find the blue-cored one in the movie.

The glowing blue presence Ash's group had encountered in the park was the blue-cored Deoxys's consciousness. Its body had been destroyed, leaving only its core, but its formidable psychic power let it move through a limited area around the park. Later, it had befriended Tory, who often withdrew there by himself.

All right, Ethan said. It's over now. You've found each other, and your companion has its body back. It's time for you to leave this place and start your own adventure.

The two couldn't speak his language, and their faces showed no change, but Ethan thought he saw a trace of gratitude in their eyes.

They turned together toward his Deoxys and made a strange sound. It answered them. The three alien Pokemon were communicating in their own way.

Ethan and the others couldn't understand them or read their expressions, so they could only guess what was being said. Gengar leaned toward his ear and whispered, I think they're asking your Deoxys to go with them.

They might be.

Fortunately, the exchange didn't last long. When it ended, Ethan's Deoxys remained beside him. He let out a breath.

He had caught it and helped fulfill its wish, but he hadn't known whether it would choose to leave with the others. An Ultra Ball could never hold a Pokemon of its power if it truly wanted to go. No one could stop it. The possibility had worried him all along.

Now it seemed to have made its choice. It had chosen him.

The heart-to-heart they had shared, and all the time they had spent together, had meant something. Deoxys really had accepted him.

As the wild pair prepared to depart, Ethan cleared his throat and told them he might need their help against a powerful enemy before long. Through Gardevoir's telepathy, he received an answer almost at once.

...We will...help you...

...

News of the nighttime disturbance, especially the defeat of the city's final defense system, spread by the next morning and frightened LaRousse's residents and visiting Trainers. The police responded quickly with an announcement: the Deoxys had entered the city by chance and had soon left. That calmed the immediate panic.

People kept talking about it over meals, though, and footage of the battle rapidly spread to other parts of Hoenn. The Trainers who had actually fought Deoxys and Rayquaza the day before were the exception. They said nothing, as if they wanted to forget the whole experience. The battle had left its mark on them.

With the Deoxys gone, the city of technology had become an ordinary city to Ethan. He didn't leave right away. Instead, he stayed with Ash's group and spent some peaceful time seeing what made LaRousse special.

After another half day, however, it was time for everyone to set out. Ethan had accomplished what he came for and gained plenty from the trip. Ash couldn't say the same.

He had promised Yuko he would help Tory overcome his fear of Pokemon. For the past two days, he had stayed with the boy whenever he could and tried everything he knew. But events hadn't unfolded as they had in the movie, and Ash never got the chance that might have changed things. In the end, Tory could tolerate Pokemon at a certain distance. He still couldn't bear them any closer.

Ash felt as though he had failed him. Ethan shook his head and offered what comfort he could. If you can't do more, you can't. Tory may just need time to grow older. I think he'll learn to manage it himself someday.

Ash was disappointed, but he knew he had done all he could.

At the harbor, they said goodbye to a reluctant Tory. Ethan and Misty watched Ash, May, Brock, and Max board a passenger ship and continue their journey. With the four of them gone, Ethan had no reason to remain either.

It was time to return to Lilycove City and wait for Zinnia. He gave Tory a polite nod. They didn't know each other well enough for much more. After a wave, he and Misty went to the light rail station and took a train away from LaRousse.

The view outside was much the same as it had been on their way in, but their mood had changed.

I miss having everyone around already, Misty said with a sigh. It's fun when we're all talking and laughing together. The time goes so quickly. Now it's just the two of us, and everything feels so quiet.

Ethan understood. Even he felt a sudden emptiness after all that noise and company. That's part of being Trainers. Traveling, challenging Gyms, catching Pokemonthat's what we have to do now. Someday, when we've retired and have time to spare, we can all gather like this whenever we want.

Retired? Misty covered her mouth with a laugh. You're already thinking about retirement at your age? Isn't that a little early?

Age has nothing to do with it. Ethan leaned back to make himself comfortable. If I get tired, I'll stop and rest. I don't mean right now, of course. First I'll challenge a few more regions and win a few more League tournaments.

That doesn't sound so far away. Misty's eyes shone as she looked at him. If you keep going at this pace, I'm sure you'll get there before long!

Then I hope you're right.

As they spoke, the train arrived back in Lilycove City. Ethan stepped off and took out his phone to ask Zinnia where she had reached. He found a message she had sent more than half an hour earlier.

He and Misty had only just boarded the train then. Between finding seats, looking out the window, and chatting, he hadn't noticed the notification. Zinnia's message said she was already in Lilycove, waiting in a forest outside the city.

That was fast. In less than two days, she had crossed most of Hoenn. She was plainly in a hurry. Even riding a Flying-type Pokemon, she must have traveled day and night with scarcely any rest.

I didn't expect her to be this eager, Ethan said. But we've rested enough. Let's go meet her.

They left the station without delay and headed out of Lilycove. From some distance away, they spotted Zinnia leaning against a large tree with her eyes closed. She looked as though she were dozing. She still wore the same outfit as before, but travel had left her dusty and her face a little drawn.

The sound of their footsteps made her open her eyes at once. Their earlier meetings had been reasonably friendly. Now the air between them felt strange, and they stood somewhere between allies and enemies.

Zinnia narrowed her eyes at Ethan for some time. Once he drew near, she said coldly, Follow me. She released Salamence and climbed onto its back.

That told him they had a long journey ahead. Ethan exchanged a glance with Misty, shrugged, and released Dragonite. They mounted together.

Zinnia was already airborne. Her Salamence flew southeast, and Dragonite followed at a distance. Misty sat in front of Ethan and looked back at him.

What did you do to her? she asked with a smile. She looks as though she's angry with you.

Ethan had taken Zinnia's call away from Misty and Ash's group. Misty had only learned afterward who had been on the phone, so she had no idea what they had said. Something unpleasant had clearly happened. Zinnia had helped them before, and although Misty found her odd and had mixed feelings about her, she didn't want Ethan to make an enemy of her.

What could I have done? I just made her angry with a few words. Ethan glanced at Zinnia ahead of them. She's too rigid. If she were more open to compromise, we could work together and both come away with something. There'd be no need for things to be like this.

He meant it. If Zinnia agreed to cooperate, Ethan would take the black Rayquaza at Sky Pillar, but he wouldn't leave her empty-handed. He intended to catch the ordinary Rayquaza they had encountered at LaRousse and give it to her in exchange. They would both end up with a Rayquaza.

Wasn't that what the Draconid people had sought for generations? Yet Zinnia's temperament had closed off that possibility. At least they weren't outright enemies yet. There was still some chance to change things.

The two Pokemon soon left the Hoenn mainland behind and flew toward Sootopolis Island. Flying was far faster than walking or sailing. Only a few hours later, the island came into view. Zinnia didn't land there. She used it as a landmark and turned southwest.

Countless small islands swept past beneath them. After almost another hour, a seemingly deserted island appeared ahead. Zinnia began to descend. Ethan signaled Dragonite to follow while he studied their destination.

It was a small island, no larger than two football fields put together. Apart from a few low hills and yellow sand covering the ground, it was bare.

Why bring us here?

He wondered, but gave no outward sign of it. The two Pokemon landed, their wings scattering sand. Ethan climbed down and looked around. Something was wrong.

The island was so desolate that there wasn't a single wild Pokemon nearby. He saw no bird Pokemon, not even a Water-type in the surrounding sea. That was far from normal.

The more unusual the place seemed, the more Ethan acted as though nothing concerned him. He kept his expression easy and gave away no hint of what he thought. Let Zinnia wonder what he knew.

It worked. Seeing him so calm, she felt some of her earlier doubts fade. Had this man really been to Sky Pillar before? Otherwise, how could he remain so composed at the sight of this place?
