Chapter 827: Eevee: Huh?

May accepted the Eevee Egg from Nicolette, still a little embarrassed by the generous gift.

She did not know what Pokemon would hatch from it. Ethan and Nicolette's family kept the secret, smiling and telling her she would find out when it hatched. The mystery gave May something to look forward to.

Ethan was relieved too. As far as he knew, in May's travels through Kanto, Johto, and later Sinnoh, Squirtle and Eevee would be the only Pokemon she added to her team. With his help, she had now received both. They could travel freely without his needing to worry about missing either opportunity.

That did not mean May would never catch another Pokemon. If they met one that suited her, Ethan would point it out and help even if she did not ask. For now, that was a question for another day.

They thanked the family and left the farm while it was still early afternoon. Their intended port was not far away. After crossing a broad plain and passing a pond, they saw it within a little over an hour: Silver Town.

The town was small but surprisingly lively. Its position between Saffron and Vermilion Cities, with the sea close by, helped its economy. At the same time, many young people had been drawn away to larger places. With fewer residents, growth was difficult.

Ethan was about to head for the docks and buy two boat tickets when he overheard important news: the Silver Town Contest would be held tomorrow.

Huh? They have a Contest here too? May asked, astonished. Silver Town was less than a day's walk from Saffron City. She had not expected another event so close by.

There are exceptions, Ethan said with a smile. I don't know every detail of the Contest circuit, but smaller places hold Contests too. Where else would Harley and all the other Coordinators earn their Ribbons? If only a few big cities hosted them, hardly anyone could collect five in a year. The Grand Festival wouldn't have enough entrants.

Oh, I see. May nodded.

Her lack of experience was understandable. Like Misty had been at her age, she was only ten. Some things did not occur to her until someone explained them.

It did mean a busy schedule for Mr. Contesta and Mr. Sukizo, the Contest judges. They must have barely finished one event before traveling to the next. Lilian, the beautiful host, would be just as busy.

However the schedule worked, May would not pass up the chance to compete. She needed more Ribbons for the Kanto Grand Festival.

They entered the town. Its Contest Hall stood at one edge of it, modest in size. A tall flight of steps and a sloping stretch of grass led up to the many-angled entrance.

May had learned from her close call in Saffron. Though there was plenty of time, she registered at once. Without Harley or Team Rocket interfering, nothing unexpected happened.

After registering and looking over the venue, they checked in at a nearby Pokemon Center. May hurried to the outdoor training ground behind it to rehearse. This time Ethan had time to watch.

He let his own Pokemon out and sat on a bench beside the field, grooming them while looking up now and then to see how May was doing.

May had changed her plan. Squirtle would perform in the first round, and Combusken would battle in the second. The division made good sense. It gave each Pokemon a role, helped both develop, and kept either from feeling overlooked.

May could design and rehearse Squirtle's appeal on her own. Combusken, however, needed a practice opponent, and her small team offered few choices. Ethan saw her hesitate to ask.

He laughed and pointed to his Pokemon. Pick whichever one you like to practice with Combusken.

May was delighted, but a look at the Pokemon around him made her frown. There were only five: Gardevoir, Gengar, Eevee, Dragonite, and Blaziken. She could not decide which to choose, so she turned to Combusken.

You pick, she told it softly. Be careful, though. Ethan's Pokemon are all strong. Don't push yourself too hard.

Combusken nodded and studied the candidates. Gardevoir and Gengar looked friendly enough, yet both gave it a frightening feeling. Gengar in particular seemed to have mischief in its eyes. Combusken looked away almost immediately.

Dragonite wore a kindly smile and even offered Combusken a thumbs-up, as though volunteering. Its powerful presence was unmistakable. Combusken knew it was no match and passed it by too.

That left Blaziken and Eevee. The instant Combusken met Blaziken's gaze, it felt a sharp pressure, almost like a blade. Facing the fully evolved form of its own species, it hardly dared breathe. It hurriedly turned away.

Only Eevee remained. It lay harmlessly on Ethan's lap while he brushed its fur. Combusken gave a small cry, choosing it.

Eevee? Ethan smiled. A sensible choice.

May's Combusken was strong by ordinary standards, but it was far behind most of Ethan's Pokemon. Eevee, being unevolved and so cute, at least looked less intimidating.

Ethan lifted his hands away. Go on, Eevee. Help Combusken practice.

Eevee called back, sprang off his lap, and approached Combusken eagerly.

May wondered how the match would go. Would Ethan's Eevee win easily, or might Combusken have a chance? Combusken was more developed physically, and that could make a difference even against a capable Eevee.

She wanted to watch, but Squirtle's rehearsal mattered more. She gave Combusken a few last instructions and turned to the appeal she was planning. Her first task tomorrow was to pass the performance round. Only then would Combusken get to battle. Helping Squirtle make the strongest possible showing had to come first.

At the start, May managed to watch both Pokemon. As she and Squirtle settled into practice, she could no longer spare the attention. One quick glance showed Combusken and Eevee trading blows without an obvious winner. She returned to Squirtle.

Ethan watched their match closely. Combusken had traveled through a region with May and fought in many battles. It was far from weak. But Eevee trained primarily for combat and had the edge in experience.

Combusken had greater striking power, and their speed was comparable. On those measures it ought to have the advantage. Yet Eevee was already controlling the rhythm, despite the even-looking exchanges. May could not see it yet. Ethan knew Combusken would eventually lose unless something changed.

Defeating Combusken was not the purpose of this practice. It was meant to help Combusken grow.

Combusken, Ethan called, don't chase Eevee. Move toward your twelve o'clock. That's where it'll land.

Combusken hesitated for an instant, then followed his instruction. Pokemon rarely took orders from someone other than their Trainer. But Combusken knew May trusted Ethan, knew his skill, and knew it could not beat Eevee unaided.

Instead of pursuing Eevee, it moved to intercept. A moment later Eevee landed exactly where Ethan had predicted. Combusken instinctively used Double Kick, sending it flying.

Eevee slid backward a long way before it stopped. Once it was safe, it did not attack. It turned to stare at its own Trainer, looking thoroughly confused. Whose side was he on?

Ethan smiled back. What are you looking at? Never seen such a handsome Trainer?

Eevee was about to protest when Combusken came running. It had to turn back and fight. Its brief advantage did not change the overall match; before long, Eevee was gaining the upper hand again.

Now, use Ember! Ethan called.

Combusken had been preparing Scratch. This time it did not hesitate at all. It abandoned the claw attack and sent flames toward Eevee. Eevee had quietly readied a defensive counter and was startled. It leaped away several times to escape the fire.

From a safe distance, Eevee complained to Ethan again. He merely spread his hands. Why are you looking at me? Don't let things outside the battle distract you. Carefulit's coming again.

Eevee turned. Combusken was already charging at it, so it had to resume the fight. Now wary of another instruction from Ethan, Eevee fought cautiously. It no longer moved with its earlier ease. Combusken found openings and recovered from its disadvantage. The practice ended in a draw.

Eevee ran to Ethan and protested at length.

All right, all right. Ethan soothed it with a smile. I gave Combusken a few pointers because I know you're stronger. Remember, this wasn't about deciding a winner. We're helping it improve.

Eevee understood that, but being undermined by its own Trainer had made it feel attacked from both sides. Still, as Ethan said, their practice had only begun. It would have another chance to settle the score.
