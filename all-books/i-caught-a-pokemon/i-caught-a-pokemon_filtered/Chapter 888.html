Chapter 888: Ho-Oh's Blessing

What had Morty seen?

High above him, a beautiful waterfall of raw energy was pouring down, filling the sky over the forest. What was itsome Pokemon's attack? An extreme kind of weather no one had seen before?

Its many colors shone against the night. For a moment, Morty was so struck by its breathtaking beauty that he nearly forgot where he stood.

A panicked voice beside him snapped him back. Damn it! What is that thing?

Morty turned. The man who had questioned him earlier was staring upward, mouth open in disbelief. Only then did Morty grasp the danger they were in.

Whatever that lovely torrent was, they were within its reach. In perhaps a dozen seconds it would fall over them. With that much power behind it, they would be obliterated.

Get out of here! he shouted.

The other Trainers were already moving. They might not have Morty's strength, but the first people to race toward a disturbance had quick minds. By the time he finished the word out, he was the only one still standing there.

They had run fast. Morty spared the thought only an instant before whistling for his Ghost-type Pokemon and retreating toward Ecruteak himself. He had arrived with confidence and was leaving in a scramble.

He did not like it, but no one knew how far the falling energy would spread. The only sensible choice was to put as much distance between themselves and Bell Tower as possible.

As if they needed more trouble, Deoxys's strange copies chased after them. The copies seemed unconcerned about the approaching attack or their own survival. They drove the fleeing Trainers on through the woods.

There they met a second group coming from Ecruteak. These Trainers had noticed the battle later and lacked suitable Pokemon to ride, so they had only just arrived. Before they could learn what was happening, they saw people running for their lives, pursued by unknown figures, beneath that descending torrent of energy. Bewildered, they turned and ran too.

It felt much longer, but barely a dozen seconds had passed since Morty first saw the attack. The Trainers had not even cleared the forest when Ho-Oh met Tri-Bird Assault above Bell Tower.

Golden light flared from Ho-Oh's body. It flew straight into the falling power without a hint of hesitation.

Night seemed to vanish. A dazzling radiance burst from the collision and spread across the sky, turning everything above Ecruteak and for kilometers around a brilliant gold. Then came a continuous, hissing roar as the two forces burned against one another.

Morty glanced back at the sudden daylight. A violent shock wave knocked him off his feet and sent him into a maple tree. Around him, the other Trainers were thrown apart as well. They struggled up from the ground, shaken but alive.

Morty looked toward Bell Tower again. The heart of the light was so bright that his eyes stung. However long he stared, he could make out nothing within it.

Across Ecruteak, people in the streets and in their homes turned toward the glow, stunned and unable to understand what had happened. Kuriu was especially shaken. Somehow, he felt certain that the man he had met at the night market was behind it all. He wanted desperately to know who that man was and what was happening at Bell Tower.

...

On the tower roof, Deoxys and Gardevoir's barriers had kept both Bell Tower and the compound below intact. Ethan, the sisters, and May, sheltered at the center of their protection, were unharmed.

They could see no more through the blinding light than Morty could. But apart from a fierce wind, nothing fell toward them. Ho-Oh must have stopped Tri-Bird Assault.

That was an astonishing feat, though Ethan was not surprised it had managed it. The question was what state Ho-Oh would be in when the light cleared. Untouched? Badly battered? Hurt somewhere in between?

For half a minute the brilliance held. Once the attack's energy was spent, it gradually faded.

The clouded sky had been swept clean, as clear as freshly polished glass. Moonlight and stars appeared again, brighter than ever. Beneath them, Ho-Oh hovered high above the tower, beating its wings slowly.

Its feathers were scorched and blackened. Gone was its earlier splendor, and its body showed the marks of the blow. Yet it still flew steadily. The damage, for all its ugly appearance, had not incapacitated it.

Ethan had to admire its strength. This was only the second time a Pokemon had endured Tri-Bird Assault head-on.

Then Ho-Oh cried out. Light flashed across its body. In the moonlight Ethan watched every injury vanish within seconds, as if it had never been struck at all.

Ho-Oh descended and came to a hover before him, looking just as it had before the battle. Had it used a move like Recover, or drawn upon its extraordinary ability to restore life? Ethan could not tell. After seeing it revive the three beasts before, and now heal itself, he knew how formidable that power was.

He shook his head, a little envious. Even Ho-Oh could hardly use such an ability without limit; it must place a great strain on its body. Still, it was an almost unfair advantage. No wonder so many legends surrounded it.

Articuno, Zapdos, and Moltres followed, breathing hard, and surrounded Ho-Oh again. Perhaps because it was so close to Ethan, or because they needed time to recover from Tri-Bird Assault, they watched without attacking.

The technique had consumed much of their strength, but less than it had on earlier occasions. They had practiced together and grown accustomed to using it. Ethan could see that they still had enough left to fight if they had to.

He waved them back. Their attack had hit Ho-Oh, and he had seen it wounded, if only briefly. That settled his grievance. There was no need to press the fight further. At his signal, the birds withdrew and landed one after another on Bell Tower's roof.

Impressive. Ethan applauded Ho-Oh. You're only the second Pokemon I've seen come through that attack intact.

While he spoke, Miki and the others studied Ho-Oh at close range. It was as beautiful, imposing, and noble as they had imagined. Even the three legendary birds seemed less commanding beside it. There was no denying that.

Ho-Oh glanced at Deoxys. It sensed considerable power in the Pokemon, but Deoxys did not move, so Ho-Oh looked back at Ethan. Its voice sounded in his mind.

Human, the fact that your three Pokemon can perform such an attack shows that you have raised them well. They are strong. You have proved yourself worthy of possessing fire, water, and thunder together.

Fire, water, and thunder? Ethan took a moment to realize Ho-Oh meant Entei, Suicune, and Raikou. People called them the three legendary beasts; Ho-Oh reduced them to the powers they embodied. It had the standing to do so, and Pokemon did not necessarily use the names people gave their group.

Still, whether Ho-Oh approved was beside the point. Ethan had already caught them. Was it going to demand that he let them go?

Your approval isn't what matters, he said evenly. I caught them, and they truly are my Pokemon now.

Ho-Oh turned toward Miki, Kuni, and Zuki. Then why have you entrusted them to these attendants? I can sense the beasts with these three women.

Attendants must have been how Ho-Oh thought of the sisters' family. They had once made offerings to it and had kept its covenant for more than a century.

For all its long life, Ho-Oh seemed not to grasp the human idea of a family, or of husbands and wives sharing responsibility for one another's Pokemon.

I left the beasts here to protect my women and keep them company, Ethan said. If I need them, they'll still come to me. And isn't this for the best? All three remain in Johto, where their legend can continue.

The sisters could not hear Ho-Oh's questions, but Ethan's answer told them roughly what the two were discussing. To be called his women in front of May and their two younger sisters made Miki, Kuni, and Zuki blush. It warmed them too. He had left such precious Pokemon with them because he cared.

Ho-Oh was pleased. Raikou, Suicune, and Entei belonged in Johto, the land where they had been born. Given that they had been caught, remaining here with the sisters was a good outcome.

I understand. Human, your actions have earned my respect, and you have passed my test. Now accept my blessing.

It gave Ethan no chance to refuse. Ho-Oh beat its wings, and a single feather detached and floated toward him.

A blessing? Ethan raised an eyebrow, unsure what Ho-Oh intended, but held out his hand and caught it.

The instant the feather touched his palm, it shone with a soft, intense light and melted into his body.

Ethan had not expected that. Heat flooded him. A vigorous energy surged through his body, and he staggered backward.

Gardevoir caught him with psychic power before he fell. The sisters had not even had time to cry out when Deoxys mistook Ho-Oh's gift for an attack. Its eyes flashed red, and it fired a bolt of energy at Ho-Oh. All three birds cried out and charged again.

Ho-Oh gave a light beat of its wings and climbed above the attacks. It kept rising until it vanished into the sky.

As it left, its voice sounded in Ethan's mind one last time.

This is the power of my life. Bear it, human, and come to understand it.
