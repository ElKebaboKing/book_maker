Chapter 712: Groudon: You're All Right

Team Aqua's members fled in disorder. Not only their speedboats but also both organizations' submarines had been caught in the blast and sunk. Each side had suffered a severe loss. Most Team Aqua members could still ride their Water-type Pokemon and swim well enough to avoid drowning.

Ethan could spare them no attention. He watched Groudon and Kyogre. The orbs were gone, but Maxie and Archie had disappeared too. At least no one was left nearby to interfere with what he had to do.

What I said about the orbs is for later, he told Shelly and Courtney. We can watch for Maxie and Archie when they show up again. Right now, we have to stop Groudon and Kyogre before Rayquaza notices them. They can't keep fighting.

He had reason to worry. Long ago, Rayquaza had stopped a tremendous battle between these two, an event that had helped inspire the Draconid people's reverence for it. Neither Groudon nor Kyogre had undergone Primal Reversion today, so the damage was limited by comparison. But Rayquaza knew their presence. If it sensed them fighting, Ethan doubted it would ignore them.

Shelly agreed. Then this is yours to handle. We can't help much. You've kept Deoxys back all this time for exactly this moment, haven't you?

Her voice was teasing, but she was right. Deoxys had rested through the earlier fighting so it could act unexpectedly when it mattered. Ethan still wanted a better opening before sending it in.

He asked Dragonite to lift off from the water. He, Shelly, and Courtney climbed onto its back. Once they were safely in the air some distance from the island, Ethan took out Deoxys's Ultra Ball and waited.

Dragonite was strong, but carrying three adults made it less nimble. That weight had helped the blast wave throw it into the sea. Holding them aloft at a steady pace was no problem, though. It circled the broken island a few hundred meters up.

Groudon and Kyogre charged each other again. Their wounds and breathing showed that the earlier battle had cost them something, but not much. Left alone, they might fight through the night and into morning without settling anything.

As they collided, Ethan opened the Ultra Ball. Deoxys appeared in the air.

Its psychic senses had followed events even while it was inside the ball. It immediately looked toward the two ancient Pokemon.

See the blue one? Ethan said, nodding toward Kyogre. Focus on it. Help Groudon put it down.

Deoxys turned its head toward him. Courtney, seated in front of Ethan, looked back too. Shelly spoke from behind him. What did Kyogre do to you? I thought you wanted to stop the fight. Why help Groudon?

The easiest way to end a fight isn't to knock them both down, Ethan said. That would take too much effort and might make both of them attack us. Help one beat the other, and the fight ends.

Then why not help Kyogre? Shelly asked. It's a Water type and Groudon is a Ground type. Kyogre already has the advantage.

She did not say that, as a former Team Aqua member, she still felt closer to Kyogre. Courtney likely felt the same attachment to Groudon. Shelly would not press the point in front of her.

Ethan sighed theatrically. I thought you were clever.

What did you say? Shelly's eyebrows rose.

We're in the middle of the sea. Kyogre is called the embodiment of the ocean. If Groudon falls and Kyogre turns on us, it can control the water all around us. That's dangerous. We take Kyogre down first. And there's something more important.

He paused until both women were listening, then smiled. Groudon can't fly.

Shelly blinked. So?

If Kyogre loses and swims away, Groudon can't catch it. Then we leave too. Groudon can rage all it likes, but there'll be nobody left to fight. The battle is over.

Shelly had not expected that answer. It felt odd, yet she could not find a flaw in it. All right. That makes some sense. Groudon ought to thank you.

Below them, the two Pokemon exchanged several more blows. Ethan turned to Deoxys. Attack Forme.

Deoxys changed at once. Its body narrowed, four tentacle-like arms took shape, and three sharp points rose from its head. Without another order, it dived toward Kyogre.

The distance was six or seven hundred meters, but Deoxys crossed it in moments. Energy gathered as it flew. At close range, it fired into Kyogre's back.

Kyogre cried out in pain. Only after the blow did it realize it had been ambushed. Groudon noticed Deoxys at the same moment.

Deoxys had arrived quietly from a Poke Ball, without the earthquakes or tidal waves that announced its opponents. It had kept its distance until the dive, moved fast, and struck while they were occupied with each other. The attack caught Kyogre completely off guard.

Kyogre spun its great body toward the attacker. Deoxys was already watching for a counterattack and slipped aside. It followed with Psychic, striking Kyogre again. A target that large was difficult to miss.

Kyogre realized a simple charge would not catch such an agile opponent and began gathering power for a wider attack. Groudon saw its rival's exposed back and struck with a heavy claw. Kyogre fell from the air onto the water. The power it had gathered scattered before it could use it.

Groudon and Kyogre drew on immense internal energy, but they did not think of combat in the fixed names of moves that Trainers used. Instinct drove them to split earth, change weather, and release power in whatever form the moment demanded. People unfamiliar with them could seldom predict an attack by waiting for a named move.

Shelly and Courtney recognized some of what they saw. Other attacks were unlike anything they knew. They might have understood the difference better after watching Gengar fight with its playful, improvised methods.

Deoxys was an extraterrestrial Pokemon and did not rely on a fixed list of moves either. The energy sphere and barrier it had used before came from its free control of its power. Legendary Pokemon differed from ordinary Pokemon that lived among humans in mind as well as in body.

Before Kyogre could rise, Deoxys closed in again. Groudon suddenly attacked Deoxys instead. It dodged, but lost its chance to strike Kyogre. It remembered Ethan's order and did not retaliate against Groudon. It changed position and continued attacking Kyogre from an angle Groudon could not reach.

Kyogre was exasperated. Deoxys was not even half its size, yet it darted all around, hit hard, and evaded every blow. Groudon remained ready to attack from the other side. Kyogre could not deal with both at once. Why did this new Pokemon attack only Kyogre and show no interest in Groudon?

After a few minutes, frustration won. Kyogre roared, plunged into the sea, and swam away. A battle that might have continued until both ancient Pokemon were exhausted ended abruptly.

Groudon looked almost baffled. It wanted to pursue, but could never match Kyogre's swimming speed. It roared and turned toward Deoxys. Deoxys floated above it, stared coldly for a moment, then flew away too.

Groudon roared after the retreating figure. Deoxys did not turn back.

...

High in the sky, Ethan felt that Deoxys still wanted to fight when it returned to Dragonite's side. He looked down at the furious Groudon below.

Not satisfied? Do you want another round with it?

Deoxys made a sound Ethan could not interpret, but its meaning was clear enough. That brief clash had been little more than a warm-up. It had left only because Ethan asked. A Pokemon that had once challenged Rayquaza and gained the upper hand would recognize the strength of both Groudon and Kyogre.

Don't worry, Ethan said. We're leaving now so we can meet again. Next time, you'll get all the fighting you want.
