Chapter 785: Ethan Advances, Ash Is Eliminated

Ethan left the competitors' tunnel and immediately spotted Ash and the others waiting outside. They applauded as he appeared. Misty had even found a bouquet of flowers to give him.

What's all this? Ethan laughed helplessly. You're making such a fuss, you'd think I'd already won the championship.

Max was at an age when he could not keep his excitement to himself, even with Ash and Morrison standing beside him. Ethan, you were amazing! Nobody could touch you! Two Pokemon, and each one beat three opponents! You should have seen the stands when you won. The crowd practically exploded!

Ash and Morrison looked uncomfortable. Nobody could touch you included them. They knew Ethan might be stronger, but Max did not have to say it in front of them. Morrison was already out, but Ash had reached the final eight and might still face Ethan. Max sounded certain Ash would lose.

Even so, they could not deny Ethan's performance. Neither of them could have achieved that result in his place.

You're exaggerating, Ethan said with a wave and a smile. I wouldn't call myself unbeatable. But at this Conference, I don't think anyone can stop me from taking the title.

Ash and Morrison fell silent. Was that any different from what Max had said? They had expected a little humility. Instead, Ethan had agreed with him.

Morrison glanced at Ash. He had been eliminated and could do nothing about Ethan's confidence; perhaps Ash would challenge it. But Ash only clenched his fists and, unusually for him, said nothing.

Why was he letting that pass? Morrison knew Ash fairly well, but he did not understand what Ethan meant to him.

Had another Trainer announced that he would win the Conference in front of Ash, Ash would have argued. Ethan was both a rival and someone Ash trusted deeply. The impression Ethan had made over their long acquaintance was so strong that Ash believed anyone capable of beating him and taking the championship would have to be Ethan. In his mind, it ought to be Ethan.

By the end of the day, all eight quarterfinalists had been decided. Beyond Ethan and Ash, there were six Trainers the group had neither met on their travels nor had a chance to get to know here.

Though all eight had reached the same stage, they received very different treatment. Reporters swarmed Ethan like sharks catching the scent of blood. Cameras crowded toward his face. The other seven could not find a single interviewer. Ash was standing right beside Ethan, yet the reporters ignored him and pushed him aside.

That's frightening, May said. They were only at the final eight. Why had everyone rushed him already?

Misty and Brock were used to it. They had seen this at both the Silver and Indigo Conferences. It's their job, Misty said with a shrug. Of course they can smell a story. Wait until you see what happens after a final. That's frightening.

At the center of the crowd, Ethan felt the reporters' eager attention more sharply than anyone. He was prepared. He announced that he had already promised exclusive interviews to Lumiose Publishing House and TV Mauville, declined every other request, and let tournament security escort him away.

Those interviews could serve as his shield. TV Mauville's staff could deal with the disappointed reporters from here.

...

The next round's pairings appeared that evening. Every competitor except Ethan felt the weight of the moment. Nobody wanted to become the stepping-stone for someone so strong. Eight Trainers would be divided into four pairs, and one unlucky person had to face him. Even those who avoided him now might meet him in the following round. The danger could only be delayed.

They had reached the final eight, yet some felt as worried about elimination as they had in the qualifiers.

The hall that had once been packed with competitors now held only a handful. Ethan and Ash stood together. The others kept their distance from one another, wary of potential opponents and perhaps afraid of revealing too much.

At precisely seven o'clock, the big screen updated. The tension was so sharp that everyone seemed afraid to breathe. Even Ash swallowed hard and clasped his hands. May, Max, and their friends caught the mood. Morrison was sweating as though he himself had a match ahead.

The display changed to show the pairings. Before Ethan could read it, someone cheered from across the hall. Beside him, Ash's shoulders relaxed, and he let out a long breath.

Ethan looked up. His portrait stood first in the top row. Opposite it was a man not yet twenty, with spiky hair and upturned eyes. Across the hall, the man scratched his head. His face crumpled, and he silently cursed his luck. Why did he have to draw Ethan?

Ethan found Ash's pairing next. Ash would face a blue-haired girl. That result would be interesting to watch.

Back on Earth, some viewers of the anime had said that Ash's loss to Tyson was forced by the plot and that, without Tyson, he would certainly have reached the final four. Ethan had defeated Tyson himself. Now that Ash faced someone else, Ethan would see whether Ash's present strength could carry him through.

...

Sableye is down! Metagross wins! The winner of this match is Ethan!

The announcer's call rose over the cheers of the audience and the applause of Ethan's friends. Ethan had won another clean six-to-zero match. In the day's opening battle, he had again looked untouchable.

He had used four Pokemon. In the first two rounds, he sent out Gardevoir and Gengar and let them have a turn. Both won with ease, giving their opponents almost no chance to answer. Gardevoir simply overpowered its opponent, even through a type disadvantage.

Gengar was more merciless. It toyed with its opponent until the poor Pokemon was exhausted. It even blocked the opposing Trainer's Poke Ball with its power before ending the fight, looking thoroughly satisfied.

Eevee and Metagross handled the remaining four rounds, winning two apiece. Neither produced another three-Pokemon sweep, and they were less showy than Blaziken and Milotic had been, but both performed well.

Overall, today's opponent was at most as strong as Shizuko. He had caused Ethan little trouble. Ethan was through to the final four.

The match had been one-sided, yet the crowd enjoyed it. Calls for Ethan to take the championship only grew louder. He needed to defeat just two more opponents to lift the Ever Grande Conference trophy.

There were four matches scheduled that day. Ash's was next. After his own match, Ethan went directly to the stands and took Ash's seat rather than leave the stadium.

Ash's face was tense and heavy with thought. This was different from how he had felt against Morrison. He had reached the final eight before and challenged for a place in the final four before, but he had never made it. Now he had another chance, and that carried its own pressure.

Win, and he would set a new personal record. Lose, and he would repeat the old result.

From his Trainer's area, Ash looked up at the ceremonial flame. Its light seemed to kindle a fire in his own eyes.

Wait for me, Ethan. I'll beat my opponent and come challenge you.

...

Pikachu is down! The score is now four to five, and Ash is in trouble! All he has left is a badly worn Grovyle. Can he turn this around?

Sweat ran down Ash's cheek beneath the announcer's ringing voice. Pikachu had failed to win again, leaving him in a desperate position. He carried Pikachu back, gritted his teeth, and threw his last Poke Ball. Grovyle appeared, panting.

The announcer was right about its condition. Its strength was nearly spent, and wounds covered its body. It looked as though it might fall without another attack. But it was Ash's last Pokemon.

This opponent had been too difficult. In strength and performance, she held a slight edge over Ash. It felt like facing Morrison at his best, perhaps an even harder fight.

Ash had been under pressure throughout. Glalie and Swellow had each traded themselves for one of her Pokemon. Pikachu, Torkoal, and Corphish had achieved nothing and been knocked out in succession.

Only Grovyle had turned the tide for a while. It had fought far beyond its usual limits, giving perhaps two hundred percent to defeat two Pokemon despite taking severe damage. That had brought the score to where it stood now. With Pikachu down after only one round of rest for Grovyle, it had to return, exhausted, and somehow seek one more win for Ash.

This is bad, Brock said. His eyes were wide, but he could see no route back for Ash.

It is, Ethan agreed, shaking his head. If Grovyle were fresh, perhaps Ash could win. In this condition, one hit might finish it, and his opponent still has two Pokemon she hasn't revealed. Even I can't see a way out.

A Tackle, or even a resisted Water Gun, could be enough. What could Grovyle do with so little left? It had already given everything. If its teammates had been stronger, if Pikachu had fought better, perhaps Ash would not be here. But there was no changing those rounds now.

Ethan had thought Ash might surpass his old result without Tyson in the tournament. It seemed too early for him. Against Morrison, he had barely won even after Morrison gave away two rounds. Against someone still harder to beat, Ash had done well to get this far, but he remained a little inexperienced. His lineup was not strong enough either.

With the Ash of his Kalos, Alola, or Galar years, Ethan thought, this match would have ended in a wide victory. He shook his head and looked back to the field.

Ash's opponent sent out her next Pokemon. Blue light flashed, and Typhlosion appeared before Grovyle.

Everyone who cared about Ash felt their hopes sink. Ash's own eyes dimmed. His last Pokemon could scarcely stand, and now it faced a Fire type strong against it. Ash had not lost his will to fight. He simply understood that he had no way to turn this around.

The referee lowered the flags, and the round began. No miracle came. Typhlosion breathed fire. Grovyle could not even dodge before the flames covered it.

So Ash had lost after all. Ethan shook his head again, sorry for him. When the fire cleared, Grovyle lay motionless. Ash's journey through the Ever Grande Conference was over.
