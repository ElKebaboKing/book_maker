Chapter 872: Truly Strong

Ethan, what do you mean by a Totem Pokemon? May asked.

It's not a term people use around here, Ethan explained. It comes from the distant Alola region. It describes a Pokemon much larger than others of its speciesone you can tell is different at a glance.

I see. May nodded, then asked, Does that mean Totem Pokemon are especially strong?

Ethan shook his head, then nodded.

Apart from their size, people haven't found any difference in their anatomy or moves compared with ordinary Pokemon. In that sense, they're the same. But a larger body can also mean a stronger build and greater physical power. So they probably do have an advantage over others of their kind.

He was not entirely certain of that. Did they really have an advantage?

In the games, numbers determined strength, and Totem Pokemon had the same base stats as ordinary members of their species. But this was a real world. Surely a larger body would bring better physical capabilities.

Still, it was only a guess. He would have to catch one and observe it to know for sure.

Despite choosing his target, Ethan did not make a move. May watched him in puzzlement. He was hardly indecisive. Why was he hesitating now?

What are you thinking about? she asked.

Ethan sighed and gave her a wry smile. Snorlax already eat an enormous amount. If I catch a Totem Snorlax this big, how much food will it go through every day?

So that was it. May glanced back at the Snowman Snorlax.

It must eat more than an ordinary Snorlax, judging by its size. But don't forget my special Pokeblocks. Snorlax love them, and they provide lots of energy!

Even with Pokeblocks, Ethan was unsure how much it would take to feed that enormous body. He would have to find an expert to improve the recipe someday.

There was no point dwelling on it now. He looked up at the Totem Snowman Snorlax again.

Its body looked as though it were covered in snow. It hardly seemed possible that such a strange Snorlax was simply a Normal type. Ethan suspected it might have the Ice type too.

The Snorlax kept tossing white fruit into its mouth, apparently oblivious to the two intruders in front of it. Ethan threw the Poke Ball he was holding onto the ice before it.

Whatever its type, that would not matter against overwhelming strength.

A flash of blue light released his Gardevoir in front of the Snorlax. She had not had a proper battle in a long time. Ethan chose her both to make sure of the result and to let her stretch her abilities and keep her edge.

Gardevoir took a few slow steps forward. Elegant as her movements were, the power she radiated put the Snowman Snorlax on alert. It was impossible to ignore.

The Snorlax lowered its fruit and stared at her with a vacant expression.

Its face was smeared with fruit juice, and its eyes looked half asleep. For a Totem Pokemon, it had remarkably little dignity. One might have thought it had grown so large simply by sitting still and eating all day.

Ethan knew better than to underestimate it. Ash's Snorlax and May's Munchlax were both powerful. Plenty of Trainers had paid dearly for assuming that a lumbering-looking Pokemon could not fight.

Gardevoir approached until she stood before the Snorlax. It scratched its broad belly, then rose to its feet. It might not enjoy fighting, but it would not back down from someone who had come looking for a battle.

The other Snowman Snorlax in the distance seemed not to notice. Even if one of them had, it would likely have tilted its head, watched for a moment, and gone back to what it was doing. That was simply their nature.

Without a word from Ethan, Gardevoir attacked. She raised an arm, and a wave of psychic force surged from her palm toward the Snorlax.

It could not see the psychic power, but it clearly sensed something dangerous coming. With a sweep of its arm, it hurled a branch laden with fruit and snow into the air.

Whether by chance or design, the branch struck the incoming force. It burst apart, scattering snow, bits of fruit, and splinters. The falling debris briefly screened the two Pokemon from each other.

Then came a rapid, pounding rhythm of footsteps. The Snowman Snorlax was charging at Gardevoir!

Its feet moved so fast they almost blurred. In an instant it closed the distance, balled up a huge fist, and punched at her face.

For a species known for its slow pace, its speed was astonishing. It overturned every expectation.

Then Ethan remembered the Snorlax Ash had caught on the Seven Grapefruit Islands. Driven by its appetite for delicious grapefruit, it had taught itself the butterfly stroke and could swim faster than a motorboat.

In a large population, one or two exceptional individuals were bound to turn up. This Totem Snowman Snorlax was remarkable for more than its size. Ethan's interest deepened.

Fast as it was, though, speed alone would not be enough to land a hit on Gardevoir.

Its fist came within a dozen centimeters of her face. It looked certain to connect a moment later, but in battle that small gap could be impossible to cross.

A surge of psychic power expanded outward from Gardevoir. The three-meter-tall Snorlax, heavy as it was, flew backward under the force. After little more than a meter, its weight brought it crashing down.

Thud!

It struck the ice hard enough to leave a hollow. Yet as it fell, it twisted and sprang straight back to its feet.

What an agile fat Pokemon.

Ethan and May appeared to have the same thought. Gone was the drowsy look the Snorlax had worn over its fruit. Once the battle began, its expression had turned serious.

It closed in again and swung another punch at Gardevoir. This one was faster, and a cloud of cold air coiled around its fist. It looked far more dangerous.

Ice Punch. Ethan had been right that it could at least use Ice-type moves, though that did not prove it was an Ice type itself.

At this distance the blow was harder to evade, but Gardevoir had no trouble. She used Teleport, disappearing from the Snorlax's path and reappearing some distance away.

She looked at its broad back and began to raise her hand. Before she could attack, the Snorlax's sharp battle instincts showed themselves again.

It whipped around and fired an Ice Beam from its mouth straight at her. The response was decisive and precisely aimed, coming almost the instant she appeared.

The Snorlax plainly had considerable experience. Ethan was delighted. A Pokemon already this strong and seasoned would need far less time to develop after capture. With some training and guidance, it could become a top-level fighter.

What a surprise, Ethan said with a chuckle. Then he shook his head. But I think it still underestimates you. Gardevoir, show it what you can do!

Gardevoir did not dodge. She raised one arm toward the Ice Beam, and a huge ball of psychic energy formed in front of her. It shot forward to meet the attack.

So much power was packed into the ball that it seemed almost solid. It held together against the Ice Beam, parted the cold stream, and struck the Snowman Snorlax. The impact sent it staggering backward and down.

It rubbed its head and got back to its feet almost at once.

May drew in a breath. That looked painful. How can it take a hit like that and stand right back up? Just how tough is it?

Very tough, Ethan agreed. But Snorlax are known for their stamina and special defense. It could probably take several more.

As they spoke, Gardevoir attacked again and landed another hit. The Snorlax staggered, then tried to counter with its remarkable speed, only for Gardevoir to evade it easily.

Her effortless performance might have made the Snowman Snorlax look less formidable than it was. Strength, however, was relative. Even Ethan's Milotic, Metagross, or Eevee would have had a difficult fight against it. An ordinary Trainer's Pokemon might not have been its match at all.

It could hit hard, endure punishment, move quickly, and fight with experience. Fortunately for Ethan, it had never been trained by a human. Otherwise it would be even harder to defeat.

He really had found a treasure.

On the battlefield, the Snowman Snorlax seemed to realize it was in danger. It prepared one last attempt.

It raised both hands toward the sky. Clouds gathered over what had been a clear day, and a fierce wind brought snow with it.

Blizzard! May cried.

The snowfall thickened until it stung their eyes. The wind made it difficult to stand. Ethan and May retreated several dozen paces and sheltered behind a few ice trees.

If the weather was this violent at the edge of the fight, it was worse where Gardevoir stood.

They could no longer see the battlefield, but Ethan remained aware of her condition through their telepathic bond. The snow battered Gardevoir like driving rain.

She formed a psychic shield around herself, keeping both the snow and wind at bay.

Whoosh! Crackle!

The Blizzard pounded against the shield. Beyond it, Gardevoir could see less than a meter. Even her senses could not locate the Snowman Snorlax. The wind and snow drowned out its running footsteps too.

Ethan and Gardevoir both knew this would be its final strike. Where it would come from, they could only wait to find out.

They did not have to wait long.

A few seconds later, a huge, round figure appeared behind Gardevoir in the snow. The Snorlax rushed close, leaped high, and brought a fist down toward her from above.

As before, blue-white frost swirled around the punch. Its power was impossible to dismiss.

Just as the fist was about to land, Gardevoir spun around.
