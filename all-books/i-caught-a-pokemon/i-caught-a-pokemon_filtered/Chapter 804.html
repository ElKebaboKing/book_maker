Chapter 804: A Humiliating Lesson

Tucker could tell that Ethan seemed hostile, but he did not consider why. Nor did he take it seriously.

After all, Tucker had publicized the match without even informing Ethan. He was self-important enough to make that decision and unlikely to consider anyone else's feelings. In his own mind, he had done nothing wrong.

Protesting to someone like that would accomplish nothing. He might even take it as weakness. Ethan had chosen to answer him in battle.

Still, Ethan's refusal to engage gave Tucker the uneasy feeling that the occasion was slipping out of his control. He studied Ethan while maintaining his smile. This must be your first visit to the Battle Dome. Why don't I explain the rules?

The rules were beside the point. With a huge crowd and television cameras watching, Tucker wanted another chance to display his charm and promote the Dome.

He was only halfway through his sentence when Ethan raised a hand to stop him.

No need, Ethan said evenly. I checked them beforehand. Unless you've changed the rules especially for me, let's begin.

It was a blunt response in front of so many people. Tucker's expression flickered. With the crowd and cameras on him, all he could do was give a short laugh and look hard at Ethan.

Arrogant, wasn't he? Let's see if he could keep that attitude once the battle began.

The host sensed the awkwardness and quickly took over before the audience noticed. Our challenger has clearly done his homework! That's how well known the Battle Dome is. Now let's see what theme Mr. Tucker has chosen for today's match!

As Ethan had seen the previous day, Tucker always sent out his two Pokemon first, regardless of his opponent's strength or team. He would then give the pair a dramatic theme to make his approach look special. The gimmick might work against an ordinary Trainer, but using it against Ethan showed too much confidence.

Before the watching crowd, Tucker tossed two Poke Balls. Twin flashes of blue light revealed Salamence and Charizard.

Fire and dragon soaring through the sky! Today's theme is the Roar of Twin Dragons!

Tucker struck a stylish pose. His fans shrieked at the sight of his Pokemon. They knew Salamence and Charizard were his two strongest partners. Both looked larger and stronger than the Pokemon he had used yesterday.

If Tucker had brought out his best, his supporters expected a spectacular match. Perhaps he could even bring the champion down.

They had always seen Tucker as immensely powerful. In their minds, only his commitment to the Dome had kept him from challenging the Indigo Conference himself. Had he entered it, the championship would surely have been his. Now they were eager to see how he would defeat Ethan.

Tucker!

Go, Tucker! Show him what you can do!

From the opposite Trainer's area, Ethan heard the shouting and looked at Tucker's Pokemon. He had only one thought: Finally, the opening act is over.

Of all the opponents he had faced, whether in official Gyms or private venues, Tucker had the most drawn-out introduction. He had turned an ordinary battle into a time-consuming spectacle. At least they could finally get to the point.

Ethan had already decided on his pair. He would use them no matter what Tucker sent out.

A Poke Ball floated from his pouch and opened by itself. Gardevoir appeared in a flash of blue light. At the same time, Gengar rose from the shadow at Ethan's feet with a wicked chuckle and hovered beside her.

Anyone familiar with Ethan's team knew what it meant when he chose these two. Either the opponent was dangerous enough to demand his full attention, or Ethan had grown so fed up that he wanted to end the battle without mercy.

Tucker fell into the second category.

He had no idea what he was facing. He was still considering the type matchup. Salamence was Dragon/Flying; Charizard was Fire/Flying. Against Gengar and Gardevoir, the only significant issue he saw was that Dragon-type moves would not affect a Fairy type.

Otherwise, it seemed like a fair contest. Neither of his Pokemon would suffer much from a type disadvantage. They could show what they could do. Tucker believed that put him on even footing with Ethan, and victory no longer looked difficult.

Of everyone in the Dome, only Misty understood what Tucker was about to face.

Watching the screen from her seat in the lounge, she smiled at May. Brother Ethan really is fed up with him. This is just a regular challenge, and he actually sent out Gardevoir and Gengar!

May knew Ethan was strong and had watched him become champion. But she had seen little of these two Pokemon. She understood they were his true aces, yet he rarely called on them.

Are they really that strong? she asked.

Of course. Misty fixed her eyes on the screen. Watch. This won't take long.

...

At the referee's signal, the battle began. On Tucker's command, Charizard and Salamence charged at Ethan's Pokemon.

Ethan lowered his gaze. In a voice barely loud enough for himself to hear, he said, Don't let them live.

Gardevoir and Gengar both received the order. Gardevoir's mind was linked to Ethan's. If he felt insulted, she felt it too, and she would not allow it. Gengar was a Ghost-type that delighted in playing tricks on others. It certainly would not stand by while someone mistreated its Trainer.

Neither had any reason to hold back.

Tucker had barely sent his Pokemon forward. He was still smiling when two blurs flashed across the field. In the next instant, Charizard and Salamence fell at his feet.

What?

His smile froze. He looked down. Both Pokemon were struggling to rise.

How was that possible? The battle had just begun. They had only just taken flight. How had they been thrown back already?

The spectators were just as confused. They turned to one another, asking what had happened. The host reacted first and ordered the footage replayed on the big screens.

Only in slow motion could anyone see it. Charizard and Salamence had rushed toward their opponents. Almost simultaneously, Gardevoir and Gengar had raised their hands. Their powers struck Tucker's Pokemon like two enormous invisible hammers, hurling them backward even faster than they had charged.

It had all happened in a flash. Only the high-speed cameras had captured it clearly.

Once they saw the replay, the audience understood. But new questions replaced their confusion. This was not the fight they had imagined. Hadn't they been promised an evenly matched contest? How had Tucker's Pokemon been sent flying by a single blow?

Even his fans stood stunned. Their cheering stopped.

Tucker's face changed color. He had never imagined his best Pokemon could be repelled so easily. These were his aces. When had anyone knocked them down with a single attack?

Fortunately for him, they were injured but could still fight. Even so, fear crept in. He might have underestimated this young man. Sweat gathered on his forehead.

He wanted to tell his Pokemon to be careful, but Ethan gave him no chance.

As soon as Charizard and Salamence struggled to their feet, Gardevoir and Gengar attacked again. With effortless movements, they knocked both Pokemon to the ground.

It was over.

The battle had lasted only a dozen or so seconds. Tucker's Pokemon had not managed an attack. Gengar and Gardevoir had defeated them with ease.

Even so, Ethan thought their performance deserved applause. Tucker's aces could certainly take more punishment than the Pokemon fielded by Team Magma and Team Aqua. Gardevoir and Gengar had needed two attacks to finish them.

The fallen Charizard and Salamence drew a roar of disbelief from the crowd. What kind of battle had that been? It had ended almost as soon as it began. Was that all a Frontier Brain could do? Even a fight against wild Pokemon might have lasted longer.

Shock gave way to suspicion. Ignoring Tucker's wide eyes and ashen face, the spectators began shouting. The publicity he had sought was turning against him.

His image changed completely in an instant.

For Ethan, the match had served its purpose. Tucker had wanted attention, and Ethan had given it to him. Tucker had hoped to show his face to all of Kanto; Ethan had exposed him to ridicule instead.

Tucker had assumed any outcome would bring him more followers. Ethan had answered with a humiliating defeat. Everyone watching across Kanto had seen how easily Tucker fell. They would remember him, and this match, as a joke.

That was the consequence of using Ethan for his own ends.

Ethan recalled his Pokemon, gave Tucker one cool glance, and walked back toward the lounge without another word.

Ordinarily, Tucker would present him with the Symbol after losing. In this situation, Ethan doubted the man was in any mood to do it. Tucker's immediate problem was finding some way to salvage the disastrous broadcast.

You love to perform, don't you? Then let's see whether you can turn the audience's opinion around this time.

The staff could bring Ethan his Symbol later.

He opened the lounge door and found Misty smiling at him.

Well? Feeling better?

She knew exactly why Ethan had treated Tucker that way. She felt no sympathy for him. He had brought it on himself.

A little, Ethan said with a shake of his head. It was a small punishment. But I didn't expect his Pokemon to go down so easily. I thought we'd have a few more exchanges.

That's because your Gardevoir and Gengar are too strong, Misty said, laughing. Don't forget who you are now. You're the champion of three regions. How could people like him stand against your strongest Pokemon?

That was Misty. Few others would have said it quite that way. It was one reason Ethan liked traveling with her. She made the journey easy, pleasant, and fun, and she knew how to help her companions when someone had upset them.

Unfortunately, with the Battle Dome challenge behind them, their next destination was Cerulean City.
