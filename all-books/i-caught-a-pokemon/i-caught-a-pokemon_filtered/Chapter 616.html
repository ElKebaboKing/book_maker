Chapter 616: The Battle at Lavaridge Gym

Torkoal's shell had protected it from injury, but Stone Edge still knocked the withdrawn Pokemon into the air.

Ethan knew that Torkoal was dangerous even with its head and limbs tucked away. Its hard shell guarded it, and it could use Rapid Spin from that position. Getting close would be a mistake.

Rather than order Tyranitar to follow up, he called, Tyranitar, use Sandstorm!

Billowing yellow sand came from nowhere and swept across most of the battlefield, hiding Tyranitar within it. Ethan had used this tactic before and knew it well.

Flannery lost sight of her target. Torkoal landed safely, but Tyranitar did not reappear. After waiting a while, she had no choice but to make Torkoal emerge from its shell.

Torkoal, use Flamethrower over there!

She dared not rush into the sandstorm, so she tried to force Tyranitar out with an attack from a distance. The field was large, though, and all she could do was aim at the place Tyranitar had last stood. Ethan had anticipated that. As soon as the sand concealed Tyranitar, he had told it to change position.

Flames poured into the storm, lighting a broad patch of sand red. When the fire died away, there was still no movement inside. The attack had missed.

Flannery ordered Torkoal to fire again, with the same result.

Tch. At this rate, she wouldn't see her opponent unless she got closer. She glanced across at Ethan, gritted her teeth, and sent Torkoal into the sandstorm.

As the Gym Leader being challenged, she could have waited patiently for Ethan to attack. She could even have waited until the storm passed. But she lacked the experience to recognize that advantage and was still treating the match like an ordinary battle between Trainers. Defense didn't suit her personality, either. She pressed forward instead.

Torkoal's thick shell protected most of its body. Only its head and limbs were exposed to the storm, and its excellent defenses made the sand's damage little more than a nuisance. Even so, Flannery had deliberately sent her Pokemon into danger. Her recklessness was about to cost her.

With both Pokemon hidden in the storm, neither Trainer could see exactly what was happening or give precise directions. Inside it, Tyranitar and Torkoal would have to fight on their own judgment.

Torkoal was one of Flannery's aces, but she herself had not traveled widely. Tyranitar had competed in a Pokemon League tournament and fought in many major battles. It had a slight edge in strength and experience, with the terrain and surrounding sandstorm firmly in its favor.

Torkoal plodded into the sand and vanished. Before long, orange flashes flickered within the storm, followed by several of Tyranitar's roars. The two Pokemon had clearly met, though no one outside could tell which had the upper hand.

Ethan listened calmly. Flannery was anything but calm. She craned her neck and cupped a hand around her eyes, trying in vain to glimpse what was happening. Her face betrayed her anxiety.

The ground shook inside the storm. Even at a distance, Flannery felt the tremor. Tyranitar must have used a Ground-type move. More worried than ever, she shouted, Torkoal, get out of there!

Her command disappeared into the storm without a response. A bad feeling settled over her.

After another anxious wait, the sounds of battle ceased. The sand slowly thinned, and at last she could see the field again.

Tyranitar was standing in front of a deep fracture in the ground. Torkoal was wedged in the crack, apparently unconscious.

Torkoal!

Flannery stared in disbelief. She knew her own Pokemon's strength better than anyone; she hadn't imagined it would lose like this. She studied Ethan again and raised her estimate of him. He was incredibly strongperhaps every bit as strong as her grandfather!

She recalled Torkoal and took out her second Poke Ball.

Here's my second Pokemon. Go, Magmortar!

A red-and-yellow Pokemon appeared on the field, its vivid body seeming to burn with flames. This was the Fire-type Pokemon Flannery had said was as strong as Torkoal.

Compared with its earlier form, Magmar, Magmortar was bulkier. The markings all over its body looked like fire, its breath seemed to burn, and its arms ended in huge cannonlike tubes.

They had seen Magmar many times on their travels, but Misty had never encountered a Magmortar. Curious, she pointed her Pokedex at it.

Beep. Magmortar, the Blast Pokemon. Fire type. Magmortar usually live near volcanic craters. It is said that only one pair lives at each volcano. Life near a crater makes its body increasingly like a volcano, with organs resembling magma chambers. It fires fireballs from its tubular arms at temperatures reaching two thousand degrees Celsius, hot enough to melt an iron pot in an instant. Its body may glow faintly white with heat, and repeated firing can even cause its wrists to melt slightly.

The description alone made clear how formidable this Pokemon was.

Ethan called Tyranitar back to him and patted its armored body in encouragement before returning it to its ball. Then he took out a second Poke Ball.

Anyone who knew Ethan knew that, except in special circumstances, he rarely made the same Pokemon fight consecutive rounds. Misty thought nothing of the switch, but Flannery was surprised. Tyranitar looked so strong and had suffered only minor burns. It could surely keep fighting. Had Ethan forgotten that Magmortar was also one of her strongest Pokemon?

Her doubts lasted only until Ethan threw the ball. Dragonite emerged in midair, and she sensed the same imposing strength she had felt from Tyranitar.

So he had good reason to make the switch! Naturally, she took both Dragonite and Tyranitar to be his ace Pokemon.

It's been ages since I faced anyone as strong as you, Flannery said, one hand on her hip. I'm completely fired up now. I'm going all out to beat your strongest Pokemon!

She thinks Tyranitar and Dragonite are my aces, Ethan realized. He smiled but didn't correct her. Telling her that they belonged to his second tier would probably crush her confidence.

The field was even more battered after the first round, though neither Trainer's resolve had suffered. For Magmortar, which had to move on the ground, the broken terrain would make things harder.

Flannery called, The second battle begins now!

The moment she finished speaking, Magmortar attacked on its own. It lifted its cannonlike arms and launched clusters of fireballs from both barrels at Dragonite in the air.

Dragonite twisted sideways. Two fireballs about the size of basketballs brushed past it. A fraction slower, and they would have hit.

It steadied itself and answered with Dragon Rage, turning the exchange into a contest of ranged attacks.

Neither Ethan nor Flannery had ordered those opening moves. Their Pokemon had reacted for themselves. Waiting for a command could cost a precious moment, and both were experienced enough to recognize an opening and act on it.

Of course, Trainers allowed that freedom only to Pokemon they could trust. If Misty's Psyduckor another Pokemon that regularly ignored ordersacted on its own, it would probably earn a shouted reprimand instead.

After that quick exchange, the Trainers took control again. At Flannery's order, Magmortar sent out another burst of flame to cancel Dragon Rage. Ethan commanded Dragonite to dive toward it with Dragon Rush.

Magmortar's attacks were powerful, and its two arm cannons let it fire from both sides. Trading shots at a distance would put Dragonite at a disadvantage. Closing in was the wiser choice.

Dragonite was fast, and Dragon Rush drove it faster still. Radiating a fierce, almost murderous pressure, it charged straight at Magmortar.

Flannery welcomed the challenge. She knew Magmortar couldn't dodge, so she chose to meet Dragonite head-on.

Use Flame Wheel!

Fire surged over Magmortar's body. Instead of retreating, it ran toward Dragonite. Moments later they collided with a tremendous bang. They pressed against each other, glaring and gritting their teeth.

Both had been hurt, but neither was willing to give ground. Dragonite was taller and heavier; Magmortar's searing skin made the contest painful all the same. After only a short struggle, Dragonite was beginning to suffer.

Ethan's next command came in time. Dragonite, use Aqua Tail!

Dragonite beat its small wings and lifted into the air. Water wrapped around its great tail, and it turned to whip the tail into Magmortar.

Flannery refused to yield. Magmortar, Fire Punch!

Dragonite's surging tail struck Magmortar, while one of Magmortar's blazing cannon arms hit Dragonite. The Water-type blow made Magmortar recoil, its expression changing as it staggered back several steps.

Dragonite was hurting too. After two Fire-type attacks in succession, it had the bad luck to be burned. Both Pokemon had taken damage: Dragonite now faced a lingering status condition, while Magmortar had suffered a super-effective hit for double damage. In that respect, the contest seemed even again.

Flannery found herself in a difficult position. Where had this man come from? How could he be so strong? But she had no time to dwell on it. The battle continued.

Magmortar and Dragonite clashed again. Perhaps the pain had made them angry, for each threw itself into the fight with its attention fixed solely on the other. Moves flew one after another. Their reckless exchange quickly reached a fever pitch.

As their injuries mounted, both Pokemon tired rapidly. In the end, Dragonite proved the stronger fighter. Another Aqua Tail knocked Magmortar to the ground.

Torkoal's defeat had happened out of sight in the sandstorm. This time Misty had been able to watch every thrilling exchange, and both Trainers were satisfied with how their Pokemon had performed. Flannery's only disappointment was that she had lost.

Even that could not dampen her excitement. It had been an intense match between powerful partners, and her admiration for Ethan had only grown.

You're amazing! Stars seemed to sparkle in her eyes. She looked delighted rather than dejected. Apart from my grandpa, you're the strongest person I've ever met! Come on, let's have the third battle. I can't wait!
