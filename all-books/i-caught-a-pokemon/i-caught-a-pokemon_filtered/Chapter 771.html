Chapter 771: A Confident Man Indeed

Tyson recalled his unconscious Metagross and considered his next choice. He was reaching for a Poke Ball when Ethan recalled Dragonite too.

Huh? Ethan was switching again?

For the second time in this match, Ethan had withdrawn a Pokemon while he held the advantage. Tyson did not know why, but after a moment's hesitation he returned the ball in his hand to his belt and chose another.

His first choice had been his best answer to Dragonite among the Pokemon he had brought. With Dragonite gone, there was no point planning around it. He had to prepare for an unknown opponent instead.

At the referee's signal, they released their Pokemon together. Two flashes of blue light revealed the teams for the third exchange: Ethan's Scizor and Tyson's Shiftry.

Ash and his friends knew Scizor well. They gave it a glance, then turned their attention to Shiftry.

Most of its body was brown. A long, thick white mane hid much of its face. It had pointed ears, a narrow nose, a wide mouth with tightly set teeth, and cloudy yellow eyes. It looked rather like a little old man with long white hair and wooden sandals.

Ash took out his Pokedex and aimed it at the unfamiliar Pokemon. Misty, May, and the others leaned in to hear.

Shiftry, the Wicked Pokemon. Grass and Dark type. A mysterious Pokemon said to live quietly deep in forests undisturbed by outsiders. One legend claims it was once a bird Pokemon whose beak and wings were taken away as divine punishment for wrongdoing. It can read an opponent's thoughts and move first. Said to appear on cold winds, it has long been feared as a guardian of the forest.

Before Ash put the Pokedex away, Morrison asked cautiously, You're not going to tell me Ethan's certain to win this one too, are you?

To Morrison's surprise, Ash shook his head. I don't know. Ethan's Scizor is strong, but that description makes Shiftry sound strong too. I can't say which one will win.

A Pokemon said to have received divine punishment certainly sounded mysterious and dangerous. The others nodded. Then Misty snorted.

Ethan's Pokemon will win, of course.

Everyone turned to her. No one in the stadium believed in Ethan more unconditionally than Misty did. She ignored their looks and lifted her chin.

If one of the Pokemon he's caught recently lost, I'd believe it. But a partner that's traveled with him for so long? I don't believe that.

Her argument sounded reasonable enough that everyone looked back at Ash. He reddened. On the rare occasion he offered an opinion, Misty had contradicted him in front of them all. She had given him no room to save face. Then again, this was Misty.

Still, with Morrison watching, Ash wanted to stand his ground. Scizor isn't one of Ethan's very first Pokemon. It can't be as strong as Blastoise. I think Shiftry has a better chance in this round.

Brock rubbed his chin. He wanted to back Ash up, if only to help him win an argument with Misty for once, but he could not. Scizor is Bug and Steel. Shiftry is Grass and Dark. Bug-type moves will hit Shiftry for four times the usual damage, while Shiftry's attacks face resistance. This matchup is rough for Shiftry.

Huh? Ash's face changed. He had forgotten about type matchups. He usually charged straight into battle without worrying much about them, and he had made the same mistake while judging this fight.

Well... I mean... He searched for another reason to favor Shiftry, but Ethan rarely lost even with a disadvantage, let alone an advantage like this. Across from him, Morrison waggled his eyebrows. Ash could hardly retreat now.

May sighed at the little spectacle unfolding beside her. Early in her travels, she had struggled with this sort of bickering. These days she could tune it out. She and Max turned back to the field.

The third exchange had begun. Ethan's strength was clear to the spectators, but Tyson's earlier performance had impressed them too. Even if Tyson was weaker, they expected him to force Ethan to fight hard. Perhaps this round would be as close as the last until a sudden decisive moment.

They were wrong.

Ethan gave them a lesson in how thoroughly a type advantage could decide a battle.

Shiftry lived up to both its name and its Wicked Pokemon classification. It fought with tricks and feints, making constant small movements to mislead Scizor. Even without Tyson's commands, it tried one deception after another.

Ethan saw through most of them. Scizor followed his orders without wavering, ignoring even openings that looked easy to exploit. Its stubborn discipline robbed Shiftry's tricks of their effect. If Scizor refused every bait, what could Shiftry do?

A few close-range tricks escaped Ethan's notice. They happened too quickly, too subtly, or outside his line of sight. Yet even when they worked, they did little damage against Scizor's steel body.

Under Scizor's attacks, Shiftry fled from one cluster of rocks to another across the pool. The crowd was startled. Tyson looked nothing like the Trainer who had fought Dragonite so fiercely. Only Tyson knew how little room he had to act.

Blastoise and Dragonite had been difficult enough. Scizor was worsenot necessarily because it was stronger than they were, but because Shiftry could not get through its defense. The type matchup had left Tyson with no answer. Like Ethan in the first two exchanges, he had gambled on a pairing and lost.

I should have kept the first Poke Ball I chose, he thought.

With his team already reduced, another switch hardly seemed practical. If he exposed yet another Pokemon and it still could not beat Scizor, he would gain nothing. At the very least, Shiftry needed to take Scizor down with it.

Tyson glanced at the pool and made his decision. He abandoned his evasive approach and ordered Shiftry to close in, hoping to knock Scizor into the water. If that worked, Scizor's weight might trap it in the pool as Metagross had been trapped. Even if Shiftry fainted too, Tyson could accept the trade.

It was the only plan he could see. He had to try.

Ethan quickly recognized the change. It could reverse the battle if Tyson pulled it off, but Ethan was not about to give him that chance. As Shiftry approached, it would have to endure Scizor's attacks.

Shiftry tried several times. It never managed to knock Scizor into the pool and came close to being knocked out itself. Tyson's heart sank further. The idea had sounded much easier than it was.

He was about to tell Shiftry to be more careful when Scizor found an opening first. As they passed each other again, Scizor ignored Shiftry's attack and struck it squarely with X-Scissor. Shiftry flew into the water.

Splash.

It sank for a moment. When it surfaced, its eyes had rolled back and it lay motionless.

The referee raised a flag. Shiftry is unable to battle! Scizor wins!

The applause was subdued. Dragonite and Metagross had raised the crowd's hopes for another thrilling exchange. Instead they had watched a plain, one-sided victory. Many felt Tyson should have done better.

They had not stood where Tyson stood. They could not feel how helpless Ethan made him. He was like a mountain across Tyson's path. Tyson had prepared himself after the previous loss, but this battle flattened his spirits again.

Another defeat. This time he had barely fought back at all.

He wanted to blame the type disadvantage. Ethan, though, had won the first two exchanges despite unfavorable matchups. The excuse rang hollow. Tyson had simply been outmatched.

It was also true that most of Tyson's Pokemon fought best on land. The Water Field limited them. If only he could change it now. But the field would not change until either side had lost three Pokemon. Beating three of Ethan's Pokemon looked extraordinarily difficult, and Tyson could not bear the thought of losing one more of his own.

After recalling Shiftry, he sent Sceptile out again. It was the only Pokemon he could use to search for an opening.

He watched Ethan withdraw Scizor and reach for yet another Poke Ball. His heart rose into his throat. Blastoise was the opponent he least wanted to see again. When a different Pokemon appeared in the blue light, he exhaledthen tensed once more.

He had not known much about Ethan before this match, but he understood one thing now: this man had tremendous confidence in himself. What other Trainer would recall a Pokemon after an easy win, then send out a new one without regard for the opponent's type?

Now Sceptile was standing on the field, and Ethan had chosen Jolteon.

Anyone with even a little battle knowledge knew that Grass types resisted Electric-type attacks. Jolteon's moves would do only half their usual damage. Tyson could not see a reason to choose it over other Pokemon except Ethan's confidence.

Perhaps Sceptile's body would still be wet from its earlier fall into the pool, allowing an Electric attack to produce an unexpected effect. But Ethan did not look as though he was relying on that. He had made his choice regardless of whether Tyson sent Sceptile back into the water.
