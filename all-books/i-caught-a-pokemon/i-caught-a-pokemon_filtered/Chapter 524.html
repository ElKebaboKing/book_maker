Chapter 524: The Water Race

More than thirty competitors surged forward almost as one.

Excited cheers rose around them, a confused, buzzing din. Ethan was wholly focused on the race and shut it all out.

With so many racers packed ahead, Blastoise had no room to use its full strength. For the moment, it stayed among the other Pokemon at a moderate pace.

That changed at a sharp ninety-degree turn. Some Pokemon could not steer well enough, while others had charged too hard to take the corner at speed. Their Trainers had to slow down, breaking up the formation and finally opening a gap in the solid wall ahead.

Blastoise accelerated at once. Its large body pushed through the other Pokemon, carrying Ethan past the middle group and up toward the front.

Ash and Misty followed close behind, their Pokemon agile enough to join the leaders.

Ash was racing with Totodile, and Misty with Corsola. Both were small. Once Blastoise had opened a path, they could slip through it easily.

Staff members waited at every turn along the waterways to point the racers in the right direction. No one had to worry about taking a wrong canal.

Spectators lined the banks throughout the course. Their numbers showed how popular the water race was.

The Pokemon swam hard. After another two or three turns, only a handful of competitors remained in the lead group, including Ethan, Ash, and Misty.

Only one racer was still ahead of them: Ross, the favorite, with his Wailmer.

As the reigning champion, Ross had started from an excellent position. With no one blocking him, Wailmer had reached full speed and opened a large gap over the racers behind.

That might seem unfair, but someone had to stand in front. Giving the place to last year's winner was the organizers' way of showing him respect.

Ethan's board cut through the canal, throwing spray to either side.

Ash and Misty kept pace nearby, and the gap between Ethan and Ross steadily narrowed.

When the course straightened again, all three accelerated and drew almost level with Ross.

The four raced abreast. Ethan and the others had speed on their side, but Ross was a local who knew these waterways intimately. He knew where to turn and where to slow down. Even when they briefly passed him, he would catch them at the next corner.

In the second half of the race, none of them held back. They pushed into an all-out sprint and left the next group farther and farther behind.

Then Ash had a mishap.

As they rounded a turn at high speed, Totodile failed to slow in time and towed Ash straight into a wall.

Ash tumbled into the water. A delay like that could ruin a race. By the time he climbed back up, several racers from the second group had passed him, and Ethan and the others were out of sight.

Ethan thought Ash would have little hope of catching up. To his astonishment, only a few turns later, Totodile and Ash's board were right behind them again!

Their speed was unbelievablealmost as though they were flying.

No, perhaps Totodile really was flying.

Ethan looked closely. Totodile was not swimming at all. It was standing on the water and running, its little legs pumping so quickly that sometimes they did not touch the surface.

It looked as though something invisible above the canal was pulling Totodile along.

Ethan looked overhead. At first he saw nothing, but when the sun struck at just the right angle, he caught the faint outline of something large holding the rope behind Totodile.

He understood and gave a helpless smile. Just as one would expect for the animated series' chosen hero, Ash had already caught the attention of Latias and Latios.

Which one was helping him? Latias or Latios?

Judging by their personalities, it had to be the younger sister, Latias.

The rest of the course seemed to pass in a flash. Around another turn, they returned to the main canal. The finish line lay just ahead.

With Latias's help, Totodile moved at astonishing speed. It shot past Ethan and the others and opened a clear lead.

Misty and Ross stared in shock. Neither had imagined Totodile could move like that.

Fortunately for the race, another shadow invisible to everyone else swooped over Totodile. It drew Totodile and Latias together into a narrow side canal.

That canal was not part of the course. Entering it took them away from the finish and left Ash almost no chance of winning.

Apparently Latios did not want his sister meddling in a human competition. He pulled Latias and Totodile off the course until they vanished from sight.

With Ash gone, only Ethan, Misty, and Ross remained in the lead group.

The finish was close. Ethan knew it was time to give everything, and he tugged on Blastoise's reins.

Blastoise understood. Its eyes hardened, and it drove toward the finish with all its strength.

The surge threw up waves that rocked even Wailmer, larger though it was. Misty's little Corsola was affected all the more.

Neither Pokemon gave up. Both pushed as hard as they could. Misty had confidently predicted Ethan would win, but now that the finish was in sight, excitement took over. She gritted her teeth and urged Corsola to sprint.

All three Pokemon fought for every bit of speed. In the end, Blastoise crossed the finish line first by the narrowest margin!

Ethan let out a breath. Before he could even feel the joy of winning, a vast roar of cheers surrounded him.
