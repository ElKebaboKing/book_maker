Chapter 863: The Stone Poke Ball

What could a disembodied human soul do against a Ghost-type Pokemon?

It might wield some dark energy and have existed for countless years, but the answer was still nothing. Next to a Ghost-type Pokemon, the power of a human soul was like a newborn child: helpless in its grasp.

Gengar's shove sent the King of Pokelantis's spirit flying backward like a kite with a broken string. It traveled a long way before it finally stopped.

Who are you? Why have you trespassed in my bedchamber?

When one could not seize the initiative, one could at least claim the moral high ground.

His surprise attack had failed, and the blow had taught him that these intruders would be hard to handle. The king changed tactics, trying to hold them in place with words.

That might have worked on reckless explorers. Ethan and Brandon were not going to fall for it. His ancient manner of speech did sound strange to their ears, though.

Who are you? Brandon asked instead of answering.

Heh heh heh... The spirit laughed with imperious self-importance. I am the ruler of the world! The King of Pokelantis!

Brandon frowned and turned to Ethan and May. Then the legend was wrong. It wasn't Ho-Oh sealed in that stone orb. It was the King of Pokelantis himself!

You have heard my name? The spirit shook violently. Excellent. Offer me your Pokemon, and this time my empire shall conquer the entire world!

Several black rays flashed from him like tentacles, shooting toward the Poke Balls at Ethan's, May's, and Brandon's waists.

Unable to possess anyone, he seemed to have decided to steal their Pokemon instead. The ghostly tendrils got only halfway before Gengar waved a hand and stopped them again.

No matter how fiercely the king threatened them, he could achieve nothing with Gengar in front of him. It was a natural mismatch, and he had no way around it.

I see. Brandon watched the defeated tendrils withdraw into the spirit and gave a cold snort. He had pieced the affair together. What was sealed back then was your own ambition! The legend is false. You spread it yourself so people would think Ho-Oh was inside. When an unsuspecting, greedy explorer touched the orb, you could take over their body and live again!

That was exactly the King of Pokelantis's scheme. Brandon glanced toward the stone orb sitting quietly on the throne.

We have to seal him again. The mechanism must be inside that orb. If we can get hold of it, perhaps we'll find a way to contain him.

Ethan shook his head. There's no need to go to all that trouble.

When Brandon looked at him, Ethan shrugged. If we seal him in the orb again, what changes? He got out on his own just now. Or the next adventurer who finds it might touch it, and he'll try to steal that person's body. We'd be right back where we started.

May's eyes brightened. Then we could hide it! Somewhere nobody would ever find it!

It's not that simple. These ruins were well hidden, and we still found them. Throw the orb into the sea or bury it deep underground, and someone will eventually come across it. There's no place that's truly safe. Hiding it would only make him wait another few hundred or thousand years.

May hesitated. Then you mean...?

Brandon glanced at Ethan. He means to destroy the King of Pokelantis's soul completely.

Destroy his soul? May's eyes widened. But he was human. Wouldn't that be...?

She was neither naive nor given to misplaced pity. Even so, the king had a human soul. If Ethan destroyed it, wouldn't that amount to killing a person?

It was understandable that the thought troubled her. She had spent so long traveling with Ash. Whatever trouble they encountered, their adventures usually ended with everyone safe and happy. This was different.

And could it cause problems for Ethan? She would never tell anyone, of course, but Brandon was there too. If word spread, what would the law say? What would people think?

Ethan had no idea how much May had considered in that brief moment, or how concerned she was for him.

He's lived long enough, Ethan said calmly. Surviving as a soul for all this time goes against the natural order. A harmless spirit would be one thing. But he's still ambitious, and he refuses to rest. We might as well help him on his way before he can hurt anyone else.

Brandon was a Frontier Brain as well as a levelheaded explorer who understood the larger stakes. After a moment's thought, he agreed.

You're right. We can't leave him like this. What do you intend to do?

Leave it to the Pokemon best qualified for the job. Ethan pointed ahead. Gengar, he's yours.

Heh heh heh! All I have to do is make him disappear? Got it!

Gengar laughed so loudly that it looked more like the villain than the King of Pokelantis.

As it drew closer with a wicked grin, the king suddenly panicked. A bodiless soul could sense, deep within itself, why a Ghost-type Pokemon was dangerous to it.

He turned and tried to slip through the wall. But how could he escape a Gengar that had lived for more than two thousand years?

A terrifying pull caught his spirit and dragged it backward. No matter how he struggled, he could not resist it.

No!

He flew toward Gengar. Looking back, he found the talking Pokemon right beside him, its mouth wide open.

Gengar did not intend to eat the King of Pokelantis. It was no man-eating Pokemon, even if the man was only a soul. Besides, an evil old creature like this looked too filthy and unappetizing. Gengar would worry about getting a stomachache. Its open mouth was merely a way to draw him close.

What are you doing to me? the king demanded.

Then, to his horror, Gengar seized him with both hands. He had no body, yet pain tore through him all the same.

Gengar gave a sharp pull. The ancient emperor who had hoped to revive himself and resume his conquest screamed as his soul was ripped apart. It dissolved into the air.

May's eyelids twitched. Unable to watch, she turned her head away, but she said nothing. She knew the spirit was wicked, full of obsession and resentment, and could not be allowed to remain.

Fragments of the King of Pokelantis's soul vanished in scattered bursts of black light. He was gone from the world completely.

Gengar dusted off its hands and returned to Ethan's side. Now he won't hurt anyone else.

Ethan shook his head. His mood had grown a little heavy, but he did not regret the decision. I suppose the legend of the King of Pokelantis ends today.

You've done a good thing, Brandon said evenly, though he studied Ethan again.

The young man before him was calm and observant, bold without being careless, decisive enough to do what he believed necessary. This was no ordinary Trainer.

Though they had only just met, Brandon knew that someone with such resolve was not a minor figure. I haven't asked your names.

Ethan, who barely knew Brandon, had no idea he had made such an impression.

We forgot to introduce ourselves. I'm Ethan Vale, a Trainer. This is May, my traveling companion and a Coordinator.

May gave Brandon another small smile.

Ethan Vale... Brandon felt he had heard the name somewhere before.

He nodded. That's settled for now. Since you came to challenge me, let's go to the battlefield.

Brandon led the way out. After a few steps, he realized Ethan had not followed and turned back.

Ethan pointed at the stone orb on the throne and smiled. The orb isn't doing anything now. Mind if I take it?

He phrased it as a question, but hardly sounded as if he meant to wait for permission. Gardevoir raised a hand, and the next moment the orb rested securely in Ethan's palm. Brandon would not have had time to stop her even if he had tried.

He looked at Ethan again without expression, gave no answer, and turned to leave. Ethan took that as tacit consent. And even if Brandon objected, he had no intention of handing over something already in his possession.

He shrugged at May, and the two followed Brandon down a passage on the other side of the chamber.

Brandon had been exploring these ruins for days. He seemed to know every passage and trap in their first section. With him leading, Ethan and May had no need to worry about a wrong turn or a hidden mechanism. Within minutes, they reached the exit.

Along the way, Ethan examined the orb. He had already seen its appearance and had no need to study the markings again. It really was made of stone, cold and heavy in his hand. It felt as though it weighed at least a pound and a half, a world apart from a light modern Poke Ball. He could almost use it as a thrown weapon.

Imagine producing it in the middle of a battle and hurling it without warning. He could split the opposing Trainer's head open! That would be a victory on and off the battlefield, devastating his opponent in both mind and body.

He was only joking with himself. If he actually did that, Officer Jenny would probably put out a worldwide warrant for him the very next day.

May leaned over and took the orb to turn it in her hands. It's heavy! Is this really a Poke Ball from thousands of years ago? Apart from the material, it doesn't look that different from a modern one.

She was right: other than its material, the two kinds of ball seemed much alike. Ethan still refused to underestimate it.

It could hold a human soul, and according to the legend, it could capture Ho-Oh. He did not know why the king had failed to catch Ho-Oh and ended up sealing his own spirit inside instead. But if any part of the legend was reliable, this stone ball might be on the level of a Master Ball even now.

Whatever, Ethan said with a smile. You can never have too many Poke Balls. I'm sure I'll find a use for it someday. When the time comes, we'll learn what it's capable of.

They followed Brandon through a hidden, half-open stone doorway and emerged aboveground.

Sunlight struck their eyes. After so long in the dim ruins, they had to stand still for a while before the sting faded and they could see properly again.

The Battle Pyramid stood a few dozen meters ahead. From this close, its height was even more impressive. To think that a structure so enormous could fly high into the sky and travel for long periods was astonishing.

At its entrance Ethan saw someone whose presence surprised him, though it made sense: Scott, the owner of the entire Battle Frontier.

Scott broke into a broad smile as soon as Ethan appeared. Well, Mr. Vale, you've kept me waiting! I came as soon as I sent you the location because I didn't want to miss this spectacular battle. I never expected you to take so long to arrive!
