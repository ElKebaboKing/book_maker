Chapter 757: May's Request

Before the watching crowd, Skitty began slapping its own face with Double Slap. Each blow sounded painful.

Most spectators could not understand it and assumed this was another strange part of the act. Those who recognized Skitty's condition saw what May was doing: using the sting of the blows to break its confusion. It worked.

The performance was nearly over. At May's command, Skitty gathered itself and sprang high into the air, spinning as it rose. It used Blizzard, shaping a spiral of icy wind beneath it. Before the wind died, dozens of sharp, smooth icicles joined into a giant crystal.

Sunlight caught the structure and made it sparkle like a diamond. Its pure, transparent beauty drew gasps. It was a fine ending, and the audience's cheers and applause showed their appreciation.

Most importantly, Blizzard was Skitty's own move, not one borrowed through Assist. It landed gracefully on the crystal's peak and held its finishing pose.

Ethan and his companions looked up at the large screen. The numbers flickered, then settled on 79 points.

Seventy-nine. A dangerous score, though understandable. May's opening and finish had been strong, but the judges could hardly have missed her two obvious mistakes. Without that impressive ice crystal at the end, her score might have been lower.

May was disappointed on the stage. In the stands, Caroline and Max looked sorry for her.

That score isn't encouraging, Max sighed.

Ethan nodded. May's fate now depended on the others. If enough remaining Coordinators performed well, they would push her below the cutoff. She could only hope some of them scored lower.

Two more contestants performed. Then came one of the Festival's three leading favorites: Drew, who had placed third in the preliminaries.

He walked slowly onto the stage to loud cheers. Like May, his looks immediately won him admirers; young women in the crowd called out for him. Objectively, Drew had an impressive appearance, bearing, and Pokemon team. He was young too. If he and May had not clashed when they first met, they might have become friends.

Their relationship had eased somewhat as they traveled and met again. They no longer treated each other as enemies on sight, but an ordinary conversation was still difficult.

Drew used Masquerain this time. It had a bright blue body and four diamond-shaped wings. Its long, droplet-shaped head resembled a large nose, and paired with the eye-like markings on its wings, it gave the impression of an odd face.

Masquerain opened with Hidden Power. Light shone through its wings, creating a brilliant display overhead. Drew lowered his head, half-closed his eyes, and raised his right hand with practiced elegance. At that signal, Masquerain scattered the light across the entire arena.

Points of light drifted down. While the audience admired them, Masquerain filled the field with enormous bubbles, then used Silver Wind to send them floating away in a waterfall of wind and shimmering spheres.

It was magnificent. The audience applauded fiercely; some people rose to their feet. The visual impact even surpassed May's ice crystal. From the strength of the response, Drew's score would surely be high.

The numbers flashed and stopped at an astonishing result: 100 points. A perfect score.

There it is! Vivian cried. Our first perfect score of this Grand Festival!

Drew had earned it. His presentation and Masquerain's movements had been flawless, fluid, and beautiful. He had shown off his Pokemon's appeal to the fullest. He had performed even better than in the preliminaries.

The contrast with May was clear. Her appeal had been colorful, but it relied mostly on moves borrowed from other Pokemon rather than displaying Skitty's own strengths. That departed from the Festival's purpose and was the main reason her score had been so low. She still had room to grow.

Ethan leaned back and watched a stray bubble drift toward him. Drew's performance would affect May, but it also put tremendous pressure on everyone scheduled to follow him. Those contestants had drawn an unlucky position.

Of course, a strong competitor would face that pressure and turn it into drive.

Drew left the stage gracefully. More than a dozen Coordinators followed, many clearly shaken by his perfect score. Mistakes multiplied. Some could not even perform at their usual level and received low marks. Inadvertently, Drew had helped May's chances.

Then Robert, the favorite to win the entire Festival, appeared. He was one of those competitors who became stronger under pressure. After Drew's triumph, Robert gave an equally spectacular performance and earned another perfect 100.

Stunned silence gave way to a roar of delight. One perfect score at a Festival would have been remarkable. Two in the same round left the audience amazed.

The contestants who remained seemed to crumble under the weight of both performances. No one stood out before the round ended, much less achieved another high score.

Wow... Max scratched his head, relieved for his sister. Good thing May went on before those two. She might actually make the next round.

The turn in her fortunes had come quickly, but they still needed the official rankings before they could celebrate.

At last, Vivian made the announcement. The Coordinators who passed the first round and will advance to the Contest battles are...

The large screen changed to a list of portraits, revealing the qualifiers one by one. Robert was first, and Drew had moved past the woman who placed ahead of him in the preliminaries to take second. Both had scored 100 this round, so their earlier rankings appeared to break the tie. Robert had performed better in the preliminaries, after all.

Other strong entrants followed. The woman who had outranked Drew yesterday, however, did not appear. The random order had placed her after Robert. Seeing both his and Drew's perfect performances had shaken her so badly that she gave a poor appeal and a low score. Despite her excellent preliminaries, she was eliminatedperhaps the unluckiest competitor in the Festival.

Harley's portrait appeared soon enough. His 89 points were well above the scores of many shaken contestants.

The list kept filling. In the competitors' waiting room, the mood grew heavier with each name. May's palms turned damp. When places 29, 30, and 31 passed without her portrait, her heart seemed to leap into her throat.

Then number 32 appeared. It was her own smiling face. She clenched her fist in excitement. With 79 points and help from other contestants' mistakes, she had narrowly reached the next round.

Misty and Max rejoiced in the stands. Caroline was so happy she secretly wiped tears from her eyes. No one wanted to see May succeed more than her mother.

After the qualifiers came the pairings for tomorrow's Contest battles. This draw was part of each day's proceedings, but the matchups had to be settled now.

The screen changed again, showing two frames with portraits cycling at random. The first two pairs were chosen. On the third draw, the portraits slowed and stopped on May and Harley.

Oh? Misty raised her eyebrows. That's interesting. Did the organizers do it on purpose? Do they know about May and Harley?

Of course not. Ethan laughed and shook his head. It's coincidence. With only 32 entrants and sixteen pairings, they had a fair chance of drawing each other.

...

Knock, knock, knock.

After dinner, Ethan showered and was getting ready for bed in a bath towel when someone knocked at his door. Curious, he opened it and found May outside.

Etha May began, then saw his wet hair, bare chest, and towel. Her face flushed.

Ethan was embarrassed too. He tightened the towel and gave her an apologetic smile. I thought you were Misty.

Misty? Did he mean he would be comfortable answering the door like this for her?

May seemed lost in thought. What brings you here so late? Ethan asked.

She gathered herself. Um... would you teach me how to battle?

Ethan blinked. She wanted him to teach her? She had Ash and Misty nearby. Why come to him?

He glanced outside. Darkness had fallen, with the moon and stars high overhead. It was not very late, but few people were walking around the dormitories.

Of course. Wait a moment while I get dressed.

When he came out, May was waiting. She had not invited anyone else, and Ethan did not suggest it. He followed her quietly downstairs and toward the nearby outdoor battle field. Far off, the snack street was still bright and noisy, a sharp contrast with the quiet around them.

Neither spoke until they reached the closest field. Then Ethan could not hold back his curiosity. Why ask me when Ash and Misty are right here?

They had known each other for a long time, yet this was the first time they had been alone together. It felt a little strange.

May lowered her head, looking shy. Because you're the strongest person I know.

Ethan had not expected such high praise. Did she even rate him above her father? He rubbed his nose. Being complimented to his face by a pretty girl was gratifying, and stirred another feeling he could not quite name.

You don't need to be so formal with me, he said with a smile. You're friends with Misty and Ash. Call me Ethan, like they do.
